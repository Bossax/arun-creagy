**Disaster Risk Management Phases**
The disaster management cycle in Thailand is structured around four distinct phases: preparedness, mitigation, response, and recovery [1, 2]. 

**Practical Application of Each Phase**
*   **Preparedness:** Focuses on establishing early warning systems (EWS) equipped with automated telemetry, sea-level gauges, and rain gauges, as well as developing volunteer networks and trained personnel to disseminate alerts down to the community level [3, 4].
*   **Mitigation:** Involves analyzing and mapping hazard data (such as floods, landslides, and earthquakes) [5, 6], investing in hard infrastructure like embankments, utilizing ecosystem-based protections like mangrove forests, and mainstreaming risk reduction into overall sectoral development [7, 8].
*   **Response:** During a disaster, the Emergency Operation Center (EOC) is activated to act as the Incident Commander [9]. Local governments function as first responders, and authority is transferred to provincial or national levels if the disaster exceeds local capacity [9]. In large-scale disasters, 18 Emergency Support Functions (ESFs) are mobilized to manage specific tasks like logistics, medical services, and military deployment [9].
*   **Recovery:** Driven by the "Build Back Better" (BBB) principle during rehabilitation and reconstruction, this phase aims to improve building resilience against future hazards and climate change rather than re-establishing previous vulnerabilities [9, 10]. It utilizes tools like Post-Disaster Needs Assessments (PDNA) to design recovery plans, coordinate financial loans, and construct more weather-resilient infrastructure [11].

**Role of Institutions and Coordination**
The National Committee for Disaster Prevention and Mitigation is the primary body guiding policy and decision-making, supported by an inter-ministerial committee that handles technical and managerial implementation [12]. The Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) is the central coordinating agency, providing training, formulating guidelines, and operating regional and provincial centers nationwide [12]. At the sub-national level, local administrative units hold full responsibility for risk management in their jurisdictions and are required to allocate specific budgets generated from local revenues for these activities [13]. To unify efforts, the government has established disaster risk reduction (DRR) focal points across various line ministries and provincial agencies, helping coordinate and mainstream activities into broad sectoral plans [7]. 

**Overall DRM Cycle Description**
The report describes the overarching disaster risk management cycle as a shift "towards holistic risk management" [1]. This transition is anchored by legislative frameworks and procedures that "support activities under all of the phases in the disaster management cycle from preparedness to mitigation, response and recovery" [2]. The cycle relies heavily on "multi-agency and multi-sectoral approaches" to achieve integrated emergency management and inclusive resilience building [2].
