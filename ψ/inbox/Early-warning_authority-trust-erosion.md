# Dynamic
The dynamic you are describing is a classic breakdown in risk communication, driven by mismatched incentives, cognitive biases, and the structural differences between bureaucratic science and viral social media.

### 1. Asymmetric Accountability

Official agencies like the Thai Meteorological Department (TMD) operate under legal, institutional, and economic constraints:

- **The "Cry Wolf" fear:** If an agency issues an extreme red alert and orders evacuations or business shutdowns for a storm that dissipates, they face immediate backlash from businesses, local governments, and the public for economic disruption.
    
- **Bureaucratic sign-offs:** Official bulletins usually require hierarchical review, standard operating procedures, and rigid meteorological criteria before release. This makes the language dry, cautious, and slow.
    
- **The influencer advantage:** Online accounts have **zero accountability for false positives**. If an influencer predicts a historic deluge and nothing happens, the audience simply forgets within 48 hours or credits luck. If the deluge does occur, they take full credit for "warning everyone when officials slept."
    
      
### 2. Information Packaging: PDF vs. Narrative

- **Raw vs. Actionable:** Official releases often rely on formal terminology, PDF tables, broad regional percentages (e.g., "scattered thundershowers covering 60% of the area"), and administrative boundaries. To an anxious resident, this does not answer the core question: _Will my street flood at 5 PM?_
    
- **The Social Media Lens:** Page admins pull single-run, worst-case model projections (like deterministic runs from ECMWF or GFS, or raw Windy visuals), add dramatic overlays, urgent red arrows, and emotionally resonant hooks. They turn probabilistic uncertainty into a definitive, urgent story.

### 3. Cognitive Biases at Play

- **Confirmation & Recency Bias:** When an influencer gets one dramatic event right (the "jackpot"), people remember the visual of them being right when disaster hit. Their dozens of false alarms during the dry spells fade from memory.

- **The Texas Sharpshooter Fallacy:** Influencers cast very wide nets—posting frequent worst-case maps. Inevitably, one storm hits hard somewhere. That hit is highlighted as proof of foresight, ignoring the cluster of misses.

- **Loss Aversion:** Humans fear loss far more than they appreciate routine accuracy. In a state of anxiety about property damage or flash floods, a message that over-warns feels safer and more caring than an official release that sounds detached or conservative.
    
### 4. Institutional Inertia vs. Modern Early Warning
The gap isn't just about meteorology; it is about **impact-based forecasting**. Modern disaster risk reduction requires shifting from _what the weather will be_ to _what the weather will do_ at the neighborhood scale. When official channels fail to bridge data to hyper-local risk in real time, non-official voices step into the vacuum.



# Key words
### Disaster Sociology & Institutional Trust
- **Crisis Informatics:** How people use social platforms, mobile apps, and informal networks to share, curate, and filter information during emergencies.
- **Information Vacuum:** What happens when formal channels delay updates—unverified actors rush in to supply certainty.
- **Social Amplification of Risk Framework (SARF):** How risk messages get magnified, dampened, or distorted as they pass through media and social filters.
- **Institutional Distrust / Institutional Legitimacy:** The erosion of trust when a state agency's performance falls short of public expectations.
    
### Cognitive & Behavioral Biases

- **Cry Wolf Effect (False Alarm Effect):** The belief that frequent false alarms cause public apathy, and the institutional fear agencies have of triggering it.
- **Outcome Bias:** Judging the quality of a forecast solely on the eventual result rather than the probabilistic evidence available at the time.
- **Heuristics in Uncertainty:** Mental shortcuts people take when evaluating complex probabilistic models under stress.
- **Availability Heuristic & Gambler’s Fallacy:** Why a single dramatic hit makes an amateur forecaster appear consistently reliable.
    
      
    

### Early Warning & Communication Design

- **Impact-Based Forecasting (IBF):** Moving from reporting physical hazard metrics (e.g., "50 mm of rain") to communicating community consequences (e.g., "road impassable by 4 PM").
- **Last-Mile Warning Communication:** Getting alerts from scientific models into clear, actionable decisions for end-users on the ground.
- **Actionable Risk Communication:** Structuring bulletins so people know exact trigger points: _who, what, where, and when to act_.
- **Citizen Science & Shadow Forecasting:** Grassroots weather watchers and independent social media forecasters who rival official agencies.

# Risk communicator specialist

Based on the literature, particularly WHO frameworks and public health emergency guidance, here are the **core skills and functions of a risk communication agency**:

## Core Functions of Risk Communication Agencies

### 1. **Risk Assessment and Monitoring**

- **Monitor and sense risks**: Continuously scan for emerging hazards, threats, and public concerns[[tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10410236.2025.2582778)]
- **Analyze information**: Evaluate scientific data, assess vulnerabilities, and identify affected populations[[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/0e012d0f-1f09-4098-8a80-b3859a4dc459/content)][[publichealthontario](https://www.publichealthontario.ca/-/media/documents/f/2016/fundamentals-risk-comm.pdf?la=en)]
- **Context assessment**: Understand social, cultural, economic, political, and security contexts of at-risk populations[[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)]

### 2. **Information Management**

- **Organize information**: Structure complex technical data into accessible formats for different audiences[[publichealthontario](https://www.publichealthontario.ca/-/media/documents/f/2016/fundamentals-risk-comm.pdf?la=en)]
- **Develop authoritative messaging**: Create accurate, workable, and acceptable information based on evolving scientific knowledge[[tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10410236.2025.2582778)][[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)]
- **Maintain common narrative**: Ensure coherent and consistent messaging across all platforms and partners[[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)]

### 3. **Communication and Dissemination**
- **Real-time information exchange**: Facilitate two-way communication between experts/officials and people at risk[[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/0e012d0f-1f09-4098-8a80-b3859a4dc459/content)][[who](https://www.who.int/emergencies/risk-communications)][[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/259807/9789241550208-eng.pdf)]
- **Multi-channel delivery**: Use all relevant communication platforms (media, internet, social media, community meetings, hotlines) preferred by target populations[[publichealthontario](https://www.publichealthontario.ca/-/media/documents/f/2016/fundamentals-risk-comm.pdf?la=en)][[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)]
- **Local language adaptation**: Deliver messages in local languages and culturally appropriate formats[[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)]

### 4. **Community Engagement**

- **Stakeholder engagement**: Engage communities, leaders, and partners at national and local levels[[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)][[who](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240092501)]
- **Behavior change promotion**: Encourage informed decision-making, positive behavior change, and protective measures[[tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10410236.2025.2582778)][[tdr.who](https://tdr.who.int/home/our-work/global-engagement/an-introduction-to-risk-communication)]
- **Trust maintenance**: Build and maintain public trust throughout preparedness, response, and recovery phases[[tdr.who](https://tdr.who.int/home/our-work/global-engagement/an-introduction-to-risk-communication)]

### 5. **Listening and Feedback**

- **Community feedback mechanisms**: Establish systems to regularly collect community concerns, perceptions, and questions[[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)]
- **Misinformation monitoring**: Track rumors, misinformation, and risky behaviors to adjust response operations[[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)][[journals.lww](https://journals.lww.com/wsep/fulltext/2020/09010/strengthening_risk_communication_systems_for.3.aspx)]
- **Adaptive response**: Use feedback to refine messages and interventions[[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)]

### 6. **Coordination and Partnership**
- **Internal coordination**: Work with technical experts, response agencies, and leadership to ensure coherent messaging[[tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10410236.2025.2582778)][[iris.who](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d80f036-e529-430c-92ec-1a11b20a6757/content)]
- **Partner coordination**: Collaborate with media, journalists, civil society, and other stakeholders[[journals.lww](https://journals.lww.com/wsep/fulltext/2020/09010/strengthening_risk_communication_systems_for.3.aspx)][[who](https://www.who.int/activities/communicating-risk-in-public-health-emergencies/communicating-risk-in-public-health-emergencies)]
- **Capacity building**: Train key personnel, engage journalists, and run simulation exercises[[who](https://www.who.int/activities/communicating-risk-in-public-health-emergencies/communicating-risk-in-public-health-emergencies)]

### 7. **Strategic Planning**
- **Policy and strategy development**: Establish risk communication policies, strategies, and plans[[journals.lww](https://journals.lww.com/wsep/fulltext/2020/09010/strengthening_risk_communication_systems_for.3.aspx)][[who](https://www.who.int/activities/communicating-risk-in-public-health-emergencies/communicating-risk-in-public-health-emergencies)]
- **Preparedness activities**: Conduct pre-emergency planning, training, and system testing[[tdr.who](https://tdr.who.int/home/our-work/global-engagement/an-introduction-to-risk-communication)][[journals.lww](https://journals.lww.com/wsep/fulltext/2020/09010/strengthening_risk_communication_systems_for.3.aspx)]
- **Recovery support**: Continue communication post-emergency to support recovery and learning[[tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10410236.2025.2582778)][[tdr.who](https://tdr.who.int/home/our-work/global-engagement/an-introduction-to-risk-communication)]

|Agency|Primary Focus|Communication Type|Time Scale|
|---|---|---|---|
|**DCCE**|Climate policy, adaptation, mitigation|Strategic, educational, awareness-building, public participation|Long-term (years–decades)|
|**TMD**|Weather, climate monitoring, forecasting|Technical warnings, forecasts, climate data|Short to medium-term (hours–seasons)|
|**DDPM**|Disaster response, emergency management|Operational alerts, mass notification, evacuation orders|Immediate (hours–days)|

## Summary

DCCE's risk communication approach is **strategic and educational** rather than operational/emergency-focused (like DDPM) or technical/forecasting-focused (like TMD). DCCE specializes in:

- **Climate literacy and public awareness**
- **Policy communication** (NAP, NDC, BTR)
- **Climate risk information systems**
- **Multi-stakeholder engagement** (education, training, participation)
- **Long-term adaptation communication**

This makes DCCE particularly relevant for your work in **climate adaptation planning, climate services development, and climate risk communication strategy** at the policy and programmatic levels.
