A high level of [biodiversity in cities](https://www.c40knowledgehub.org/s/article/Why-biodiversity-matters-for-cities-and-the-climate) improves city resilience and ecosystem stability. [Natural infrastructure](https://www.c40knowledgehub.org/s/article/Nature-based-solutions-How-cities-can-use-nature-to-manage-climate-risks) and biodiverse ecosystems act as buffers to climate hazards, helping to regulate climate impacts and protect urban residents and infrastructure from their impacts.

The City Biodiversity Index (CBI) (also known as the Singapore Index on Cities’ Biodiversity) is a self-assessment tool for cities to evaluate and monitor the progress of their biodiversity conservation efforts against their own individual baselines.

The index is comprised of two parts:

1. **The ‘Profile of the City’, which provides the city’s background information.**
2. **28 indicators that measure native biodiversity, ecosystem services provided by biodiversity, and governance and management of biodiversity in a city.** These indicators are based on guidelines and methodology provided in the Handbook on the [Singapore Index on Cities’ Biodiversity](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/-/media/nparks-real-content/biodiversity/singapore-index/handbook-on-the-singapore-index-on-cities-biodiversity-cbd-ts98.ashx).

The online tool uses a quantitative scoring system where each of the 28 biodiversity indicators is assigned a scoring range between zero and four points, with a city scored out of 112 points in total. The year in which a city embarks on this scoring system is taken as the baseline year, and future scores are measured against the baseline to chart progress in conserving and increasing biodiversity.

The index can work in three ways to help cities to accomplish their biodiversity goals, as described below.

- First, by creating baseline measurements and quantifiable scores and targets, the Index builds a data foundation upon which future action can be planned and measured.
- Second, the tool serves as a platform upon which biodiversity awareness raising exercises can be launched and the public can hold a city accountable to commitments.
- Finally, the Index can act as portal among various departments within city governance, academics, NGOs, schools, the public and businesses, hence, encouraging better communication, stronger networks and more co-operation, through data collection and sharing of mutual goals.
