Under the GHG Protocol, whether pond methane is counted depends on what the pond actually is: if it is a **wastewater treatment pond or anaerobic lagoon**, it belongs under wastewater methane; if it is a **flooded land / reservoir-like water body**, it belongs under flooded land methane; and land-use change (LUC) is counted only if the pond was created by converting land to flooded land. The organizational boundary is then set by your chosen consolidation approach: under equity share you count emissions in proportion to ownership, while under operational control you count 100% of emissions from ponds you operate, even if you do not own them fully.australianpork+1

## Which emissions to count

- **CH4 from wastewater**: count this when the pond is part of anaerobic wastewater treatment, lagoon treatment, sludge treatment, or similar waste effluent treatment systems. EPA’s wastewater treatment guidance shows that anaerobic lagoons generate methane from organic material in wastewater and are treated as a wastewater source category.[nepis.epa](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P100BBRV.TXT)
    
- **CH4 from flooded land**: count this when the pond is better characterized as flooded land or reservoir-type flooded area; the GHG Protocol Land Sector guidance says companies can estimate methane from reservoirs and other flooded lands using IPCC Tier 1 methods and activity data.[ghgprotocol](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/Land-Sector-and-Removals-Guidance-2022-Draft-Pilot-Testing-Review-Part2-2022.9.1-Archival.pdf)
    
- **LUC / land conversion**: count this only if the pond was created by converting previously non-flooded land into flooded land, because IPCC flooded-land methods explicitly cover land converted to flooded land as well as flooded land remaining flooded.ipcc+1
    

## Boundary choice

- **Equity share**: you include emissions according to your economic interest in the pond operation, not necessarily 100% of the facility emissions.[australianpork](https://australianpork.com.au/sites/default/files/2021-06/FACT-SHEET-Reducing-GHG-Emissions-from-Piggeries.pdf)
    
- **Operational control**: you include 100% of emissions from ponds over which your company or subsidiary has authority to implement operating policies, even if legal ownership is shared or partial.[australianpork](https://australianpork.com.au/sites/default/files/2021-06/FACT-SHEET-Reducing-GHG-Emissions-from-Piggeries.pdf)
    
- The Corporate Standard allows companies to choose among equity share, financial control, or operational control for the whole inventory, and the same consolidation approach should be applied consistently across Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3.[australianpork](https://australianpork.com.au/sites/default/files/2021-06/FACT-SHEET-Reducing-GHG-Emissions-from-Piggeries.pdf)
    

## Acceptable method and data

For flooded-land methane, the Land Sector guidance points to **IPCC Tier 1 emission factors and activity data** as an acceptable approach; the broader IPCC wetlands guidance also supports estimating CH4 from flooded land using established equations and emission factors. For wastewater ponds, accepted inputs in the EPA-style method include wastewater flow, COD or BOD5, methane conversion factors, biogas recovery, leakage, and operating time, which is consistent with using measured or well-documented site data rather than a single generic factor.ipcc-nggip.iges.or+2

## Residence time

Residence time is generally **relevant and acceptable for wastewater ponds**, because wastewater methane generation depends on how long organic matter stays under anaerobic conditions; EPA’s wastewater guidance explicitly uses operating time and process characterization, and in practice residence time is part of the engineering basis for estimating methane generation in lagoons. For flooded land / reservoir-style ponds, residence time is not usually the core required data item; the GHG Protocol framing there is more about flooded area, methane emission factors, water-body characteristics, and IPCC method selection.ghgprotocol+2

## Practical rule

A practical way to classify your pond is:

1. If it treats effluent or organic wastewater, treat it as **wastewater methane**.
    
2. If it is a purposely flooded area or reservoir, treat it as **flooded land methane**.
    
3. If the pond was created by converting land to water, include **LUC-related flooded-land emissions** where applicable.
    
4. Then apply either **equity share** or **operational control** consistently across the inventory.nepis.epa+2
