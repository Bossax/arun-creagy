# Deeper adoption research — why NbS does not become concrete at scale

**Additional search date:** 2026-07-31  
**Focus:** implementation gap, governance, finance, public administration, project pipelines, and the supplied IUCN Western Balkans case.

## Brave Search CLI findings

### Implementation gap and governance

- Implementation-gap review: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301479725019838
  - Search result reports 301 enablers and 307 barriers across institutional, legal, regulatory, social, and economic dimensions.
  - Repeated enabling factors: inclusive stakeholder engagement and true co-design, evidence on performance and co-benefits, quantitative cost-benefit analysis, knowledge products, NbS expertise, and earmarked funds.
  - Repeated barriers: lack of expertise and evidence, stakeholder conflict, grey-infrastructure path dependency, and fragmented governance.
- PubMed record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40449422/
  - Search result highlights polycentric governance as a potential enabler and path dependency toward grey options as a limiting factor.
- Local-policy integration case study: https://ecologyandsociety.org/vol28/iss2/art25/
  - Search result identifies fragmented responsibilities across technical and administrative services and rigid urban-regime conditions as adoption barriers.
- Policy-instrument study: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264275124005626
  - Search result identifies limited knowledge and practical experience, land-tenure complexity, and insufficient financing instruments as barriers to institutional embedding.

### Public administration, procurement, and finance

- OECD climate-resilient infrastructure chapter: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/infrastructure-for-a-climate-resilient-future_a74a45b0-en/full-report/component-7.html
  - Traditional policy, regulatory, and financing frameworks often treat grey options as simpler, less risky, and more familiar.
  - NbS often require different spatial and temporal scales and specialised skills for planning, implementation, and maintenance.
  - Enabling conditions include policy and institutional integration, technical training, stakeholder involvement across the project life cycle, improved appraisal and procurement, and funding mechanisms.
  - Funding is fragmented across sources, creating a patchwork that project owners must navigate.
  - Project portfolios, blended finance, green bonds, payments for ecosystem services, and land-value capture can help create investable pipelines.
- OECD infrastructure governance: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/improving-the-landscape-for-sustainable-infrastructure-financing_bc2757cd-en/full-report/component-5.html
  - Government priorities: align long-term infrastructure vision with environmental goals, strengthen project selection and appraisal, and build public-sector capacity.
- UNEP Finance Initiative: https://www.unepfi.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Primer-1-Introduction-to-Nature-based-Solutions.pdf
  - Search result identifies unclear definitions and weak science-based guidance as barriers to investment and calls for clearer standards and evidence.

## Supplied IUCN source

### Advancing Climate Resilience through Nature-based Solutions in the Western Balkans

https://iucn.org/news/202409/advancing-climate-resilience-through-nature-based-solutions-western-balkans

- The ADAPT initiative connected NbS adoption to a regional conference, policy integration, practical pilots, and disaster-risk reduction.
- It produced knowledge materials, policy guidance, training, and capacity building for local communities, practitioners, and decision-makers.
- It developed six NbS scoping studies and a regional compendium of best practices.
- It trained more than 120 regional participants and 48 decision-makers and produced comparative policy analysis.
- Pilot projects in Serbia and Albania made the approach concrete through site-specific forest restoration, erosion control, flood-risk reduction, livestock water security, and local socio-economic benefits.
- Implementation lesson: adoption becomes more concrete when a programme connects evidence and knowledge products to policy guidance, trained actors, local pilots, and a pathway for scaling into regional policy.
