## 1. Strategic Mandate & Policy Context (Feeding S - Situation)
* **National Policy Relevance**: Under Thailand's National Adaptation Plan (NAP) for Agriculture and Water Resources, the Lower Chao Phraya River Basin (CPRB) represents the epicenter of national off-season rice production (นาปรัง) and rural livelihoods.
* **Geographic & Experimental Scope**: Centered at Pathum Thani Province (Pathum Thani Rice Research Center and Asian Institute of Technology Agricultural Research Farm: 14.083°N, 100.608°E).
* **Baseline Context (2010–2022)**: Intensive irrigated rice systems operating on clay soil profiles, utilizing high-yielding photoperiod-insensitive cultivars (specifically RD57 / พันธุ์ กข57).
* **The Emerging Challenge**: While total annual rainfall remains relatively steady, rising ambient temperatures (both day and night) impose direct physiological heat stress on crops and significantly drive up evapotranspiration, challenging the long-term sustainability of irrigated rice ecosystems.

---

## 2. Core Tension & Policy Paradox (Feeding C - Complication & Q - Question)
* **The Double Squeeze**:
  1. *Yield Collapse*: Under the severe climate scenario (SSP5-8.5), unadapted rice yields decline by **11.76% by mid-century** and **32.76% by late-century** (crashing to 3,194 kg/ha compared to the 4,749.77 kg/ha baseline).
  2. *Surging Water Consumption*: Rather than decreasing alongside yields, irrigation water requirements surge by **53.34%** (reaching 185.66 mm vs 121.08 mm baseline) due to intense crop transpiration and soil evaporation.
* **The Structural Paradox**: Can traditional farm-level management alone sustain rice production, or is genetic restructuring indispensable?
  * *Agronomic Management*: Raking in early planting (1–2 weeks) and optimizing nitrogen timing/dose (1.5x baseline) yields near-term gains (+5.6%–6.6%), and lowering soil irrigation thresholds saves up to **90% of water** without yield loss. However, management adjustments quickly hit biological limits once ambient temperatures exceed critical physiological thresholds.
  * *Genetic Restructuring*: Developing heat-tolerant cultivars (enhancing the THOT trait) delivers the single largest yield recovery (**+37.9% under late-century SSP5-8.5**), yet heat tolerance alone does not decrease water usage. Conversely, traits that save water (early flowering) truncate the vegetative phase and sacrifice total yield.
* **Strategic Question (Q)**: How can DCCE and agricultural agencies formulate an integrated "Genotype-by-Management" (G×M) adaptation framework that resolves the trade-off between yield protection and water conservation?

## 4. Modular Fact Blocks (Toulmin Evidence Bricks)

### Modular Block A: Experimental Baseline & Model Rigor
* **Baseline Period**: 2010–2022 (13-year continuous weather records from AIT onsite and Pathum Thani meteorological stations).
* **Baseline Measurements**: Average annual rainfall 1,345.0 mm; mean Tmax 33.75°C; mean Tmin 25.30°C. Baseline rice yield is **4,749.77 kg/ha**; baseline irrigation requirement is **121.08 mm**.
* **Validation Statistics**:
  * Calibration (2017–2018 experiment): Willmott’s agreement index $d = 0.85$, $R^2 = 0.73$, RMSE = 17.91%, Percent Error (PE) = 11.73%.
  * Evaluation (2021–2022 experiment): $d = 0.85$, $R^2 = 0.75$, RMSE = 9.95%, PE = 8.49%.
* **Climate Projections**: 5 GCMs (ACCESS-ESM1-5, CNRM-CM6-1, EC-Earth3-Veg, MPI-ESM1-2-LR, MRI-ESM2-0). Projected average temperatures rise by ~2°C (SSP2-4.5) and ~4°C (SSP5-8.5) by late-century (2071–2100), with April daytime maximums routinely exceeding 40°C.

### Modular Block B: Unadapted Rice Yield Projections
* **Near-Future (2023–2040)**:
  * SSP1-2.6: 4,378 kg/ha (-7.83%, z = -1.25)
  * SSP2-4.5: 4,595 kg/ha (-3.26%, z = -0.52)
  * SSP5-8.5: 4,477 kg/ha (-5.74%, z = -0.92)
* **Mid-Century (2041–2070)**:
  * SSP1-2.6: 4,423 kg/ha (-6.88%, z = -1.10)
  * SSP2-4.5: 4,375 kg/ha (-7.89%, z = -1.26)
  * SSP5-8.5: 4,191 kg/ha (-11.76%, z = -1.88, significant at 10%)
* **Late-Century (2071–2100)**:
  * SSP1-2.6: 4,335 kg/ha (-8.73%, z = -1.40)
  * SSP2-4.5: 4,238 kg/ha (-10.78%, z = -1.72, significant at 10%)
  * SSP5-8.5: **3,194 kg/ha (-32.76%, z = -5.24, highly significant at 1%)** — a catastrophic one-third drop in productivity.

### Modular Block C: Surging Irrigation Water Demand
* **Near-Future (2023–2040)**:
  * SSP1-2.6: 143.56 mm (+18.57%)
  * SSP2-4.5: 159.98 mm (+32.13%)
  * SSP5-8.5: 145.73 mm (+20.36%)
* **Mid-Century (2041–2070)**:
  * SSP1-2.6: 139.38 mm (+15.11%)
  * SSP2-4.5: 158.00 mm (+30.49%)
  * SSP5-8.5: 174.69 mm (+44.28%)
* **Late-Century (2071–2100)**:
  * SSP1-2.6: 143.91 mm (+18.85%)
  * SSP2-4.5: 157.63 mm (+30.19%)
  * SSP5-8.5: **185.66 mm (+53.34%, z = 1.96, significant at 1%)** — over 1.5 times the historical baseline water requirement.

### Modular Block D: Field Management Interventions (Short/Medium-Term)
1. **Planting Date Shift**:
   * Advancing planting 1–2 weeks earlier (from baseline 20 July to 13 July or 6 July) improves yield and reduces seasonal water requirement by shifting grain-filling into slightly cooler windows. Delaying planting by 1–2 weeks (to 27 July or 3 August) causes consistent yield loss.
2. **Fertilizer Management**:
   * Advancing panicle initiation N application 1–2 weeks earlier (to 13 or 6 September) increases grain yield by 5.6%–6.6% under SSP5-8.5 without demanding additional water.
   * Increasing fertilizer dose to **1.5 times baseline (112.5 kg N/ha, 90 kg P/ha)** optimizes yield response. Doses higher than 1.5x (2.0x–4.0x) plateau due to genetic limits, offering no extra yield.
3. **Irrigation Threshold Reduction (Controlled Deficit / AWD)**:
   * Baseline threshold is 50% remaining soil water.
   * Reducing to **45% threshold**: saves ~30% irrigation water while keeping yield completely stable.
   * Reducing to **40% threshold**: saves ~55% irrigation water with stable yield.
   * Reducing to **30% threshold**: saves **up to 90% irrigation water** with stable yield across all climate scenarios and timelines.

### Modular Block E: Genetic Ideotype Innovations (Long-Term Structural Resilience)
1. **Heat Tolerance (THOT Trait)**:
   * Elevating THOT by 5% to 15% (raising high-temperature threshold from 30°C to 32°C–36°C) delivers the single highest yield gain: **up to +37.9% increase** under SSP5-8.5 in late century.
2. **High Spikelet Potential (G1 Trait)**:
   * Increasing potential spikelet number coefficient G1 by 15% (from 65 to 74) improves grain yield by **+8.3%** in late-century SSP5-8.5 (to 3.46 t/ha).
3. **Grain Filling Extension (P5 Trait)**:
   * Extending grain filling duration P5 by 15% yields a modest **+1.1% gain** (3.23 t/ha), but increases seasonal crop water use.
4. **Early Flowering Trade-offs (P1 Trait)**:
   * Decreasing basic vegetative phase P1 (to flower earlier and escape late drought) reduces water demand slightly, but causes net yield loss due to inadequate biomass accumulation.
5. **Drought Tolerance (RWUMX Trait)**:
   * Modifying root water uptake parameter RWUMX showed negligible impact under irrigated basin conditions, confirming that heat—not root water uptake capacity—is the primary bottleneck in irrigated rice.
