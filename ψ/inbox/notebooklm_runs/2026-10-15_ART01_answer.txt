### **1. Core Models, Equations, and Calibration/Validation Metrics**

* **Core Models**:
  * **EPIC (Environmental Policy Integrated Climate) Model (v0810)**: Used to simulate crop growth, crop yield, and stress responses under changing atmospheric and climatic conditions [1].
  * **CROPWAT 8.0 Model**: Used to calculate reference evapotranspiration (\\(ET_0\\)), crop evapotranspiration (\\(ET_c\\)), and effective rainfall (\\(P_{eff}\\)) to determine crop water footprints [2-4].

* **Core Equations**:
  1. **EPIC Crop Yield (\\(YLD\\))** [1]:
     \\[YLD = HI \cdot \sum_{i=1}^n \left[ WA \cdot RA_i \left(1 - \exp(-0.65 \cdot LAI_i)\right) \cdot 5000 \right]\\]
     *(where \\(HI\\) = harvest index, \\(WA\\) = biomass-energy ratio / radiation-use efficiency, \\(RA\\) = solar radiation, \\(LAI\\) = leaf area index)* [1].
  2. **Biomass Regulation under Stress** [5]:
     \\[\Delta B = \Delta B_p \cdot REG\\]
     *(where \\(\Delta B_p\\) is potential biomass and \\(REG\\) is the minimum stress factor among water, temperature, nutrient, and aeration stress)* [5].
  3. **Temperature Stress (\\(TS_i\\))** [6]:
     \\[TS_i = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} \cdot \frac{TG_i - Tb_j}{To_j - Tb_j}\right)\\]
     *(where \\(TG\\) is average soil surface temperature, \\(Tb\\) is base crop temperature, and \\(To\\) is optimal crop temperature)* [6].
  4. **CROPWAT 8.0 Reference Evapotranspiration (\\(ET_0\\), FAO Penman–Monteith)** [3]:
     \\[ET_0 = \frac{0.408 \Delta (R_n - G) + \gamma \frac{900}{T + 273} U_2 (e_s - e_a)}{\Delta + \gamma (1 + 0.34 U_2)}\\]
  5. **Water Footprint (\\(WF_{total}\\)) Calculation** [2, 7, 8]:
     \\[WF_{total} = WF_{green} + WF_{blue} + WF_{grey} = \frac{CWU_{green}}{Y} + \frac{CWU_{blue}}{Y} + WF_{grey}\\]
     \\[WF_{grey} = \frac{\alpha \cdot \sum (N + ON) / (C_{max} - C_{nat})}{Y}\\]
     *(where \\(\alpha = 0.1\\) for nitrogen leaching/runoff, \\(C_{max} < 5\text{ mg L}^{-1}\\) \\(\text{NO}_3\text{-N}\\), \\(C_{nat} = 0\text{ kg m}^{-3}\\), and \\(Y\\) is crop yield in \\(\text{ton ha}^{-1}\\))* [8].

* **Calibration & Validation Performance Metrics**:
  * *Note on PBIAS*: The study evaluated EPIC model performance using \\(R^2\\) (coefficient of determination, threshold \\(> 0.5\\)) and \\(NSE\\) (Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency, threshold \\(\ge 0.60\\)) [9]; **PBIAS was not evaluated or reported** in this study.
  * **Model Calibration (2013–2014 field data)** [10, 11]:
    * **Rice** (\\(n=12\\)): Observed \\(5170 \pm 241\\) kg/ha | Simulated \\(5024 \pm 225\\) kg/ha | **\\(R^2 = 0.73\\)**, **\\(NSE = 0.67\\)** [11]
    * **Maize** (\\(n=11\\)): Observed \\(3714 \pm 544\\) kg/ha | Simulated \\(3676 \pm 588\\) kg/ha | **\\(R^2 = 0.67\\)**, **\\(NSE = 0.65\\)** [11]
    * **Soybean** (\\(n=10\\)): Observed \\(1444 \pm 134\\) kg/ha | Simulated \\(1451 \pm 105\\) kg/ha | **\\(R^2 = 0.65\\)**, **\\(NSE = 0.63\\)** [11]
    * **Mung bean** (\\(n=10\\)): Observed \\(418 \pm 77\\) kg/ha | Simulated \\(425 \pm 72\\) kg/ha | **\\(R^2 = 0.65\\)**, **\\(NSE = 0.62\\)** [11]
    * **Cassava** (\\(n=10\\)): Observed \\(13,250 \pm 1399\\) kg/ha | Simulated \\(13,465 \pm 1083\\) kg/ha | **\\(R^2 = 0.64\\)**, **\\(NSE = 0.63\\)** [11]
  * **Model Validation (2021 field data)** [10, 11]:
    * **Rice** (\\(n=18\\)): Observed \\(5722 \pm 253\\) kg/ha | Simulated \\(5872 \pm 237\\) kg/ha | **\\(R^2 = 0.75\\)**, **\\(NSE = 0.66\\)** [11]

### **2. Study Area Specifics**

* **Location & Geomorphology**:
  * **Province**: Phichit Province, Lower Northern Thailand [12].
  * **Hydrology**: Yom and Nan rivers flow parallel through the province before merging into the Chao Phraya Basin [12].
  * **Geomorphological Zones**:
    1. **Alluvial Fan** (West side): Sand and gravelly sand; prone to **repeated drought** [12].
    2. **Flood Plain** (Middle): Fine-grained clay and sandy clay; prone to **repeated floods** [12].
    3. **Alluvial Plain** (East side): Gravel, sand, silt, and clay; prone to **repeated drought** [12].
* **Districts, Sub-districts, and Topsoil Properties (0–10 cm)** [13]:
  * **RRR** (Triple Rice, Irrigated): Sam Ngam District (Rang Nok Sub-district) — **Clay**, pH 5.3–5.6, Organic Matter (OM) 2.38–2.73% [13].
  * **RR** (Double Rice, Rainfed): Taphan Hin District (Thap Man Sub-district) — **Sandy clay**, pH 5.1–5.2, OM 1.02–2.18% [13].
  * **RM** (Rice–Maize): Wang Sai Phun District (Nong Phra Sub-district) — **Sandy clay**, pH 4.9–5.9, OM 1.55–2.05% [13].
  * **RS** (Rice–Soybean): Pho Prathap Chang District (Phai Tha Pho Sub-district) — **Sandy clay**, pH 4.8–5.6, OM 2.01–2.15% [13].
  * **RB** (Rice–Mungbean): Bueng Na Rang District (Pho Sai Ngam Sub-district) — **Silty clay**, pH 5.7–6.0, OM 0.69–1.35% [13].
  * **MM** (Double Maize): Dong Charoen District (Samnak Kun Nen Sub-district) — **Silty clay**, pH 5.2–5.6, OM 2.24–2.34% [13].
  * **CS** (Cassava): Thap Khlo District (Thap Khlo Sub-district) — **Sandy clay loam**, pH 5.7–5.9, OM 0.99–1.83% [13].
* **Historical Baseline (1981–2014 Climate Data)** [14, 15]:
  * Annual precipitation: **1648.4 mm/year** [15] (1983–2013 average: 1314.6 mm, wettest month = September at 321.3 mm) [16].
  * Mean maximum temperature (\\(T_{max}\\)): **32.6 °C** (hottest month = April at 36.1 °C) [15, 16].
  * Mean minimum temperature (\\(T_{min}\\)): **22.8 °C** (coldest month = January at 18.8 °C) [15, 16].

### **7. Key Scientific Conclusions, Counter-Intuitive Facts, and Policy Recommendations**

1. **Inverse Yield–Water Footprint Relationship (Core Scientific Principle)**:
   * Water footprint per unit mass (\\(\text{m}^3/\text{ton}\\)) is **inversely proportional to crop yield** (\\(Y\\)) because \\(WF = \frac{CWU}{Y}\\) [2, 30]. When yields increase under moderate CO2 fertilization and precipitation gains, the unit water footprint drops—and vice versa [30].
2. **CO2 Fertilization Threshold Paradox**:
   * Under moderate emissions (**SSP2-4.5**), increased rainfall and elevated CO2 concentration offset temperature increases, leading to yield gains in irrigated rice (+10.1% to +33.0%) [31, 32].
   * Under extreme emissions (**SSP5-8.5**), excessive temperatures exceed optimal thresholds during flowering, triggering severe heat and water stress that overwhelms CO2 fertilization benefits, causing rice yields to collapse by up to **-18.5%** [31, 32].
3. **Mung Bean & Cassava High Climate Resilience**:
   * **Mung bean** thrives in the dry season because optimal temperatures (28–30 °C) fit dry-season conditions, and high dry-season temperatures suppress biotic stressors like Mungbean Yellow Mosaic Virus [29].
   * **Cassava** exhibits exceptional drought tolerance and water-use efficiency, consistently increasing yields (+1.0% to +5.7%) across all scenarios [27, 33].
4. **Policy Recommendations**:
   * **Irrigated Areas**: Continue **Triple Rice Cropping (RRR)** under moderate warming scenarios, as guaranteed irrigation buffers heat stress [34, 35].
   * **Rainfed Areas**: **Abolish double rice cropping (RR)** due to severe future yield losses (-6.0% to -17.7%) [31, 36]. Replace the 2nd rice crop with low water-consuming crops (**Mung bean, Maize, or Soybean**) [34-36].
   * **Land-Use Conversion**: Transitioning rainfed lands to **Double Maize (MM)** or **Cassava (CS)** cuts total agricultural water consumption by **~50%** while maintaining agricultural stability against severe flood/drought cycles [22, 23, 35].
