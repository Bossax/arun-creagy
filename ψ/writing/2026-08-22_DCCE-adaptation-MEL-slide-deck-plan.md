# Slide deck plan — DCCE adaptation MEL synthesis

*Thirteen-slide deck for internal communication, drawn from `ψ/writing/2026-08-22_DCCE-adaptation-MEL-system-synthesis.md`. Section references in the speaker notes point back to that report. Audience is the internal team rather than DCCE, so the harder findings are stated directly. Headings are short labels; each slide's key message sits in the subtitle as one plain sentence. Visual system: Soft Editorial (`bold-template-pack/templates/soft-editorial/design.md`) — Cormorant Garamond on cream paper with translucent and pastel cards.*

*Revised twice from the original ten-slide plan (2026-08-22). First revision: the deck dropped the reader into "DCCE is at stage two of five" without ever showing what the five stages are, assuming background the audience does not have. Two background slides were added — what MEL means as a concept, and the generic five-stage development pathway before DCCE is placed on it. Second revision: the deck showed the generic cycle and the first pilot but never stated the target operating model in one place — what runs at what scale, where data comes from, how it becomes usable numbers, and what happens to the result. A dedicated slide was added after the comparator evidence and before the pilot proposal, so the pilot reads as a first step toward a stated target rather than a free-standing recommendation.*

**Objective.** Bring the team to a shared position on four things — where DCCE actually stands on the standard development pathway, which constraints are real rather than assumed, what the target operating model looks like, and what the first cycle of work should be.

**Pacing.** Roughly 38 minutes. Slides 1 to 6 build the shared vocabulary and frame — what MEL is, what each part produces, how a system normally develops, and where DCCE sits on that path — about 14 minutes. Slides 7 to 9 carry the constraint argument and the comparator evidence, about 12 minutes. Slide 10 states the target operating model, about 3 minutes. Slides 11 to 13 are the proposal and the decisions, about 9 minutes.

### Slide 2 — What MEL means (new)

**Title:** What MEL means

**Subtitle:** Monitoring, evaluation and learning are three connected functions, not one blended activity, and MEL is the actual global standard.

**Show:**
- One line grounding the acronym — MEL: Monitoring, Evaluation, Learning
- Why this is the standard now — the 2023 UAE Framework for Global Climate Resilience sets a 2030 target for every country to have a working MEL system in place
- A one-line preview of the three functions, without the full detail — that comes next
- A short note that DCCE's own documents sometimes say "MERL," adding a fourth letter for Research, and that this is DCCE's own framing rather than the global standard

**Speaker note:** Report section 2. This slide exists purely to establish shared vocabulary before anything else is said. Keep it brief — a few sentences, not an argument. If the MERL point raises questions, note only that Research is not treated as a separate stage in the literature; the detail is in the full report, not this deck.

### Slide 5 — How systems like this develop (new)

**Title:** How systems like this develop

**Subtitle:** Countries build a working MEL system through five stages, and a different trigger moves each stage to the next.

**Show:**
- The five-stage bar, shown generically here — no country marked yet
- Stage 1, strategy and coordinating body — triggered by political recognition or international commitment
- Stage 2, indicator design and consultation — triggered by stakeholder consultation and technical assistance
- Stage 3, pilot reporting — triggered by an institutional champion running a first cycle
- Stage 4, mainstreaming into budgets and plans — triggered by a fiscal or legal instrument
- Stage 5, repeated cycles with independent oversight — triggered by accumulated administrative habit
- One line stating this pattern is not unique to Thailand — it shows up across very different countries

**Speaker note:** Report section 3. This is the map the next slide places DCCE on. Deliver it as a neutral, generic pattern — no DCCE content yet — so the positioning on the following slide lands as "here is where a known map puts a known country," not as new information to absorb all at once.
