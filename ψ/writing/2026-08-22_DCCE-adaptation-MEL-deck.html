What MEL means

Monitoring, evaluation and learning are three connected functions, not one blended activity, and MEL is the actual global standard.

Monitoring

Is it on track?

Evaluation

Did it work?

Learning

What changes next?

The 2023 UAE Framework for Global Climate Resilience sets a 2030 target for every country to have a working MEL system in place. Three letters — MEL is the term the obligation is actually measured against.

A note on “MERL”: some of DCCE’s own documents add a fourth letter, for Research. That is DCCE’s own framing, not the global standard.

What M, E and L produce

Each of the three produces something different.

Monitoring

Continuous tracking of whether implementation matches plan.

Produces: indicator data, status registers, financial records, baselines.

Evaluation

Periodic judgment against relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, equity.

Produces: mid-term & end-term reports, contribution analysis, recommendations.

Learning

Deliberate collective process that changes knowledge, attitudes or behaviour.

Produces: shared understanding, revised assumptions, institutional change.

Running learning activities is not learning. The process has to produce a change.

Authority and instruments

Nothing currently requires a line agency to report.

  • Authority is memoranda and accumulated standing. No budget-review role, no statutory reporting duty.
  • No independent evaluator of project effectiveness exists anywhere in Thai government.
  • No convening authority with standing to resolve disputes between divisions.
  • No dedicated provincial presence.
Partial

Budget tagging categories already work. Climate is not one of them.

Who does what today: the Office of the Public Sector Development Commission (OPDC) and the National Economic and Social Development Council (NESDC) check whether targets were met. The Office of the Auditor General audits budget use. Project owners assess themselves.

What is being measured

The plan being measured comes from policy dialogue rather than risk analysis.

  • The National Adaptation Plan does not build on a Thailand-specific climate risk assessment.
  • Sector scope and strategy were negotiated, not derived from data.
  • The action plan is largely existing agency projects relabelled as adaptation, with no consistent test for what qualifies.

What other countries did

Every country that made reporting routine attached it to something agencies already could not skip.

Tonga
Five questions added to the planning office’s existing mandatory quarterly report — no legal requirement, no funded post; agencies answered because the submission was already required.
Philippines
Budget circular making climate tagging a condition of budget approval — compliance was structural; the audit office still found the arrangement not institutionalised.
Kenya
Integration into the existing national monitoring system — worked because a statistics bureau already verified agency submissions.
Bangladesh
Reporting tied to disbursement from a national climate fund — needs a fund the coordinating body administers.
Finland
Voluntary qualitative self-assessment, no separate funding — ran before legal instrument existed; stayed fragile.
France
Central body monitors, convened cross-agency committee evaluates — works without an independent evaluator existing.

None of them built a parallel process and asked ministries to adopt it.

What to do first

One complete cycle, inside one sector, using only data agencies already report.

Collect
→
Score
→
Evaluate
→
Identify weak point
→
Learn
→
Carry back
  • Start from the collection round already completed. Do not design fresh.
  • Indicators — ten to fifteen, from the platform’s own set, not the international reporting list.
  • Scope — one sector, because sectors are how the whole system describes itself.
  • Data — only what agencies already submit.
  • Scoring — plain three-level, not the weighted composite index.
  • Evaluation — a convened cross-agency committee, following France.
  • Learning — sent to whichever sector the evaluation identifies.
  • Run it twice if the calendar allows.

Getting to stage four

The first cycle is also how we find out which instrument could carry reporting afterward.

Philippines · Indonesia
Add adaptation category to existing tagging

Mechanism already runs. Watch for divided authority between ministries.

Tonga
Attach to an existing mandatory planning cycle

Lowest cost. Depends on finding the Thai equivalent.

Kenya
Connect to OPDC / NESDC monitoring

Carries the reporting flow but not the assessment.

Thailand
Route through existing statistical work

Green GDP, sectoral emissions accounting, national surveys.

  • What the pilot produces toward this — where reporting stalls, who owns which gap, which data never arrives.
  • Local work is already happening. The task is testing what exists before formalising it, not waiting for a signal to begin.
