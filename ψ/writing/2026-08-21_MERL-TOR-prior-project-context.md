## 1. What the 2025 M&E Platform actually is

Confirmed against the primary Phase 2 Progress Report (`DCCE_ระบบติดตามประเมินผล_เล่มงวดที่ 2_ V.07_25032026.pdf`, copyrighted to DITTO (Thailand) PCL) — this **corrects one detail** in the earlier secondary summary already in ψ (`ψ/incubate/DCCE/CRDB/inbox_source/DCCE-MandE-platform-2025.md`), which described the platform as manual-entry-only with "no automated ingestion of raw environmental data."

The primary source says otherwise, as *design intent*: the system is architected for **hybrid ingestion** — manual E-form entry and file upload, plus a genuine automated path via RESTful/SOAP API integration with scheduled batch pulls (e.g. from water-gauge stations, disaster statistics). Architecture is a 3-layer stack: PostgreSQL as single source of truth, KNIME Business Hub for ETL, Tableau Server for dashboards — matching the earlier summary on the tech stack itself.

The correction matters for one reason: **as of the report date (March 2026), the automated layer was designed but not yet built or operating.** The report places the project at the stage of "analyzing/designing indicators and screen mockups, drafting E-form intake" — i.e., still in co-development with agencies, not running in production. So the earlier summary's real-world characterization (a manual-entry KPI portal) is likely accurate for what actually existed on the ground at the time it was written, but conflates "current operating state" with "designed capability." Both things are true and worth keeping separate when judging whether MERL's data ambitions are already unsupported by the existing platform, or just not yet realized.

**Indicator count has moved at least twice.** The Phase 2 report (March 2026) lists **233 indicators** across the 6 sectors (Water 25, Agriculture 18, Tourism 85, Health 33, Natural Resources 44, Human Settlement 28). A launch-event transcript from a month later (April 20, 2026, Asst. Prof. Dr. Pawitr Chuenchoom, Chulalongkorn Faculty of Engineering) states the number was negotiated down to **137**, after starting even earlier at roughly 150–160 (Chulalongkorn's original academic study, done before the UNFCCC's Belém/GGA 59-indicator framework was finalized at COP30 in 2025). The professor states explicitly, on record, that **the indicator set is now closed**: *"เราไม่มีโอกาสแก้ตัวชี้วัดแล้ว"* ("we no longer have the opportunity to revise the indicators") — agencies are told to answer what they can and leave blank what they can't; any revision is deferred at least 2 years.

This is directly relevant to the MERL TOR's second objective (§2: "develop tools, indicators, and approaches... applicable nationally and locally"). **The indicator base already exists, was already negotiated with all 18 focal agencies across 6 sectors, and has already been declared closed by the platform's own technical lead.** A MERL contractor proposing new indicators would either be duplicating a negotiation DCCE has already spent two years on, or would need the TOR to explicitly say it's reopening that closed set — which the TOR text does not say.

**Same professor, on record, confirms the platform is Monitoring-only in practice today**: many indicators track activity/exposure data, not outcome data, because quality/quantity of underlying data isn't there yet — evaluation-grade indicators are aspirational, revisited "in two years." He also states plainly: *"ระบบตรงนี้ยังไม่ได้เชื่อมโยง API ตอนนี้มันเป็นเฟสแรก"* ("the system isn't yet connected via API, this is currently phase one") — direct, spoken confirmation (as of April 2026) that the automated-ingestion design in the March 2026 written report had not yet gone live a month later.

## 2. What the DCCE M&E Guideline manual already contains

Queried directly from `คู่มือการติดตามและประเมินผล.pdf` in the same notebook. This is genuinely new ground — nothing in ψ summarized it before this session.

The guideline already:
- Follows a UNFCCC (2023) 4-stage M&E infrastructure model: Context → Content → Operationalization → Products.
- Structures indicators around a standard vulnerability framework: **Exposure, Sensitivity, Adaptive Capacity** — further split into **Monitoring indicators** (track the above) and **Evaluation indicators** (track outcome-level results like mortality/morbidity rates or economic loss).
- Explicitly frames M&E as feeding an **iterative policy cycle** — evaluation results loop back to revise plans and strategy.
- States its own purpose includes serving "researchers" (นักวิจัย) among its target users, and says evaluation depends on climate and socioeconomic research data to assess policy gaps accurately.
- Most importantly: **it does not use "MEL" as its formal framework name** — it's named plainly "the M&E system" — **but the Phase 2 Progress Report, written by Ditto for the same platform, explicitly names the long-term goal as "Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning: MEL"** in its own text (cited directly: "...การเรียนรู้ (Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning: MEL) อย่างต่อเนื่องในระยะยาว").

This is the single most consequential finding for evaluating the new TOR's premise. **DCCE's own prior contractor had already framed the platform's long-run goal as MEL — Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning — a full year before the 2026 MERL TOR was drafted.** The 2026 TOR's rationale (§1) states current practice is MEL and proposes upgrading it to **MERL** by adding "Research." But the prior guideline manual and Phase 2 report already treat learning as a first-class goal, already cite research as a required input to evaluation, and already use language nearly identical to what the new TOR presents as a conceptual leap forward. The novelty the TOR claims — going from MEL to MERL — is much smaller than advertised once you read what's already on paper. What's actually new in the 2026 TOR is not the concept, but the deliverables: pilot fieldwork, forums, published research synthesis, and public communications materials — a delivery/dissemination layer, not a new methodological framework.

## 3. What the MERL TOR inherits vs. changes

- **Same 6 sectors, same institutional structure** (18 focal-point agencies), same underlying NAP framework and the same GGA alignment — the MERL TOR doesn't introduce a different scope of "what counts as adaptation," it operates inside the frame the 2025 platform already built.
- **The indicator base is not mentioned at all in the MERL TOR.** Given the 137-indicator set is described by its own author as closed, and the MERL TOR's §5.1.2 output is "identify sectors with clarity in NAP implementation" plus indicator/target synthesis, there's a real question the TOR leaves unanswered: is this MERL work supposed to operate on the existing 137 indicators, propose a separate/parallel indicator layer, or unknowingly reopen a negotiation DCCE already closed?
- **The MERL TOR does not name the 2025 platform, Ditto, or the guideline manual anywhere in its text** (confirmed by reading `TOR-DCCE-MERL-2026.md` directly — no such references exist). It reads as though written to stand alone. This matches a documented pattern from the CRDB TOR review five months earlier: a TOR-Section-5-Audit found the concurrent National Climate Adaptation Database TOR risked being treated by its contractor "as a Blank Slate project, re-designing the architecture from scratch and ignoring the months of work done" (`TOR-Section-5-Audit.md`). The same risk applies here: a MERL contractor with no explicit mandate to build on the existing platform and guideline could plausibly re-derive a framework DCCE already has, at the department's expense, a second time.
- **The MERL TOR's methodology (research review, pilot fieldwork, policy synthesis) has no named system to receive its outputs.** The 2025 platform is a KPI dashboard tied to 137 fixed indicators; nothing in either TOR specifies that MERL's research findings, contribution-analysis logic, or pilot lessons feed back into that platform's data model. The CRDB Phase 2 Requirements document already drafted in this workspace (`ψ/outbox/CRDB_Phase2_MERL_Requirements.md`) is the one artifact that proposes an actual technical home (a `CONTRIBUTION` bridge entity, semantic governance gates) for exactly this kind of output — but the MERL TOR doesn't reference CRDB either, and CRDB's own status (design/requirements phase, not yet built by TOR70) means there's no live system either TOR could plug into today even if they wanted to.

## 4. Does the standing "domain-light contractor" risk apply here?

The May 2026 CRDB TOR-hardening episode produced a specific, documented finding about Ditto (`Contractor-Fit-Analysis-Ditto.md`, `2026-05-18_tor-hardening-domain-vs-infrastructure.md`): strong as a systems/infrastructure integrator, **domain-light** on climate adaptation science, historically dependent on partner TEAMG for domain logic, prone to defaulting to document-management-style, manual-entry architectures unless the TOR explicitly forces otherwise.

The MERL TOR is not an infrastructure build — it's closer to applied research and M&E methodology consulting, which is a different competency question than the one raised about Ditto specifically. But the underlying pattern the CRDB review named — an "Expertise Illusion" where operational/delivery track record gets mistaken for domain depth — transfers directly: **nothing in §4 (Consultant Qualifications) of the MERL TOR requires published research experience in climate adaptation M&E, contribution analysis, or MEL/MERL methodology specifically.** The qualification clauses are entirely procurement boilerplate (legal capacity, no bankruptcy, registered with the Ministry of Finance consultant registry in the environment category, etc.) — the same gap the CRDB review flagged and redlined for its own TOR (mandating a "Lead Climate Knowledge Broker" with a specific research background). Whether the same redline is warranted here is a judgment call, but the absence is structurally the same absence already found once.

## 5. Where the platform's real capability contradicts the TOR's methodological ambition

The MERL TOR's field-level ambition (§5.2: contribution analysis, cross-sector methodology piloting, gap/trend synthesis across 6 risk areas) assumes a level of data granularity and cross-agency comparability the 2025 platform's own creator says doesn't exist yet in practice. Direct quote from the launch transcript: data scarcity/fragmentation is described as *the* central obstacle ("การขาดแคลนข้อมูลในการที่จะติดตามประเมินผลเนี่ยมันไม่มี หรือมีมันก็กระจัดกระจายมาก" — "there's a lack of data for M&E, or where it exists, it's scattered"), and most indicators today are Monitoring-only, not yet capable of outcome-level Evaluation. A MERL pilot asking to run contribution analysis and cross-sector synthesis in 2026, on top of a data foundation whose own architects say isn't ready for that yet in 2026, is asking for the Research/Learning layer before the Monitoring/Evaluation layer underneath it is functioning as designed.
