## AI Diary

This was the first time I ran the full writing-th safeguard pipeline (contract → map → verbalize → lint → dual Stage-5 review) end to end for a section built on the new `reader_bridge` mechanism, and I think that's the most interesting part of what happened. The `reader_bridge` field exists precisely because a parallel §2.3 rewrite earlier in the week passed every mechanical gate and still read as abrupt AI prose to a human. I fully expected that adding it to every argument unit would close that gap here. It didn't, not fully — the cold reader found the same failure mode again (self-narrating structure, redundant restatement, terms dropped in before being explained) even with `reader_bridge` populated on all 25 units. That was a useful, slightly humbling result: a structural field in the map doesn't automatically produce clean prose if the verbalizer still reaches for the same rhetorical habits (forecasting the next paragraph, restating a fact right after forecasting it) regardless of what the bridge fields say. I didn't second-guess the subagents' work at any stage before handing it forward — each one flagged its own uncertainties honestly (the verbalizer didn't invent missing table data, the mapper didn't force the 162/214 numbers to agree), and that discipline is exactly why the dual Stage-5 review caught real, specific, fixable problems instead of me having to reverse-engineer where things went wrong from a finished draft. I stopped at the report-and-ask point rather than immediately running a fix pass, partly because Boss's instruction was to read Stage 5 output, and partly because at least one of the two major findings (162 vs 214 vs 266) needs a decision about which of plan-slice's own numbers is right, which isn't mine to guess at.

## Honest Feedback

**Friction 1 — the 162/214/266 discrepancy is a Phase-A data problem masquerading as a drafting problem.** It was already sitting in `plan-slice.md` §2, silently, before any prose existed: the register is described as "214 items matched" in one place and "162 items — the core Table 5-2 data" in another. Nobody reconciled it during Phase A, so it flowed forward through the argument map into the prose, and it took two independent Stage-5 reviews to surface it as a blocking issue. If I revisit a plan-slice like this again, I should treat any two-different-totals-for-the-same-population statement as a Phase-A exit-gate failure, not something to carry forward and flag downstream.

**Friction 2 — `reader_bridge` alone isn't a strong enough guarantee against self-narration.** The field asks the verbalizer to state an `opening_link` and `transition_to_next`, but nothing in the contract or map schema explicitly forbids writing those as visible meta-sentences ("หัวข้อนี้จะ...") rather than folding the transition into the substance of the next paragraph. The safeguard needs a companion rule, something like "reader_bridge content shapes the paragraph, it is never itself a sentence in the paragraph" — right now that's implicit and got violated repeatedly.

**Friction 3 — running Stage 0 by hand versus delegating Stage 1/3/5 to purpose-built agents was the right split, but it means I hand-wrote ~18 rules and ~9 required_structures for the contract without a second pair of eyes on it before the mapper consumed it.** Given the contract is the thing every later stage is graded against, a contract-review pass (even a quick self-check against the plan-slice) before handing it to `th-argument-mapper` would have caught the boilerplate `terminology.first_mention` field (flagged later by editorial as unused boilerplate, F3) earlier and cheaper.

## Lessons Learned

- A `reader_bridge` field on every argument unit reduces but does not eliminate self-narrating prose; the verbalizer needs an explicit rule that bridge content must be dissolved into substance, never left as a visible "this section will..." sentence. See `ψ/memory/style/evidence/2026-09-25_00-31_TH_diff-evidence.md` for the earlier diagnosis this built on.
- When a plan-slice or evidence table states two different totals for what should be the same population (162 vs 214 items here), treat it as a blocking data-reconciliation task before argument-mapping starts, not a note to flag downstream — flagging it forward just moves the same unresolved question through three more pipeline stages before a human sees it.
- Running Stage 5 as two independent reviews in parallel (mechanical rubric + fresh cold-reader roleplay with zero pipeline visibility) is worth the extra agent call: the mechanical reviewer caught the number-reconciliation issue formally, but only the cold reader caught the pervasive self-narration and the quantitative-vs-methodological labeling contradiction on the 40 product items — neither review alone would have been sufficient.
