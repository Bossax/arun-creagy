## Lessons Learned

1. **Count paragraph jobs, not the label above them.** When a plan says “five paragraphs” but lists seven independent rhetorical jobs, compressing to the label recreates omitted logic and abrupt transitions. Resolve the inconsistency before drafting.
2. **Encode user terminology decisions in the contract before verbalization.** A preference such as spelling out “การประชุมกลุ่มย่อยครั้งที่ 2” must become a report-specific rule, not an after-the-fact search-and-replace convention.
3. **A synthesis table belongs before the closing transition.** The table consolidates evidence already explained; the final paragraph should remain free to interpret status and carry the reader into the next section.
4. **Independent review does not replace integration review.** A clean reviewer can judge the supplied artifact without drafting bias, while the parent must still check adjacent heading levels, cross-section continuity, and placement in the larger document.
5. **Attribution is part of source fidelity.** Separating meeting feedback from consultant synthesis applies inside table rows as strictly as it applies in prose.
6. **Mechanical and editorial gates catch different failures.** The linter found term-level drift; editorial review tested reasoning and reader flow; the final parent check found document-integration and attribution issues. None of the three checks subsumes the others.
7. **Every draft change invalidates the receipt.** After moving tables and correcting attribution, replacing the review scaffold and running a fresh independent review was necessary even though the earlier verdict was a pass.
