## Honest Feedback

Three friction points worth naming honestly. First, and most substantive: my first draft of arg-07 buried methodology under results and walked through full persona user-journeys, which is exactly the shape of over-detailing a diagnose-type unit that Boss had to redirect me away from. I had all the source material in front of me (the persona document, both evaluation reports) and still defaulted to reproducing the reports' own structure — results-first, journey-by-journey — instead of judging what a report reader actually needs, which is methodology context before results and a summary rather than a walkthrough. I should have made that editorial judgment myself before presenting it, not after Boss pointed at "argument seven" and asked me to expand it.

Second: when Boss asked whether other arguments had subarguments, I gave a reasonably careful answer (checked the schema, distinguished arg-05/06's shape from arg-07's) — but I framed arg-05/06 as "borderline, probably fine as-is" and then, one turn later, split them anyway once Boss said to generalize the treatment. That's not necessarily wrong — Boss's later instruction was broader than my earlier judgment — but it's worth being honest that my first read undersold how similar their shape actually was. A colleague reading my exchange might reasonably ask why I called something borderline and then rewrote it completely one message later without flagging that my assessment had changed.

Third, smaller: I created a directory (`mkdir -p`) for a contract I ended up not needing to write, before checking whether one already existed. No harm was done here (the directory already existed with the approved contract inside it, so the mkdir was a no-op), but the sequencing — act, then check — is the wrong order, and I got lucky that this particular case wasn't destructive. Worth tightening into "always list/check before creating" as a hard habit, not a lucky outcome.

## Lessons Learned

- **A pipeline stage compressing evidence into a structured field is a distinct failure mode from a stage being denied raw-source access** — fixing the second (the two-lane split, from the prior session) does not automatically fix the first. When a downstream stage is deliberately blind to raw sources, the upstream stage that IS allowed to see them has to carry enough real detail forward, or blindness just relocates the fidelity loss instead of preventing it.
- **Before drafting any new artifact in a project with a batch/session history, check the target location for existing work first** — a habitual `ls`/list-before-write catches duplicated or superseded work that a plan built purely from documentation (like a spine doc) won't reveal, since the spine doc doesn't necessarily reflect what a different earlier session already executed.
- **When restructuring a dense argument unit, judge by rhetorical shape (multiple distinct methods vs. multiple findings from one method), not just by "does it feel long"** — the distinction between arg-07 (three genuinely separate evaluation methods, needed splitting) and arg-05/06 (three findings from one coherent event, borderline) was the right frame, even though Boss ultimately asked for consistency across all three.
- **A results section for a technical evaluation should default to methodology-then-summary, not results-first-with-full-user-journeys**, unless the report's audience specifically needs the journey detail — this is a general report-writing judgment call I should apply proactively rather than waiting to be redirected.
