## AI Diary

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points worth naming. First, the Edit-tool whitespace mismatch (non-breaking spaces after list-item numbers) cost real time — I should default to grepping+xxd-checking a failed Edit's surrounding bytes immediately rather than retrying variations of the same string blind. Second, I under-scoped the "review this draft" request early on — Boss's first "review the edit, run it through the TOR criteria" turned into a full document audit across five sections, and I should have asked upfront whether "this draft" meant the section or the whole proposal, rather than defaulting to the narrow reading and needing a follow-up. Third, on the Royal Gazette finding, I let a plausible-sounding, well-sourced claim (backed by two separate web searches) stand without first asking myself the more basic question — is this something the consultant has to *draft*, or is it just how any legal instrument gets promulgated. Boss's one-line challenge exposed that in seconds; I should have applied that filter before presenting the finding, not after being challenged on it.

## Lessons Learned

- **Legal-instrument-completeness checks need a "who writes this" filter, not just a "is this legally required" filter.** A step being legally necessary for enforceability doesn't mean it needs its own drafted deliverable — distinguish content-requiring instruments from standard procedural/administrative channels before proposing an addition to a tools/deliverables list.
- **When an Edit tool call fails with "string not found" on text that visually matches the Read output, suspect invisible characters (non-breaking spaces, alternate dashes) before assuming a stale read.** `xxd` on the specific substring resolves it fast; anchoring the edit mid-line (avoiding the suspect whitespace) is often faster than fixing the whitespace representation in the old_string itself.
- **A "review this" request on a document that's part of a larger proposal should be scoped explicitly** (this section only, or the whole document) rather than assumed, especially once earlier turns in the session have already established that the file's scope has grown beyond its original name.
