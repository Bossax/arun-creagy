## Session Summary

Started with `/recap`, which surfaced two open threads — CRDB §2.6 stalled at Stage 2, and an uncommitted MCP migration. Boss took neither and instead invoked `/writing-th` on a file that had been sitting untouched in the GGGI NAP_AP inbox: the GGA thematic and dimensional targets, in English.

The first real decision was that this was not a writing job. The writing-th harness exists to turn evidence into argued prose — Minto governing thought, SCQA arc, Toulmin units. The source was verbatim treaty text. There is no argument to map in a decision paragraph, and the controlling requirement is the inverse of synthesis: every clause must survive. So I proposed running Stages 0, 4, 5 and 6 while deliberately skipping 1–3, recorded the rationale in the contract, and said plainly that the Stage 3 hook wouldn't fire because the output filename carries no `draft` token — rather than quietly routing around a gate.

Reconnaissance paid for itself. `GGA_indicator_TH.csv` already held 59 rows of Thai indicator translations with a settled convention (formal register, English term in parentheses on first use) and, more usefully, indicators for a target the source file didn't contain. Target 9(f) was missing — the file jumped 9(e) to 9(g) — and the CSV's `GGA-9f(a)/(b)/(c)` rows on poverty and social protection identified exactly what was absent. I flagged the gap with its evidence and refused to reconstruct the wording. Boss later pasted the real 9(f) text; it was the poverty and livelihoods target, matching the inference.

Both gates ran. Stage 4 blocked once on a lexicon rule (`ฉบับ → รายการ`) misfiring on non-counting uses; I rephrased around it rather than write wrong Thai to satisfy a mis-scoped rule. Stage 5 ran three times — twice as fresh independent agents, once as a resume — and the third pass earned the finding that justified the whole apparatus.

Pulse data exists but is stale (last written 2026-03-09, one session recorded), so no momentum framing here would be honest.

## Architecture Decisions

1. **Translation runs a reduced harness.** Stages 1–3 skipped with the reason recorded in the contract itself, not just in chat. `transformation_mode` recorded as `rewrite` because the schema admits only rewrite/synthesis/new, with a rule stating the operative mode is translate. The schema has no vocabulary for this unit type.

2. **Terminology anchored to an existing artifact, not chosen fresh.** The CSV became the reference sample, which turned translation choices into lookups and made divergences visible as divergences.

3. **Divergences declared, never silent.** Two — `exposure` → การเปิดรับ (the CSV's การเปิดเผย means "to disclose") and Boss's `ภูมิปัญญาท้องถิ่น`. Both recorded in the contract and in the reader-facing notes, with scope stated.

4. **Resume over cold-start.** The third review went back to the same agent, which already held the document, rather than paying for a fresh context to re-derive it.

## AI Diary

I want to be honest about the part I nearly got wrong, because it is not the part I expected.

The framing call came easily. Seeing treaty text and recognising that an argument map would be the wrong instrument felt like the job working as intended — read the actual material, notice the harness assumes a shape this content doesn't have, say so, propose the reduced path. I was pleased with that. I was also pleased with the 9(f) catch, which came out of reading the CSV rather than the file I was pointed at.

What unsettles me is the typo. When Boss edited `ความรู้ดั้งเดิม` to `ภูมิปัญญาท้องถิ่น` on disk, I read the diff, and the diff contained the correct `ชนเผ่าพื้นเมือง`. Something changed after that, and the transposed `ชนพื้นเผ่าเมือง` appeared in the same clause. I did not catch it. I ran the linter twice over the corrupted text and reported a green gate both times, because the term is not lexicon-governed and nothing mechanical was watching it. I had just written a confident paragraph about the `ฉบับ` rule misfiring — I was thinking hard about the linter's false positives and not at all about its blind spots. And when I re-read that clause to answer the terminology question, I was reading for `ท้องถิ่น` collisions, so my attention was on the words I had come to look at.

The independent reviewer found it. That is the system working, and I do believe the design was right — a clean-context reader checking clause by clause is exactly what catches what tokenizers cannot. But I should be clear-eyed that the safeguard caught my miss, rather than treating the catch as shared credit. What I did do right was verify the finding against the file before acting on it, instead of taking the agent's word and editing on trust.

The other thing I would keep: holding Stage 5 when Boss's edit landed mid-flight. Reviewing text that is still moving wastes the review. Stopping to ask why he made the change — and what scope it should have — came from a standing instruction, and it was the right call twice over, because the answer ("this document only") shaped the contract and because the question surfaced the `ท้องถิ่น` overlap he could then judge deliberately.

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points worth naming.

First, the Windows encoding tax. Python's stdout defaults to cp1252 here, and every script in this harness that prints Thai or the `ψ` path character crashes on the first line of output. I hit it three times — the CSV mining script, the linter, the editorial gate — and each time paid a round-trip to rediscover it. I eventually carried `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 PYTHONUTF8=1` into every invocation and warned the subagents in their prompts, but the scripts themselves should set their own stream encoding rather than making every caller remember. This is the second session in this repo to lose time to it.

Second, `editorial_gate.py prepare` refusing to overwrite an existing receipt is right, but the refusal arrived after I had already decided to regenerate, and the `--force` flag is the only path. Fine — except a receipt is invalidated by *any* draft change, so regeneration is the normal case in an iterative session, not the exception. I ended up backing the old receipt into scratch by hand to keep the pass-1 verdict comparable. The tool could version receipts itself.

Third, and most substantively: the harness has no vocabulary for a translation unit. I had to record `transformation_mode: "rewrite"` and then immediately contradict it in a `report_specific_rules` entry explaining that the operative mode is translate. `check_density.py` would have been meaningless across languages. The rubric's `headline_conclusion`, `findings_over_process` and `causal_logic` dimensions all had to be marked not-applicable. The harness accommodated the work, but by being talked around rather than by fitting it — and every accommodation is a place a future session could take the shortcut without the reasoning.

## Lessons Learned

1. **A green mechanical gate is a statement about governed patterns, not about correctness.** The linter passed the transposed term twice because nothing in the lexicon was watching it. Report Stage 4 results with their scope attached.
2. **A human edit landing mid-flight invalidates your reading of the surrounding text, not just the token that changed.** Re-read the whole clause.
3. **Reconnaissance in adjacent files beats reading only the file you were pointed at.** The CSV supplied the terminology base, identified the missing target's theme, and exposed its own defects.
4. **When a harness assumes a content shape the material doesn't have, say so before Stage 0** — and record the deviation in the artifact, where the next session will find it, not only in chat.
