## AI Diary

[→ AGENT DECISION] I initially treated the letter as a request for approval of the broad evaluation approach and its resulting assessment. That was the wrong decision object. The plan-slice comment made clear that the actual item for approval is the questionnaire before deployment. I corrected the contract, argument map, and letter before drafting proceeded, rather than trying to hide the distinction inside generic wording.

The formal-letter example proved especially useful as a constraint: it made the required compression concrete. `TOR5.6.md` was evidence for the questionnaire's high-level dimensions, not a reason to place scores, respondent profiles, or findings in the approval letter. The independent review also caught an operational lesson: a semantically sound review receipt still fails if it does not meet the gate's exact schema.

## Lessons Learned

1. Identify the exact approval object before drafting. Approval of a questionnaire before use, approval of a method, and endorsement of completed results lead to materially different institutional letters.
2. Use the reference letter to set the reader-facing altitude, while using the technical source only to constrain claims. The attachment carries the detail; the letter carries the decision.
3. A successful editorial review must be verified as structured data. A reviewer can reach the right judgment but still produce an invalid receipt when required field names are wrong.
4. Preserve unknown administrative data as explicit placeholders rather than importing it from an unrelated sample.
