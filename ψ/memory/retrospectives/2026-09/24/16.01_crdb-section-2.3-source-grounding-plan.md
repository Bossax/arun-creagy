## Lessons Learned

1. For a TOR-facing report, begin with the deliverable folder organized by TOR clause. Use ancestry records only to resolve gaps or contested causal links.
2. Treat consultation evidence and revision evidence as separate classes: `feedback source → documented change`. Never let the presence of a recommendation prove that it was implemented.
3. When several four-part structures coexist, explicitly distinguish the TOR minimum content groups, conceptual-model domains, and user-facing content groups.
4. Define temporal boundaries. Role categories may be described in Chapter 2.3, but named domain-owner assignments belong to the later FGD3 stage.
5. Keep platform roles precise: a metadata catalogue or dissemination channel is not automatically the authoritative operational data platform.
6. Verify task-specific files before reporting completion; a dirty worktree can contain unrelated or pre-existing modifications.
