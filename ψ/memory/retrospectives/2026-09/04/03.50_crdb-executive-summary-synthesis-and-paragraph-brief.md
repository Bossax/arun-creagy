## Lessons Learned
1. **Executive Briefings for Institutional Reports Must Default to Flowing Narrative Prose**: In policy and executive report drafting, a "brief" often means concise, continuous prose that can be directly pasted into an official document, not bulleted presentation notes. Providing narrative paragraphs first—or offering both narrative prose and summary tables—prevents unnecessary iteration rounds.
2. **Contract Clause Pruning Elevates Policy Altitude**: Describing project scope (TOR 5.1–5.7) with dense contract paragraph numbers, sub-clause references, and minimum participant counts drags executive text down to administrative compliance. Abstracting into 4 thematic work groups with clean descriptive narrative conveys strategic intent and deliverable value much more effectively to high-level decision makers.
3. **Proactive Lexicon Pre-flight Prevents Gate Rejection**: Even in newly drafted synthesis sections, strict adherence to established project lexicon rules (e.g., prohibiting bare `NCAIF`, replacing `หมวดหลัก` with `หัวข้อหลัก`, avoiding `ห่วงโซ่` and `ชิ้นงาน`) must be internalized during drafting rather than treated as a post-hoc linting patch.
