# Retrospective: Shattered Fluff & Gentle Compression

## Session Context
The user requested a 25% reduction in length for three specific, highly quantitative articles (ART01, ART02, and ART04) to align their size with the rest of the deliverables, and also asked for a rewrite of an introductory paragraph in section 5.3.3.

## What Happened
- **The Over-Summarization Glitch**: I initially spawned three subagents to compress the articles. However, my prompt instructed them to "condense the quantitative sections into an executive summary format". The subagents aggressively optimized for this and reduced the articles by 60-70% (down to ~10KB from ~35KB), stripping out much of the academic narrative and replacing it with bare bullets.
- **[→ AGENT DECISION]**: I made a poor choice in prompting the subagents without providing a strict hard-floor boundary for the length reduction. I instructed them to reduce by 25% but also used the phrase "executive summary format," which they took as a license to obliterate the narrative structure.
- **The Correction**: I restored the files to their pre-compressed state by extracting the original text from the subagents' `view_file` logs. I then re-dispatched the subagents with explicit boundaries ("Must be EXACTLY 75% of original length" and "Do NOT convert into bulleted executive summaries"). The second pass succeeded flawlessly.
- **Introductory Rewrite**: I analyzed the 8 services described in Section 5.3.3 and rewrote the introductory paragraph to accurately reflect the actual structure of the document (Role, Minimum Service Package, Future Development, Success Criteria), after the user pointed out my initial draft assumed an inaccurate structure.

## Learnings & Next Steps
- **Harness Discipline**: Subagents need rigorous guardrails when modifying complex academic text. Target lengths must be expressed as exact bounds (e.g., "Must be 75% of original length"), not just "reduce by 25%".
- **Self-Correction**: Recovering from the over-summarization glitch by parsing the subagents' own logs was a clean way to restore uncommitted state without losing the user's previously approved edits.
