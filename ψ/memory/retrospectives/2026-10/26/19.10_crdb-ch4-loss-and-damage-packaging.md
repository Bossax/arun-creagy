## 4. Lessons Learned

1. **Role Separation Between Systems Architect/Packager and Production Writer**: In a multi-agent or modular orchestration workflow, the agent responsible for deep institutional research, data modeling, and planning must strictly produce **planning packages and reference materials** (`plans/<chapter>/`), not write directly into target draft endpoints (`ψ/incubate/drafts/<chapter>/`). Blurring this boundary risks premature commits and disrupts specialized downstream drafting agents.
2. **The Cardinality Paradox ($M:N$) as the Root Cause of Fiscal-Disaster Integration Failure**: In Thai public administration, the failure to reconcile disaster damage with GFMIS fiscal data is not a database query issue; it is a structural cardinality paradox. A single administrative decree can cover multiple dispersed flash floods and landslides ($1:M$), while a single regional storm (e.g., Noru) triggers separate decrees across 15–20 provinces ($M:1$). Introducing `EVENT_DECREE_BRIDGE` with population-duration weighting is the only sound mathematical way to prevent double-counting while preserving SAO audit compliance.
3. **Behavioral Realism Over Idealized Data Standards**: Data collection forms that require local municipal technicians (นายช่าง อปท.) to estimate monetary values will always produce severe under-reporting (less than 30% of real economic loss), because technicians deliberately cap recorded damages at statutory relief limits (e.g. 49,500 THB) to protect themselves against SAO disciplinary investigations. The **"Zero-Monetary-Valuation at Local Level Rule"**—where field staff record only observable physical units and central algorithms compute economic values—is essential for data fidelity.
4. **Institutional Modesty in Stage 1 Roadmaps**: New agencies like DCCE cannot dictate IT standards to veteran fiscal authorities (CGD/GFMIS, DLA/e-LAAS) at inception. The roadmap must place Stage 1 strictly on internal R&D, pilot validation, and bilateral collaboration with immediate data allies (GISTDA, DDPM, DLA, NESDC). Broader government-wide mandate pushes must be deferred to Stages 2 and 3 after empirical credibility is proven.
