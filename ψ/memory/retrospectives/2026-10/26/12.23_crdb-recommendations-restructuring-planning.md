## 4. Lessons Learned

1. **Strict Contractual Framing as a Structuring Constraint, Not a Roadblock**: Technical analysis often evolves organically beyond the rigid terminology of initial project TORs (e.g., modern software development introduces Agile lifecycles and in-house data engineering, which the original TOR did not explicitly mention). Rather than abandoning modern technical imperatives or distorting the contract, the correct strategy is semantic dissection: decomposing recommendations into their constituent operational mechanics and tucking them cleanly into the mandated contractual axes (Policy/Technical $\times$ Baseline/Products/Management).
2. **Narrative Outlines vs Bullet Outlines for Downstream Agent Guidance**: Bullet-point outlines summarize topics, but completely obscure the connective reasoning, institutional mechanics, and rhetorical transitions. Expanding the outlines into full narrative prose in a neutral administrative voice provides downstream writing subagents with an unambiguous roadmap of how paragraphs flow into one another.
3. **Automated Verification Harness Before Writing**: Compiling `plans/crdb_recommendations_evaluator.json` before drafting establishes a rigorous, automated safety net. By declaring Toulmin structures, mandatory keywords, and invariant constraints upfront, the writing agent can be evaluated against objective rubrics without relying on subjective human memory during post-draft review.
