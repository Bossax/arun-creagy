## 4. Lessons Learned

1. **"Originally in This State" Architectural Voice**:
   When updating institutional and government policy reports, never narrate changes from an external developer's perspective ("we added 12 new datasets", "we updated from v4 to v6"). Instead, articulate the catalog and findings as an authoritative, self-contained truth ("the baseline catalog comprises 246 datasets from 46 agencies"), seamlessly incorporating the assets into the narrative without temporal diff commentary.
2. **Human-Centric WBS vs. Technical Barcodes**:
   Technical requirement codes (`REQ-041`, `matched_asset_ids: PUB-012,DAT-004`) create severe cognitive friction for line-agency staff. Grouping requirements into actionable Work Packages (`DEL-0` to `DEL-14`), assigning a single responsible division to each package, and replacing barcodes with full Thai titles and direct links transforms an abstract IT specification into an operational production plan.
3. **Multi-Section Chapter Composition**:
   In formal TOR project reports, Chapter numbers and TOR clause numbers frequently diverge (e.g., Chapter 5 houses both TOR 5.3.8 Gap Analysis as §5.1 and TOR 5.3.9 Recommendations/Roadmap as §5.2). Always consult the master spine document (`00-โครงเรื่องและแผนการเขียนรายงานฉบับสมบูรณ์.md`) before confirming whether a section draft represents the entire chapter.
