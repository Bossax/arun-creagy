## 💭 Honest Feedback

First, the session ran more serially than it needed to. The mapper took between four and twelve minutes per pass, each review round took four to seven, and a one-clause change (a lexicon word, a footnote format) still cost a lint, a receipt and a full review. Boss asked "why does it take so long", and the honest answer was that every step waited for the previous file. A cheaper route for small edits (lint only, review once at the end) was available and I did not offer it until late.

Second, hand edits and agent work overlapped. Boss edited the same file the writer, my footnote script and the reviewer were reading, and I only found out from the reviewer's hash failure. A short "tell me when you are done editing" message at the start of each review round would have saved a round.

Third, my own early framing cost the most time: the recap and first plan treated the section as a length and polish problem, and later I accepted the catalog's owner column without checking it. Both came from reading the top of a document and not its provenance.

## Lessons Learned

- **Pattern**: When a reviewer says a section is off topic, re-derive its scope from the governing document and its sibling sections before touching the prose; polishing answers the wrong question.
- **Mistake**: Attributing a dataset to the agency in a catalog's owner column without reading the row's source and notes fields. Ownership is unestablished until those agree.
- **Discovery**: An independent review receipt binds to a file hash, so any edit, including a one-word lint fix, forces a new review; run small fixes through the lint gate and batch them before asking for review.
