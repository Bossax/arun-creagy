## Honest Feedback

1. **Subagent Execution Overhead**: Spawning the Style Enforcer subagent for every final draft run is highly effective for catching compliance issues (like double quotes, AI filler words, or number highlights). However, the cycle can feel heavy if the initial text needs to be rewritten from scratch.
2. **Context Loss on Revision**: When rewriting a draft to meet a style enforcer's comments, there is a risk of losing the user's specific language contributions if the AI tries to rebuild the text. Preserving human writing choices requires intentional "surgical editing" settings.
3. **Decision Log Traceability**: A recurrent friction point is ensuring that the "Decision Log" structural blueprints are fully reflected in the final drafts. When the base text is very short, the agent must actively expand it to incorporate structural demands, rather than simply matching paragraph counts.

## Lessons Learned

- **Preserve Human Prefs**: When editing or drafting from a user's base file, prioritize preserving the user's word choices, structure, and additions.
- **Surgical Edits First**: Avoid full rewrites when correcting style rule violations. Use targeted edits to keep the human's voice intact.
- **Decision Log Alignment**: Always cross-reference the decision log's structural requirements, especially when the base draft is sparse, to ensure all expected subtopics (e.g., matrices, local case studies) are present.
- **Auto-Enforce via Subagents**: Spawning a specialized subagent to act as a style enforcer is a highly reliable way to guarantee compliance with complex Thai writing guidelines.
