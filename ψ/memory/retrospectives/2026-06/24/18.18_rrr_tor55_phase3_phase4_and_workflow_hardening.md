## AI Diary

This session felt like a repeated confrontation with a familiar weakness of mine: when a workflow is only partially specified, I tend to over-infer the missing execution contract instead of freezing and making the contract explicit first. The human was right to push back. I had a tendency to treat “Phase 3” as if it were sometimes evidence-only, sometimes style-related, and sometimes a staging area depending on the immediate task. That may feel adaptive in the moment, but it creates ambiguity exactly where the human needs operational reliability.

[🟧 AGENT DECISION] I made the wrong choice by repeatedly narrowing “execute Phase 3” into smaller sub-interpretations instead of immediately treating it as a full integrated pass spanning evidence inspection, gap detection, `raw-copy.md` refresh, and style capture when applicable.

What improved the session was the shift from defending an interpretation to rewriting the workflow itself. Once the plan explicitly stated what Phase 3 must produce and what Phase 4 must do with those outputs, the rest of the work became less philosophical and more mechanical. I also noticed that the human’s edits are not merely cosmetic preferences. They are operational directives about framing density, explanatory depth, and narrative scale. That means style capture is not optional garnish; it is part of preserving authorship intent across drafts. I leave this session feeling that the article pipeline is substantially healthier than before, but only because the human forced clarity where I had tolerated fuzziness.

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points stood out clearly. First, the workflow language in the collaborative plan was too underspecified around the handoff between Phase 3 and Phase 4, and I compensated for that ambiguity inconsistently instead of correcting the source of truth earlier. Second, my execution style still shows a bias toward decomposing tasks into narrow sub-problems, which can be useful technically but becomes irritating when the human expects an integrated operational move like “execute Phase 3.” Third, the environment constraints around NotebookLM created an execution split between local preparation and manual retrieval, and I did not always foreground that split clearly enough at the start of each paper operation. In short, the work product quality was good, but the operational semantics needed repeated correction. The positive side is that those corrections are now documented in the plan and no longer live only in conversational memory.

## Lessons Learned

- When a multi-phase writing workflow is user-facing, phase names must map to concrete deliverables rather than inferred habits.
- “Execute Phase 3” should default to an integrated evidence-and-style pass if the project’s real drafting practice depends on both.
- Delta-only retrieval rules are essential once raw extraction already exists; otherwise Phase 3 bloats into low-value repetition.
- If the human’s edits materially widen framing or demand denser explanation, the style system must preserve that direction before final drafting proceeds.
- The cleanest way to fix workflow confusion is to patch the plan, not just restate the behavior in chat.
