## Honest Feedback
The strongest friction in this session was scope control. The human asked for naturalization, not redesign, and that required repeated discipline because the source text itself had many signs of being assembled from interim-report material. It would have been easy to over-edit and start reshaping structure, but that would have violated the brief.

A second friction point was the contrast between the article-leaning style benchmark and the report function of the CRDB sections. The benchmark was still useful, but only as a tone guide. I had to keep stripping away article habits that would have been too loose or too narrative for an institutional report section. That judgment took attention in every section.

A third friction point was the file and session logistics around the retrospective workflow. The session log was unavailable, so timestamps could not be recovered and had to be marked as unavailable rather than invented. That made the timeline less precise than ideal, but it preserved honesty and avoided false certainty. Overall, the session showed that the main risk in this kind of work is not lack of content. It is letting the prose drift back into memo-like or machine-like phrasing after the content is already correct.

## Lessons Learned
- When a report chapter is already substantively complete, the right first move is usually prose naturalization, not structural redesign.
- Agentive wording matters in formal Thai. If the project is only the container, the team should be the subject of action.
- A style pack built for public-facing writing can still help formal reporting, but only through the parts that improve rhythm, clarity, and human readability.
- Preserve content blocks first, then make the transitions and sentence rhythm do the humanization work.
