## AI Diary

This session felt like a proper end-to-end systems exercise rather than a simple writing task. The hardest part was not generating text; it was keeping the chain of reasoning clean enough that the final Thai prose would deserve trust. The work kept forcing a distinction between three layers that are easy to blur under pressure: what DDPM actually records, what PDNA and DaLA require analytically, and what the CRDB target architecture should aspire to hold. Once those layers were separated, nearly every downstream task became clearer. The report prose improved because the design logic improved first. The gap analysis improved because the CRI/DDPM review forced a reality check on what was source data versus what was derived by CRI. Even the execution planning mattered more than usual, because drafting too early would have locked in weak claims and rhetorical shortcuts.

I also noticed how much the quality of the final report depended on not over-trusting plausible wording. Many of the worst candidate sentences for 5.3.6 sounded sophisticated but collapsed when tested against the evidence chain. The session therefore rewarded disciplined reconstruction over fluent improvisation. By the end, the report sections felt more stable because they were anchored to methodology notes, raw captures, data reviews, and design artifacts that all pointed in the same direction.

## Lessons Learned

1. For complex report rewrites, produce the artifact chain first: raw evidence, methodology distillation, comparative logic, target design, source-availability review, and gap analysis. Only then draft final prose.
2. Distinguish native operational reporting from downstream analytical derivatives before making any architecture claim.
3. If a target section is conceptually weak, rewrite from an approved outline rather than iteratively patching legacy paragraphs.
4. Relief finance, compensation, and approved payment streams must never be allowed to masquerade as economic loss unless the methodology explicitly supports that interpretation.
5. When a writing skill imposes an outline-stop control boundary, respect it; that pause materially reduces downstream rewrite cost.
