## Architecture Decisions

## AI Diary
I initially fell into a trap of being too literal. When analyzing the workshop outputs, I saw highly specific technical demands—like "Engineering standards for transport" or "EEC water scarcity mapping"—and immediately flagged them as "New Use Cases" because the pre-workshop menu used softer, policy-oriented language. I was looking for exact semantic matches rather than functional intent. The human caught this drift. They forced me to look at the *Canonical* use cases. Once I re-anchored myself to the canonical menu, the entire strategic picture flipped: the stakeholders weren't rejecting the initial design by asking for new things; they were validating the design (77% match!) but demanding it be delivered as a hard utility (APIs) rather than a soft library (Dashboards). It was a good lesson in reading the *intent* behind the demand, not just the keywords.

## Honest Feedback
1. **The "Assumption" Friction**: I assumed the workshop's highly technical requests were novel deviations from the plan, leading to an over-count of "New Use Cases" (11 vs 6). I should have verified the bounds of the original use cases first.
2. **The "Search" Friction**: I struggled to locate the original menu definitions initially because of inconsistent file naming (`user_use_case_raw.md` vs `NCAIF_Use_Cases.md`). Better tagging or a central "Glossary" file would smooth this out.
3. **The "Too Fast" Friction**: I generated all 5 analysis deliverables in one go before asking for a sanity check. While efficient, it meant I had to rewrite three of them when the mapping logic was corrected.

## Lessons Learned
- **Functional Intent over Semantic Matching**: When normalizing stakeholder demands against a framework, look at the *functional intent* (e.g., "Assessing risk to roads") rather than the *delivery mechanism* (e.g., "API for engineering parameters") to determine if a use case is truly "New."
- **Anchor Before You Analyze**: Never synthesize data against a numbered taxonomy (like "1.1") without first reading the canonical definition of that taxonomy.
