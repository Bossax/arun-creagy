## Lessons Learned

- **Deleted content stays deleted until re-requested, full stop.** Even with a plausible-sounding path to reintroduce it as "supporting" material, a user's explicit removal is a boundary, not a suggestion to route around.
- **Read the style pack before drafting, not after being told to.** Citing a style pack's rule numbers without having read the actual document is worse than not citing it at all — it creates false confidence.
- **When compressing a multi-section document for altitude, track cross-section dependencies.** A fact cut from section A for brevity can silently orphan a claim in section B; a coherence pass after drafting a batch of sections is not optional busywork, it's where these actually surface.
- **"Any reference? Never heard of it" is a legitimate, answerable challenge — verify before either capitulating or doubling down.** The IPCC claim was real; I'd just cited it wrong. Checking beats guessing in either direction.
