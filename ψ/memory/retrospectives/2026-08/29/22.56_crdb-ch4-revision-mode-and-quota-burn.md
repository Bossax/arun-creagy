## AI Diary

This session had two very different halves, and I think I handled them differently well. The build-and-run half — designing revision mode, running the four-section pipeline, catching the Article 13 citation and the plan's own 4-vs-6 contradiction — felt like the harness working exactly as intended. I trusted the process enough to let the mapper flag problems rather than paper over them, and that paid off concretely three separate times.

The second half was harder and more honest. When Boss asked "how could this happen" about the quota, my first instinct was to explain the mechanism, which I did, but I want to be clear with myself that I didn't see it coming. I launched three full-depth Explore agents in parallel at the very start without weighing whether one would do, and I never once, across eight subsequent high-effort subagent calls, thought about the account's usage window as a resource to budget against — I was optimizing for harness correctness in isolation, not for the actual constraint I was operating under. When Boss then pointed me at the Antigravity retrospective, I could have been defensive about the comparison, or explained away the difference as "they cut corners." Instead reading it straight showed something more useful: they *did* cut a real corner (Stage 5 independence), and I *did* pay a real, avoidable cost (redundant cold-start reads, no model/effort tiering, no exploration scoping). Neither session was simply right. Writing the fork/fresh hybrid plan was more satisfying than the initial diagnosis, because it's a synthesis rather than a defense of either approach.

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points, stated plainly rather than softened.

First, I ran an expensive four-section pipeline with no way to know how much account quota it was consuming until it failed mid-batch. Three Stage-3 subagent calls returned `failed` simultaneously with no warning beforehand — I only learned the actual work had completed by reading the files directly, which is not a check I should have needed to do reactively after a failure notification told me something was wrong.

Second, I spent real cost on redundant work without noticing at the time. Every one of the eight Stage-1/Stage-3 subagent calls re-read the writing plan, the sources, and (for the mappers) the prior draft from a cold context, when my own orchestrating context already held nearly all of that. I know the `fork` mechanism exists and shares cache — I know the tool's own description says so — and I didn't reach for it, because the harness's canonical guidance (`subagent-prompts.md`) only shows `Agent(subagent_type: "th-...")`, never `fork`, and I followed that pattern uncritically rather than asking whether it was still the right one for a Claude Code session specifically.

Third, and this one is squarely on me: I never asked Boss, before launching the four-section batch late in a long session, whether now was a good time to start something this expensive, or whether it should be split across sessions. The user found out the hard way, mid-pipeline, that the session was close to a hard wall I hadn't surfaced.

## Lessons Learned

1. **A harness invariant about content isolation is not automatically an invariant about subagent freshness.** Re-reading `th-argument-mapper.md` and `th-verbalizer.md` closely showed their "must never load" lists are about *what enters the stage's context*, not about *whether the context is newly booted*. Conflating the two led straight to paying for eight cold-start subagent calls when at least some could safely have been forks.
2. **A same-day comparison session is a genuine audit tool, not just a curiosity.** The Antigravity Chapter 3 retrospective was sitting in the same memory tree the whole session; reading it on request, closely, surfaced both a real cost lever (cache reuse via in-line/fork execution) and a real correctness gap in the cheaper approach (self-graded Stage 5) that neither session had separately noticed on its own.
3. **"Do not economize on effort" is stage-specific guidance, not blanket guidance.** `SKILL.md` says this explicitly about Stage 1 (argument construction) — I had been applying the same high-effort setting to Stage 3 (verbalization, which the harness's own `prose-kernel.md` frames as transcription) without noticing the guidance never said to.
4. **Verify a subagent's actual disk output before trusting its reported status**, especially under rate-limit conditions. All three "failed" Stage-3 calls had in fact completed their writes; treating `status: failed` as `work: lost` would have meant needlessly redoing four verbalization passes.
