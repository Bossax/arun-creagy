## Honest Feedback

Three friction points worth naming honestly.

First, I made a structural content mistake early in the deck build that Boss had to catch: I designed slide 2 with three colored cards for "Monitoring / Evaluation / Learning" and then implemented only the labels, dropping the one-line definitions the deck plan itself called for. This wasn't a subtle bug — the cards were visibly near-empty in the rendered PDF I had already reviewed. I should have compared the built slide against my own plan document before calling it done, not just checked for overflow and contrast.

Second, the original 10-slide deck assumed background knowledge the audience didn't have — dropping "DCCE is at stage two of five" on the reader with no prior explanation of what the five stages were. This is a real design failure, not a preference difference: I built toward "cover the content" rather than "build the reader's understanding in order," and it took Boss's direct pushback to restructure the arc properly (background slides before application). I should be checking new decks against "would someone outside this conversation follow this" before presenting them, not after.

Third, on the process side: I never got explicit confirmation before diving into the second full deck rebuild (Soft Editorial). Boss's "ok and use Soft Editorial template style" was clear enough to proceed, but a full rebuild is a meaningfully bigger commitment than a style tweak, and I could have flagged that distinction before starting rather than after handing over 13 fully-rebuilt slides.

## Lessons Learned

- **Verify a built artifact against its own plan document, not just against general quality bars.** Checking a deck for overflow/contrast/readability is necessary but not sufficient — the plan itself (message hierarchy, what each slide is supposed to say) is the actual spec, and skipping that comparison let an empty-looking slide through.
- **When background knowledge is genuinely uneven between the deck author and the audience, sequence matters more than content completeness.** A deck can contain every needed fact and still fail if a term or number appears before it's been introduced. This is a distinct failure mode from missing content, and needs its own check pass.
- **When a tool used for verification (browser extension) is unavailable, look for an alternative verification path in the same toolset before falling back to "I can't check this."** The frontend-slides skill's own PDF export script was sitting right there and gave real, trustworthy verification — better than either guessing or telling the user to check it themselves.
- **A full style-system rebuild (different type system, different card language, different chrome) should be treated as a bigger commitment than a "restyle," even when the user's request is a short sentence.** Worth a beat of explicit scoping before executing, not just proceeding at the same pace as a small edit.
