## AI Diary

The key failure was not technical. I interpreted “use the Thai version to rebuild the slide deck” as permission to retain a prior visual structure and selectively translate it. That was not the task: the Thai draft was the complete source of truth. Boss had to correct that directly. The correct recovery was a full content replacement, not further patching.

[→ AGENT DECISION] I chose to patch the old English-source deck instead of first discarding its structure and rebuilding directly from the Thai draft. That decision created avoidable defects, including the missing cover and leftover source assumptions.

The PDF work then exposed a second, concrete issue: the direct browser method was the wrong tool, while the proven Playwright exporter relies on a `.slide` selector that the rebuilt deck does not satisfy. The browser runtime download also remained incomplete, so reporting an export would have been false.

## Lessons Learned

- When the user declares a document the “sole reference,” rebuild from that source rather than translating or repairing an earlier derivative.
- Preserve the export contract of the selected presentation tool: the frontend-slides PDF exporter requires `.slide` elements at a fixed 1920×1080 stage.
- For HTML deck PDFs, use the Playwright screenshot-based exporter rather than raw Chrome headless printing; direct print has known scaling, animation, and table-rendering failure modes.
- Verify the actual PDF artifact exists before saying an export succeeded.
