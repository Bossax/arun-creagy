## Honest Feedback

**Friction 1 — I initially treated "the DSDLC framing" as settled fact from the plan rather than as something to re-derive.** I had read the TOR70 SDLC storyline document once, weeks of session-time earlier in the conversation (during the fact-check pass), but only skimmed the "Reference lifecycle" section rather than fully absorbing it. When Boss asked which lifecycle I meant, I had to go back and actually read it properly. The lesson isn't "read everything fully the first time" (that doesn't scale), but rather: when a document contains a proposed alternative framework, don't just extract the parts relevant to the immediate question and move on — flag internally that there's more in there worth a second pass before it gets load-bearing.

**Friction 2 — the mechanical lint's lexicon conflict (กรมฯ vs กรม สส.) cost a full round-trip that could have been anticipated.** The chapter-4 plan's own style section explicitly overrode the project's default department shorthand, but I didn't check whether the global lexicon file had been updated to match before drafting. This is a known category of problem (newer, more specific rule vs. older, more general one) and I should default to checking for that kind of conflict before running the mechanical gate, not after it fails.

**Friction 3 — I didn't proactively flag that the master chapter-4 plan itself was falling out of sync with the exec-summary drafts.** By the end of the session, the plan document describes 4.3/4.4 differently than what actually got drafted (the plan doesn't yet have the 5-product strategy, the two-track structure, or the NESDC/MVP detail). I did flag this at the end, but I let it accumulate across three sections before saying anything clearly. A better practice would have been to say, right after the first structural departure from the plan, "this will need a plan-sync pass eventually — want me to do it now or batch it?"

## Lessons Learned

- **When a source document proposes an alternative framework to something already "settled" in a plan, read that section fully before the question about it comes up — not after.** Partial extraction under time pressure creates exactly the kind of gap that surfaces later as "wait, is this the right source?"
- **Before running a mechanical style gate on writing governed by a newer, more specific style rule, check whether the older/more general lexicon has been reconciled with it.** A contract-approved override is not the same as a lexicon update, and the gate will fail on the gap between them every time until one is fixed.
- **When a content change causes a document's structure to diverge from its own governing plan, say so immediately, not retrospectively.** Batching that observation until the end of a multi-section session makes the eventual sync work bigger and the drift easier to lose track of.
- **/fyi is the right tool for "good idea, wrong moment"** — deferring the NESDC/MVP content rather than derailing the in-progress §4.4 draft kept both threads clean and let Boss confirm the deferral explicitly rather than assuming it.
