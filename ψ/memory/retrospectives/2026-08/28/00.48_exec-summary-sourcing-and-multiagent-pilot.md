## Honest Feedback

Three friction points, stated plainly. First, I ran an entire multi-stage pipeline before establishing what "done" actually looks like beyond script exit codes — `lint_thai_writing.py` and `check_density.py` check narrow mechanical things (lexicon terms, character ratios), and I let two green checkmarks stand in for "this is good writing" when the actual quality bar lived in dimensions no script touches: does this belong in the document, does this section have real content, is this formatted the way a reader would want it. Second, when a correction landed in one place, I didn't generalize it to the rest of the document until asked — Boss eliminated "NCAIF" in one section and I revised a different section of the same file without checking whether his correction applied there too. That's a cheap check I skipped four separate times before finally doing it as policy. Third, my fixes were sometimes too broad in one direction and too narrow in another — cutting a whole paragraph to remove one bad phrase (losing real content that then needed restoring), while under-applying a legitimate lesson (reducing numbered-enumeration-as-rhetorical-hook) into banning list formatting outright, which was never what was asked. The throughline across all three: I was pattern-matching on the most recent correction instead of building a standing checklist and running the whole document against it before presenting anything. That's the fix now in place — the two new `LEXICON_TH.json` entries (NCAIF ban, artifact-citation ban) and writing-plan `## 10` were both add-once, apply-forever fixes, and I should default to that shape of fix rather than the reactive one.

## Lessons Learned

1. **Automated gates check a narrow slice of quality; don't let "gates passed" stand in for "this is done."** `lint_thai_writing.py` and `check_density.py` catch lexicon violations and compression — not scope drift, not thin subsections, not internal artifacts, not acronym consistency across a document. A real self-review pass against those dimensions needs to happen before presenting work, not be discovered by the human three rounds later.
2. **A correction made in one section of a document applies to the whole document until proven otherwise.** When a human eliminates a pattern in their own edit, grep the rest of the file for it immediately — don't wait to be told it recurred elsewhere.
3. **The strongest fix for a recurring mistake is one that doesn't depend on remembering it next time.** Prose added to a capture log is weaker than an actual `kind: literal`/`kind: regex` lexicon rule that blocks mechanically at Stage 5 — convert lessons into gates whenever the lesson is pattern-matchable, and only fall back to "written in the brief" for genuine judgment calls.
4. **A benchmark or lesson learned from one failure mode can be over-applied to suppress a different, legitimate pattern** — "reduce numbered-enumeration-as-rhetorical-hook" is not the same instruction as "avoid list formatting," and conflating them produced a new defect while fixing the original one.
