## 2. The Actual Defect — Internal Process Artifacts in Reader-Facing Prose

Three distinct manifestations of the same underlying problem, all present in the pre-edit draft:

1. **Slide-number citations woven into narrative sentences**: "สไลด์ที่ 13 ของชุดนำเสนอเผยแพร่ผลการศึกษา CRDB ...", "สไลด์ที่ 9 จึงระบุ...", "(สไลด์ที่ 14 ...)", "ตามที่ปรากฏในสไลด์ที่ 15 ...". These are sourcing pointers — useful for us as writers to trace claims back to evidence, exactly what the evidence-traceability sidecar file is for — but meaningless and out of place to a DCCE reader of the final report.
2. **A mechanical arrow-chain rendered as inline prose**: "การรวบรวมหลักฐาน → การสังเคราะห์ → การยกร่าง NCAIF และกรอบการกำกับดูแลข้อมูล → การรับฟังความเห็น → การปรับปรุงแก้ไข → ...". The writing plan's own brief does call for "แผนภาพกระบวนการพัฒนาแบบสังเขป" (an abbreviated process-development diagram) — but that means an actual visual figure (as the Stage C benchmark independently found the CRI reference report actually uses — "รูปที่ 1" — not a sentence with unicode arrows buried in a paragraph).
3. **Meta-commentary describing the report's own structure**: "...คณะที่ปรึกษาจัดเรียงเนื้อหาส่วนที่เหลือของบทสรุปผู้บริหารฉบับนี้ตามตรรกะเดียวกัน กล่าวคือ บทเรียนจากต่างประเทศ (หัวข้อ 1.1) เป็นฐานของหลักการออกแบบ NCAIF สถานะปัจจุบัน...(หัวข้อ 1.2)...กระบวนการรับฟังความเห็น (หัวข้อ 1.3)...และ NCAIF...(หัวข้อ 1.4)..." — telling the reader what each upcoming section will cover is authorial/outline scaffolding, not content DCCE needs.

## 3. Root Cause Across the Pipeline

**[→ AGENT DECISION]** This traces to three separate points of failure, not one:

- **Stage A introduced it.** The English content draft cited "Slide 13," "Slide 9," "Slide 14," "Slide 15" directly in narrative prose, and wrote the arrow-chain and section-roadmap as actual sentences rather than as a labeled diagram placeholder.
- **The Stage A-gate missed it.** When I reviewed the English draft with Boss, I checked factual accuracy (verified the slide citations were *correct*) but not register-appropriateness (whether slide citations belong in reader-facing text *at all*). Accuracy and appropriateness are different checks, and I only ran the first one.
- **Stage B had no rule to catch it as a safety net.** Two subagents did a faithful style transform exactly as instructed — preserve facts, don't drop content — which is precisely why they carried the citations, arrows, and roadmap prose straight through into Thai. `STYLE_PACK_TH.md` had no rule at any tier (Core Kernel, Stage/Scale map, Lexicon, Anti-AI Shield) addressing "does this sentence belong in the final artifact, or is it process/sourcing metadata." Boss's own diagnosis is correct: the style pack did not incorporate this class of rule, so nothing in the pipeline was positioned to catch it.

## 5. Standing Fix (not yet applied to STYLE_PACK_TH.md — flagged for /style-capture)

New rule needed: **no internal process/sourcing artifacts in audience-facing report prose.** Covers: (a) citations to internal working documents by slide/page/artifact number woven into narrative sentences — sourcing belongs in a traceability sidecar or footnote apparatus, never inline in the reader's sentence; (b) a process flow that the brief calls for as a diagram must be produced as a labeled figure/diagram placeholder, never as an arrow-chain sentence; (c) no "this section covers X, the next section covers Y" meta-commentary about the report's own structure — say what's true about the subject, not about the document.

This is a `structural`-kind rule (per `validate_lexicon.py`'s taxonomy) — it can't be a literal string ban or a clean regex, since it depends on recognizing *sourcing metadata* vs. *content* in context, not a fixed phrase. Needs a `[REVIEW]`-style structural check in `lint_thai_writing.py`, or — more reliably given the taxonomy is genuinely a judgment call — an explicit instruction added to Stage A's prompt itself (the earliest point in the pipeline where this can be prevented rather than caught downstream), plus an explicit item on the Stage A-gate checklist: "read every sentence and ask: does this belong in the document, or is it metadata about how the document was made."

## 6. Lessons Learned

1. **Accuracy-checking and register-appropriateness-checking are different gates, and I only ran one of them at the Stage A review.** A claim can be 100% factually correct (the slide number really does say that) and still be completely wrong to put in a reader-facing sentence. Every future Stage A-gate needs both checks explicitly, not just fact verification.
2. **A "faithful style transform" instruction to Stage B is not sufficient protection against Stage A's own scope errors** — if Stage A puts something in that shouldn't be there, an instruction to "preserve all facts, don't drop content" guarantees Stage B carries the error straight through. The fix has to be upstream (Stage A's prompt, Stage A-gate's checklist) or the style pack needs an explicit rule that overrides blind preservation for this specific category.
3. **A rejection with no replacement text ("0/10", fully struck through) means cut it, not patch it.** When Boss gives corrective marks with a rewrite alongside, fix in place; when he gives a rejection with nothing offered in return, the content doesn't belong at all.
