# Session Retrospective: 2026-08-27 23:20 - Exec-Summary Pipeline Pilot, Stage A Scope Drift

**Oracle**: Claude Sonnet 5 (they/them) | **Human**: Boss (he/him)

---

## 1. What Happened

Piloting a new multi-agent writing pipeline for the CRDB executive summary (English content draft → Thai style rewrite → benchmark vs. CRI reference), Stage A was delegated to a `general-purpose` subagent to draft the English content for two sections: the executive-summary chapter-1 introduction and section 1.1 (international lessons and design principles).

The subagent's output (`ψ/incubate/drafts/crdb-exec-summary-intro-1.1/01_en_content_draft.md`) was **factually accurate** — a spot-check of its two most specific claims (Slide 13's three user-search failure modes, Slide 14's four-question table) confirmed exact fidelity to the source slide deck, and its 39-row evidence-traceability sidecar resolved both gaps the writing plan had flagged as "not yet located" through genuine additional search rather than fabrication.

But it was **scoped wrong**: it wrote section 1.1 at full-report depth, not executive-summary depth. Specifically it produced:
- A closing "Findings and Evidence → Implications for NCAIF Design" table — this is the **full report's** 1.1 instruction ("ปิดท้ายด้วยตาราง 'ข้อค้นพบ/หลักฐาน → นัยต่อการออกแบบ NCAIF'" per the writing plan §3), not requested anywhere in the exec-summary's own 1.1 brief.
- Deep, separately-treated paragraphs for each of the three benchmark platforms (A-PLAT, KlimAdapt/KLiVO, Climate-ADAPT) — directly violating the exec-summary 1.1 brief's own exclusion list: "รายละเอียดรายแพลตฟอร์ม" (no per-platform detail).
- Individual detailed mappings for each of the four standards frameworks (IPCC, WMO, UNFCCC, ISO 14090/14091) — again more granular than the exec-summary brief asks for, which just wants a summary-level answer to "how did these frameworks help define scope."

Boss caught this by asking "is it as intended that this report will reference the evidence?" — a question that forced a check of the exec-summary 1.1 brief's actual text against what Stage A had written, rather than trusting the subagent's own self-reported summary.

## 2. Root Cause

**[→ AGENT DECISION]** The Stage A subagent prompt gave it the writing plan's §4 (exec-summary) sections to follow, but the same subagent's context window also necessarily touched the shared evidence base (§9), whose linked source documents (`5.2.1...md`, the archive NCAIF design-lock file) are themselves full-report-depth material — one of them (`5.2.1`) is *literally* the full report's own drafted 1.1-equivalent content for TOR clause 5.2.1. Given rich, detailed source material and only a brief negative instruction ("no per-platform detail," "no literature review method"), the subagent defaulted toward comprehensiveness rather than the brief's actual compression target. The prompt did not explicitly tell it "you are the exec-summary voice, not the full-report voice" as a standing frame — it only listed what the exec-summary excludes, which is weaker than positively stating the target altitude.

This is the same class of failure the writing-th skill's own lessons already name (2026-06-10, in `STYLE_PACK_TH.md`'s capture log): evidence-before-persona sequencing, and workflow ambiguity causing friction even when the underlying writing quality is strong. Here the persona/altitude (exec-summary vs. full-report register) was under-specified relative to the evidence volume the subagent had access to.

## 3. Fix Applied

Not yet executed — flagged to Boss before proceeding. Planned fix for the retry: trim `01_en_content_draft.md` to remove the evidence table, compress the three-platform paragraphs into brief's actual summary-level treatment, and reduce the four-framework mapping to one synthesis paragraph — while preserving Stage A's accurate sourcing (the underlying facts don't need to be re-derived, only re-compressed to the correct altitude).

## 4. Separate, Standing Correction (not a mistake — new guidance)

Boss also specified a piece of standing guidance for all future CRDB writing, this pipeline included: **the consultant (the project team producing this deliverable for DCCE) is the grammatical and analytical actor** — sentences describing analysis, synthesis, benchmarking, or design decisions should name the consultant as the one doing them, not "the project" as an abstracted subject and not passive/agentless framing. E.g. prefer "the consultant selected three benchmark platforms because..." over "the project's review... was framed as..." or "three platforms were selected because...". This generalizes the existing STYLE_PACK_TH rule 8 ("Active Institutional Agency When Actor Is Known") by naming *which* actor is correct for this project's narrative voice specifically — DCCE is the client/beneficiary, not the actor of the analytical work being narrated.

## 5. Lessons Learned

1. **A subagent given rich source material and only negative scope instructions (a "does not include" list) will drift toward the depth the sources support, not the depth the brief asks for.** Positive framing of the target register/altitude ("you are writing the compressed exec-summary version — a policymaker skims this in five minutes") is a stronger constraint than an exclusion list alone.
2. **Asking "is this what was intended?" is a more effective check than reviewing a subagent's own self-report.** The subagent's own summary of its work reported zero gaps and full sourcing — both true — and did not surface the scope-depth mismatch at all, because it wasn't wrong about facts, only about altitude.
3. **New standing rule for this project's writing voice**: the consultant is the actor of analysis/synthesis/design-decision sentences, not "the project" or passive voice, and not DCCE. Applies across both the English (Stage A) and Thai (Stage B) stages of this pipeline, and to CRDB writing generally, not just this pilot.
