## Honest Feedback

Three friction points, described plainly:

1. **I mis-sorted evidence by document form instead of content twice in a row before catching the pattern.** First miss: didn't read the founding NCAIF doc at all in my first trace pass, because the v5-era spec and May 12 presentation looked complete enough on their own — I stopped searching once I had "enough" rather than the *right* root document. Second miss: correctly found the UX principles doc but then filed it wrong because it was citation-heavy. Both are the same failure mode — trusting surface signals (existence of a plausible answer, academic formatting) over verifying against the actual argument or actual chain of dependency. I should default to asking "is this the origin, or just where I found it" before treating a document as settled.

2. **The grep-vs-trace redirect earlier in the session was a useful correction I should generalize.** Boss stopped me mid-grep and said "use trace then, dont just use grep" for a factual question (workshop date) that had real stakes (misdating evidence changes what counts as authoritative). I complied but didn't proactively apply the same discipline to the later "core references" question until Boss again said "use trace" explicitly. I should treat any date-sensitive or authority-sensitive factual claim as trace-worthy by default, not wait to be told each time.

3. **Pulse data is stale (last updated March 2026) and I'm still reading it into every recap/rrr even though it's clearly not tracking real activity.** It added no signal this session and I should probably flag to Boss that pulse tracking looks broken rather than silently including a "1-day streak" that's almost certainly wrong.

## Lessons Learned

When asked to trace or explain the origin of a design decision, verify you've found the *root* document (earliest, most foundational) before answering — a complete-looking answer built on a mid-chain document is still wrong, even if internally consistent. Separately: sort evidence by what it actually claims, not by its surface presentation (citations, formatting, academic tone) — a UX research brief is IA content regardless of how many footnotes it has.
