## Findings That Matter

The domain distribution shows that the inventory has substantial candidate material for describing exposed assets, populations, and vulnerability conditions. It does not, by itself, prove that the system can reliably identify who and what is at risk. That stronger claim depends on provenance, spatial and temporal resolution, accessibility, update frequency, verification, licensing, lineage, and the ability to link exposure and vulnerability records to hazards and observed impacts.

The inventory therefore supports a narrower conclusion: Thailand has a broad but uneven stock of candidate exposure and vulnerability evidence suitable for initial screening, while operational risk assessment remains constrained by restricted access and weak metadata quality.

The product inventory points in the same direction. Dashboard and GIS presentation is relatively mature, but traceable APIs, machine-readable delivery, and complete domestic product accounting are less evident. The unresolved 99-versus-76 discrepancy should be closed before product-level percentages are quoted as definitive.

## Reusable Lesson

Inventory volume is evidence of catalogued stock, not evidence of operational capability. Capability claims require row-level tests of provenance, accessibility, granularity, currency, verification, licensing, duplication, stewardship, and cross-domain linkage. Likewise, a service narrative should be anchored in the canonical service identity: blockers and implementation mechanisms may shape the business case, but they should not redefine the service itself.
