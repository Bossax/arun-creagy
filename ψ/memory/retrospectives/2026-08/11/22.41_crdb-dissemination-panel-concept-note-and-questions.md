## Honest Feedback

**Friction point 1 — scope-checking discipline.** I answered two full turns of "what would make this panel worth attending" before actually checking whether CRDB had anything to demo. The project's own AGENTS.md says clearly that CRDB is Planning+Requirements+Design, hands off to TOR70. I should treat "check the project's own orientation doc before proposing content" as a standing first move for any CRDB task, not something I do only after being corrected.

**Friction point 2 — treating corrections as scoped narrowly.** Twice this session a correction applied to one instance of a problem (a specific question), and I fixed that instance without checking siblings (backups, follow-ups, other speakers) for the same pattern. This cost Boss a second round of the same feedback. When a stated rule is "don't do X," the right response is a full-text audit for X, not a spot-fix.

**Friction point 3 — cultural/tone judgment on panel questions.** Two of my own drafted questions (forcing MOU commitments, "will your members actually use it or go elsewhere") read as reasonable "sharp journalism" framing to me in isolation, but were genuinely inappropriate for a Thai government multi-stakeholder dissemination panel — putting a public official on the spot to commit institutionally, or implying criticism of DCCE's own work to a guest's face, isn't the register this kind of event runs in. I don't have a strong intuition for this register by default and needed direct correction twice. Worth internalizing as a standing constraint for any future DCCE/government-facing panel content: no forced live commitments, no implied criticism of a speaker's own past work or agency, ever, checked across every variant of every question before it's presented as done.

## Lessons Learned

- For CRDB tasks specifically: re-check `ψ/incubate/DCCE/CRDB/AGENTS.md` (or the current project state) before assuming what has or hasn't been built — CRDB is planning/design only, TOR70 builds.
- When a correction is stated as a rule ("don't do X"), apply it as a full-document audit across all variants (mains, seconds, follow-ups, backups) in the same pass — not just to the flagged instance.
- For Thai government dissemination/panel content: never ask a speaker to commit their agency to something on the spot (MOU, formal position), and never frame a question as if a speaker's own past work failed — both read as genuinely disrespectful in this context, not just stylistically off.
