- Forking for large, mechanical-but-judgment-requiring work (260-row catalog scan, 31-node storyboard writing) kept quality high while managing context — and the fork's own initiative (flagging `DDPM_2_1`) was a genuine, unprompted catch.

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points worth naming. First, the sheer length of the execution phase risked losing the thread — 30 decisions across one document and 5 CSVs is a lot of state to hold, and I leaned on the task list heavily to avoid dropping something; that's the right tool, but it also means a shorter, more frequently-checkpointed approach might have been safer for a task this size. Second, I made a judgment call to fork 5 parallel agents for the storyboard guide without asking Boss first — reasonable given the plan already flagged this as the largest component, but worth naming that I didn't pause for confirmation before spawning that many parallel forks, since it's a real resource commitment. Third, and most honestly: the diff shows a fully-built presentation HTML file that I have no direct record of creating within what I experienced as this conversation. I flagged that uncertainty in the retrospective rather than paper over it, but it's a real gap in my own accounting of what happened this session, and I'd rather surface it than pretend the record is cleaner than it is.

## Lessons Learned

- **When a review surfaces a structural fact mid-execution (not mid-review), stop and flag it rather than silently absorbing it into the plan.** The Slow-Onset Hazards consolidation was discovered while executing an already-approved plan; flagging it and getting explicit confirmation before deviating was the right call, even though it slowed things down.
- **Mechanical, well-bounded catalog/data-matching work is a strong fit for forking, and forks can genuinely find things a human review missed** (`DDPM_2_1`). Don't treat that as luck — a full-catalog scan is exactly the kind of task forking is for.
- **Long single-session multi-part execution (24+ interlocking decisions, 5+ files) benefits from more frequent explicit checkpoints with the user**, not just internal task tracking — the task list kept me honest, but Boss only saw progress in occasional summary messages across a very long stretch.
