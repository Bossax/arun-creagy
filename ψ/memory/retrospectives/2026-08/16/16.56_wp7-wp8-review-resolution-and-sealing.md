## Honest Feedback

First, I offered the combined WP7+WP8 seal proposal too eagerly, without being asked to combine them. Boss had asked to seal "each" WP, plural but distinct, and I read that as an invitation to batch rather than sequence. Sealing is a ledger-integrity operation; batching two work packages into one chain would have blurred which mandate justified which artifact, and Boss caught this before any ledger write happened. I should default to one seal per deliverable unless told otherwise, since that's what the ledger structure itself implies (Deliverable Map rows are per-artifact).

Second, I initially treated "Trigger" too loosely, almost as a slot for "whatever most recently caused a file to change" rather than "what created the obligation for this deliverable to exist." The seal skill's own Hard Rule 1 (motive/artifact separation) gestures at this, but I still needed the direct correction to apply it correctly at the trigger-selection level, not just the motive-vs-artifact level within one entry.

Third, on the plan-mode fact-check: I ran the WebSearch and Perplexity queries reasonably well, but I didn't proactively suggest incorporating the citation into the recommendation text itself until Boss asked for it explicitly. Given that the whole point of a fact-check mid-plan is usually to strengthen the actual deliverable, I could have offered "want this cited in the text itself, or just confirmed for my own confidence" as a next-step question rather than waiting to be told.

## Lessons Learned

A Trigger in the T-E-D-A ledger pattern must trace to the artifact's originating mandate (the plan, TOR item, or decision that created the obligation for the deliverable to exist), never to the most recent session that touched it. When resuming work on an already-mandated deliverable, check the sprint plan or equivalent source-of-truth for the deliverable's own row before proposing a trigger — the real trigger is very often already written down and just needs to be found, not invented.
