## Honest Feedback

Three friction points worth naming plainly, not softened.

First, I treated "the user is engaging with my plan" as equivalent to "the user has approved my plan." Those are different signals and I collapsed them. A refinement to an unapproved plan is not the same speech act as approval, and I should have asked directly rather than inferred.

Second, I over-produced early. The first standalone-section draft, the one built around a five-row table with sub-notes, took real research and drafting effort, and Boss rejected the entire shape of it on the next turn. Some of that research was reusable (the WP5/WP7/June-chapter reads), but the structural work wasn't. A tighter check-in before committing to a full section structure would have caught the "should this even be a separate section" question before I'd built one.

Third, the tone corrections came late and had to be applied twice, once narrowly to section 1 and once broadly to everything. If I'd internalized "write forward-facing, no audit contrasts" as a default posture for DCCE-facing prose earlier in the session, rather than something Boss had to name explicitly partway through, the first full draft would have needed less rework. This is now captured in memory, which should help going forward, but it cost a redraft this time.

## Lessons Learned

Refinement is not approval. When a user is iterating on a plan's content across multiple messages, none of those messages authorize execution unless one of them says so directly or a tool-level approval mechanism (like ExitPlanMode) confirms it. Wait for the explicit signal even when the conversation feels collaborative and moving toward a shared answer.
