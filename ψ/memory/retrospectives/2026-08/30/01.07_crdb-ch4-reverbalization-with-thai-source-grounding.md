## AI Diary & Agent Decisions

[→ AGENT DECISION] In the initial Chapter 4 run, I treated the strict context isolation invariant ("Verbalizer must never load raw sources") as an absolute dogma without recognizing its linguistic side-effect: when an argument map is specified entirely in English logical notation, stripping all Thai reference text deprives the verbalizer of language and domain cadence. As a result, the model treated verbalization as a translation task rather than a synthesis task. I should have recognized earlier that Thai source grounding is necessary as a stylistic and terminological anchor even when argument boundaries are fixed by the map.

---

## Lessons Learned & Heuristics

1. **Content Authority vs. Language Anchor**:
   - Isolating the verbalizer from raw facts is essential to prevent argument drift, but isolating it from **Thai domain language** starves the model's idiomatic generation.
   - The correct boundary is: `argument-map.json` is the **sole authority on facts and claims**, while `source_paths` and `reference_samples` serve as **language anchors for cadence and phrasing**.

2. **The 'Missing Middle' in LLM Writing Pipelines**:
   - A pipeline with only L1 (Lexicon regex linter) and L4/L5 (Toulmin argument map + Minto rubric) leaves L2 (Syntax) and L3 (Sentence Agency) unverified.
   - Structural rules (`STR-006` Subject Anchoring, `STR-007` Direct Forward Syntax) must be explicitly checked during Stage 5 review to prevent translated calques from slipping through.

3. **Subagent Model Tier for Verbalization**:
   - Pro models significantly outperform smaller Flash models in synthesizing complex administrative and technical concepts into fluent Thai without falling back on rigid translation templates.
