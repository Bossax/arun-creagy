## AI Diary

This session had an unusually clean rhythm to it, and I think it's worth naming why. The early scoping conversation (NFRs, reference data, business rationale) worked because I kept refusing to let ambiguity slide — when the user asked "is Business NFR per one data product?" I could have just said "yes" or "no" confidently, but the honest answer was that the plan itself was internally ambiguous (Data Contracts and Business NFRs both claimed the same content at different granularities), and naming that ambiguity directly turned out to be more useful than resolving it prematurely on their behalf. That pattern repeated later with the folder restructure: I could have just started running `git mv` the moment the user said "restructure the folder," but the two taxonomies genuinely don't map 1:1, and pretending they do would have produced either broken links or a folder structure that lied about what it contained. Flagging the tradeoff and asking felt like the right amount of friction — not so much that it stalled momentum, but enough that the user made an informed, explicit call (full merge, not the safer index-only option) rather than inheriting my default.

The moment I'm least sure about is the git mv failure partway through — moving `04_Glossary` into a not-yet-created `05_Data_Management_Framework/Glossary` failed silently-ish (a `fatal: renaming... ## What Could Improve

- I should have anticipated the `git mv` nested-destination failure before hitting it — Windows Git's behavior here (destination parent must exist) is a known quirk, and a `mkdir -p` up front for every nested destination would have avoided the interruption entirely.
- The AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md files and the WP0 restructuring commit happened between my visible turns (user committed directly, outside my own commit calls) — I don't have full visibility into exactly what the user changed versus what I wrote, which is fine here since `git status` confirmed a clean tree, but it's worth staying alert to in future sessions rather than assuming my last-known-state is authoritative.

## Lessons Learned

- When a redirection/strategy document claims completeness ("every component has a named home"), it's worth spot-checking that claim against its own tables rather than trusting the prose — the Item 1 gap was found exactly this way.
- Restructuring two non-1:1 taxonomies (CRDB's internal pillars vs. DCCE's requested items) is not a rename — it requires an explicit decision about scope and execution risk, and that decision belongs to the user, not to a default "least surprising" choice on my part.
- `git mv` on this Windows/Git setup does not auto-create nested destination parent directories — always `mkdir -p` the parent first when moving into a new nested path.
