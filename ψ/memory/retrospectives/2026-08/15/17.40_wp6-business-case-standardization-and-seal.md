## Honest Feedback

Three friction points worth naming. First, I initially treated Boss's "let's seal these artifacts and close wp6" as if the causal story was self-evident from the conversation I'd just had — it wasn't, and I should have asked what the trigger actually was before drafting a T-E-D-A proposal, rather than drafting one and waiting to be corrected. The correction cost a full extra round-trip that a single clarifying question up front might have avoided. Second, the business-case restructuring itself involved judgment calls I flagged but didn't always resolve before writing (dropping the "three loss categories" count without a replacement number, moving the SEC/BoT disclosure trend from side note into the core case) — Boss let both stand implicitly by not objecting, but I'd have more confidence in those calls with an explicit yes rather than an inferred one. Third, this session ran long and dense (10 individually-grounded comments, a full 9-service + 2-deliverable rewrite, a ledger seal, a plan amendment) without a natural checkpoint besides the Service-1 sample — if the business-case reframe had gone wrong, I'd have discovered it after rewriting all nine sections, not after one. The sample-first approach Boss suggested was the right instinct; I should propose it myself earlier in similarly broad rewrites rather than waiting to be asked.

## Lessons Learned

When a user says "let's seal X" right after a body of work, the trigger is not always "the work I just watched happen" — ask what the actual decision or motive was before drafting the T-E-D-A chain, especially when the work spanned multiple distinct threads (comment-fixing vs. a structural standardization decision, in this case). Confirm scope explicitly on any correction that could touch files beyond the ones directly in view (the NFR-drop question turned out to affect the sprint plan in six places, not just the two narrative files).
