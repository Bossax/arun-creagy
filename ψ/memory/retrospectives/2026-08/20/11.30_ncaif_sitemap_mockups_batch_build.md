## AI Diary

This session marked a massive leap in consistency and speed for platform visualization. Building mockups for an official government climate portal requires a delicate balance: on one hand, every page must look like an active, functional software system; on the other hand, it must strictly honor the legal and institutional taxonomy agreed upon with government stakeholders (DCCE, NESDC, TMD, GIZ).

By embedding high-resolution base64 assets directly into the HTML source, every mockup became completely portable, rendering identically in headless Playwright Chromium at 1280×1024 without network lag or broken relative paths. The Climate Analytics gridded cards approach for Page 4 gave the diverse assortment of tools (GIS, macroeconomic loss models, open data APIs, finance trackers) a coherent, unified feel.

## Honest Feedback (3 Friction Points)

1. **Aspect Ratio Cramping on High-Density Dashboards**: Embedding dense dashboards (like `MandE_water.png` or `ccic_risk_map`) into fixed 10:8 frames requires aggressive removal of decorative helper elements so that the primary data figures remain readable at full slide resolution.
2. **Terminology Cascades**: Changing a core term like *"ระบบรับรองการหลีกเลี่ยงความสูญเสีย"* to *"เครื่องมือการประเมินทางเศรษฐศาสตร์ของการลงทุนด้านการปรับตัว (Economics of Climate Adaptation Tool)"* creates a cascade across DRD requirement sheets, gap analysis CSVs, and slide deck plans. Flagging this systematically ensures we do not leave orphaned legacy terms behind.
3. **Escaped Backtick Syntactic Bug in Quick Scripts**: Creating multi-line template literal strings inside scratch scripts occasionally causes syntax errors when nested console backticks are not escaped properly. Using standard string concatenation or raw file writers avoids unnecessary rerun cycles.
