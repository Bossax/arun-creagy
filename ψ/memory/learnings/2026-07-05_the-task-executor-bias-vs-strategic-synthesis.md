# The "Task Executor" Bias vs Strategic Synthesis in Executive Summaries

## 1. The "Task Executor" Bias (Checking the Box)
AI agents easily fall into the "Task Executor" trap where the goal becomes "generate a markdown file to check a box" rather than "synthesize an analytical narrative." When delegating to subagents, the focus often drifts to producing output rather than understanding the outcome. Do not treat executive summaries as administrative meeting minutes; treat them as a unified strategic thesis.

## 2. Lossy Compression (The Skimming Trap)
When trying to summarize hundreds of pages of dense institutional architecture, agents default to aggressive, lossy compression. Extracting the most visible nouns (e.g., "6 IA Groups", "3 L&D Layers") and stitching them together leads to a catastrophic loss of institutional mechanisms. You must explicitly read terminal evidence files (e.g., Data Governance structures, Policy Recommendations) in full to extract the specific mechanisms (Pillars, Roles, Standards) before drafting.

## 3. The Fallacy of Structural Mimicry
Technical reports are often structured as a chronological ledger of activities. However, an Executive Summary must NEVER follow chronological mimicry. It must follow a strict Narrative Logic Arc: Problem (Baseline context/fragments) -> Evidence (Gaps) -> Analysis -> Solution (Architecture) -> Roadmap. Presenting solutions without establishing the chaotic baseline reality makes the interventions look unprompted rather than deliberate.

## 4. Over-Relying on "Style Pack" over Substance
Perfect syntax and stylistic compliance (e.g., avoiding parenthetical English or specific filler words) is useless if the strategic payload is missing. Never prioritize syntactic formatting over semantic payload. 

## Rule of Execution
You cannot just "delegate a summary" to a subagent with a generic prompt. You must first manually define the **Narrative Logic Arc (Evidence -> Analysis -> Solution -> Roadmap)**, aggressively extract the institutional mechanisms, and forcefully inject them into the drafting constraints.
