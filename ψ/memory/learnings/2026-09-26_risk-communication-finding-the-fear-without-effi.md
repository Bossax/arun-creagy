# Risk-communication finding: the \"fear without efficacy\" failure mode.

Risk-communication finding: the "fear without efficacy" failure mode.

A disaster/hazard warning that raises threat perception (how severe, how likely, how personal) without also building coping appraisal (the recipient's confidence they can act, and belief the recommended action actually works) tends to produce fatalism, feeling overwhelmed, or outright rejection of the warning — not preparedness. Threat appraisal alone is not sufficient; both threat appraisal AND coping appraisal have to land together to convert awareness into protective action.

This explains why technically accurate, well-disseminated warnings still fail to move people: they scare without empowering. Fix direction: pair every threat statement with a concrete, doable action and a reason to believe that action works (not just "evacuate" but "evacuate to X, here's why that keeps you safe, here's how long it takes").

Source: Abunyewah et al., "A Conceptual Framework for Motivating Actions Towards Disaster Preparedness Through Risk Communication," Procedia Engineering 212 (2018) — read via NotebookLM notebook "Disaster Risk Communication".
