# CRDB chapter 5.2 naturalization lesson

When a report section is already substantively complete, the highest-value edit is often not structural redesign but sentence-level naturalization. Preserve the section order, preserve the evidence, and let rhythm, transitions, and noun choice do the work of making the prose feel authored.

Formal Thai reports become more natural when the acting subject is the real team rather than the project container. If `โครงการ` is only a context label, do not let it act as the grammatical subject. Replace that role with `คณะทำงาน` or another real actor so the sentence reads like human work rather than abstract process.

Style guides built for public-facing writing can still improve formal report prose, but only the compatible parts should be borrowed. Use them for cadence, clarity, and smoother transitions, not for article-style looseness or overly expressive openings.

## Generalizable pattern
- Hold structure fixed when the content is already stable.
- Focus the edit on rhythm, transitions, and subject choice.
- Treat typo-like wording defects as readability issues, not just spelling issues.
- Keep the human actor visible in every action sentence.
