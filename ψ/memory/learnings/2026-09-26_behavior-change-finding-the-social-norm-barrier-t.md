# Behavior-change finding: the social-norm barrier to protective action, and the f

Behavior-change finding: the social-norm barrier to protective action, and the fix (Bangladesh Cyclone Preparedness Programme).

Even when people fully understand and believe an official warning, many still won't act on it out of fear of being judged by neighbors for "trying something new" or overreacting — evacuating alone can feel socially risky even when it's physically safer. Pure information or even a credible warning doesn't remove this barrier, because it's a social-conformity problem, not a knowledge or trust problem.

Documented fix: a national TV program that showed whole communities evacuating and preparing together, normalizing the behavior as a shared, socially accepted action rather than an individual, judgment-inviting one. Result: 47% of viewers took protective action after watching. This is distinct from — and complementary to — building source credibility (see the Cuba trust-rebuild finding); it targets social proof, not authority.

Broader context: this fix is one part of the Bangladesh Cyclone Preparedness Programme, a joint government/Red Crescent venture running ~56,000 local volunteers doing house-to-house engagement and drills ("bridging the last meter, not just the last mile"), credited with cutting cyclone mortality from ~300,000 (Cyclone Bhola, 1970) to ~3,000 (Sidr, 2007) to 26 (Amphan, 2020).
