# Organizational-design finding: five specialist roles/structures that close the \"

Organizational-design finding: five specialist roles/structures that close the "communication isn't a specialist discipline" gap in hazard/disaster agencies.

When an agency treats risk communication as something any technical staffer can produce (rather than as its own professional discipline), the literature points to five concrete fixes, each evidence-backed:

1. Meteorologist-communicators — trained technical experts deliver warnings on-air themselves, rather than handing content to generalist media presenters (avoids misinterpretation, projects authority).
2. Media-trained scientists — experts specifically trained and willing to engage journalists directly, closing the common "we can't find a scientist willing to talk to us" gap reporters describe.
3. Risk-communication/persuasion specialists — staff with a social-science background (not just physical science) who understand motivation, self-efficacy, and audience segmentation, and design messages accordingly.
4. A formally mandated communication/dissemination unit — a structural fix, not just individual hires: a unit with real budget and mandate, equal in institutional standing to the forecasting/monitoring unit, not an afterthought bolted onto it.
5. Trusted intermediary networks — volunteers, civil society organizations, faith-based or traditional local leaders who carry the warning "the last meter" (not just "the last mile") and are trusted more than a central authority in low-trust or hard-to-reach contexts.

Precondition: this fix only sticks if the underlying institutional mentality shifts from "communication = broadcasting, job done once transmitted" to "communication = a high-value task with its own expertise that determines whether the technical work matters at all." Without that shift, new posts/titles get filled by whoever is available and the old pattern repeats under a new name.

Source: synthesized across Abunyewah et al. (2018), Perera et al. (2020), Parida et al. (2021, Cyclone Amphan media study), and "Should I stay or should I go now" (Bangladesh CPP) — read via NotebookLM notebook "Disaster Risk Communication".
