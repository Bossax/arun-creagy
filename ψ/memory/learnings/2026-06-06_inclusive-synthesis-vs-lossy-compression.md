# Inclusive Synthesis vs. Lossy Compression: Prioritizing Stakeholder Visibility

In the design of multi-agency technical systems like the NCAIF, "Synthesis" is often misapplied as a reductive process. This leads to **Lossy Compression**, where granular agency needs are "swallowed" into broader architectural narratives to achieve a "cleaner" design.

### The Problem: Narrative Erasure
When optimizing for "Institutional Scenarios" or "Service Stories," there is a bias toward the most prominent agencies (e.g., Treasury, National Planning). Fringe but critical agencies (e.g., Public Health, Land Development) are often deleted from the report because their specific functions do not fit the dominant narrative.

### The Solution: Platform as Portfolio (Inclusive Synthesis)
A successful synthesis in a government context must follow a **Domain-First** modular architecture:
1.  **Skeleton (The Portfolio)**: Maintain an exhaustive list of agency-named modules (the "Menu"). Every agency must find its name in the report.
2.  **Flesh (The Scenarios)**: Inject the "Why" (Forensic Scenarios) and "How" (International Benchmarks) into each module.
3.  **Governance Baseline**: Use the high-level Service Platforms only as "Containers" for these modules, rather than as the primary products themselves.

### Key Learning:
A technical specification's success is measured by how well it protects the **Institutional Sovereignty** of each participant while providing the shared **Authoritative Baseline**.
