# Legal-instrument-completeness checks need a \"who writes this\" filter, not just a

Legal-instrument-completeness checks need a "who writes this" filter, not just a "is this legally required" filter.

Context: Reviewing §4.2's list of legal instruments (เครื่องมือ) in a Sub-law TOR technical proposal for completeness, checking whether any legally-necessary step was missing a corresponding deliverable.

What happened: Found that Chapter 12's adaptation plans have no legal force until published in the Royal Gazette (ราชกิจจานุเบกษา) — confirmed both from the Act's own text and from web research on Thai administrative law and comparative international practice. Presented this as a missing instrument to add to the tools list. Boss pushed back: other national plans already have established publication channels — why would this need a new instrument just for adaptation plans? Correct — Gazette submission is a standard promulgation channel every ประกาศ or กฎกระทรวง uses regardless of subject matter. There is no separate document with legal content that needs drafting for it; the already-drafted, already-approved plan just gets submitted through the same channel as any other departmental announcement. This is categorically different from a genuine gap (e.g., a sector lead-agency designation ประกาศ, which does have substantive content — specific agency names, selection basis, legal citations — that someone has to actually write).

Lesson: When checking a document for missing legal instruments, apply two filters, not one: (1) is this step legally required for enforceability, and (2) does someone actually have to draft content for it, or is it a standard administrative/procedural channel that already exists independent of the subject matter. Only findings that pass both belong in an instruments/deliverables list. A step can be legally necessary without being a drafting gap.

How to apply: Before flagging a "missing instrument" in any legal-completeness review, ask whether the proposed addition would have any unique content to author, or whether it's just "submit the already-finished document through the channel every similar document uses." If the latter, it belongs as a process step inside an existing SOP/workflow description, not as a new deliverable line item.
