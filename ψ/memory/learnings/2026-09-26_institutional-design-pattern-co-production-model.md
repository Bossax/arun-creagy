# Institutional-design pattern: co-production model for impact-based warnings (Pur

Institutional-design pattern: co-production model for impact-based warnings (Puri district, Odisha case).

Hazard-forecasting agencies (e.g. a national met department) typically hold the technical mandate and hazard science but not local exposure/vulnerability data (who lives where, what livelihoods, what infrastructure). Local government bodies hold that vulnerability data (via things like district Hazard Risk Vulnerability analyses) but have no forecasting capability and no formal role in warning production. The result: official warnings state the hazard accurately but miss the specific consequences people actually need (e.g. an accurate cyclone forecast that doesn't warn about the specific crop losses that matter most to farmers).

Finding: residents already informally bridge this gap themselves — blending official hazard warnings with local knowledge (wind direction, tidal behavior, proximity to specific geographic features, compound-hazard reasoning like "trees fall not from wind alone but because prior rain first weakens roots") to work out real consequences. The capability to translate hazard into impact already exists locally; it is simply unrecognized and uninstitutionalized, not absent.

Recommended institutional fix: a collaborative-governance / co-production model where the hazard-forecasting agency keeps the lead forecasting role, but formally shares warning-production with the agencies that hold local vulnerability/sector data (e.g. agriculture department for crop data, disaster-management authority for evacuation/insurance mandate) via: (a) redefined legal mandates making these agencies active co-creators rather than passive downstream recipients, (b) mandated real-time data-sharing protocols connecting local vulnerability databases to the central forecasting tool, and (c) — critically — an explicit accountability framework for false alarms and over/under-warning across the now-shared pipeline, since without an agreed rule for who bears blame for a bad call, multi-agency co-production stalls.

This is a reusable institutional pattern for any "single technical agency has the hazard science but not the local consequence data" situation, regardless of country or hazard type.

Source: Dash, "Gap between impact-based and impact forecast and warning: people-centric EWS in India," Natural Hazards Research 4 (2024) — read via NotebookLM notebook "Disaster Risk Communication".
