# A DFR source document's account of its own methodology (e.g. \"collection ran thr

A DFR source document's account of its own methodology (e.g. "collection ran through 44 agencies via a standard form", "the option list was derived from reviewing platforms A/B/C") is a claim the document makes about itself, not a verified fact — it needs the same source-fidelity scrutiny as any other claim, especially once the same document set has already produced one confirmed error (a citation to a non-existent source file). In the CRDB §3.4 session, two such self-described methodology claims turned out to be false when checked against the human who actually did the work. Lesson: when an argument-map unit's grounds rest on a source's account of its own process, and it cannot be cross-checked against an independent artifact, flag it for human sign-off rather than treating "the source document says so" as verification.
