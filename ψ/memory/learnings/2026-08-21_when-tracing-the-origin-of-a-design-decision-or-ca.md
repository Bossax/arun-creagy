# When tracing the origin of a design decision or categorizing source material, ve

When tracing the origin of a design decision or categorizing source material, verify the root document (not a downstream restatement) and sort evidence by what it actually argues, not by its surface form (citations, formatting, academic tone). On the CRDB project specifically: a UX research brief with citation-heavy formatting was mis-filed as "academic/domain content" when its actual argument (progressive disclosure, decoupled architecture, narrative-driven IA) was pure information-architecture content. Separately, a v5-era technical spec was treated as the origin of NCAIF's design principles when an earlier founding design-lock doc (Jan-Mar 2026, Pack A/B/C matrix + IPCC/WMO/UNFCCC/ISO standards check) was the actual root. Lesson: before answering "why does X exist" or categorizing a source into a framework, check what the document itself cites as its inputs, and sort by content/argument rather than form/tone.
