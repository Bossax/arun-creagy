# Trust-repair case: Cuba's recipe for rebuilding public trust in official hazard

Trust-repair case: Cuba's recipe for rebuilding public trust in official hazard warnings.

The Cuban Institute of Meteorology regained public trust in its warnings (after earlier credibility erosion) through three combined changes, not one silver bullet:

1. Improved forecast accuracy (the underlying technical work actually got better).
2. Replaced generalist TV broadcasters with trained meteorologists to deliver warnings directly on air — eliminating a layer of potential misinterpretation and projecting visible technical authority.
3. More transparent, two-way communication of emergency plans, organizational structure, and institutional responsibilities across government, disaster-management bodies, and at-risk communities (not just one-way alert broadcasting).

The mechanism: credibility comes from the combination of (a) a source with visible, direct expertise, (b) a demonstrated track record of accuracy, and (c) openness about how the system works, rather than from any single intervention alone. This is a reusable diagnostic: if trust in an authority's warnings is eroding, check whether the warning is being delivered by a visible expert, whether the accuracy record actually is improving, and whether the institution is being transparent about its own process — a fix that only addresses one of the three tends to be insufficient.

Source: cited in Perera et al., "Identifying societal challenges in flood early warning systems," IJDRR 51 (2020) — read via NotebookLM notebook "Disaster Risk Communication".
