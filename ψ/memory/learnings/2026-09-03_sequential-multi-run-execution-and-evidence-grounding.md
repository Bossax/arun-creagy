# Learning — Sequential Multi-Run Execution & Evidence Grounding in Technical Reporting

## Pattern
1) **Deconstruct Monolithic Delivery into Discrete Sequential Runs**:
   Complex institutional deliverables (such as system architecture blueprints with both sitemap and data governance layers) must never be written in a single massive turn. Monolithic generation produces prose density, bypasses human checkpoints, and increases the likelihood of lexicon and syntax errors slipping through.
   - **Run 1**: Vision & High-Level System Framing (Data Platform vs Web Platform, user personas, mission-based navigation).
   - **Run 2**: Information Architecture down to Page and Subpage Minimum Specifications.
   - **Run 3**: Data Management Framework (Glossary, CDM Domains, Governance Roles) and Chapter Forward Pointers.

2) **Dissemination Deck Cross-Checking as Empirical Grounding Gate**:
   Initial conceptual drafts frequently lack operational depth. Cross-checking against stakeholder dissemination materials restores verified empirical figures:
   - Specific dataset inventories (260 datasets, 99 products).
   - Climatological baselines (1991–2020) and extreme weather indices (Rx1day, CDD, CWD).
   - Historical economic loss totals (1.4 trillion baht peak in 2011 across 5 NESDC sectors).
   - Verifiable indicator frameworks (25 core indicators across 77 provinces, 59 Belem GGA indicators).

3) **Rigid Content Boundary Enforcement**:
   To prevent conceptual redundancy across report chapters:
   - §2.3 strictly owns design history, process iterations, and focus group refinements.
   - §2.5 strictly owns workshop logistics, attendee counts, and schedules.
   - §2.6 strictly owns finished specifications, minimum content, and governance roles.
   - Chapter 5 strictly owns implementation roadmaps, phased SDLC, and procurement strategies.

## Why it matters
- Prevents cognitive overload for both the model and the human reviewer.
- Guarantees 0-violation mechanical compliance against strict institutional lexicons.
- Directly yields procurement-ready technical specifications (TOR) without requiring subsequent downstream reinterpretation.
