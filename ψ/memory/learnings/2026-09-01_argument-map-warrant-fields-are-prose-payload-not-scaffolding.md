# Argument-map warrant/application_to_design fields are prose payload, not internal-only scaffolding

During CRDB full-report §3.3 (service synthesis chapter), Stage 1 produced an argument unit for Service 4 (Historical Loss & Damage) whose justification leaned on "this service's methodology is closing, and it will be developed in more depth in the next chapter." Boss's correction: that forward-reference is legitimate *internal* reasoning for scoping decisions (how much depth to put in §3.3 vs. save for chapter 4), but it must never appear in the argument map at all — not just excluded from the final Thai prose.

The reason: `/writing-th` Stage 3 treats each unit's `claim`/`grounds`/`warrant`/`application_to_design` as the paragraph's payload — the verbalizer drafts directly from those fields. A forward-reference written into `warrant` or `application_to_design` as "internal logic" is not actually contained; it is exactly the kind of connective reasoning a verbalizer will turn into a sentence like "this is discussed further in the following chapter," because that's what those fields are for.

**Fix applied:** revised the unit to ground Service 4's inclusion in two things that are true and citable on their own — stakeholder demand (from source material) and a TOR-mandated requirement to study this domain — dropping any reference to how later chapters treat the same domain. `application_to_design` was written as an explicit instruction to the verbalizer to keep the unit self-contained and add no forward references.

**Generalizable rule:** if a piece of reasoning is genuinely internal-only (why we scoped something this way, not what the reader should be told), it does not belong in an argument-map unit at all — argument units are the boundary between "things we know" and "things stated in the deliverable," and Stage 3 does not distinguish between a unit's internal rationale and its verbalizable content. Keep internal-only scoping decisions in a separate note (writing-contract commentary, a handoff, a chat message) — never in the argument map.
