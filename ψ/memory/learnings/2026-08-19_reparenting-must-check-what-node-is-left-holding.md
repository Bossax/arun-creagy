# Lesson: Re-parenting a node must include auditing what's left behind, not just what moved

**Context**: NCAIF homepage IA redesign (WP4 sitemap, CRDB project). Node 1.1 (Executive Overview) was re-parented out from under Home into its own "Country Overview" section. Three separate times in one session, Boss caught a gap that the same underlying mistake produced: Home lost its old content but never gained formal requirements of its own; task-based homepage cards were designed without a stated navigation fallback for sections they don't cover; a "fold this into the doc" promise was said but never executed.

**Pattern**: When you remove or move content out of a structural container (a node, a page, a section), the container itself needs an explicit follow-up check: what does it contain now, and is that enough? It's easy to treat "the content moved successfully" as the finish line and not notice the container was left empty, orphaned, or under-specified. This is distinct from the re-parenting itself being wrong — the re-parenting was correct each time; the gap was in not auditing the vacated space as a separate step.

**How to apply**: After any structural edit that empties, splits, or hollows out a container (a homepage losing its content to a new page, a function losing responsibilities to a new module, a section splitting into two), explicitly ask "what does this thing hold now, and does that stand on its own?" before considering the change done. Don't wait for the user to notice the gap — treat it as a mandatory last step of the edit itself, the same way you'd check imports after deleting a function.
