# Learning: Formal Technical Terminology as a Strategy for Stakeholder Empowerment

**Date**: 2026-05-22
**Context**: CRDB Project (DCCE) - Negotiation between Project Lead (IT) and Data Owners (Academic/Policy)
**Author**: Arun (Strategic Knowledge Auditor)

## The Pattern
In complex multi-agency or multi-divisional projects where there is an "Authority vs. Responsibility" gap, the use of formal, industry-standard technical terminology (e.g., DAMA-DMBOK) serves as more than just a descriptive tool. It acts as a **Strategic Shield** and an **Empowerment Mechanism**.

### 1. Linguistic Authority (The Professional Shield)
Replacing informal or conceptual metaphors (like "Expert Drift" or "Ghost Town") with formal terms (like "Operational Knowledge Attrition" or "Lifecycle Obsolescence") provides the project with "borrowed authority." This register signals to senior leadership that the proposed changes are not "opinions" but are grounded in established global standards.

### 2. Framing Governance as Empowerment
When proposing Data Governance to busy sub-division leads (Data Owners), framing the requirements as "Empowerment" (e.g., **Data Sovereignty**, **Decision Rights**) is far more effective than framing it as "Workload" (e.g., **Data Stewardship**, **Cleanup**). By asserting that the sub-division is the "Ultimate Arbiter of Truth," you align the project's technical goals with the sub-division's administrative self-interest.

### 3. Protecting Against the "Portal Trap"
Formalizing the role of "Product Owner" within the department ensures that the "intent" of the project is not lost during the technical implementation phase. It forces the contractor to adhere to a **Frozen Technical Baseline** rather than defining their own requirements.

## Practical Application
When drafting strategy memos for government leadership:
- **Avoid**: Catchy metaphors, informal nicknames, or "clever" persona-based descriptions.
- **Prioritize**: DAMA-DMBOK terminology, references to ROI (Return on Investment), and clear definitions of **Accountability** (RACI).
- **Strategy**: Always translate "imperatives" into the local language (Thai) to ensure there is no semantic loss during internal advocacy sessions.
