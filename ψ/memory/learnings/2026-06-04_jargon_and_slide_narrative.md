# Learning: Transitional Thinking in Stakeholder Engagement

**Date**: 2026-06-04
**Topic**: Stakeholder Engagement / Slide Deck Narrative
**Context**: DCCE Director-level coordination (Toey/Nid)

## 1. The Pattern
LLMs often attempt to "squeeze" complex technical strategies (Governance, Data Models, Use Cases) into a minimal number of slides. This results in a lack of **Transitional Thinking**—the bridge that moves an audience from their current mental model (e.g., "We just want a website") to the necessary technical safeguards (e.g., "We need content ownership nodes").

## 2. The Correction (The Narrative Bridge)
A professional engagement deck for senior government leadership should follow a **Context -> Challenge -> Solution -> Action** flow, with explicit transition slides between each:

| Phase | Purpose | Transition Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vision** | Align with their goals (Portal/Service). | "To make this vision last..." |
| **Challenge** | Surface the risks of static decay (T-PLAT). | "To protect the department from this..." |
| **Solution** | Present the 3 Milestones (Accountability). | "To make this easy for staff..." |
| **Operations** | Address capacity (Verification Rubrics). | "To move forward by June 12th..." |
| **Action** | Call for the Ownership Matrix sign-off. | |

## 3. Institutional Nuance
- **Domain Gap (DEQP Legacy)**: Recognizing that staff are awareness specialists rather than climate scientists allows for the framing of governance as a **Verification Rubric** (Workload reduction) rather than a **Technical Mandate** (Extra work).
- **Conceptual vs. Common**: Ensure "Conceptual" is used to represent the *logic* of the system, avoiding the "Common" label which implies a degree of technical integration not yet achieved.
