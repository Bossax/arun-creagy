# Grounded Institutional Narrative vs. Consultant Jargon

## 🔍 The Pattern
AI agents, when tasked with "enhancing" or "polishing" reports, often default to "Consultant Jargon"—creating catchy metaphors or slogans (e.g., "Data Hunt", "Budgeting Wall") to represent technical problems. While these can seem professional in an English/Corporate context, they often sound artificial or "robot-generated" when translated or applied to formal Thai institutional contexts.

## ⚠️ The Problem
Artificial terminology obscures the real institutional pain points. In the NCAIF project, using "Data Hunt" instead of "ข้อมูลที่กระจัดกระจายและขาดการรับรอง" (fragmented and uncertified data) reduced the document's credibility and failed to resonate with the intended "human" sounding voice of the user.

## ✅ The Correction (Grounded Institutional Narrative)
1.  **Anchor in Real Pain**: Use the exact descriptions of struggles provided by stakeholders (e.g., "ข้อติดขัดในการขออนุมัติงบประมาณ" instead of "Budgeting Wall").
2.  **Why before What**: Always lead with the *Institutional Necessity* (Why) before describing the *Service Function* (What). This frames the technology as a solution to a verified struggle.
3.  **Human-First Voice**: Audit the prose against user-provided "enhanced" samples. If the user sample is professional but direct, avoid adding metaphorical layers.
4.  **Terminology Discipline**: Maintain a "Grounded Terminology" list in the Writing Plan to explicitly ban artificial buzzwords.
