# Differentiating Regional Infographics Copy under Shared Biological Stressors

## 🔍 The Pattern
When generating simplified public-facing communications (such as infographics) from multiple crop growth modeling papers, the underlying biophysical stressors (e.g., heat accumulation, vegetative phase contraction, pollen sterility) often share identical scientific mechanisms. This similarity creates a high risk of "textual convergence" and data cross-contamination between distinct regional studies in the communication design files.

## ⚠️ The Problem
Simply restating the biological impact on the plant (e.g., "reduced biomass due to heat") leads to redundant infographic points across papers. Additionally, close proximity of datasets during parallel updates increases the risk of carrying over metrics (like Chao Phraya's 53.34% water demand increase) into comparative studies (like Phichit's system analysis), undermining research fidelity and local context.

## ✅ The Correction (Differentiated System Framing)
1. **System-Level vs. Cultivar-Level Scoping**: Frame crop models that compare systems (like EPIC's Irrigated RRR vs. Rain-fed RR) around systemic tradeoffs (resilience gap, grey vs. blue water footprints), rather than generic plant stunting. Reserve detailed physiological impacts (like spikelet sterility) for cultivar-specific studies (like DSSAT's RD57 analysis).
2. **Context-Specific Vocabulary**: Infuse regional indicators directly into the copy (e.g., "ระบบหมุนเวียนพิจิตร RRR/RR" vs. "ข้าวลุ่มเจ้าพระยาพันธุ์ กข57").
3. **Dual-Pass Fact Verification**: Always run a separate pre-flight pass comparing final generated infographic metrics side-by-side with original draft parameters to isolate cross-contamination.
