# Lessons Learned: Scientific Hegemony and Database Vacancies as a Policy Roadmap

In the context of designing a national Common Data Model (CDM) for climate risk and adaptation reporting (such as the UNFCCC A-BTR), inter-agency pushback and data silos are primary hurdles. Two strategic principles were established to bypass these hurdles:

1.  **Leveraging International Scientific Hegemony:**
    By mapping our conceptual CDM entities directly to recognized international standards (e.g., **GCOS** for Essential Climate Variables, **GOOS** for Essential Ocean Variables, and **GEO BON** for Essential Biodiversity Variables), the model gains instant scientific authority. This prevents local bureaucratic or political pushback regarding the "necessity" of specific variables, as they are backed by global consensus (WMO, UNESCO, UNEP).

2.  **Database Vacancy as a National Development Roadmap:**
    Instead of viewing missing datasets or empty columns as database failures, they are codified as **strategic gaps**. A defined but unpopulated table (e.g., soil moisture indices or wave heights) becomes an explicit development indicator. This provides a clear, scientifically validated roadmap for the government and line agencies to justify funding requests to the Bureau of the Budget for telemetry infrastructure.

3.  **Framing as a Semantic Translation Layer (Crosswalk):**
    To prevent turf wars, the CDM must not be positioned as a replacement for existing agency databases. Instead, it must be framed as a metadata "crosswalk" that leaves their internal systems untouched while enabling unified national reporting.
