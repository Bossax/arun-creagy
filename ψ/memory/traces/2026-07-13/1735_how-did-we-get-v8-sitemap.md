## Evolution Summary: How We Arrived at Sitemap v8.0

The transition from Sitemap v6.0/v7.0 to Sitemap v8.0 was triggered by the **A-BTR requirement gap analysis**. Directly mapping all UNFCCC reporting mandates created a massive, bureaucratic, and academic front-end structure that cluttered the user experience for local practitioners (อปท., planners, and engineers).

To resolve this, the sitemap was realigned under a **De-escalation & De-bloating Strategy** to keep the user interface clean while preserving 100% compliance mapping on the back end:

1.  **Nesting Academic/Policy Details**:
    *   **Slow-Onset Hazards Profile** was nested under the main **Risk Analysis** page as sub-node `3.2.2.1`.
    *   **Adaptation Finance Directory & Support Tracking** and **Institutional Arrangements & Coordination** were nested under the **Policy Maker Information Center (2.3)** as sub-nodes `2.3.1` and `2.3.2`.
2.  **Merging Content Gaps**:
    *   **Gender Safeguards & Social Inclusivity (GESI)** metrics were merged directly into **Planning Guidelines** (`3.3.1`).
    *   **Systemic Barriers and Challenges** were merged into the **National Roadmap** (`3.3.2`).
3.  **Language and Tone**:
    *   Purged complex AI/technical jargon. Rewrote labels to use terms familiar to national and local stakeholders (อปท., สตง., planners) while keeping BTR compliance annotations `[Section, Level]` in the background code database.

This de-bloating successfully capped the final structure at **41 total sitemap nodes**, with BTR requirements mapped to 19 nodes (100% coverage across 379 BTR rules).
