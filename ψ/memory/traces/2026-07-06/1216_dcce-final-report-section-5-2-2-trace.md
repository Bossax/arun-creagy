### Detailed Summary of Section 5.2.2 Findings

The draft final report section **5.2.2** details the current landscape and history of DCCE's climate adaptation web presence:

#### **1. T-PLAT Platform Evolution (History)**
*   **Initial Concept (2018)**: Modeled after Japan's A-PLAT (domestic) and AP-PLAT (Asia-Pacific) with assistance from the Ministry of the Environment of Japan (MOEJ) and using National Adaptation Plan (NAP) data. The pilot did not achieve full operational status.
*   **Coastal pilot (T-PLAT.INFO)**: Launched in August 2020 and active through November 2024 in 4 pilot provinces (Surat Thani, Songkhla, Phetchaburi, and Rayong), supported by the UNDP. It provided a web-based GIS map displaying climate risk layers.

#### **2. Detailed As-Is Website Mapping & Services**
*   **Climate Projections**: Under the CCE system, Risk MAP provides climate projections (max/min/average temp, rainfall) across various scenarios (SSP2-4.5, SSP5-8.5) and periods (1960–2100).
*   **Impact Assessment Gaps**: DCCE currently lacks deep impact assessment services or localized tools for spatial analysis on its portal.
*   **Vulnerability/Risk Assessment**: Risk MAP provides province-level filtering on Exposure, Sensitivity, and Adaptive Capacity across the 6 NAP sectors.
*   **Case Studies & Best Practices**: Curated inside monthly e-books/journals, showcasing community adaptations (e.g., coastal planting in Songkhla).
*   **Glossary**: Currently missing a dedicated terms and definitions page.

#### **3. Comparative Matrix (Table 1)**
Compares the DCCE website against **A-PLAT** (Japan), **KlimAdapt** (Germany), and **Climate-ADAPT** (EU) across user segments (national, local, private, citizen), data options, monitoring strategies, financial funding platforms, and publicity media.

#### **4. Current Data Governance & Structural Gaps**
*   **Project-Centric Fragmentation**: Most database systems (Green Hotel, Risk MAP, e-Learning) are funded and designed as separate, independent projects. This leads to inconsistent data structures, metadata schemas, and portal subdomains.
*   **Data Governance & Ownership**: Policy/academic units act as "Data Owners" (defining scope/use), while the IT division acts as database/infrastructure administrators. However, a formal Data Governance framework (roles, stewardship, quality controls) is absent.
*   **Administration-First Design**: The legacy sitemap mimics administrative and organizational hierarchies rather than user-centric, workflow-driven pathways (e.g., local risk lookup or adaptation options).
