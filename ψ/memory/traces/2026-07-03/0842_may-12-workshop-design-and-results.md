## 2. Design Philosophy & Grouping Logic

### A. The Pivot from "Co-Creation" to "Guided Validation"
Previous workshop iterations assumed participants could think like system engineers and build portals from scratch. This led to "Expert Drift" (designing for hypothetical users) and "Consultation Fatigue". 
The May 12 design shifted to a **Reality Audit (The Climate Information Handshake)**:
1. Show what data and tools already exist (The "Mirror" poster).
2. Validate and correct that landscape.
3. Present 4 concrete Minimum Viable Product (MVP) concepts with side-by-side Service Levels.
4. Encourage participants to extend, critique, and audit these concepts.

### B. Grouping Logic: "Mission-Led" rather than "NAP Sectors"
Traditional division by National Adaptation Plan (NAP) sectors (Agriculture, Health, Transport) creates administrative chimneys and redundant data silos. The workshop grouped the 80+ participants into 8–10 tables across **4 Mission Themes**:
- **Theme A: National Planning**: Focus on long-term projections and authoritative data to protect the 2030 budget and infrastructure.
- **Theme B: Disaster Safety**: Focus on real-time data and tactical response in the first 48 hours.
- **Theme C: City Life**: Focus on local-level high-resolution maps and health alerts to keep neighborhoods livable.
- **Theme D: Business Security**: Focus on asset-level risk and financial disclosure to price climate risk into loans and insurance.

## 3. Morning Activities Design

### Morning Activity 1: Existing Data & Product Landscape Validation
- **Objective**: Validate the existing Data Landscape Map containing 100+ datasets, and identify missing, misclassified, or inaccessible files ("Ghost Data").
- **What Participants Did**:
  - **Operational Reveals**: Each participant spent 2 minutes stating one data asset they provide and one daily operational hurdle.
  - **Poster Walk**: Walked to large "Mirror Posters" on the wall, using correction stickers to annotate errors or mark datasets that are officially listed but practically inaccessible.
  - **Problem Anchoring**: Table teams synthesized their shared operational bottlenecks into a single, concrete problem statement.

### Morning Activity 2: Guided Service & Product Expansion
- **Objective**: Co-produce service standards by forcing participants to define what level of detail they actually need to act with confidence.
- **Format**: Evaluated 4 prepared MVP concept cards using a "Three-Box Framework" (Pain Point, Level 1 vs Level 2 Concept, User Journey) and a **Decision Utility Matrix**:
  - **MVP-1: Policy Briefing Service**: Level 1 (Provincial Qualitative Trends) vs. Level 2 (Municipality-level Climate Allowance safety margins).
  - **MVP-2: Disaster Data Pipeline**: Level 1 (Spreadsheet Reliability Flags) vs. Level 2 (Common Sectoral Impact Schema for automated loss & damage).
  - **MVP-3: Endorsed Data Inventory**: Level 1 (Curated Link Directory) vs. Level 2 (Automated SSOT with official agency endorsement).
  - **MVP-4: Uncertainty Data Shield**: Level 1 (Decision Readiness Labels) vs. Level 2 (Statistical Model Confidence Intervals).
- **Proposals & Peer Review**: Tables proposed up to 2 additional desired services/products (specifying intended users, problem, service level, and data needs) and conducted a gallery review round using stickers: *Useful for us too*, *Similar exists*, or *Hard to implement*.

## 4. Synthesis of Results & Discussions

Post-workshop synthesis normalized 26 service concepts generated by the tables, leading to these critical insights:

### A. Demand Clusters
- **Cluster A: Authoritative Baselines & SSOT**: Highest strategic momentum (led by banks/ONWR). Demanded a "Single Source of Truth" with 20–50yr baselines.
- **Cluster B: Localized Vulnerability & Risk**: High operational momentum (led by DCCE/MSDHS). Demanded Sub-district (Tambon) resolution maps overlapping hazards with socio-economic vulnerability data.
- **Cluster C: Sectoral Impact & Standards**: Policy-driven demand (led by Transport/Tourism) to quantify sectoral loss & damage and resilient infrastructure engineering rules.
- **Cluster D: Urban Resilience & Land Use**: Spatial planning demand (led by DPT/BMA) for city design, sponge cities, and heat resilience.
- **Cluster E: Emerging Domain Frontiers**: Unique emergent requirements (e.g., sinkhole prediction, marine coral temp tracking, sea water quality monitoring).

### B. Key Technical Shift: "Utility over Dashboards"
- **API Dominance**: Universal demand for direct API feeds rather than visual dashboards, signaling that agencies want to ingest data into their own internal planning systems.
- **Sub-district (Tambon) Resolution**: Province-level summaries were rejected as operationally unusable for local budgeting.
- **The "Authority Gap"**: Stakeholders did not want *more* data; they wanted *authoritative* data. They viewed DCCE's primary value as a **SSOT Certifier** of national baselines, ensuring protection against legal and planning liabilities.

## 5. Strategic Implications for the CRDB Roadmap
The results shifted the project from speculative design to strict production:
1. **API Prioritization**: Shifted software development priorities from frontend "dashboard UI" to robust backend spatial APIs.
2. **Sitemap Granularity Restore**: Although earlier design drafts attempted to simplify the sitemap, the workshop outcomes demanded the restoration of full scientific granularity (resulting in Sitemap v6.1) to avoid a synthesis bottleneck.
3. **Data Governance & Asset Focus**: Reframed all content pages as **governed assets** tied to clear ownership, formatting guidelines, and reliability flags.
