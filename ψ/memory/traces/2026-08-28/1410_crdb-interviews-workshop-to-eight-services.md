# Trace: Interviews and workshop to eight services

## Verified sequence

1. Stakeholder interviews were the starting point. They documented real agency activities, decisions, data needs, formats, constraints, and unmet needs.
2. The 12 May 2026 workshop produced additional group-level use cases and refinement material. Its intended role was to build on earlier evidence, not replace it.
3. The project normalized interview and workshop material into more than 40 use cases. The transformation is analytic: cases with a similar user, decision, information need, analytical action, and desired output were clustered.
4. The clusters were synthesized into eight proposed climate-information services. A service is therefore a reusable response to a family of use cases, not a direct transcription of a workshop idea or a deployed system.
5. Priority is downstream of service synthesis. The dissemination deck identifies three high-priority services. A later draft business-narratives document separately selects one service (Historical Loss & Damage Assessment) and one enabling use case (the national reporting pipeline supporting Adaptation M&E) for detailed specification.

## Reporting clarification

The national reporting pipeline is not a ninth service. It is an enabling use case attached to Service 7, Adaptation Monitoring & Evaluation. Its evidence includes Thailand's adaptation reporting needs, including Global Goal on Adaptation reporting; it should not be described as the origin of all eight services.

## Writing implication

Section 2.2 should stress the compression logic: the project did not turn 40+ cases into eight services by counting them. It compared repeated decision problems, users, required inputs, analytical tasks, outputs, and constraints, then grouped cases that required the same enduring service function. The workshop tested, refined, and added inputs to this evidence base.
