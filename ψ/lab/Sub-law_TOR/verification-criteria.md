# Verification Criteria — Technical Proposal Quality

Grounded in real consulting-proposal evaluation frameworks (World Bank/QCBS, ILO scored evaluation annex, GSA federal-proposal criteria). Scoped to the technical/methodology section of a proposal — team/institutional capacity and pricing are separate proposal sections and not covered here.

## 1. Responsiveness to the TOR (traceability)
- Every scope item in the TOR is explicitly answered somewhere in the proposal — not just implied.
- A traceability device exists (a mapping table, cross-reference, or numbering scheme) linking proposal sections back to TOR clauses/legal provisions.
- No TOR requirement is silently dropped or merged without explanation.

## 2. Understanding of context/subject matter
- The proposal elaborates on the substance of the TOR's content (the actual legal provisions, sector, institutions) rather than restating it generically.
- Definitions and concepts specific to the domain are used correctly and specifically, not generically.
- Institutional relationships and administrative reality of the requesting agency are demonstrated, not assumed.

## 3. Methodology soundness
- The proposed method is stated as concrete, named instruments (e.g., a specific mapping/analysis technique) rather than vague verbs ("we will analyze," "we will study").
- Method matches the deliverable type — e.g., a legal-obligation mapping method produces a legal-obligation mapping deliverable, not a narrative report.
- Sequencing and dependencies between steps/phases are shown, not just a flat list.

## 4. Scope-of-work coverage
- Stakeholder engagement is defined: who, how, at what stage.
- Geographic/institutional/administrative-level scope is addressed explicitly (central, regional, local, private sector, etc., as applicable).
- Boundary decisions (what's in/out of scope) are justified with reasoning and reference, not asserted.

## 5. Work plan / feasibility
- Timeline, staffing effort, and narrative align — the plan doesn't promise more than the proposed effort/schedule can support.
- Phasing reflects real constraints (e.g., before/after a legal effective date, dependency chains between deliverables).
- Risk points and how they'll be handled are named, not left implicit.

## 6. Deliverables clarity
- Each output is specific and verifiable (a named document, tool, or system), not a vague aspiration.
- Outputs are traceable back to which TOR scope item and which methodology step produced them.

## 7. Internal coherence
- The narrative's claims are consistent with what the tables/annexes actually show — no proposal section contradicts another.
- The framing/thesis stated in the introduction is actually carried through, not just decorative.

## 8. Value-add beyond compliance
- The proposal shows added judgment — connecting to adjacent legal mechanisms, identifying integration opportunities, anticipating friction points — not just restating the TOR back as a plan.
