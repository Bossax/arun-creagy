# Is Research a formal stage, or only an input

## Question
Is "Research" ever formalized in the literature as a distinct institutional stage in an adaptation MEL cycle — with its own trigger, inputs, outputs, and owner — or only as an activity/input within the other stages?

## Evidence
Simple-search grounding this turn (`~/.pplx-cli/history/2026-08-22_03-05-22...md`, sources: UNFCCC Adaptation Committee technical paper, Climate-ADAPT, NAP Global Network MEL toolkit): "research" is generally **not treated as a formal stage** in MEL/MERL literature. The formal elements are monitoring, evaluation, learning — sometimes with reporting added as a fourth ("MERL" with R = Reporting, see [[q2.3]]). Where "research" appears, it's an input, method, or supporting activity folded into data collection, evaluation design, or MEL-system development — never a named, separately-triggered stage of its own. This corrects an earlier framing in this session's own writing, which had used the OECD's *project-activity taxonomy* (research as one of five spending categories alongside risk reduction, policy management, education, coordination) to answer this question — a genuinely different classification (what adaptation *projects spend money on*) mistakenly substituted for the governance-stage question Boss actually asked.

## Judgement
Closed, and the correction matters: the honest finding is not "R does forward-looking scenario work that M/E/L don't cover" (the earlier, too-generous framing) but **no institutional literature source treats Research as a fourth stage of an adaptation M&E cycle at all**. DCCE is not missing a stage the field forgot to name — it's naming a stage the field doesn't have. Feeds directly into the TOR redirect in [[q4.2]]: the "Research" work package needs either reframing to what research actually is (an input to evaluation/learning design, not a parallel stage) or dropping the R framing outright.
