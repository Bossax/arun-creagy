# What forcing mechanism compels sector-ministry compliance

## Question
Absent command authority, what mechanism actually gets sector ministries to comply and act on MEL findings?

## Evidence
Perplexity `ask` results, three countries (`~/.pplx-cli/history/2026-08-21_15-52-49...md`, `...15-53-21...md`):
- **Germany**: the 2024 Federal Climate Adaptation Act (KAnG) creates a statutory duty to produce the monitoring report; data is largely pulled from statistics ministries already collect for other reasons, moving toward automatic updating rather than new manual reporting.
- **UK**: the Climate Change Act's Adaptation Reporting Power lets the Secretary of State legally direct named "reporting authorities" to report, backed by parliamentary accountability behind the CCC's biennial findings.
- **Philippines**: Climate Change Expenditure Tagging (CCET) makes tagging climate-relevant budget lines a *precondition of the annual budget process itself*, embedded in the General Appropriations Act — agencies comply because non-compliance risks fund release.

None of the three achieved compliance by inventing a parallel institutional rhythm and asking ministries to adopt it (directly resolves [[q1.3]]). Each attached reporting to something ministries could not skip regardless of climate policy — a budget precondition or a statutory duty.

## Judgement
Closed as a general finding; DCCE-specific application stays open. DCCE's platform currently has neither mechanism — agencies are asked to log in and enter numbers with nothing consequential following if they don't. That absence, not a missing concept, is the structural reason Dimension 4 (see [[q3.2]]) sits unused. Whether DCCE can build or access a comparable mechanism (budget-linked, per [[q3.4]], or statutory) is unresolved — see [[q3.1]] (open) and [[q4.6]] for what a single 240-day TOR can honestly commit to on this front.
