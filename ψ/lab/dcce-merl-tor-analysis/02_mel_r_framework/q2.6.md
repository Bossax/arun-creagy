# Distributed root-cause ownership, validated against precedent

## Question
Should root-cause diagnosis be centralized in the M&E/coordinating body, or distributed to the domain-owning functions?

## Evidence
Boss's model (fully stated in [[q1.2]]) checked against real-country precedent via Perplexity `ask` (`~/.pplx-cli/history/2026-08-21_15-53-52...md`, `...15-53-21...md`, `...15-52-49...md`):
- **Germany**: UBA runs DAS monitoring but explicitly does not evaluate effectiveness of specific measures, "because implementation falls under the responsibility of the competent authorities"; UBA works "in close cooperation with," not as an auditor of, responsible ministries.
- **UK**: the Climate Change Committee is a statutory external assessor, not an implementer — it recommends government "coordinate relevant delivery and statistics agencies" rather than investigating causes itself.
- **Philippines**: the Climate Change Commission leads NCCAP M&E but is explicitly not the root-cause investigator — that sits with implementing agencies and LGUs, with the Commission compiling and reporting gaps. The Commission on Audit's 2024 finding that NCCAP's M&E system "has yet to be institutionalized" is a caution, not a refutation, of the model.

## Judgement
Closed. In every case checked, the coordinating body flags deviation and compiles; it never itself diagnoses cause. Boss's model isn't a departure from real practice — it's what real practice already does. What's still open is whether DCCE's own structure has the convening authority this model requires — see [[q3.5]] — and what compels the domain-owning ministries to actually respond, which is a separate question answered in [[q2.7]].
