# Candidate sector ministries for a first pilot reporting cycle

## Question
Which of the 18 line agencies have the most existing data infrastructure or prior reporting habit, making them realistic candidates for the first Phase 3→4 pilot cycle the notebook recommends?

## Evidence
[[q3.1]] established that DCCE's authority over the 18 agencies is soft — MOUs and accumulated momentum, no legal or budget lever — which makes ministry choice for a pilot a matter of willingness and existing capacity, not command.

Round 1 returned three distinct viable entry-point models with country examples: agriculture/natural-resources ministries (Kenya, Vietnam, Namibia) — viable via donor funding plus existing local data streams; regulated critical infrastructure (UK water/energy/transport) — viable via pre-existing risk-reporting habit plus a statutory hook; a low-burden qualitative self-assessment model (Finland) — viable precisely because it needs no data infrastructure, just willingness. Round 2 added comparator sectoral/technical bodies that made pilots viable elsewhere: Kenya's KNBS, Indonesia's BPTP/BMKG, Vietnam's MONRE-managed databases — but confirmed the corpus does **not** name any specific Thai sectoral or technical body by title, so mapping these models onto actual Thai line agencies is outside what this notebook can supply.

Boss then explained why naming a single ministry is the wrong first move: Thailand's adaptation work is not organized by government function, it is organized by the six sectors named in the National Adaptation Plan, and those six sectors do not share one consistent basis for being grouped the way they are. The six are water resource management, food and agriculture, tourism and the economy, human settlement and security, public health, and natural resources. Some of these are defined by a type of resource (water, natural resources), some by an economic activity (agriculture, tourism), and some by a type of vulnerability (human settlement and security, public health) — three different organizing logics inside one list of six. This is the concrete, sector-level version of the fragmentation already established in [[q3.15]]: the sectors themselves were not built from one shared logic, so each one likely maps to a different mix of line agencies rather than one clean agency per sector.

## Judgement
**Open.** This sharpens the question rather than closing it: before naming a candidate agency, DCCE first needs to pick which of the six inconsistently-defined sectors to enter through, since the answer to "which agency" will look different depending on whether the sector in question is resource-defined, activity-defined, or vulnerability-defined. The three entry-point models from Round 1 still apply as criteria (existing data infrastructure, donor funding availability, or simple willingness) but now need to be applied sector by sector rather than to the 18 agencies as one undifferentiated list. Feeds directly into [[q4.9]]'s pilot design — choosing an unwilling or low-capacity ministry, or a sector whose internal logic is especially unclear, could sink the pilot before Phase 4 mainstreaming is even attempted, given DCCE has no lever to force participation.
