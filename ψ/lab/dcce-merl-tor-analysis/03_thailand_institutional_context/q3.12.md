# Existing evaluation capacity or precedent at DCCE or any sector ministry

## Question
Does DCCE, or any of the 18 line agencies, have any existing evaluation capacity or precedent — any body that has ever produced a "why did we miss this target" analysis, on any topic, adaptation or otherwise?

## Evidence
The notebook establishes that root-cause/evaluation work typically sits in sectoral technical units, not the central coordinator, in comparative cases — echoing [[q2.6]]'s already-validated distributed root-cause model. Round 1 sharpened this: centralized evaluation capacity is "almost always absent" at Phase 2/3, but capacity is "rarely entirely non-existent" — it typically exists fragmented, in donor-funded project M&E and sectoral technical units, with the realistic central-coordinator role being to aggregate/flag/convene rather than evaluate directly. Round 2 named concrete comparator examples: Kenya's KNBS (national statistics bureau) cross-checks agency submissions for anomalies and runs a dedicated sampling-audit team; Indonesia's BPTP and BMKG hold existing technical monitoring capacity in agriculture and water; Vietnam's MONRE manages existing hydrometeorological/disaster databases; South Africa's Western Cape runs its own provincial M&E system independent of the national one. The corpus does not name any specific Thai sectoral or technical body by title.

Boss then supplied the actual Thai answer directly: "สำนักงานคณะกรรมการพัฒนาระบบราชการ (OPDC) and NESDC are the main technical body that sets KPIs for line agencies and monitor and evaluate budget receiving body their performance. But we won't go that far yet. Most evaluation of project effectiveness in answering to the initial needs is done by each project owner. No third party verification. The only third party, and controversial, auditor is Office of the Auditor General of Thailand (สำนักงานการตรวจเงินแผ่นดิน). They are like accountant who audits budget use efficiency rather than project effectiveness, that is the job of OPDC."

## Judgement
Closed. Thailand has general-purpose performance-monitoring capacity (OPDC and NESDC set KPIs and monitor budget-receiving agencies) and budget-efficiency auditing capacity (the Office of the Auditor General) — but **no independent body evaluates whether a project or policy actually achieved its intended effect.** That evaluation, where it happens at all, is done by the project owner itself, with no third-party verification. This means [[q2.6]]'s distributed-evaluation model has no existing independent evaluator to route through anywhere in the Thai system — DCCE building any real adaptation-effectiveness evaluation function would be creating an institutional first, not piggybacking on precedent the way Kenya (KNBS) or Vietnam (MONRE) could. This is a harder starting position than the comparator cases in the Evidence above, and should be named as such in the TOR rather than assumed away. Directly informs [[q3.11]] (no existing "third party evaluates" model to adopt wholesale) and [[q4.9]] (evaluation, if scoped into the pilot at all, should be modest and DCCE-internal, not modeled on an external auditor).
