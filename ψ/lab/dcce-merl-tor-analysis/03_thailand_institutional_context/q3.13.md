# Existing learning mechanism in DCCE's practice

## Question
Does DCCE have any existing learning mechanism — a cross-ministry review forum, workshop series, or anything resembling France's post-evaluation environmental conferences — in current or planned practice?

## Evidence
Nothing established yet in conversation or documents reviewed about DCCE specifically. [[q2.2]] defines learning as a designed social process requiring facilitation and institutional fit, not incidental stakeholder interaction. If no such forum currently exists at DCCE, Learning as a MEL component would need to be built from zero rather than adapted from an existing habit.

Round 1 returned four precedent patterns for what a country's *first* learning mechanism typically looks like, before anything as formal as France's post-evaluation conferences exists: an ad hoc, self-funded coordination group (Finland — 32 members across ministries/research bodies/regions, no separate budget); embedded qualitative reflection questions bolted onto an existing reporting cycle (Tonga — literally added "what lessons and opportunities can be drawn from this reporting period?" to a routine NPD questionnaire, the same piggyback logic surfacing for [[q3.8]]); informal researcher-mediated niches operating outside formal government process entirely (Indonesia's Tim Kolaboratif); and learning as an unplanned byproduct of other consultations, with no dedicated learning plan at all ("instrumental learning").

Boss then flagged the same gap he raised on [[q3.11]]: "scale?" — none of these four patterns specify whether they were operating at project, sectoral, regional, or national scale, which matters for picking a realistic first mechanism for DCCE. Queued for Round 3.

Round 3 answered this directly: when resources are limited, a country's first learning activity almost never happens at the national scale. It happens locally, or within a single sector. In Indonesia, the first learning work ran in one province, testing ideas in specific districts before touching national planning. In Kenya, Uganda, and Ethiopia, early learning work ran within one sector — livestock — bringing government and non-government people together to work through problems in that sector before any attempt to scale up. The one case where learning happened at national scale from the start, Finland, was informal and unfunded, run by a volunteer coordination group assessing itself rather than a designed national program. This suggests DCCE's realistic first learning activity is more likely to work at the sector or project level than the national level, matching the pattern already surfacing for [[q3.8]] and [[q3.14]], where a sector-by-sector start also looks more workable than moving all 18 agencies together.

## Judgement
**Open.** The four precedent patterns above are ready for Boss's judgment on which fits DCCE, with the Tonga piggyback model particularly relevant given it reuses the same mechanism logic as [[q3.8]]'s leading option — but Boss's scale question is a real gap, not yet answered. If genuinely a blank slate at whatever scale DCCE settles on, this remains the hardest of the three MEL components to stand up within the fixed 240-day/฿3,000,000 constraint confirmed in [[q4.8]], and should be scoped modestly in the TOR rather than assumed feasible in one contract cycle.
