# Existing learning mechanism in DCCE's practice

## Question
Does DCCE have any existing learning mechanism — a cross-ministry review forum, workshop series, or anything resembling France's post-evaluation environmental conferences — in current or planned practice?

## Evidence
Nothing established yet in conversation or documents reviewed about DCCE specifically. [[q2.2]] defines learning as a designed social process requiring facilitation and institutional fit, not incidental stakeholder interaction. If no such forum currently exists at DCCE, Learning as a MEL component would need to be built from zero rather than adapted from an existing habit.

Round 1 returned four precedent patterns for what a country's *first* learning mechanism typically looks like, before anything as formal as France's post-evaluation conferences exists: an ad hoc, self-funded coordination group (Finland — 32 members across ministries/research bodies/regions, no separate budget); embedded qualitative reflection questions bolted onto an existing reporting cycle (Tonga — literally added "what lessons and opportunities can be drawn from this reporting period?" to a routine NPD questionnaire, the same piggyback logic surfacing for [[q3.8]]); informal researcher-mediated niches operating outside formal government process entirely (Indonesia's Tim Kolaboratif); and learning as an unplanned byproduct of other consultations, with no dedicated learning plan at all ("instrumental learning").

Boss then flagged the same gap he raised on [[q3.11]]: "scale?" — none of these four patterns specify whether they were operating at project, sectoral, regional, or national scale, which matters for picking a realistic first mechanism for DCCE. Queued for Round 3.

Round 3 answered this directly: when resources are limited, a country's first learning activity almost never happens at the national scale. It happens locally, or within a single sector. In Indonesia, the first learning work ran in one province, testing ideas in specific districts before touching national planning. In Kenya, Uganda, and Ethiopia, early learning work ran within one sector — livestock — bringing government and non-government people together to work through problems in that sector before any attempt to scale up. The one case where learning happened at national scale from the start, Finland, was informal and unfunded, run by a volunteer coordination group assessing itself rather than a designed national program. This suggests DCCE's realistic first learning activity is more likely to work at the sector or project level than the national level, matching the pattern already surfacing for [[q3.8]] and [[q3.14]], where a sector-by-sector start also looks more workable than moving all 18 agencies together.

Boss then worked through how this connects to [[q3.11]]'s decision to keep evaluation centralized at DCCE, since at first that seemed to conflict with learning starting at sector or project level. His resolution: what DCCE is actually measuring is results, not process, and the point of measuring results is to improve the process that produced them. Those results run through a long chain — national policy becomes activities, regulations, funding mechanisms, funding criteria, and eventually what happens on the ground, which in turn produces (or fails to produce) an improvement in overall risk and resilience. Boss described two ways to work with that chain: start at the project level and trace backward, or start from the aggregate resilience signal and the middle layer of the chain (for example, how money actually moves through the Budget Bureau) and ask which part of the pathway looks like it isn't converting into results.

Talked through together, these two turned out not to be competing options. DCCE's evaluation function is the one positioned to watch the aggregate signal and the middle of the pathway, and to flag roughly where something looks like it is breaking down — this is the job a central body can do without visiting every project, using data it already centralizes. Once a weak point is flagged, a learning activity at the sector or project closest to that point is what finds out why, the same distributed-diagnosis logic already validated in [[q2.6]] (root-cause work sits with the people closest to the problem, while the central coordinator aggregates, flags, and convenes rather than doing the on-the-ground tracing itself). What gets learned there then feeds back into the specific link in the chain that was flagged — a funding criterion, a regulation, how a program is run — ahead of the next cycle.

## Judgement
Closed. DCCE's evaluation function, working from the aggregate resilience signal and its already-centralized data, decides where along the policy-to-results chain something looks like it is not working. A learning activity is then run at whichever sector or project sits closest to that flagged point — matching the comparator pattern above (Indonesia's province-level work, the East Africa livestock-sector work), not a standing national-scale forum. What is learned there is carried back by DCCE into the specific part of the chain that was flagged, rather than assumed to generalize on its own. This is a repeating handoff, not a one-time structure: evaluation flags where to look, learning finds out why, DCCE carries the answer back into the pathway, and the next cycle's evaluation checks whether it worked. Given the fixed 240-day/฿3,000,000 constraint confirmed in [[q4.8]], the first cycle of this handoff should be scoped modestly — one flagged weak point, one sector or project-level learning activity, one specific change carried back — rather than attempting the full pathway at once.
