# Is DCCE's NAP risk-based, and is 'adaptation' homogeneously defined

## Question
Is DCCE's NAP action plan actually derived from risk assessment, or is it business-as-usual (BAU) sector projects retrospectively relabeled "adaptation" without a clear, homogeneous definition of what counts as adaptation? Relatedly, does the "sectoral resilience" concept named in DCCE's M&E guideline manual have a real assessment framework behind it, or is it a term with no operational content?

## Evidence
Boss, per conversation, while reviewing the indicator-type evidence gathered for [[q3.10]]: indicator-filling currently works backward — DCCE looks for data already available from line agencies and asks stakeholders what could be used to fill in GGA indicators, rather than starting from local risk context per theme/indicator. "Even NAP is not risk based. The NAP action plan is nothing more than asking the line agencies about their BAU projects that could be labeled adaptation without clear and homogenous [definition] of what [is] considered adaptation." Separately: DCCE's current indicators are "mostly outputs... not framework to really assess 'sectoral resilience', even though this term is coined in the DCCE M&E guideline manual."

This connects directly to Boss's framing at the start of this research thread: the whole undertaking is driven by UNFCCC compliance, and "having the structure look complete... could be hollow inside."

Boss then closed this directly, with precision on what "not risk-based" means: "NAP is not risk based in the sense that it references climate risk assessment of Thailand. However, the strategy and the premise of adaptation sectors are not data driven, more like coming from policy dialogue rather than data. The rest is true and it highlights the fragmentation of adaptation in Thailand."

## Judgement
Closed. The NAP does not build on a Thailand-specific climate risk assessment. Which sectors count as "adaptation sectors," and the strategy built on top of that choice, come from negotiated policy dialogue among stakeholders and agencies, not from data or risk analysis. Combined with the earlier finding that "adaptation" has no homogeneous operational definition, this confirms the object being measured is not stable: BAU projects get labeled "adaptation" by dialogue and negotiation rather than by a shared, risk-based test, so any MEL system built on top of this measures compliance-labeling, not effectiveness against actual climate risk. Boss names this directly as fragmentation — sectors setting their own adaptation scope independently, with no unifying risk logic connecting them. This should be named honestly in the TOR redirect (per [[q4.7]]'s "give the full picture" framing) rather than assumed away by [[q4.9]]'s pilot design — a pilot that formalizes reporting on BAU-relabeled projects without naming this gap would entrench the fragmentation, not expose it. Per [[q3.10]]'s closing distinction, this reading applies most directly to the M&E platform's own indicator groundwork, since the GGA-mapping side was already understood to be a retrofit exercise rather than a measure of anything risk-derived.
