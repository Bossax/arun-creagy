# Type of DCCE's current adaptation indicators

## Question
Are DCCE's GGA-aligned indicators process-based, output-based, or outcome-based (per the literature's three-way typology), or an undifferentiated mixed list?

## Evidence
Boss describes the indicators as being set now, aligned to the Global Goal on Adaptation, but without specifying their type. The literature (GIZ indicator repository, in this notebook) distinguishes process indicators (capacity-building), output indicators (implementation — "they show governments kept word, but not whether vulnerability was reduced"), and outcome indicators (actual vulnerability/resilience change). [[q2.5]] already established that evaluation needs some basis for judgment, whether or not it's a formal target value.

Round 1 confirmed the general pattern: indicator sets at Phase 2/3 are heavily process/output-weighted everywhere, with outcome indicators typically absent because of baseline-data gaps (Tonga's 92-activity tracking is process-only; even the UK, far more advanced, still has 60% of its indicator wishlist unpopulated for lack of data). Round 2 added the GGA-specific layer: GGA/UAE FGCR indicators formally split into process, output, outcome, and impact, with named cross-country tensions — standardization-vs-context, an aggregation bottleneck between subnational and national data, severe baseline gaps, an "intent vs. reality" trap (counting plans rather than verified implementation), and multi-decadal attribution lag.

Boss then confirmed directly, twice: "mostly outputs. not framework to really assess 'sectoral' resilience, even though this term is coined in the DCCE M&E guideline manual." And: "not fully aligned and not fully 1-1 match with GGA. Many GGA indicators can not find a match with existing reported data." A third comment reframes the whole process as backward: "They look for data from line agencies and ask the stakeholders what could be used to fill in GGA indicators. However, the local context per theme or GGA indicator is not really envisioned yet." That third comment — about whether the underlying NAP/indicator process is risk-derived at all — is broader than this note's original scope and is now tracked separately in [[q3.15]].

## Judgement
**Open — evidence above now covers this well; leaving the close call and its wording to Boss.** The type question itself has a clear answer in the evidence (mostly output, partial/one-directional GGA alignment, "sectoral resilience" named but not operationally framed), which confirms the concern flagged at the note's opening: evaluation will have very little outcome data to work with once it starts. Relevant to scoping what [[q3.12]]'s evaluation capacity would even have to evaluate, and to whether the TOR should flag indicator rebalancing — or, per [[q3.15]], a more fundamental definitional fix — as part of the doable slice.
