# The imaginary adaptation cycle problem

## Question
Does "the adaptation cycle" exist as a lived institutional rhythm at DCCE, or as an abstraction only DCCE itself sees?

## Evidence
Boss's own observation: DCCE has "this kind of imaginary cycle looping around, but nobody sees it" — other agencies have their own calendars, mostly aligned to the government fiscal year, not to any DCCE-defined adaptation cycle. DCCE's authority is coordinating, not commanding (see [[q3.1]], open). Cross-country precedent (`~/.pplx-cli/history/2026-08-21_15-52-49...md`, `...15-53-21...md`) shows the same risk elsewhere, and shows what actually resolves it: no country got sector-ministry buy-in by asking agencies to adopt a separate adaptation calendar. Germany, the UK, and the Philippines all attached adaptation reporting to something ministries already couldn't skip — a budget precondition or a statutory duty — not a shared cycle.

## Judgement
Closed as a diagnosis, open as a fix (see [[q2.7]]). The adaptation cycle is real as an analytical model (per [[q1.1]]) but not as a lived governance rhythm unless it's attached to something with its own forcing power. DCCE's actual authority to build such an attachment is unknown — see [[q3.1]] — which is why this note stays flagged even though the diagnosis itself is settled.
