# Root-cause routing across adaptation-cycle stages

## Question
Who owns root-cause investigation across the four adaptation-cycle stages ([[q1.1]]), and how do findings route between them?

## Evidence
Boss's own model, proposed and refined in conversation: M&E asks questions and produces evidence of what falls short or works, but does not itself explain root cause. The burden of explaining a deviation is distributed to whichever of the other three functions — risk/vulnerability assessment, planning, implementation — has the domain standing to investigate within its own scope. Each produces suggestions/recommendations, not proof. These get pooled centrally, and a decision-maker (e.g. head of adaptation division) resolves competing accounts into one institutional conclusion — a political process, not a purely analytical one — potentially revising targets, assumptions, or even what "resilience" means. This model was independently validated against real-country practice in [[q2.6]]: Germany, the UK, and the Philippines all show the same pattern — the central coordinating body never investigates root cause itself.

## Judgement
Closed. This is the single most load-bearing model in the whole analysis — it resolves why a standalone "Research" work package never made conceptual sense ([[q2.4]]) and gives the TOR's redirect ([[q4.1]]) its actual mechanism. The open question is not whether this model is right (precedent confirms it), but whether DCCE's actual org chart has a decision-maker with real convening weight to do the resolving step — see [[q3.5]] (open).
