# Timing relationship between monitoring, evaluation, and impact

## Question
How do monitoring, evaluation, and impact-level change relate in timing — bounded to one implementation window, or aggregate across many?

## Evidence
Boss's own formulation: monitoring starts right after implementation starts and ends when implementation ends; evaluation can extend beyond that window because it also covers impact-level change, which is an aggregate across many projects or implementation cycles, not a property of any single one. This matches the OECD's timing dimension (`ψ/inbox/notebooklm_runs/2026-08-21_1539.../q1_monitoring_vs_evaluation.json`, citations 3, 11, 13): "for many adaptation initiatives no discernible change may take place over the lifetime of the project" — credible evaluation of climate outcomes often has to happen years after a project closes, not the standard 2–5 year development-evaluation window.

## Judgement
Closed. This is why a single-visit pilot inside a 240-day contract can never produce a genuine impact-level evaluation finding (reinforces [[q2.5]]) — the honest claim available at that timescale is about mechanism function, not resilience change. This directly bounds what the redesigned site visit ([[q1.4]], [[q4.3]]) can honestly claim to test.
