# What a site visit can and cannot test

## Question
What can a physical site visit actually test in this cycle, and what can't it?

## Evidence
Worked through across several turns, in sequence rather than settled in one pass. First instinct: skeptical — a single visit, one session, six areas cannot establish a before/after or validate a methodology. Sharper objection raised next: if the real bottleneck is that 18 line agencies aren't feeding data into the platform (see [[q3.3]]), that problem lives inside those agencies' own offices and workflows, not in a physical risk location — a trip to a flood-prone district doesn't test whether an agency contact person logs into a dashboard. That objection was then itself corrected: TOR clauses 5.2.3–5.2.4 (`ψ/inbox/TOR-DCCE-MERL-2026.md`) explicitly ask for gaps/constraints/needs *of the study area itself* and for findings linking policy, sector, area-level agencies, and the study area together — a genuinely place-bound question a database or HQ interview cannot answer.

## Judgement
Closed, and it's a real distinction, not a reversal: a site visit is the wrong instrument for testing the *national reporting mechanism* (that's an agency-workflow problem, see [[q3.3]]), but the right instrument for an *area-based diagnostic* — tracing where a sector's indicator data would actually originate at that location, and how national/sector plans land (or fail to land) locally. The TOR redirect in [[q4.3]] depends on keeping this distinction rather than collapsing it back to a single "yes/no" on site visits.
