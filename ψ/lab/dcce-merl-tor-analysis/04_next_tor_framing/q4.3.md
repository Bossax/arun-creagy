# Reframed site-visit scope for the next TOR

## Question
Does the site visit (TOR §5.2.2) stay in scope for the next TOR, and if so, how should it be framed?

## Evidence
[[q1.4]] establishes the site visit is the wrong instrument for testing the national reporting mechanism but the right instrument for an area-based diagnostic. Payment-schedule constraint identified in conversation: Milestone 2 falls at day 150, only 90 days after workplan approval at day 60 (TOR §7.1–7.2), ruling out a second physical round spaced far enough apart to observe real change. [[q3.3]] establishes what's actually unknown and needs local presence to resolve: where each sector's indicator data would physically originate, who holds it, on what cycle.

## Judgement
Closed. Keep one physical visit per area (6 areas), but: (1) specify method — structured interview with the data-holding office plus a records walkthrough, not just "a meeting or dialogue forum"; (2) site selection should favor areas where a relevant project is mid-implementation and the agency's own area office is the natural data source; (3) redirect the "second round" instinct into a *remote* verification round inside the otherwise-idle days 150–240, testing whether the area keeps submitting data once the consultant isn't present — the one thing a second physical visit would have tested, without doubling the travel budget.
