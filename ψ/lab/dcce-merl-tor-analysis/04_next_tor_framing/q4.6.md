# Continuity ownership after day 240

## Question
Who owns continuity after the contract ends, and what can a single 240-day, ฿3,000,000 TOR honestly commit to, given how long real mainstreaming took elsewhere?

## Evidence
Germany's incremental history (`~/.pplx-cli/history/2026-08-22_03-05-36...md`): ~3 years from strategy adoption (2008) to ministries owning targets within their own budgets (Adaptation Action Plan I, 2011); ~7 years to the first real monitoring cycle (2015); ~12 years to routine (2020); legal force only added in 2024, 16 years in. [[q2.7]] establishes the forcing mechanism (budget or statutory) is what made mainstreaming stick, and it came late, locking in something already working informally — not first.

## Judgement
Closed. A 240-day contract cannot honestly claim to build a self-sustaining forcing mechanism or complete institutionalization — that's a multi-year process everywhere it's been observed. What it *can* honestly deliver: naming who inherits the loop at DCCE, and on what recurring schedule, as an explicit deliverable — the first concrete step toward mainstreaming, not the whole journey. This should be stated plainly in the TOR rather than implied to be achievable in one contract. Depends on [[q4.8]] for whether budget/timeline flexibility changes this ceiling.
