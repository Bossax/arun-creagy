# What the Research work package should actually deliver

## Question
Given [[q2.4]] (Research is not a formal MEL stage anywhere in the literature), what should the current TOR's §5.1.3 Research task actually be redirected toward — or should the "R" framing be dropped?

## Evidence
Current TOR §5.1.3 (`ψ/inbox/TOR-DCCE-MERL-2026.md`) asks the contractor to review ≥10 existing studies on adaptation M&E methodology — retrospective literature synthesis. [[q2.3]]/[[q2.4]] establish that no framework treats Research as its own stage, and that a four-letter form, where it exists, more often means R = Reporting. This session's earlier writing (`ψ/writing/2026-08-22_MERL-concept-note-and-tor-direction.md`) had proposed redirecting "R" toward forward-looking scenario/target-setting research (climate projections, moving baselines) as "the one place R does work" — itself now understood to be based on the wrong source (OECD's activity taxonomy, not a governance-stage framework, per [[q2.4]]'s correction).

## Judgement
Closed, with the correction folded in: rather than searching for what "R" should mean, the more honest move is to stop treating Research as a fourth acronym letter at all. Whatever forward-looking scenario/target-setting work is genuinely useful (still true on its own merits — climate baselines do shift, and someone needs to research that) should be scoped as an explicit input to the Evaluation and Planning stages, not labeled as a parallel "R" stage that implies a status the literature doesn't support. This changes how the TOR redirect describes this work package's *name*, not necessarily its underlying content.
