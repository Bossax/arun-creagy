# Is this analysis for redirecting DCCE or guiding a bidder

## Question
Is this whole body of analysis meant to redirect DCCE's TOR text before it's issued, or to guide how a bidder should interpret and execute a TOR that's already fixed?

## Evidence
The two audiences produce genuinely different deliverables: a redline memo addressed to DCCE (which can propose changing TOR clauses, budget, qualifications) vs. a bidder's execution strategy (which has to extract the most real value from an unchangeable text). Boss, per conversation: "we want to redirect DCCE. give them a full picture of what needs to do and pick a slice of it that is doable within the given budget."

## Judgement
Closed. The analysis is explicitly meant to redirect DCCE before the TOR is issued — the deliverable should lay out the full scope of what real adaptation-MEL operationalization requires, then recommend a deliberately bounded slice that fits the fixed budget/timeline (confirmed fixed in [[q4.8]]), rather than either scoping the whole problem into one contract or treating the current TOR text as unchangeable. This settles how [[q3.1]]'s authority findings and [[q4.1]]'s activation-vs-design question should be framed — as asks and options for DCCE to choose from, not constraints a bidder must work around — and means the final output should explicitly separate "full picture" from "the slice this TOR should fund," naming the rest as a roadmap for later engagements.
