# National-to-local sequencing: trigger and precondition

## Question
What should trigger DCCE extending MEL from national to local/area level, and is it premature for this TOR to address at all, given DCCE lacks even a working national-level Phase 3 cycle?

## Evidence
Boss's framing this session was explicit: national level first, local level only after. [[q1.4]] (site visit as area-based diagnostic, not a fix for the national reporting mechanism) and [[q3.5]] (no real convening authority yet exists within DCCE) both point the same direction — local-level MEL work depends on national-level plumbing existing first.

Round 1 (query targeting this note directly) returned three different examples of what actually triggered a country to extend from national to local level, and none of them happened quickly. The UK ran its national system for about fourteen years, covering only central government and large regulated infrastructure, before extending to local authorities at all — and the trigger was a formal recommendation in the national review body's own progress report, which then led to a trial with 35 local authorities before any wider requirement. South Korea's extension to its cities and provinces was required by law from early on, but the part that actually made it work was the environment ministry finishing a web-based tracking system in 2020 — the legal requirement existed well before local governments had a working way to comply with it. Peru ties the extension to finishing specific national-level work first: reporting downward only becomes mandatory once national pilot models and reporting guidance are formally published, which Peru had targeted for 2024–2025.

Boss agreed with all three comparator examples, and named a specific, real risk they were each built to avoid: DCCE has a tendency to take a methodology just developed in one project and move it straight into full operation and enforcement, without solid grounding, without testing, and without genuinely mainstreaming it first. He also corrected the note's original premise: he could not name a single trigger for "going local" because DCCE is not actually waiting to start — DCCE already runs a lot of local-level activity, through site visits and direct engagement with provinces and municipalities, running alongside its national-level work rather than sequenced after it.

## Judgement
Closed, with the note's original framing revised. The real question was never when DCCE starts local-level work — it already does, informally, through site visits and provincial/municipal engagement (see [[q1.4]]). The actual risk, confirmed directly by Boss and consistent with all three comparators, is DCCE's tendency to take something built and tested at small scale and push it straight into full national operation without a genuine testing and grounding step in between — exactly what the UK's pilot-then-expand approach, Korea's tool-before-mandate sequencing, and Peru's formal publication gate were each built to prevent. The next TOR's job on this point is not to gate local-level work behind some national milestone. It is to make sure whatever local-level MEL practice already exists gets properly tested and grounded, at whatever scale it is already happening, before DCCE moves to formalize any of it into a national requirement. This lines up directly with [[q3.13]]'s closed model: local or sector-level activity is where testing happens, and DCCE's role is the deliberate step of checking what worked before carrying it into policy or making it mandatory — the caution is about skipping that step, not about which scale the work starts at.
