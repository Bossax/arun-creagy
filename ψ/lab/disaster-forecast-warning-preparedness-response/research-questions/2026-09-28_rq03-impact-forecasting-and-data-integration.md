# RQ03: impact forecasting and data integration

## Lead question

Do existing forecast, hydrological, exposure, vulnerability, infrastructure, and local-observation systems provide information with enough skill, resolution, latency, and interoperability to support anticipatory decisions?

## Decision informed

Whether institutional triggers can use current information or require prior technical and data-governance investment.

## Related atoms

ART-F03, ART-F04, ART-C01, ART-A01, ART-A05, LAW-C04, LAW-P12, LAW-A04, REL-12, REL-13.

## Assumptions to test

- Large investment implies operationally usable capability.
- Agency systems can be joined into a coherent forecast-to-impact chain.
- Exposure and vulnerability data are current enough for warning operations.
- Local observations can improve decisions without undermining scientific quality control.

## Subquestions

1. What forecast products exist for rainfall, runoff, river levels, drainage, inundation, and cascading urban impacts?
2. What are their lead time, spatial resolution, update frequency, skill, uncertainty, and failure modes?
3. Which datasets describe population, vulnerable groups, infrastructure, shelters, transport, land use, and local resources?
4. Who owns each authoritative dataset, and under what sharing, licensing, privacy, and security rules?
5. Can systems exchange data in real time, or do staff manually transfer files and interpretations?
6. How are conflicting models or sensor readings reconciled?
7. What local and citizen observations could be assimilated, verified, and returned to decision-makers?
8. Does a central system need to hold data, or can it provide governed access to distributed authoritative sources?

## Evidence and method

- Create a data-and-model inventory tied to decisions rather than counting assets alone.
- Review validation reports, historical hindcasts, uptime, latency, APIs, data dictionaries, and operational logs.
- Test a small number of past events end to end: what information was available at each plausible trigger time?
- Interview forecast producers and operational users separately to expose differences between availability and usability.

## Completion criteria

- Each proposed decision has a documented minimum information requirement and current data source.
- Forecast skill and uncertainty are reported at the scale relevant to action.
- Interoperability gaps are classified as technical, semantic, legal, organizational, or capacity-related.
- The research can say which triggers are feasible now, feasible after defined improvements, or unsupported.

## Boundaries

This is not a procurement inventory or a proposal for a new national platform. It evaluates fitness for operational decisions.
