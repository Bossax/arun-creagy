# RQ06: local preparedness and operational capacity

## Lead question

Can provinces, Bangkok districts, municipalities, and local authorities execute staged anticipatory actions, and what minimum capability package is missing?

## Decision informed

How statutory local duties should be differentiated, funded, supported, and phased rather than imposed as uniform unfunded mandates.

## Related atoms

ART-C07, ART-P03, ART-P07, ART-P08, ART-A06, LAW-P05, LAW-P07, LAW-A05, REL-05, REL-14.

## Assumptions to test

- Local actors hold operational knowledge not available nationally.
- Plans, shelters, equipment, staff, and community networks exist but vary widely.
- A common minimum standard can accommodate different hazard and capacity profiles.
- National support can close gaps without centralizing local decisions.

## Subquestions

1. Which local actors monitor hazards, interpret consequences, warn communities, open shelters, arrange transport, deploy equipment, and request support?
2. Are local plans tied to observable thresholds and named action owners?
3. What staff, duty rosters, communications, vehicles, pumps, shelters, supplies, and procurement arrangements are actually available?
4. How quickly can resources be mobilized outside office hours and across jurisdictions?
5. How often are plans exercised, and are exercise findings used to revise them?
6. What mutual-aid and escalation arrangements exist when local capacity is exceeded?
7. Which requirements should be universal, hazard-specific, or capacity-tiered?
8. What national technical assistance and recurrent funding are necessary?

## Evidence and method

- Select contrasting case sites by hazard, administrative type, urban/rural context, and capacity.
- Review plans and inventories, then verify them through drills, site visits, and staff/community interviews.
- Use scenario walkthroughs to test lead time, decision authority, communications, logistics, and handoffs.
- Distinguish documented assets from assets that are staffed, maintained, accessible, and deployable.

## Completion criteria

- A capability baseline covers people, authority, plans, equipment, facilities, finance, data, communications, and partnerships.
- Minimum action packages are specified for each trigger stage.
- Capacity gaps are assigned to local improvement, shared services, mutual aid, or national support.
- Proposed legal duties include feasible phasing and funding responsibility.

## Boundaries

This is a capability assessment, not a compliance ranking intended to punish low-capacity jurisdictions.
