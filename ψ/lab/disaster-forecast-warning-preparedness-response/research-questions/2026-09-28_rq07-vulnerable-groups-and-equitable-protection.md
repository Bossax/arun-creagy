# RQ07: vulnerable groups and equitable protection

## Lead question

What assistance, accessibility, data, and accountability are required so people facing mobility, income, language, tenure, health, age, disability, or connectivity barriers can act on a warning?

## Decision informed

The minimum protection obligations that must accompany warning delivery and local anticipatory-action plans.

## Related atoms

ART-F09, ART-C07, ART-N03, ART-P05, LAW-N02, LAW-P05, LAW-P13, REL-07, REL-08.

## Assumptions to test

- Vulnerability categories predict practical barriers to protective action.
- Existing administrative data can identify support needs accurately and lawfully.
- Community organizations can help reach people without shifting state responsibility onto unpaid actors.
- Uniform warning delivery can widen rather than reduce unequal outcomes.

## Subquestions

1. Which groups fail to receive, understand, trust, or act on current warnings, and why?
2. What transport, accompaniment, medical support, cash, animal care, documentation, language, accessibility, or shelter arrangements are needed?
3. Who maintains support-needs information, with what consent, update cycle, security, and emergency-access rules?
4. How do informal tenure, migrant status, homelessness, or fear of authorities affect willingness to seek assistance?
5. What responsibilities belong to government, health and social services, community organizations, families, and private providers?
6. What minimum accessibility and anti-discrimination standards should apply to messages, transport, shelters, and relief?
7. How should affected groups participate in designing and testing plans?
8. Which outcome measures reveal unequal protection rather than only aggregate reach?

## Evidence and method

- Conduct participatory research with affected groups and representative organizations, not only agency interviews.
- Trace real household decision journeys from receiving information to reaching safety.
- Audit warning channels, shelters, transport, and registration processes for accessibility and exclusion.
- Review privacy, personal-data, disability, migration, child-protection, and social-welfare requirements.

## Completion criteria

- Barrier and support profiles are grounded in lived experience and disaggregated evidence.
- Each trigger-stage action package names who receives assistance, who provides it, and what resource is reserved.
- Data use has a lawful, minimal, secure, and updateable design.
- Performance indicators report differential reach, action, safety, and recovery outcomes.

## Boundaries

This brief does not treat “vulnerable groups” as a fixed list or assume that vulnerability is an individual trait rather than a product of conditions and systems.
