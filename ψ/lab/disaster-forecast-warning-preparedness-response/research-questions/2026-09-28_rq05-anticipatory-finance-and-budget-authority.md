# RQ05: anticipatory finance and budget authority

## Lead question

Which ordinary-budget, local-budget, advance-payment, contingency, insurance, or fund mechanisms can finance pre-impact action quickly, lawfully, and accountably?

## Decision informed

Whether Thailand needs a new fund, revised spending rules, pre-approved budget lines, trigger-linked disbursement, or primarily clearer interpretation of existing authority.

## Related atoms

ART-F08, ART-C05, ART-C06, ART-P04, ART-A03, ART-A04, LAW-C03, LAW-P04, LAW-P09, LAW-N05, LAW-A06, LAW-A07, REL-06, REL-09, REL-15.

## Assumptions to test

- Current rules prevent or deter expenditure before formal disaster declaration.
- A dedicated fund is faster than ordinary or contingency budgets.
- Audit and procurement rules can distinguish justified false alarms from waste.
- Agencies will mainstream prevention if duties and budget classifications are clear.

## Subquestions

1. What expenditure is currently lawful before declaration for national, provincial, Bangkok, and local authorities?
2. Which barriers are legal prohibitions, approval delays, unclear interpretation, audit fear, missing budget lines, or lack of cash?
3. Which preventive measures belong in routine agency budgets, and which justify a supplementary fund?
4. What event or trigger authorizes commitment, obligation, procurement, transfer, and payment?
5. Who controls release, within what deadline, and with what documentation?
6. How are unused allocations, false-alarm spending, replenishment, fraud risk, and cross-boundary action handled?
7. Should finance be attached to pre-approved action protocols rather than released as unrestricted emergency money?
8. What evidence is required to estimate benefit, avoided loss, and distributional impact?

## Evidence and method

- Review budget law, treasury rules, local finance rules, emergency advances, funds, procurement exceptions, audit findings, and actual pre-impact spending cases.
- Trace one proposed action—such as assisted evacuation or pump deployment—through the complete authorization and payment process.
- Compare international forecast-based finance mechanisms by legal form, speed, control, and false-alarm treatment.
- Interview finance, budget, audit, disaster, provincial, and local officials.

## Completion criteria

- A current-state map identifies lawful routes, practical blockages, approvals, and timing.
- Each proposed action package has an eligible funding route or a precisely stated legal gap.
- A design option distinguishes ordinary responsibility from supplementary funding.
- Controls permit timely action without removing auditability.

## Boundaries

This brief assesses financing mechanisms. It does not assume a new national fund is the preferred answer.
