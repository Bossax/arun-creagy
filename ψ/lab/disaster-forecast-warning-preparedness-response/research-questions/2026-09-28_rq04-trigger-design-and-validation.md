# RQ04: trigger design and validation

## Lead question

How can hazard-specific, staged triggers activate proportionate action early enough to help while managing uncertainty, false alarms, missed events, and local variation?

## Decision informed

The minimum technical and governance standard for giving a trigger operational or legal effect.

## Related atoms

ART-C02, ART-C04, ART-C05, ART-P02, ART-P07, ART-A01–ART-A03, ART-A07, LAW-C05, LAW-P14, LAW-A03, REL-03, REL-04, REL-16.

## Assumptions to test

- A trigger can be more reliable and accountable than case-by-case discretion.
- Staged thresholds are more appropriate than a single binary warning.
- False-alarm costs are tolerable and can be governed explicitly.
- National standards can coexist with local calibration.

## Subquestions

1. Which variable or combination should trigger readiness, deployment, assisted evacuation, and emergency action for each hazard?
2. Should thresholds use observed conditions, probabilistic forecasts, forecast impacts, rates of change, or combined indicators?
3. What lead time does each protective action require?
4. How should false alarms, misses, near-threshold cases, conflicting models, and rapidly changing conditions be handled?
5. Who validates, approves, updates, suspends, or overrides a trigger?
6. What explanation and audit record must accompany activation or non-activation?
7. Which actions can activate automatically, and which require human confirmation because of rights, safety, or cost?
8. How often should triggers be recalibrated from event outcomes and climate change?

## Evidence and method

- Review operational trigger systems and anticipatory-action protocols for comparable hazards.
- Back-test candidate triggers against historical events and record hits, misses, lead time, and action cost.
- Use cost-loss analysis with explicit value choices rather than optimize only technical accuracy.
- Co-design action thresholds with forecast agencies, operational authorities, local implementers, and affected groups.

## Completion criteria

- A trigger-governance specification defines inputs, stages, actions, owner, override, expiry, audit trail, and review.
- Back-testing shows performance and uncertainty transparently.
- Each trigger stage is connected to a feasible action package and required lead time.
- Legal drafting can distinguish mandatory activation, bounded discretion, and prohibited automation.

## Boundaries

This brief defines principles and validates candidate triggers. It does not assume one threshold can serve all hazards or locations.
