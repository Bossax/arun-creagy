# Failures across disaster forecasting, warning, preparedness, and response

## Research theme

The two sources describe one system from different directions. The article starts with an observed failure and argues that forecasts did not become protection. The legal proposal starts with institutional design and proposes duties, information systems, budgets, anticipatory powers, and review mechanisms.

Their shared theme is direct:

> Thailand may possess substantial forecasting and monitoring capability while still failing to warn, prepare, and protect people because the links between information, authority, finance, and operational action are incomplete.

This is broader than risk communication. Communication remains one necessary component, but the research unit here is the full disaster forecasting–warning–preparedness–response system.

## Integrated logic

```text
Forecast and monitoring
        ↓
Local impact and vulnerability assessment
        ↓
Agreed, validated trigger
        ↓
Named authority + binding or time-bound duty
        ↓
Pre-agreed action package + accessible finance
        ↓
Agency action + public warning + assisted protective action
        ↓
Reduced avoidable loss
        ↓
Performance review, accountability, and recalibration
```

A technically successful forecast can still produce a policy failure at any later link. Conversely, a clear public warning cannot protect people if shelters, transport, staff, funds, and assistance are unavailable.

## What the sources jointly establish as hypotheses

1. **Information abundance can coexist with operational failure.** The relevant question is not how much data exists, but whether it reaches a decision with sufficient lead time.
2. **Warning is both communication and institutional activation.** It should tell the public what to do and activate government resources and duties.
3. **Preparedness must be financed before impact.** Plans without pre-authorized expenditure remain dependent on discretionary escalation.
4. **Local execution is indispensable.** National agencies cannot substitute for local knowledge, shelter readiness, transport, vulnerable-person support, and last-meter communication.
5. **Avoided loss is the intended outcome.** Equipment purchases, messages sent, and relief delivered are intermediate outputs rather than proof of protection.
6. **Law can enable but cannot by itself supply capability.** Duties must be paired with staffing, data, budgets, operational procedures, drills, and feedback.

These are working hypotheses, not completed findings.

## Missing assumptions and background knowledge

### 1. Factual baseline

The event chronology, rainfall totals, warning timestamps, expenditure totals, agency counts, quotations, Nan pilot, international benefit claims, and climate-attribution statements have not been independently verified. The argument should not inherit their certainty before that work is done.

→ Research brief: `research-questions/2026-09-28_rq01-factual-and-legal-baseline.md`

### 2. Present legal position

It is unknown whether existing statutes, national plans, financial regulations, governor powers, and local-government rules already permit parts of anticipatory action. The problem may be absent authority, unclear authority, unused authority, or fear of audit.

→ RQ01 and RQ05.

### 3. Actual decision workflow

The sources infer fragmentation but do not reconstruct the real sequence from forecast production through impact interpretation, warning approval, Cell Broadcast, provincial orders, and field action. Formal mandates may differ from practice.

→ `research-questions/2026-09-28_rq02-warning-workflow-and-decision-rights.md`

### 4. Technical readiness for impact triggers

The availability, accuracy, lead time, resolution, update frequency, and interoperability of forecast, hydrological, exposure, vulnerability, and local observational data are unknown.

→ `research-questions/2026-09-28_rq03-impact-forecasting-and-data-integration.md`

### 5. Trigger validity and acceptable error

Neither source explains how thresholds should be calibrated, staged, localized, expired, overridden, or evaluated. The tolerable balance between false alarms and missed events is a public-policy choice, not only a modelling question.

→ `research-questions/2026-09-28_rq04-trigger-design-and-validation.md`

### 6. Financial feasibility

The proposed distinction between ordinary budgets and a supplementary fund is conceptually clear but operationally unspecified. Eligible costs, release authority, audit treatment, replenishment, unused funds, and false-alarm expenditure require research.

→ `research-questions/2026-09-28_rq05-anticipatory-finance-and-budget-authority.md`

### 7. Local capability

No baseline shows which provinces or local authorities can maintain hazard plans, staged actions, shelters, transport, pumps, communications, vulnerable-person support, and drills.

→ `research-questions/2026-09-28_rq06-local-preparedness-and-operational-capacity.md`

### 8. Equitable protection

The sources identify vulnerability but do not specify lawful data practices, accessibility standards, responsibility for assisted evacuation, community participation, or minimum protection packages.

→ `research-questions/2026-09-28_rq07-vulnerable-groups-and-equitable-protection.md`

### 9. Accountability and learning

There is no agreed definition of preventable loss, no end-to-end performance scorecard, and no settled division between learning review, administrative responsibility, political accountability, and legal liability.

→ `research-questions/2026-09-28_rq08-accountability-performance-and-learning.md`

## Boundary with risk-communication research

The two research themes should remain separate but connected.

- **Risk-communication research** studies trust, message content, channels, audience response, trusted intermediaries, and two-way communication.
- **This research** studies the institutions and operational arrangements that connect forecasts to warning decisions, preparedness, finance, protective action, response, and review.

The connection point is a warning event. Risk-communication research asks whether the message can be received, believed, understood, and acted upon. This research asks whether institutions produced that message in time and delivered the authority, services, and resources required for action.

## Research sequence

1. Establish factual and legal baselines before treating the source narratives as evidence.
2. Reconstruct the actual workflow and decision rights.
3. Test whether current data and models can support impact-based triggers.
4. Design and validate trigger principles only after Steps 2–3.
5. Determine lawful, auditable pre-impact finance.
6. Test what local implementation capacity exists and what minimum package is feasible.
7. Apply equity and accessibility requirements across the complete design.
8. Define performance, review, and accountability after the operational model is known.

## Provisional synthesis

The sources plausibly identify a missing middle between technical forecasting and disaster response. The article names the failure; the legal proposal supplies much of the institutional architecture. Neither yet proves that the forecast inputs are adequate, that existing law is insufficient, that proposed actors can perform their duties, or that automatic triggers and finance can be made both fast and accountable. Those are the next research tasks.
