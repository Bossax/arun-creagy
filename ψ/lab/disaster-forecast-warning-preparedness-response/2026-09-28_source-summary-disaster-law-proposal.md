# Source summary: disaster-law amendment proposal

## Source purpose and thesis

The proposal seeks to shift the Disaster Prevention and Mitigation Act from a law centred on emergency command and post-impact assistance toward a law governing the full disaster-risk cycle. It retains the existing command structure while adding duties and mechanisms for risk reduction, preparedness, climate adaptation, risk information, anticipatory action, finance, Build Back Better, and performance review.

The document combines policy architecture with illustrative statutory language. It is not yet a legal opinion or a verified amendment bill.

## Internal tensions and unresolved drafting issues

1. **Authority versus duty.** Proposed Section 66 gives an authorized official power to order anticipatory measures (`มีอำนาจ...ได้`) but does not require action when a trigger is reached.
2. **Trigger precision.** The national committee may define criteria, but the proposal does not specify validation, staged escalation, expiry, override, or review.
3. **Finance activation.** The fund can support anticipatory action, but no provision yet links a trigger directly to time-bound release.
4. **Accountability.** Sections 73–75 require monitoring and reports but do not state consequences, independent review, remedies, or responsibility for failure to act.
5. **Fund structure inconsistency.** The executive summary describes at least three subaccounts while the detailed discussion describes four by separating anticipatory action from recovery/Build Back Better.
6. **Central data risk.** The proposal mandates a central system but does not define operational latency, authoritative datasets, conflict resolution, service levels, or privacy governance.
7. **Legal overlap.** The allocation between disaster law, climate law, local-government law, planning law, water law, and public-finance law remains unresolved.
8. **Repetition and status.** Parts of Section 6 repeat the design rationale, and the statutory text is illustrative rather than a completed amendment bill.

## Summary judgment

The proposal offers a broad institutional blueprint and usefully separates coordination, sector ownership, routine budgets, supplementary finance, information, anticipatory action, recovery, and review. Its decisive missing layer is enforceability: the draft enables many actions but does not yet establish when action becomes mandatory, how quickly it must occur, how money is released, or what accountability follows non-performance.
