# Stakeholder mental models in Thai weather and climate warning

This is an analysis built from desk sources and how the agencies are set up. None of it comes from interviews. Treat each mental model as a hypothesis to test with people from that group.

## One structural fact shapes everything

The public blames **TMD**, but TMD does not own the public warning. TMD produces the forecast and sends it to the NDWC. The NDWC sits under **DDPM** and is the mandated issuer of disaster warnings. Provincial governors then order the response. Blame lands in one place while the authority to warn and act sits in another.

The step in between, turning "rain over X mm" into "your area floods tonight," has no clear owner. The NFCS workshop (21 Sep 2026) assigned impact-based forecasting to TMD in the 2027–2030 plan, but for this rainy season that job is still unowned.

## 1. TMD (Thai Meteorological Department)

**Rewarded for:** scientific accuracy, following procedure, WMO standing, never being wrong in writing.

**Punished for:** false alarms that disrupt business and draw official complaints, and misses that make the news. The penalty for a false alarm is visible and personal. The penalty for being slow is spread thin and shared with other agencies.

**Mental model (their inner voice):**
> "Our job is to be correct, not loud. We issue hazard forecasts by the criteria. Warning and evacuating are DDPM's and the governor's job. If we over-warn, we lose credibility and get blamed for the economic cost. The influencers are irresponsible amateurs reading raw model runs. The public doesn't understand probability."

**Blind spot:** TMD measures trust by accuracy, but the public measures it by timeliness and whether the agency seems to care. Being right three hours late counts as being wrong. TMD also doesn't see that its public brand carries the blame for the whole warning chain, whatever the org chart says.

## 2. DDPM (Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation, including the NDWC)

**Rewarded for:** visible response on the ground, like boats, sandbags, relief kits, and the minister on site. New alert channels such as Cell Broadcast and LINE Safety Check also count as wins.

**Punished for:** slow response and badly handled relief. Warnings it never sent attract less blame because the public doesn't see DDPM as the forecaster.

**Mental model:**
> "We act on what the technical agencies give us. Our strength is mobilisation and channels, and we've built Cell Broadcast. If a warning was weak, the forecast input was weak. Our real test is how fast we respond once water arrives."

**Blind spot:** DDPM treats warning as sending out TMD's content, not as risk communication it owns. It has the channels and the legal mandate, yet it lets TMD take the credibility hit. That is comfortable for DDPM, so it has little reason to change.

## 3. DCCE (Department of Climate Change and Environment)

**Rewarded for:** policy outputs like the NAP, BTR, risk maps, and the data platform, plus international reporting and NFCS co-leadership.

**Punished for:** almost nothing in this event. DCCE is not in the operational warning chain.

**Mental model:**
> "This event proves the climate risk is rising, and that supports the NAP and the case for a national data platform. Short-term warnings are TMD's and DDPM's business. Our role is long-term risk information and awareness."

**Blind spot:** DCCE runs the risk-map and exposure data that impact-based warnings need, yet it sees itself as working on a timescale of years. The data it holds is the missing link between TMD's hazard forecast and the public's question, "does *my* place flood?" DCCE also has the least at stake in the trust crisis, so it is the least likely to push for fixing it, even though it co-leads the NFCS.

## 4. The general public (people in the eastern and central flood zones)

**What they want:** early, local, practical certainty. Do I move the car, lift the furniture, leave?

**What it costs them to act:** preparing on a false alarm costs little. Getting caught unprepared costs a lot. So they are drawn to whoever warns earliest and loudest.

**Mental model:**
> "The government always tells us late and softens it so people don't panic, or to protect someone. Last time we trusted them and lost everything. The page I follow warned three days early and was right. Better to over-prepare than trust the officials."

**Blind spot:** people only tally the hits. Nobody keeps score of the influencers' false alarms, and the official channel is scored on its worst moment. Many people also can't tell TMD, DDPM, and the NDWC apart. To them, "the government" is one actor that failed them.

## 5. The social media influencer (weather pages and "whistleblowers")

**Rewarded for:** reach, shares, followers, and in the end money from ads, sponsors, or a personal brand.

**Punished for:** almost nothing. False alarms are forgotten within 48 hours. A hit gets remembered and repeated.

**Mental model:**
> "The officials are too slow and too scared to tell the truth. I show people what the models actually show. If I'm wrong, at least people were ready. If I'm right, I saved them. The more people follow me, the more lives I protect."

Many of these people believe this sincerely. They are not just chasing clicks.

**Blind spot:** they read one model run at face value and treat it as a firm forecast. Their motive (they care) and their incentive (they gain followers) push the same way, so they cannot see the cost of their false alarms. Each over-warning quietly wears down the public's response to all warnings, official ones included.

## Side by side

| Entity | Wants most | Fears most | Pays for being wrong? | Sees itself as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TMD | Scientific credibility | False alarm backlash | High (both directions) | Correct scientist |
| DDPM | Visible response | Slow relief | Low for warnings | Responder, not forecaster |
| DCCE | Policy momentum | Little in this event | None | Long-term planner |
| Public | Early local certainty | Being caught unprepared | Pays in losses | Victim of late warnings |
| Influencer | Reach | Being ignored | None | Truth-teller, lifesaver |

## Where the mental models collide

1. **Blame and mandate don't line up.** The public blames TMD, TMD points to DDPM's mandate, and DDPM points to TMD's input. Because each explanation holds up on its own terms, nobody takes ownership of the failure.
2. **Nobody owns the "so what" step.** TMD has the hazard data, DCCE has the exposure data, and DDPM has the channels. Joining them into "your area floods tonight" is nobody's job. Influencers fill that gap, badly but quickly.
3. **The penalties are lopsided.** The actor who pays most for being wrong (TMD) is the one people trust least. The actor who pays nothing (the influencer) is the one people trust most. That is stable, so the situation won't fix itself.
4. **Nobody keeps score.** No one publishes a hit/miss record for official or unofficial forecasts. Without one, public trust follows memory and emotion, and that favours the influencer every time.

## What this suggests for the NFCS work

The UIP goal in the NFCS action plan (feedback loop, last-mile delivery, and the system admin role still undecided between DCCE and TMD) is where this problem would get addressed. Right now the plan treats the UIP as a platform-building task. This event shows it is really a question of who owns trust and who translates the forecast into local impact.

## Open questions to test

- Do TMD staff actually see the public warning as DDPM's job, or do they feel they own it?
- How often does the NDWC rewrite or add to TMD's content before sending it, and how long does that take?
- Is DCCE's risk and exposure data usable at the speed a warning needs, or only for planning?
- Which influencer pages carry the most weight in the eastern and central provinces, and what is their real hit rate this season?
