# NFCS tackling EWS failure and authority trust erosion

## Why this note exists

`2026-09-26_root-causes.md` named six root causes of the trust crisis around Thai weather warnings (RC1–RC6). This note checks each root cause against the draft NFCS HS&S Action Plan (Goals 1–5, as circulated for the 21 Sep 2026 validation workshop) to see which activities, as literally written, already work on that root cause, and what each root cause still needs that the current activity wording does not cover.

Activity codes follow the source files (e.g. `RMP-M2-A1` = Goal 3, Measure 2, Activity 1). Agencies are as listed in the draft; several cells are still blank in the source or only partly filled in.

## RC1 — Impact forecasting is assigned to one small team, not shared across the chain

| Goal          | Activity        | What it does                                                                                                                              | Agencies                                                                                                                   | How it addresses RC1                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Goal 3 (RMP)  | RMP-M2-A1       | Develop water hazard models and data                                                                                                      | TMD, HII, RID, ONWR (water-question focal point)                                                                           | Assigns the hydrology link of the chain to the agencies that already hold the modelling capability, matching the division proposed in the RC1 capacity note |
| Goal 3 (RMP)  | RMP-M3-A4       | Develop impact/loss simulation for key urban-sector use cases                                                                             | Provincial/Local authorities, GISTDA, DCCE                                                                                 | Matches the "scenario library" idea — pre-built impact simulations instead of live modelling by TMD                                                         |
| Goal 2 (CSIS) | CS-M1-A3        | Climate Risk and Adaptation Information System development, incl. non-climate data linkage                                                | DCCE (กปอ) named as national focal point and lead for impact/risk data, with sector focal points, DOLA, provincial offices | Names an owner for the impact/risk-data step — the closest existing answer to "who joins the forecast to local impact"                                      |
| Goal 4 (UIP)  | UIP-M2-A1 to A5 | National multi-hazard EWS governance/architecture, impact thresholds, impact-based forecast + verification, warning-to-action SOPs, pilot | MDES (กระทรวง DE) for A1; unspecified for A2–A5                                                                            | Builds the operational stage that turns impact data into an actual warning                                                                                  |
| Goal 5 (CD)   | CD-M3-A1        | Capacity gap assessment and role-based capacity development across the climate services value chain                                       | Data WG                                                                                                                    | Directly checks whether assigned agencies (including TMD's small team) have enough staff for their link                                                     |
| Goal 5 (CD)   | CD-M3-A2        | Resource and budget readiness assessment for NFCS implementation                                                                          | Data WG                                                                                                                    | Checks whether the divided workload is actually funded                                                                                                      |

**Remaining issues not covered by the literal activity text:**
- No activity states a binding mechanism (joint SOP, NDPMC decision, or MoU) that would make the division of labour enforceable rather than just written down — the "division exists on paper" risk from the capacity note.
- UIP-M2-A1 assigns EWS governance/architecture to MDES, a fourth actor not in the original chain (TMD / HII–RID–ONWR / GISTDA / DCCE / DDPM). How MDES's role relates to TMD's forecasting mandate and DDPM/NDWC's legal warning authority is undefined.
- No activity specifies a decision rule for when the chain's inputs disagree — TMD's rain scenario vs. HII's river model vs. GISTDA's real-time satellite read.
- No activity sets a timing SLA between stations (e.g., by what time TMD's forecast must land for HII to run hydrology in time for a same-day warning).
- CD-M3-A1/A2 (the capacity and budget check) are scheduled only for 2027, with no visible funding commitment attached in the same activity to act on what the assessment finds.

## RC2 — Blame lands where the mandate isn't

| Goal | Activity | What it does | Agencies | How it addresses RC2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goal 5 (CD) | CD-M1-A1 | NFCS Governance Structure and Operating Framework | Policy WG & Data WG | Establishes formal governance, so accountability inside NFCS is no longer implicit |
| Goal 5 (CD) | CD-M1-A2 | National–Sector–Local Coordination Mechanism | Policy WG & Data WG | Defines coordination lines across levels, which is exactly where blame currently gets lost between agencies |
| Goal 4 (UIP) | UIP-M2-A4 | Warning-to-Action Protocols and area-level SOPs | Unspecified | Assigns explicit response ownership once a warning is issued, closing the gap between TMD's forecast and DDPM/governors' action |

**Remaining issues not covered by the literal activity text:**
- No activity names a single public-facing spokesperson or "one face" for warnings during an event — CD-M1 organizes internal coordination, not who the public sees or hears from when a warning is issued.
- Nothing addresses why blame lands on TMD specifically as the recognizable meteorological brand, even where DDPM/NDWC holds the legal warning mandate — that is a public-perception problem, not only an internal-SOP problem.
- CD-M1's governance structure sits under the climate policy committee (กนภ.), while warning operations sit under the NDPMC (Disaster Prevention and Mitigation Act). No activity states how a division agreed under NFCS becomes binding on the operational, disaster-law side of the chain.

## RC3 — Penalties are lopsided

| Goal | Activity | What it does | Agencies | How it addresses RC3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goal 4 (UIP) | UIP-M2-A2 | Development of impact-based warning thresholds and triggers | Unspecified | Converts an individual official's judgment call into an agreed protocol, reducing personal exposure for issuing or withholding a warning |

**Remaining issues not covered by the literal activity text:**
- Nothing addresses the economic-backlash side of the "cry wolf" fear directly — no liability protection or agreed cost-sharing for a threshold-triggered warning that turns out to be a false alarm.
- Nothing touches the asymmetric side of the incentive problem — unofficial influencers still face zero penalty for false alarms; the plan only lowers the agency's cost of warning early, it does not change the influencer's incentive.
- UIP-M2-A2 is scheduled for 2028–2029 only. As drafted, nothing in the plan changes this dynamic for the current or next rainy season.

## RC4 — No public track record

| Goal | Activity | What it does | Agencies | How it addresses RC4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goal 4 (UIP) | UIP-M2-A3 | Impact-based forecast system by hazard type, with performance verification | Unspecified | Introduces forecast verification, though framed as an internal technical activity |
| Goal 3 (RMP) | RMP-M5-A2 | Standard framework for verifying and certifying model and data quality | All working-group agencies, DCCE | Technical verification standard for the models feeding the chain |
| Goal 5 (CD) | CD-M1-A3 | NFCS Monitoring, Reporting and Learning (MEL) system | Policy WG & Data WG, or DCCE and TMD | Indicators, data owners, reporting cadence, linked to NAP/GGA reporting — the closest existing accountability mechanism |

**Remaining issues not covered by the literal activity text:**
- All three activities verify performance internally or for policy reporting (NAP/GGA); none publish a scorecard the public or media could use to compare official forecast accuracy against social media pages.
- No activity tracks or verifies unofficial/social-media forecaster accuracy — even fully implemented, the track record stays one-sided.
- No activity specifies a public communication product, such as a post-season "how did our warnings do" report — the MEL system (CD-M1-A3) reports to policy processes, not to the public whose trust is actually eroding.

## RC5 — Warnings are hard to act on

| Goal | Activity | What it does | Agencies | How it addresses RC5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goal 4 (UIP) | UIP-M2-A2 | Impact-based warning thresholds and triggers | Unspecified | Turns hazard metrics ("90mm, 60% of area") into "what will happen" triggers |
| Goal 4 (UIP) | UIP-M3-A1 | Multi-channel warning dissemination, including social mechanisms (village heads, community leaders) | Ministry of Education, DDPM, TMD, DCCE, MDES | Extends delivery beyond apps/SMS into locally trusted, informal channels |
| Goal 4 (UIP) | UIP-M3-A3 | Development of risk communication and climate-service-user capacity | Unspecified | The one activity explicitly named risk communication |
| Goal 4 (UIP) | UIP-M4-A1 | Gather user needs, co-design services with users | TMD, MDES | Comment on this activity explicitly adds "should invite users to co-design communication of information" |

**Remaining issues not covered by the literal activity text:**
- No activity specifies message-format standards, such as plain-language templates, consequence-based wording, or standard probability language — the activities build channels and general "capacity," not the message templates themselves.
- UIP-M3-A1, UIP-M3-A3, and UIP-M4-A1 are all scheduled from 2028 onward. Nothing in 2027 means the current and next rainy season are not covered by any planned redesign.
- Nothing addresses the specific "formal PDF bulletin" format problem named in the root-cause note — no activity commits to a mobile-first, plain-Thai warning product.

## RC6 — Communication runs one way

| Goal | Activity | What it does | Agencies | How it addresses RC6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goal 4 (UIP) | UIP-M4-A1 | Gather user needs, co-design and improve services based on feedback | TMD, MDES | Closest match to a feedback loop, though framed as a slow, multi-year service-review cycle |
| Goal 4 (UIP) | UIP-M3-A2 | Track coverage and reach of warning information | Unspecified | Measures whether warnings reached people, but this is a one-directional reach metric, not two-way listening |

**Remaining issues not covered by the literal activity text:**
- No activity funds or assigns a real-time listening or rumor-monitoring function during an actual event — the feedback loop that exists is a slow, multi-year service-review cycle, not an in-event capability.
- No activity assigns ownership of correcting misinformation while it is spreading, such as a monitoring team that flags and responds to viral but inaccurate posts during a live flood event.
- UIP-M4-A1 is scheduled only for 2029–2030 — several rainy seasons before even the slow feedback mechanism would exist.

## Pattern across all six

The plan is strongest exactly where RC1 lives, since CS-M1-A3 and RMP-M2-A1 already assign most of the division of labour the RC1 capacity note proposed independently, before this mapping was done. It is weakest wherever a root cause needs something public-facing and fast: no activity publishes a track record to the public (RC4), no activity gives the public one accountable face during a warning (RC2), and no activity builds real-time listening during an event (RC6). Everything scheduled for 2027 sits in Goal 1 (observation infrastructure), Goal 3 (scenario and hazard modelling), and Goal 5 (capacity/budget assessment). Nearly everything that would be visible to the public — thresholds, message redesign, dissemination, feedback — starts in 2028 at the earliest.
