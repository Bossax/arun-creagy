sa# Project brief — Climate data downscaling roll-ups

## Purpose

Create a two-roll-up Thai-language series that helps a non-specialist audience understand what climate projection data is, why it matters, what scenarios mean, how the data can be used, and how downscaling produces more locally useful information for Thailand.

## Audience and intended use

- Primary audience: Thai public-sector practitioners, planners, researchers, and partners who need climate-projection data for risk assessment, planning, or adaptation decisions.
- Intended use: physical roll-ups and supporting digital display at a DCCE-facing event or information setting.
- Display language: Thai first, with restrained English technical terms only where they aid recognition (for example, GCM, RegCM, WRF-Chem, CSV, and NetCDF).

## Series objective

The reader should leave with two connected ideas:

1. Climate projection data describes plausible future conditions under defined scenarios and supports planning under uncertainty.
2. Statistical and dynamical downscaling help translate broader climate-model information into higher-resolution information, which should be interpreted across multiple models and scenarios.

## Format and production constraints

- Two coordinated vertical roll-ups; final physical dimensions, printer requirements, safe zones, and logo placement remain to be confirmed.
- Final type, values, data labels, source notes, and instructions must remain editable in Canva or the chosen layout application.
- Image-generation outputs, if used after design approval, are individual reusable visual elements—not finished roll-ups—and must follow the approved visual language.
- Do not present generated imagery as climate data, a real portal interface, an official map, or evidence.

## Source boundary

- Primary supplied source: five DCCE climate-downscaling communication images in `ψ/inbox/ฐานข้อมูลภาพฉายภูมิอากาศความละเอียดสูง/`.
- Primary live reference: Thailand Climate Projection Platform, `https://clim-webbased.dcce.go.th/Home`, checked 2026-07-30.
- The Stage 1 evidence plan must verify every quantitative, methodological, and platform-function claim before it appears in final copy.

## Roll-up narrative

### Roll-up 01 — Climate projection data

- What climate projection data is.
- Why it is important for Thailand and climate-risk decisions.
- What scenarios represent, including scenario differences that must be verified before final copy.
- How the data can support risk assessment, adaptation planning, infrastructure and land-use planning, agriculture and water management, disaster preparedness, and policy or investment decisions.

### Roll-up 02 — Downscaling and the product family

- What statistical downscaling is.
- What dynamical downscaling is.
- Why many climate models and scenarios are needed to represent a range of plausible futures rather than one certain forecast.
- How users should interpret projection data by considering the model, scenario, period, variable, and spatial resolution.
- The four key data products: GridData, WRF-Chem, RegCM5, and Statistical Downscaling.

## Required content themes

- CMIP6 / global climate models, downscaling, and high-resolution climate data for Thailand.
- Statistical and dynamical downscaling as distinct approaches.
- Time coverage, spatial resolution, variables, models, and scenarios only where the supplied sources or platform confirm them.

## Prohibited inferences

- Do not imply a projection is a weather forecast, a certainty, or a guaranteed local outcome.
- Do not equate climate drivers with impacts or claim that downscaling by itself reduces climate risk.
- Do not claim all platform data have the same resolution, variables, models, or use conditions.
- Do not reproduce portal screenshots as if they are final interface documentation without current verification.

## References and visual direction

- Use the supplied blue DCCE communication material as a content and brand reference, not as a composition to copy.
- Use `reference.jpg` as the primary reference for the illustrated infographic treatment.
- Capture the reference illustration style as the project visual style lock: flat vector construction, bold simplified silhouettes, clean geometric forms, thick dark outlines, solid fills with subtle tonal shading, slightly dimensional layered forms, a limited palette, clear visual metaphors, and a consistent editorial infographic character.
- Use illustrated infographic vector iconography as a core storytelling system, not merely decoration.
- Apply the approved palette: Egyptian blue `#0A369D`, Sapphire sky `#4472CA`, Glaucous `#5E7CE2`, Baby blue ice `#92B4F4`, and Pale sky `#CFDEE7`.
- Preserve an authoritative public-information tone through clear Thai hierarchy, strong sectional composition, climate/data cues, and legible technical distinctions.
- Establish the final design system in Stage 2 after the evidence and audience takeaway are approved.
