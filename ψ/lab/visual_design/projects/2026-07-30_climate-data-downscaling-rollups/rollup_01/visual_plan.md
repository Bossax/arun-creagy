# Draft 3 Visual Plan — Roll-up 01

## Purpose

Explain that climate projection data describes possible long-term futures under different scenarios. It supports flexible planning; it is not a daily weather forecast.

## Format and visual authority

- **Final size:** 60 × 160 cm vertical roll-up
- **Aspect ratio:** 3:8
- **Reference language:** Use Draft 2's DCCE illustration language: blue-led flat vector forms, thick rounded Egyptian-blue outlines, pale-blue surfaces, restrained green accents, soft shading, rounded panels, and clear public-information iconography.
- **Typography:** Thai text remains editable; use Sarabun or Kanit for Thai and Outfit for English technical labels.
- **Safe zones:** Reserve the top hardware area for branding and the bottom area for source notes, QR code, and partner marks.
- **Text authority:** `layout_text_th.md` is the exact copy source. Do not paraphrase or generate text inside artwork.

## Reading sequence

### Zone A — Establish the idea

1. DCCE identity and headline.
2. Sub-headline stating planning, decision, and future response.
3. Definition card: what climate projection data is.
4. Side-by-side forecast versus projection comparison.

**Reader job:** Understand the basic distinction before seeing technical details.

### Zone B — Show possible futures and consequences

5. Central message banner: “เห็นอนาคตที่เป็นไปได้”.
6. SSP scenario branching diagram from the present into SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, and SSP5-8.5.
7. Five decision-application cards: water, agriculture, infrastructure, disaster preparedness, and policy/investment.

**Reader job:** See how scenarios create a range of possible futures and why that range matters for practical planning.

### Zone C — Explain responsible use

8. Insight card explaining why multiple models and scenarios are necessary.
9. Five-question checklist for interpreting data before use.
10. Closing message, DCCE source/platform, QR code, and citations.

**Reader job:** Leave with a usable interpretation rule rather than a list of technical terms.

## Layout constraints

- Use one dominant scenario diagram; do not add Roll-up 02 downscaling or product-family content.
- Give the definition, comparison, scenario note, insight body, and five applications enough space for the exact copy in `layout_text_th.md`.
- Keep the five application cards concise but descriptive; do not reduce them to sector names only.
- Keep the five checklist questions short and readable from a distance.
- Use generated artwork only for text-free visual elements. Add all Thai copy, labels, SSP names, source notes, logos, and QR codes as editable layout elements.
- Do not present a universal downscaled resolution value in Roll-up 01.

## Visual element register

| ID | Element | Reader job |
|---|---|---|
| `V01-01` | Forecast icon and projection icon | Make the time horizon and purpose contrast visible. |
| `V01-02` | Present-to-SSP branching diagram | Show several plausible futures rather than one forecast. |
| `V01-03` | Five decision-sector icons | Connect projection information to planning consequences. |
| `V01-04` | Insight-panel supporting icons | Reinforce comparison, uncertainty, and flexible action. |
| `V01-05` | Five checklist icons | Support the five editable questions without replacing their text. |
| `V01-06` | Footer landscape layer | Close the composition without adding geographic or data claims. |
