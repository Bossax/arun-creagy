# Edit Note — Roll-up 01 Draft 1

**Asset reviewed:** `draft1.png`  
**Project:** Climate Projection Data Roll-up Series  
**Iteration:** Draft 1 → Draft 2

## Overall assessment

The draft has a strong public-sector visual language: the DCCE identity is clear, the blue palette is consistent, the illustrations are approachable, and the vertical reading direction is easy to follow.

The main issue is strategic rather than decorative. Draft 1 has become a combined Roll-up 01 and Roll-up 02. It contains useful information, but the central message of Roll-up 01 is diluted and the amount of text is too high for comfortable physical-banner reading.

## What did not go as planned

### 1. Scope drift

The agreed Roll-up 01 focus was:

- What climate projection data is
- Projection versus weather forecast
- SSP scenarios and future uncertainty
- Five decision-use areas
- Why multiple models and scenarios are needed
- Five interpretation questions

Draft 1 additionally includes downscaling, GCM-to-RCM scale conversion, and the four DCCE products: GridData, WRF-Chem, RegCM5, and Statistical Downscaling. These topics belong primarily to Roll-up 02.

### 2. Text density increased without a corresponding layout reduction

The banner now contains many stacked cards and explanatory blocks. The result is:

- Body text that is too small for comfortable distance reading
- Product labels and lower-section explanations that compete for attention
- Decision-use icons that read more like labels than explanations
- A dense insight card at the bottom
- Too many sections with similar visual weight

### 3. The central thesis is diluted

The strongest Roll-up 01 message should be:

> Climate projection data does not predict a specific day. It helps planners examine possible long-term futures under different scenarios.

In Draft 1, this message competes with the downscaling and product-family content. The audience may remember the four products more easily than the purpose of climate projection data.

### 4. Resolution wording is too definite

The visual presents GCM at approximately 100 km and downscaled data at approximately 5 km. Product-specific resolution values were previously marked provisional in the evidence map. The next draft should not present 5 km as a universal product value without separate verification.

### 5. Visual hierarchy is too uniform

The palette and card system are coherent, but repeated dark-blue headers, rounded containers, and large illustrations give most sections similar emphasis. The design reads as an information catalogue rather than one clear explanatory story.

## Required changes for Draft 2

### Priority 1 — Restore Roll-up 01 scope

Remove the downscaling mechanism and four-product section from this banner. Move those elements to Roll-up 02.

### Priority 2 — Strengthen the explanation

Use the recovered space to explain more clearly:

- How projection data differs from a daily forecast
- How SSP scenarios represent different possible futures
- Why no single model or scenario should be treated as the answer
- How planners use ranges and trends to choose flexible actions

### Priority 3 — Improve readability

- Reduce the number of cards and section headers.
- Increase Thai body-text size.
- Keep one short but descriptive sentence for each of the five decision areas.
- Shorten the checklist to five direct questions.
- Preserve whitespace around the main explanatory diagrams.

### Priority 4 — Make the scenario diagram the visual anchor

The SSP branching diagram should be the main visual proof of uncertainty. It should connect directly to the planning message rather than appearing as one equal-weight section among many.

### Priority 5 — Correct the resolution language

Replace the fixed `~5 km` claim with:

> แปลงข้อมูลสู่ความละเอียดที่เหมาะสมกับการวิเคราะห์ระดับพื้นที่ โดยขึ้นอยู่กับผลิตภัณฑ์และวิธีการลดสเกล

## Recommended Draft 2 structure

1. Header: climate projection data for planning
2. Short explanation: projection versus forecast
3. Main visual: SSP scenario branching and possible futures
4. Five decision-use areas with concise descriptive copy
5. Insight card: why multiple models and scenarios matter
6. Five-question interpretation checklist
7. Source note and DCCE footer

## Acceptance criteria for Draft 2

- No Roll-up 02 product-family content remains in Roll-up 01.
- The projection-versus-forecast distinction is understandable without reading every section.
- The SSP diagram is the dominant explanatory visual.
- Thai body text remains readable at physical-banner viewing distance.
- The five decision areas explain practical use, not only sector names.
- No unverified universal product-resolution claim appears.
- The banner communicates one memorable idea: projections support flexible long-term decisions under uncertainty.
