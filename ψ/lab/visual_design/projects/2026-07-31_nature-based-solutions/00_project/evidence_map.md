# Evidence map — Nature-based Solutions

| Claim/module | Finding | Mechanism or relationship | Reader consequence | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. NbS definition | NbS protect, conserve, restore, sustainably use, or manage natural or modified ecosystems to address social, economic, and environmental challenges while providing human well-being, ecosystem-service, resilience, and biodiversity benefits. | Ecosystem condition and management are used to address societal challenges. | The reader can distinguish NbS from a generic “green” project or isolated planting activity. | S01; S02, UNEA 5/5 resolution definition |
| 2. NbS–EbA relationship | EbA is the use of biodiversity and ecosystem services as part of an overall adaptation strategy to help people adapt to climate change. | EbA applies ecosystem-based action specifically to climate vulnerability and resilience. | The reader sees EbA as a climate-adaptation subset/application within the broader NbS family. | S05, IPCC AR6 WGII Ch. 2, section 2.6; S10 |
| 3. NbS–CCA mechanism | NbS/EbA can reduce exposure, reduce sensitivity, increase adaptive capacity, and provide social, economic, health, and biodiversity co-benefits. | Wetlands, mangroves, forests, soils, catchments, and urban ecosystems regulate water, heat, erosion, storms, livelihoods, and ecosystem services. | The reader understands how nature changes climate risk rather than seeing NbS as a slogan. | S06, IPCC AR6 WGII Technical Summary TS.D.4.1, TS.D.4.7, TS.E.3.5 |
| 3. Limits | NbS are themselves vulnerable to climate change and can cause maladaptation when placed or designed poorly. | Ecosystem failure, ecological mismatch, exclusion, or misplaced interventions can reduce benefits or create harm. | The rollup must present NbS as context-specific and complementary, not universally sufficient. | S06, IPCC AR6 WGII Technical Summary TS.D.4.6–TS.D.4.7 |
| 4. Enabling conditions | Scaling requires policy integration, multilevel and cross-sector governance, finance and budgeting, knowledge, local and Indigenous participation, workforce capacity, monitoring, and valuation of ecosystem benefits. | Institutions connect ecological action to planning, budgets, implementation authority, maintenance, and accountability. | The reader sees enabling NbS as a public-administration and investment task, not only a conservation task. | S07; S08; S09; S06 TS.D.4.7 |
| 4. Adoption gap | NbS often remain less used than their potential because grey options are more familiar, easier to appraise, embedded in existing regulations and procurement, and supported by clearer project pipelines. | Institutional path dependency, fragmented responsibilities, limited skills, scattered finance, and weak evidence/value capture make NbS harder to select and maintain. | The reader understands why public commitment does not automatically become funded, procured, implemented, and maintained action. | S11, OECD 2024, sections 4.2 and 4.4; S14 |
| 4. Concrete adoption pathway | The IUCN ADAPT initiative linked knowledge materials, policy guidance, training, decision-maker capacity, scoping studies, pilots, and comparative policy analysis. | Evidence and skills are converted into policy instruments and place-based demonstrations that can be scaled. | The reader gets a practical model for moving from “NbS is important” to “actors can implement it here.” | S13, IUCN Western Balkans, lines 74–81 |
| 4. Finance and project pipeline | Funding is fragmented and private finance remains limited; NbS need appraisal, project preparation, risk-sharing, and investable portfolios. | Public budgets, blended finance, green bonds, payments for ecosystem services, land-value capture, and risk-sharing can connect benefits to finance. | Budgeting must fund project preparation, implementation, maintenance, and monitoring—not only construction. | S11, OECD 2024, sections 4.4.1 and 4.4.3; S15 |
| 4. Governance and legitimacy | Effective NbS require cross-sector and multilevel coordination, stakeholder ownership, co-design, and attention to land tenure and distributional effects. | Ecosystems cross administrative boundaries and benefits/costs are distributed unevenly. | Participation must influence decisions and ownership, not function as a late consultation step. | S08; S11; S14; policy-integration study |
