# DESIGN.md — Nature-based Solutions rollup

## Project metadata

- **Project:** Nature-based Solutions — single rollup
- **Reference:** `iunc-reference.png`
- **Reference role:** illustration style, information hierarchy, icon language, and editorial composition. Do not copy its exact composition, text, logos, or proprietary graphics.
- **Primary output:** one 60 × 160 cm educational rollup infographic
- **Audience:** policy, climate-adaptation, public-administration, and general professional audiences
- **Communication goal:** explain NbS, distinguish EbA, show how NbS supports climate adaptation, and make the enabling conditions for adoption concrete.

## Style synthesis

Create a clear, institutional editorial infographic built from flat vector illustrations, bold black typography, warm linen space, deep teal framing, and simplified ecosystem scenes. The design should feel practical, optimistic, and authoritative rather than decorative or corporate.

The visual language uses a strong top-to-bottom narrative: a high-contrast definition header, open explanatory icon groups, a large continuous illustrated ecosystem/application section, and a clean source/footer area.

Every visual should feel like a **clear public-information poster about living systems**, never like a generic corporate card layout, photorealistic environmental artwork, or decorative “green” branding.

## Composition system

- Use a strong vertical reading path: **definition → relationship → adaptation mechanism → enabling conditions**.
- Begin with a large colored header containing the main question or finding and a concise explanation block.
- Use open icon groups for definitions, challenges, benefits, or enabling factors; connect them through the illustration rather than enclosing them in cards.
- Use one dominant lower illustration to show ecosystems and applications in context.
- Use thin vertical dividers and teal rules to organize content without heavy boxes.
- Preserve generous warm-linen negative space around icons and text.
- Use callout labels attached to illustrations with thin leader lines or short teal connectors.
- Treat the rollup as one continuous visual field. Avoid a grid of independent panels or dashboard-style compartments.
- Keep one primary visual anchor per section; avoid competing hero illustrations.
- Use a light footer for sources, attribution, and final notes.

### Recommended layout zones

1. **Header zone:** 15–20% of the rollup; deep teal or dark teal field with bold title and short definition.
2. **Concept zone:** 20–25%; NbS umbrella, EbA relationship, and key climate-adaptation pathways.
3. **Application zone:** 40–50%; large ecosystem landscape showing distinct NbS applications and outcomes.
4. **Enabling zone:** 15–20%; evidence-to-policy-to-finance-to-implementation pathway, with a concise caution or accountability note.

## Shape language

- Use circles, rounded capsules, arches, flowing river shapes, simple hills, canopies, leaves, and organic landforms.
- Use thin dark outlines for icons and medium teal outlines for circular containers.
- Use flat fills and layered silhouettes; avoid bevels, glossy effects, and realistic surface texture.
- Use simple connector lines with small circular endpoints or restrained arrowheads.
- Let shapes carry meaning: water regulates, roots stabilize, canopy cools, wetlands store, and governance connects.
- Avoid generic dashboard cards, boxed module grids, random leaf motifs, unrelated stock icons, and meaningless decorative geometry.

## Illustration system

- **Mode:** flat vector editorial illustration with simplified ecological scenes.
- **Perspective:** mostly front-facing or lightly oblique; keep perspective consistent within each scene.
- **Simplification:** high enough for rollup viewing distance, but retain recognizable ecosystems and interventions.
- **Outlines:** thin to medium, dark evergreen or dark teal; never heavy cartoon outlines.
- **Shading:** minimal flat layering only; no photorealistic gradients.
- **Texture:** none by default; a very subtle paper grain is allowed only in the background.
- **People:** small, simplified figures used to show public benefit, stewardship, or participation; do not make them the main subject unless the evidence requires it.
- **Ecosystems:** forests, wetlands, rivers, farms, urban green-blue spaces, coastal systems, and soils should be recognizable through a few strong silhouettes.
- **Icons:** one idea per icon, built from simple line forms inside circular outlined containers and placed in the open composition.

Use the supplied palette as the project authority. The 8-digit values include full opacity; use the corresponding 6-digit values in applications that do not accept alpha notation.

### Color rules

- Soft linen is the dominant background and breathing-space color.
- Evergreen is the primary text and line color; do not use pure black.
- Dark teal establishes authority and large structural fields.
- Yellow-green marks ecological function, resilience, and constructive action.
- Cayenne red is reserved for climate hazards, adoption barriers, risks, or urgent gaps.
- Keep the palette flat and high-contrast; do not introduce additional colors without approval.
- Use color with labels or shape differences so meaning is not color-dependent.

### Suggested color proportion

- Soft linen: 45–55%
- Evergreen and dark teal: 25–35%
- Yellow-green: 10–15%
- Cayenne red: 3–8%

## Typography system

- Use a bold, highly legible sans-serif with a strong editorial display weight.
- Titles should be large, compact, and left-aligned or aligned to the dominant reading path.
- Body copy should be neutral, open, and readable at physical rollup distance.
- Use evergreen or white text against dark teal fields; use evergreen text against soft linen.
- Keep final text outside generated artwork and add it later in Canva.

| Role | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Main title | Heavy sans-serif, large, short question or finding |
| Section heading | Bold sans-serif, sentence case or compact title case |
| Key term | Bold or colored emphasis, never excessive all-caps |
| Body text | Regular sans-serif, short paragraphs, high line spacing |
| Callout label | Bold heading plus concise explanation in a linen box |
| Source note | Small but readable, evergreen or dark teal |

## Information and diagram rules

- Make the source-specific finding visible in the title, subhead, or first visual read.
- Use the NbS–EbA relationship as a clear umbrella/branch or nested-system diagram, integrated into the continuous visual field.
- Show climate-adaptation mechanisms as a causal chain, not as a list of benefits.
- Show enabling conditions as a connected implementation system:
  `knowledge → policy integration → capable administration → budget and finance → local implementation → monitoring and learning`.
- Use cayenne red for the adoption gap or barrier side and yellow-green for the enabling pathway.
- Keep evidence-bearing labels editable in Canva.
- Use icons only when their meaning is explicit and source-supported.

## Accessibility and production

- Maintain strong contrast between text and background.
- Do not rely on color alone to distinguish risk, benefit, or pathway categories.
- Preserve readable type at the intended physical rollup size.
- Keep final titles, labels, numbers, citations, and source notes editable.
- Generated illustration assets must be text-free unless a non-final placeholder explicitly requires otherwise.
- Preserve sufficient negative space around all callouts and source notes.
