# Roll-up 3 — Generation and layout handoff

## Objective

Create a **text-free composition prototype** for a 60 × 160 cm vertical roll-up. The first message is spatial comparison: one changing climate produces different regional pressures across Thailand. A pale Thailand silhouette is a geographic watermark, while six opaque regional callout cards carry the content.

## Main instruction

Design a tall scientific-editorial climate infographic for Thailand. Reserve a calm header with a clear title area and a small status-key area. Below it, create a narrow spatial-framing card with room for one short statement about terrain, water systems, urban form and coastlines.

In the central field, use a correctly proportioned, independently redrawn Thailand silhouette as a pale blue-grey background watermark. It must be recognisable but visually quiet: narrow, vertical, lightly outlined and mostly empty. Do not place text inside it. Build the actual reading hierarchy through six opaque, rounded regional callout cards: three on the left, three on the right. Connect each card with a short dotted leader line to a broad region or coastal area, never a province pin.

The six card themes must each contain a climate condition and a sectoral consequence: North—heavy rain and steep terrain / landslides threatening mountain ecosystems, settlements and infrastructure; Northeast—uneven rain and drought / water shortages straining rain-fed farming, rural incomes and food security; Central and Chao Phraya—intense rain and river overflow / flooding disrupting production, settlements, transport and water systems; Bangkok Metropolitan Area—widespread heat concentrated by dense urban form plus intense rain / disruption to health, work, travel and city services; Eastern Gulf coast—warmer sea / coral bleaching pressuring marine ecosystems and coastal tourism; South—intense monsoon rain and coastal change / disruption to production, tourism and coastal livelihoods.

Below the map field, reserve one full-width white rounded card for **same hazard, different ability to cope**, with three equal-weight comparison spaces: heat and outdoor travel/work; drought and reliable water/irrigation; coastal erosion/flooding and secure land rights/assistance. Below that, reserve two quieter side-by-side cards: Thai protected-area biodiversity, and global slow-onset sea-level rise / the physical limits of nature-based protection. Leave a restrained footer for sources and the bridge to the next panel.

All typography, labels, figures, status markers, citations and logos must be added later in controlled layout. The prototype must contain only blank, usable text areas.

## Image-generation prompt

```text
Create a text-free 60 × 160 cm vertical scientific editorial infographic composition about regional climate pressures in Thailand.

At the top, reserve a calm title and subtitle area, plus a very small blank status-key zone. Below it, place one slim rounded spatial-framing card. In the central 37–103 cm field, place a correctly proportioned, independently redrawn Thailand silhouette as a pale blue-grey background watermark, about half the banner width, with a minimal navy outline and low visual contrast. The map is only a spatial reference, not the hero image, not a choropleth, and not a risk ranking.

Make six opaque rounded callout cards the primary reading layer: three arranged on the left and three on the right of the Thailand watermark. Give each card a blank dark circular badge space, a small simplified flat editorial illustration, a short dotted leader line ending in a broad region or coast, and clean blank space for four controlled text tiers: climate condition, affected systems/visible consequence, one named/time-bound/numerical evidence cue, and status. The regional visual cues are: northern mountain slope with heavy rain; northeastern dry soil and broken rainfall band; central river and overflow; Bangkok city grid with heat halo and runoff; eastern Gulf warm-water ripple with a coral silhouette; southern peninsula with monsoon cloud and gradual coastal waterline. Do not put text, labels, data, province pins, or coloured risk zones on the map.

Below the map, add a full-width white rounded card for three “same hazard, different ability to cope” comparisons: sun + bus/hat for heat and outdoor travel/work; dry field + water source for drought and reliable water/irrigation; wave + home/document for coastal erosion/flooding and secure land rights/assistance. Below it, add two smaller pale cards: one with a protected-area biodiversity motif of forest canopy, bird and leaf; one with mangrove, reef and dune layers facing a gradual rising waterline and a small limit marker. Finish with a restrained footer strip with blank source and next-panel areas.

Style: flat public-interest editorial vector illustration, serious climate communication, simplified layered atmosphere-land-water forms, rounded cards, dark navy circular badges, dotted connectors, generous negative space, limited navy outlines. Palette: deep navy, cyan water/rain, amber heat/dryness, pale blue-grey map watermark, restrained DCCE green only in footer accents, pale grey and white cards. Professional and approachable, never childish.

Do not generate text, letters, numbers, logos, flags, watermarks, copied stock maps, data graphics, heat-map colouring, province borders, fake statistics, photorealism, glossy 3D, low-poly geometry, disaster-poster imagery, or generic sustainability graphics.
```

## Avoid

- a dark, oversized, opaque or text-bearing Thailand silhouette;
- a national risk score, heat map, choropleth or red/yellow/green regional ranking;
- province pins, exact boundaries or unsupported geographic precision;
- treating Bangkok as the only place with heat, or treating all Bangkok residents as equally exposed;
- attaching the ART06 protected-area scenario to a particular site, or attaching ART10 global sea-level framing to a Thai rate, date or location;
- distressed people, poverty stereotypes, disaster imagery or icons that make a region look like a tourism destination;
- text embedded in artwork, fake labels, fake statistics, watermarks, logos, flags or citations;
- photorealism, glossy 3D, low-poly/faceted geometry, heavy black outlines or decorative magenta.

## Production parameters

- **Canvas:** vertical 60 × 160 cm; keep critical content within 8–152 cm height and 4 cm side margins.
- **Reserved zones:** header 8–25 cm; spatial-framing card 25–37 cm; comparison field 37–103 cm; unequal-impacts card 103–125 cm; ecosystems/slow-onset cards 125–143 cm; footer 143–152 cm.
- **Output:** high-resolution, text-free composition; use a scalable/vector workflow wherever possible.
- **Priority:** callout-card hierarchy and usable text space first; watermark-map recognition second; decorative detail last.
- **Prototype review:** validate silhouette proportion, card spacing, leader-line precision, text contrast and the separation between regional comparison and cross-cutting modules before refining illustration detail.

## Controlled layout copy

Use the authoritative editable Thai master in `layout_text_th.md`. `layout_text_en.md` remains an English drafting reference only. Typeset all display copy in the layout application.

## First-prototype review gate

- [ ] The six regional cards, not the map watermark, are the first thing a viewer reads.
- [ ] Thailand is recognisable, quiet and free of text, province pins and regional risk colouring.
- [ ] All six callouts have broad, non-pinpoint leader-line targets and usable label space.
- [ ] Bangkok communicates concentrated urban exposure to a wider heat hazard.
- [ ] The unequal-impacts module is prominent but does not assign groups to fixed regions.
- [ ] ART06 is visibly scenario-based and Thailand-wide; ART10 is visibly global framing.
- [ ] Blank zones remain usable for controlled typography, status markers and sources.
- [ ] The composition remains coherent with the Roll-up 2 flat editorial visual system.
