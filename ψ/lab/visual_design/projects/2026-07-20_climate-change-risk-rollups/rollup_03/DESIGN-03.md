# DESIGN-03.md — Roll-up 3 Design System

## 1. Inherited visual system

Use `../rollup_02/DESIGN-02.md` as the visual-system baseline for this panel.

Retain without change:

- 60 × 160 cm vertical format, safe margins, 8-column grid, and generous negative space;
- flat public-interest editorial illustration informed by the Arctic reference;
- modern scientific typography, rounded information cards, dark circular badges, dotted connectors, and layered environmental bands;
- the established palette: navy, cyan, amber, restrained DCCE green, pale grey, white, and annotation grey;
- Thailand silhouette redraw and geographic-form validation using `00_project/reference_images/thailand-travels-map-2236148789.jpg`;
- controlled layout for all final text, statistics, labels, citations, and source notes;
- the prohibition on stock-map reuse, third-party image watermarks, low-poly rendering, photorealism, glossy 3D, and generic sustainability graphics.

Roll-up 3 should feel like the next page of the same publication, not a new visual system.

## 2. Panel-specific override

**Working title:** Where does climate risk concentrate in Thailand?

**Panel function:** Make risk spatial and concrete after Roll-up 2 has introduced changing climate conditions. Explain that risk concentration reflects the interaction of climate conditions, exposure, and vulnerability, rather than the intensity of a climate condition alone.

**Primary visual:** A recognisable but pale Thailand-map watermark behind six source-verified regional callouts. The callouts—not the map fill—carry the primary content. The map is an organising device, not a national risk ranking or heat map.

## 3. Content-to-visual rule

Every regional callout must carry four readable tiers: one dominant climate condition, the affected systems and visible consequence, one named/time-bound/numerical evidence cue, and a status marker. The separate “same hazard, different ability to cope” module explains the concrete options that make burdens unequal.

Do not use a hazard icon alone as a regional conclusion. Do not colour all regions as high/medium/low risk unless a comparable national method and dataset is verified.

## 4. Planned composition change from Roll-up 2

Roll-up 2 leads with a CID framework card. Roll-up 3 leads with a short spatial-risk framing card, then moves into the Thailand map.

Suggested vertical sequence:

1. Title and short message: risk differs across places.
2. Compact spatial-framing card: terrain, water systems, urban form and coastlines shape regional pressures.
3. Six concise regional callouts arranged around a pale Thailand-map watermark.
4. Two small cross-cutting layers: same hazard, different ability to cope; ecosystems and the limits of natural protection.
5. Footer bridge: `Next: how do risks cascade across systems?`

## 5. Map and callout rules

- Redraw Thailand independently; validate the northern mass, central narrowing, and southern peninsula against the geographic-form reference.
- Render Thailand as a pale blue-grey background watermark, approximately 28–32 cm wide and 8–12% visual weight; retain only enough navy outline to preserve recognition.
- Place three opaque callout cards on each side of the watermark. Use regional bands and leader lines; do not falsely imply provincial precision.
- Avoid regional icons that look like tourism symbols. Use simplified systems: farms, water, housing, transport, health, ecosystem, coast, or service infrastructure.
- Give each selected risk context a clear observed/projected/conditional marker where relevant.
- Preserve the map interior as readable negative space; keep all text cards outside it and never let the watermark reduce text contrast.
- Use six concise regional callouts: North, Northeast, Central / Chao Phraya, Bangkok Metropolitan Area, Eastern Gulf coast, and South.
- Frame Bangkok as an urban concentration of a wider heat hazard: do not imply heat occurs only in Bangkok.
- Keep the map as a spatial comparison; explain unequal burdens through three concrete coping-option comparisons in a dedicated lower module rather than attaching a fixed vulnerability score to each region.

## 6. Evidence gate

The evidence gate is satisfied by `evidence_map.md`: S02 is the common primary source for the six regional contexts. ART06 and ART10 support separate cross-cutting modules, not map zones. Preserve observed/projected and Thailand-specific/global boundaries in the layout.

## 7. Series coherence

- Use Roll-up 2’s climate-condition colours only as supporting cues.
- Reserve the risk accent for the concluding spatial-risk synthesis, not for decorative map zones.
- Maintain the same flat editorial line weight, card shape, badge style, and footer treatment so viewers recognise the five panels as one series.

## 8. Stage 3 production lock

- Generate only a text-free composition prototype. Add final typography, numbers, status markers, citations and sources in controlled layout.
- Use `generation_handoff.md` and the separate `layout_text_th.md` companion together.
- The Thai copy is the authoritative editable layout master. `layout_text_en.md` remains a drafting reference only; do not bake either language into generated artwork.
- Label ART06 as `Scenario-based · Thailand`; label ART10 as `Global framing` and do not attach it to a specific Thai location or rate.
