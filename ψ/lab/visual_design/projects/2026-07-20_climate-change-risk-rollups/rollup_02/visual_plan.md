# Roll-up 2 — Visual plan

## Selected concept — Thailand climate crossroads

**Concept:** A prominent CID framework card establishes what a climatic impact-driver is before any examples appear. A recognizable Thailand silhouette then becomes the shared physical setting for five illustrated CID examples: heat, heavy rainfall, drought/rainfall variability, coastal/ocean change, and compound extremes. A compact bottom band reconnects the drivers to the risk logic introduced in Roll-up 1.

**Why this concept:** It makes Thailand central without pretending to be a regional risk map. It lets viewers distinguish physical climate conditions from impacts, and holds all five drivers together as one system rather than as five disconnected icons.

**Reading order:** CID framework → climate context → Thailand and five changing drivers → CID-to-risk bridge → next panel.

## Evidence translation

| Module | Evidence | Main visual | Status marker | Do not imply |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme heat | E02 | high sun and heat contours around a neutral urban/field vignette | `OBSERVED · 2024` and `+1.1°C above 1991–2020 normal` | a national heatwave map or a specific city impact |

| Compound extremes | E07 | overlapping heat, rain, river, and coastal-water symbols | `INTERACTING CONDITIONS` | a full sectoral impact chain or a hotspot |

## Composition blueprint

### Canvas and negative space

- **Format:** 60 × 160 cm vertical roll-up.
- **Working zone:** 8–152 cm high; keep critical content at least 4 cm from side edges.
- **Grid:** 8 columns; text cards align to the grid while environmental bands overlap gently.
- **Text placement:** final titles, badges, labels, source notes, and caveats are typeset in the layout application, never generated in the illustration.

### A. Header — 8–22 cm

- Left: series marker `02` and title.
- Right edge: a cropped, concentric sun/atmosphere arc inspired by the Arctic reference. It signals warming context without becoming a globe graphic.
- Thin navy rule under title; no large image behind text.

### B. CID framework card — 22–45 cm

This is the panel’s first major content block—not a subtitle or caption.

- Heading: `What are climatic impact-drivers (CIDs)?`
- Definition: `Physical climate conditions — means, events, and extremes — that affect people and ecosystems.`
- Three connected illustrated capsules: `Climate means` / `Climate events` / `Climate extremes`.
- Closing logic: `In a harmful context, a CID is evaluated as a hazard.`
- Use a wide white rounded card, navy heading, minimal weather-condition symbols, and a single dotted or curved connector that moves down into Thailand’s five examples.
- Do not put a person, sector, damage, or disaster illustration inside the card. Its job is to distinguish the framework from impacts.

### C. Climate context band — 45–60 cm

- Broad pale-blue atmosphere and ocean band.
- Curved, broken cloud/wind ribbons cross the band and turn toward Thailand; they represent a tropical monsoon setting and variability, not a one-way causal pipeline.
- Small label space for `Monsoon variability and ENSO can shape when and where rain falls.`
- Do not place an ENSO map or numeric ENSO claim here.

### D. Main field — 60–125 cm

- A large, independently redrawn Thailand silhouette sits slightly below centre and spans approximately 60–65% of the panel width.
- Check its northern mass, central narrowing, and long southern peninsula against `00_project/reference_images/thailand-travels-map-2236148789.jpg`; remove all tourism-map content.
- Keep its interior pale and largely unlabelled. It is an organising form, not a climate-risk zoning map.
- Build a continuous environmental field through and around it: light atmosphere above, land/river/settlement texture across the middle, and water/coast near the peninsula.

**Module placement around the silhouette**

1. **Extreme heat:** upper-left of the silhouette. Use the strongest dark badge; this is the visual starting point.
2. **Heavy rainfall:** upper-right. Use cloudburst and short cyan rain strokes.
3. **Drought / rainfall variability:** mid-left. Link visually to the rainfall band through a broken dotted connector, not a direct arrow.
4. **Coastal / ocean change:** lower-right, adjacent to the sea edge; this geographic adjacency is appropriate to the driver but not a claim about a specific province.
5. **Compound extremes:** lower-centre, where the five-module field resolves. Use overlapping simple symbols, not an impact chain.

Each module uses a rounded label capsule, one-line explanation area, and a dotted connector that ends on the surrounding system field rather than a pinpoint inside Thailand. This prevents accidental regional attribution.

### E. CID-to-risk bridge — 125–143 cm

Use one narrow, high-contrast explanatory band:

`Changing climate conditions → can become hazards when they meet exposure and vulnerability → climate risk`

- Use three simple stages and neutral silhouettes for people, ecosystems, services, and infrastructure.
- Give the final `climate risk` node the restrained Roll-up 1 magenta accent only.
- No adaptation, maladaptation, cascade, or sector impact detail; those belong to other panels.

### F. Footer — 143–152 cm

- Left: short source note and observed/projected key.
- Right: DCCE identity area and `Next: where does risk concentrate in Thailand?`
- A quiet dark-green waterline or city/land silhouette can ground the panel.

## Style and reference-image use

| Reference | Use | Do not use |
|---|---|---|
| Arctic infographic | flat editorial scene, concentric sun, rounded labels, dark badges, dotted connectors, layered environmental bands | its text, statistics, Arctic subjects, exact composition, or coral palette |
| Thailand travel map | validate the redrawn country outline and peninsula proportion | watermark, tourist icons, region labels, locations, or stock illustration |
| iStock low-poly image | spacing and connector rhythm only | faceted/low-poly rendering or its globe composition |

**Visual language:** flat vector illustration, rounded forms, limited dark outlines, pale background, bold navy badges, cyan water/rain, amber heat/dryness, restrained green footer/action framing. Use magenta only for the small risk bridge.

## Print and accessibility rules

- Use direct labels plus distinctive shapes; colour alone must not carry a driver’s meaning.
- Keep dotted connectors and rain strokes heavy enough to survive print at 60 cm width.
- Do not put text over the Thailand silhouette where environmental detail reduces contrast.
- Keep the 2024 heat badge large enough for 1–2.5 m viewing; omit the optional SST number if it competes with hierarchy.
- Use a small legend in the footer: `Observed` / `Projected` / `Variable or interacting conditions`.

## Risks to control during production

- **Map risk:** a stylised Thailand outline may become distorted. Validate it against the geographic-form reference before finalising.
- **Causal risk:** wind ribbons, ENSO, or arrows could imply direct cause. Keep paths broad, broken, and explicitly contextual.
- **Category risk:** floods, crop loss, or illness may appear as driver labels. Keep modules on physical conditions only.
- **Framework risk:** the CID card can become a small caption. Give it its own full-width zone and let it appear before the examples.
- **Overload risk:** five modules can become a dashboard. Maintain one dominant Thailand field, one badge hierarchy, and generous white space.
- **Time-status risk:** distinguish observed 2024 heat from scenario-based projected rainfall and future sea-level information.
