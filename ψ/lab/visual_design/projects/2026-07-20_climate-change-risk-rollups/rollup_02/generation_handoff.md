# Roll-up 2 — Generation and layout handoff

## Objective

Create a **text-free composition prototype** for a 60 × 160 cm vertical roll-up. The first message must be the CID framework: climate impact-drivers are physical climate conditions, not impacts. Thailand’s five climate-driver examples follow only after that framework is established.

## Main instruction

Design a tall scientific-editorial climate infographic for Thailand. At the top, reserve a calm title zone. Directly below, create a large, unmistakable CID framework card with space for a heading, definition, three linked categories — climate means, climate events, climate extremes — and a small transition to a `hazard` chip. This framework card must be visually stronger than any individual weather icon.

Below it, create a thin climate-context band with atmosphere, ocean, monsoon clouds, and broad broken wind ribbons. Then build the central composition around a correctly proportioned, independently redrawn Thailand silhouette. Around this silhouette, arrange five clearly differentiated but non-regional climate-driver modules: extreme heat, heavy rainfall, drought/rainfall variability, coastal/ocean change, and compound extremes.

End with a narrow lower bridge that reserves room for the relationship from changing climate conditions to hazard to climate risk. Leave all titles, labels, badges, numbers, citations, and source notes blank for controlled layout.

## Image-generation prompt

```text
Create a text-free 60 × 160 cm vertical scientific editorial infographic composition about climatic impact-drivers in Thailand.

The visual hierarchy is essential. At the top is a quiet title area. Directly below it, place the dominant conceptual framework: a wide white rounded card with three linked visual capsules representing climate means, climate events, and climate extremes. This card must visibly lead into a small blank hazard chip, showing that physical climate conditions can become hazards in a harmful context. Do not include people, damage, disasters, or sector impacts inside this framework card.

Below the framework, create a slim tropical climate-context band: warm atmosphere, subtle concentric sun arc at the upper right, pale blue ocean, monsoon clouds, and broad broken curved wind ribbons. The ribbons are contextual, not direct causal arrows.

The main body is a large, independently redrawn Thailand silhouette with accurate north-to-south proportions and a long narrow southern peninsula. Use it only as shared geographic context, not as a regional risk map. Surround it with five separate flat editorial climate-driver vignettes: extreme heat with sun and heat contours; heavy rainfall with a dense cloud and short rain strokes; drought and rainfall variability with dry soil and a broken seasonal-rain cue; coastal and ocean change with warm-water ripples, wave, gradual waterline, and mangrove/coast cue; compound extremes with overlapping weather and water symbols. Use dotted connectors ending in the shared system field rather than pinpointing places inside Thailand.

At the bottom, reserve a narrow explanatory band for a future climate-condition-to-risk bridge and a quiet footer for sources. Use generous negative space.

Style: flat public-interest editorial vector illustration, inspired by a serious climate infographic; soft simplified shapes, layered atmosphere-land-water-settlement bands, rounded label-card spaces, dark circular badge spaces, limited dark navy outlines, dotted connectors, pale grey background. Palette: deep navy, cyan water/rain, amber for heat/dryness, pale derived tints, restrained DCCE green in footer accents only; reserve magenta only for the future risk bridge. Professional, scientific, approachable, not childish.

Do not generate text, letters, numbers, logos, flags, watermarks, maps with labels, charts, or data values. Leave clean blank areas for all controlled typography.
```

## Avoid

- distorted Thailand outline or shortened southern peninsula;
- the watermarked Thailand travel-map image, tourist icons, regional labels, or any copied stock artwork;
- a full IPCC risk diagram or an impact-chain diagram;
- treating flood damage, crop loss, illness, or infrastructure failure as climate-driver icons;
- a uniform flood, drought, or heat overlay across all of Thailand;
- a single arrow from ENSO, global warming, or the ocean to a Thai outcome;
- photorealism, glossy 3D, low-poly/faceted geometry, generic green sustainability poster, disaster-poster imagery, or heavy black outlines;
- crowded dashboard cards, tiny text, fake statistics, unreadable labels, or text baked into the image;
- magenta used as a decorative colour.

## Production parameters

- **Canvas:** vertical 60 × 160 cm; keep critical content within 8–152 cm height and 4 cm side margins.
- **Output:** high-resolution text-free illustration; use a scalable/vector workflow where possible.
- **Illustration priority:** CID framework card first; Thailand field second; five driver modules third; risk bridge last.
- **Reserved zones:** title 8–22 cm; CID card 22–45 cm; climate context 45–60 cm; main illustration 60–125 cm; risk bridge 125–143 cm; footer 143–152 cm.
- **Iteration goal:** validate hierarchy, Thailand geometry, negative space, and the distinction between physical conditions and impacts. Do not polish decorative detail until those pass review.

## Controlled layout copy — English draft

Thai production copy is maintained separately in `rollup_2_v01_thai_text.md`. Both files are Stage 3 handoff deliverables; the English below remains a source-traceable drafting reference.

### Header

- `02`
- `What climate hazards are changing?`
- `Climate impact-drivers are physical climate conditions that shape risk in Thailand.`

### CID framework card

- `What are climatic impact-drivers (CIDs)?`
- `Physical climate conditions — means, events, and extremes — that affect people and ecosystems.`
- `Climate means` · `Climate events` · `Climate extremes`
- `In a harmful context, a CID is evaluated as a hazard.`

### Thailand modules

- `Extreme heat` — `Observed · 2024: +1.1°C above the 1991–2020 normal`
- `Heavy rainfall` — `Projected: more intense rainfall in many places`
- `Drought and rainfall variability` — `Rain can become more uneven across seasons and places`
- `Coastal and ocean change` — `Warming waters and sea-level change vary across the coast`
- `Compound extremes` — `Climate conditions can interact`

### Context and bridge

- `Monsoon variability and ENSO can shape when and where rain falls.`
- `Changing climate conditions → can become hazards when they meet exposure and vulnerability → climate risk`
- `Next: where does risk concentrate in Thailand?`

### Source note

`Sources: IPCC AR6 WGI/WGII risk and climatic impact-driver framing; Thailand 5NC/2BTR Adaptation Chapter draft. Observed and projected information are distinguished.`

## First-prototype review gate

- [ ] The CID framework card is visibly the first major explanatory module.
- [ ] The viewer can tell that CIDs are physical conditions, not impacts.
- [ ] Thailand is recognizable and not used as a risk-zoning map.
- [ ] All five driver modules are visually distinct without implying regional hotspots.
- [ ] No causal arrow overstates ENSO, monsoon, or global-warming relationships.
- [ ] Blank zones remain usable for controlled text, badges, and sources.
- [ ] The composition remains coherent with the flat editorial reference style.
