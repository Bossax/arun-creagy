# Roll-up 2 — Content plan

**Status:** Stages 1–3 complete — ready for first composition prototype

## Role in the series

Move from the risk framework introduced in Roll-up 1 to the physical climate conditions that create or intensify risk in Thailand.

## Working title

What climate hazards are changing?

## Draft primary message

Climate impact-drivers (CIDs) are physical climate conditions, not impacts. As the climate warms, CIDs such as extreme heat, heavy rainfall, drought, sea-level rise, and compound extremes change; they become hazards when they cause harm in a specific exposure and vulnerability context.

## Audience takeaway

Climate change is experienced through changing physical conditions, not through one uniform hazard; these conditions become the inputs to the risk framework shown in Roll-up 1.

## Message hierarchy

1. **CID framework:** physical climate conditions are not impacts by themselves.
2. **Thailand examples:** five changing CIDs make the framework concrete.
3. **Risk bridge:** a CID becomes a hazard in an exposed and vulnerable context.

## Evidence boundary for review

- **S03 — IPCC AR6 WGI/WGII extract:** definitions and framing of climatic impact-drivers; global physical-climate context.
- **S01 — TOR 5.5 article package:** Thailand-specific, source-verified examples of changing climate conditions and related risk contexts.
- **S02 — Thailand BTR/5NC adaptation chapter draft:** national climate context and any defensible observed hazard statements.

Use S03 for the scientific framework. Use S01/S02 only after claim-level verification for Thailand-specific statements, maps, trends, or measurements.
