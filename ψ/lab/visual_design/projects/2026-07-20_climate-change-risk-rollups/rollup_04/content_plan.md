# Roll-up 4 — Content plan

**Status:** Stage 1 — evidence and narrative plan complete

## Role in the series

Move from Roll-up 3’s spatial comparison to the cross-sector consequences that unfold after a climate pressure is realised. Show that a direct impact is not the final consequence.

## Working title

How do climate risks cascade across systems?

## Primary message

Impact-chain analysis links a climate hazard to direct and cascading impacts, then tests how exposure, sensitivity and adaptive-capacity gaps shape the resulting risk. The method reveals where action can interrupt or reduce risk.

## Audience takeaway

Flooding, drought and coastal change do not remain inside one sector. The same hazard produces different risk because people, assets and services are exposed differently and have unequal capacity to cope and recover.

## Message hierarchy

1. **Impact-chain analysis is a method:** identify the hazard, direct impact and at least two cascading impacts; then map exposure, sensitivity and adaptive-capacity gaps.
2. **Three evidence-anchored illustrative chains:** urban infrastructure and livelihoods; agriculture, water and income; coast, ecosystems and tourism.
3. **Risk determinants explain unequal outcomes:** exposure, sensitivity and adaptive capacity shape how far disruption travels and how quickly recovery is possible.
4. **Decision implication:** use the whole chain to identify the risk and the points where adaptation can break or weaken it.

## Core visual grammar

Use one consistent reading pattern in every pathway:

`Climate hazard → direct impact → cascading impact`

Place a concise outer annotation around every lane:

`Exposure → sensitivity / adaptive-capacity gap → resulting risk`

The final bubble must name a system consequence, not a distant or unsupported outcome. Cross-links between rows should be sparse and only show the shared role of water, services, livelihoods and ecosystems.

## Risk amplifiers — compact explanatory layer

Do not build a second full risk diagram. Use a small side or lower rail to define the three analytical conditions that determine the scale and duration of a cascade:

- exposure: people, assets and services lie in the path of disruption;
- sensitivity: systems depend on reliable water, transport, energy or ecosystem services;
- adaptive-capacity gap: people or institutions lack savings, information, public services, backup systems or alternative livelihoods.

These are generic risk determinants. Do not attach a fixed vulnerability score to a place or group.

## Evidence boundary

- **S04 — Climate Impact Chains Guidebook:** primary method and the structure of direct and cascading impacts.
- **S02 — Thailand BTR/5NC adaptation chapter:** Thailand’s observed and potential cross-sector context for water, agriculture, infrastructure, health, ecosystems, tourism and settlements.
- **S03 — IPCC AR6 extract:** cascading risks can compound across sectors and regions; use for the panel’s conceptual bridge only.

## Do not show

- a national cascade map, ranking or quantified economic-loss forecast;
- a single universal chain that claims every hazard produces the same outcome;
- province-level impacts, unless a specific later source is selected;
- a complete impact-chain assessment with indicators; this panel teaches the logic, not a local assessment result;
- adaptation solutions in detail; this panel identifies analytical entry points only. Roll-up 5 carries the action pathway.
