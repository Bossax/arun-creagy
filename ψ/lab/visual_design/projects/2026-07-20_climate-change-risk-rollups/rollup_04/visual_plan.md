# Roll-up 4 — Visual plan

## Selected concept — Impact chain as scientific diagram

**Concept:** A large reconstructed impact-chain diagram occupies the centre of the roll-up, in the same visual role that the IPCC response-risk diagram occupies in Roll-up 1. It adopts the supplied IPCC causal-loop reference’s minimal grammar: a small number of soft circular bubbles, compact category dots, generous white space and uncluttered arrows. Three stacked pathways show how a climate hazard produces a direct impact and then a cascading impact in a connected system. The primary cascade arrows are deliberately thick and near-black; thin grey lines remain only for secondary annotation.

**Primary message:** An impact chain is an analytical method: it traces a climate hazard through direct and cascading impacts, then explains how exposure, sensitivity and adaptive-capacity gaps produce the resulting risk.

**Why this concept:** It makes the impact-chain method visible at a glance. Reusing Roll-up 1’s scientific composition signals that this panel extends the same risk logic rather than introducing an unrelated illustration style.

**Reading order:** title → what impact-chain analysis does → three evidence-anchored cascade lanes with their mechanisms → determinant rail → connected-response bridge.

## Composition blueprint

### Canvas and hierarchy

- **Format:** 60 × 160 cm vertical roll-up; main working zone 8–152 cm high; minimum 4 cm side margins.
- **Grid:** Roll-up 1’s 8-column grid, 6–7% outer margin, 2–3% horizontal gutter and 3–5% vertical section spacing.
- **Background:** pale climate light grey `#F1F2F3`; white cards and pale-grey interaction field create the scientific reading surface.
- **Hierarchy:** the three-lane impact-chain diagram is dominant. Context strip, callouts and amplifier rail must serve it.
- **Text:** all titles, labels, source notes and status markers are controlled layout elements, never generated inside artwork.

### Zone A — Header / title — 8–22 cm

- Series marker: `04`.
- Title space: `How do climate risks cascade across systems?`
- Subtitle space: `A first climate impact can trigger disruption elsewhere.`
- Optional thin DCCE dark-green rule beneath the header.
- Keep the field clean: no scene illustration, large icon or network pattern.

### Zone B — Impact-chain context strip — 24–42 cm

- A compact four-move method strip, visually analogous to Roll-up 1’s AR5→AR6 context strip.
- First move: a soft-grey climate-hazard bubble with navy dot; second: a direct-impact bubble with cyan dot; third: a cascading-impact bubble with magenta dot. Link these with two short near-black arrows.
- Add a compact determinant label beneath or beside the sequence: `Exposure + sensitivity + adaptive capacity`.
- End with a small bounded label: `Resulting risk + entry points`.
- Reserve a two-line definition: `Impact-chain analysis maps cause-and-effect relationships and the vulnerability factors that shape them.`
- This strip teaches the method; it must not repeat the evidence in the three pathways below.

### Zone C — Main impact-chain diagram — 44–112 cm

- This is the panel’s dominant scientific anchor, centred on a white or very pale-grey field approximately 44–48 cm wide.
- Build three horizontally aligned lanes. Each lane has three same-size circular bubbles and two directional connectors:

`Climate hazard` → `Direct impact` → `Cascading impact`

- Use a shared very pale-grey interaction field behind the lanes. It signals common dependencies without adding a web of arrows.
- Give each lane a small numbered navy marker and a controlled label: `Illustrative cascade`.
- Add a low-detail flat editorial scene fragment at the outer edge or as a shallow background band in each lane, inspired by the Arctic reference’s layered-scene restraint. It must not enter a bubble or cross an arrow.

| Lane | Left bubble: climate hazard | Middle bubble: direct impact | Right bubble: cascading impact | External mechanism annotation | Supporting visual cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 Urban infrastructure and livelihoods | `High accumulated rain / tropical cyclone` | `Flooded roads; power outage` | `Transport and economic activity disrupted` | dense built areas and flood-prone routes; water management / backup-grid gap; service continuity and worker income at risk | rain cloud, road/water, transit/service silhouette |
| 02 Agriculture, water and income | `Drought` | `Reduced irrigation water; crop damage` | `Farmer income loss; livelihood and food pressure` | farms and farmers exposed; inadequate irrigation, sensitive crops, limited savings/alternative income; income and food security at risk | dry field, water/crop silhouette |
| 03 Coast, ecosystems and tourism | `Storms, sea-level variability / warmer sea` | `Tourism assets damaged; coral bleaching pressure` | `Operations and visitor demand decline; local income affected` | coastal assets and ecosystem-dependent services exposed; degraded ecosystems and tourism dependence; income/service continuity at risk | wave/sea, reef/coast, tourism-service silhouette |

**Supporting scene fragments**

- **Urban lane:** pale rain band, simplified road edge and one quiet service/transport silhouette.
- **Agriculture lane:** muted dry-field band, water channel or vessel cue and a simple crop silhouette.
- **Coastal lane:** pale cyan waterline, one reef/mangrove/coastal edge form and a quiet tourism-service cue.
- Use only two or three flat layers per fragment, with no text, data, people in distress or landmark-specific skyline. Keep each fragment at roughly 10–15% visual weight of its lane.

**Bubble treatment**

- Diameter: approximately 10–11 cm each at final print size. Keep three equal bubbles aligned in each lane, with ample white space between rows.
- Fill: white or soft grey `#F1F2F3`; outline: neutral grey, 1.0–1.25 mm; use only a very restrained soft shadow.
- Left bubbles: navy `#17375E` category dot and `climate hazard` label.
- Middle bubbles: cyan `#00A6CF` category dot and `direct impact` label.
- Right bubbles: magenta `#8A1B61` category dot and `cascading impact` label.
- Put the small category label at the top of each bubble, with the plain-language chain statement centered beneath. Do not make the bubble itself a large colour block.
- Primary connectors: near-black `#202020`, 1.5–2.0 mm at final print size, with calm curved paths and clear arrowheads. Arrows run left-to-right only within each lane.
- Annotation connectors: grey `#8A8A8A`, no heavier than 0.75–1.0 mm, with white circular endpoints. They must visibly recede behind the primary cascade arrows.
- Do not use a fourth node, a place label, a number or a forecast badge.

**Cross-links**

- Default: no cross-links. The first composition review should test the three direct chains alone.
- If one shared dependency is essential, use one pale-grey dotted link, 0.75–1.0 mm, with no more than a two-word label.
- A cross-link never terminates in a bubble label or crosses a main directional connector.

### Zone D — Translation callouts — integrated around Zone C

Use a small reading key plus three lane-specific external annotation groups. Each group uses thin grey connectors and white endpoints, and contains:

1. `Exposure` — the people, assets or services in the path of disruption.
2. `Sensitivity / capacity gap` — the dependency or limitation that amplifies the impact or slows recovery.
3. `Resulting risk` — the risk to system continuity, livelihoods or wellbeing.

Give every group source-controlled, pathway-specific wording. Do not add a fourth causal node or a second chain inside a callout.

### Zone E — Amplifiers and response bridge — 114–143 cm

**E1. Determinant rail**

- White or pale-grey compact panel headed: `What makes a cascade travel further?`
- Three concise analytical factors, each with a small amber `#F2A000` chip:
  - `Exposure: people, assets and services lie in the path of disruption`
  - `Sensitivity: systems rely on water, transport, energy or ecosystem services`
  - `Adaptive-capacity gap: backup systems, savings, public services or livelihood alternatives are limited`
- Use neutral systems icons, not demographic stereotypes or a Thailand location marker.

**E2. Connected-response bridge**

- A small linked-node strip below or beside the rail.
- Reserve DCCE dark green `#165A3C` for this controlled line only: `Connected risks need connected responses.`
- This is a bridge to Roll-up 5, not an adaptation solution diagram.

### Zone F — Footer / sources — 145–152 cm

- Source note space: `Sources: Climate Impact Chains Guidebook; Thailand BTR/5NC adaptation chapter; IPCC AR6 cascading-risk framing.`
- Small boundary note: `Illustrative impact chains — not a local forecast or risk ranking.`
- DCCE identity area at right, if supplied.

## Evidence-to-visual controls

| Visual element | What it may communicate | What it must not imply |
|---|---|---|
| Three cascade lanes | One disruption can create effects in connected systems | every event follows the same pathway or outcome |
| Directional arrows | the selected illustrative sequence | certainty, a complete causal model or a forecast |
| Optional cross-link | one selected shared dependency | a full national systems map |
| Amplifier rail | exposure and limited capacity extend disruption | a fixed vulnerability score or a stereotype tied to people or places |
| Response bridge | cascades require connected responses | the detailed adaptation plan, which belongs to Roll-up 5 |

## Print and accessibility rules

- Keep every main node and its label readable from 1–2.5 m; reduce the context strip before reducing node scale.
- Pair category colour with position, node label and icon; colour alone must not carry meaning.
- Set primary cascade arrows at 1.5–2.0 mm and bubble outlines at 1.0–1.25 mm at final print size. Secondary links remain at 0.75–1.0 mm so hierarchy survives at 60 cm width.
- Preserve white space around the scientific diagram; do not place body text over nodes or inside the shared interaction field.
- Keep supporting scene fragments low-detail and low-contrast; they may establish a system context but must never read before the circle bubbles and thick arrows.
- Keep the total final display copy around 250–330 words, excluding source microcopy. If space is constrained, remove cross-links and simplify scene fragments before reducing the method strip or the pathway mechanisms.

## Stage 2 decision

Proceed with a **Roll-up 1-style central scientific diagram containing three illustrative cascade lanes**, supported by four translation callouts and a compact amplifier/response bridge. Stage 3 should create the text-free generation/layout handoff and Thai controlled-layout copy.
