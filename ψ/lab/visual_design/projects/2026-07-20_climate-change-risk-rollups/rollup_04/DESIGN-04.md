# DESIGN-04.md — Roll-up 4 Design System

## 1. Purpose

This design system governs Roll-up 4 of the five-panel climate-change-risk series.

**Roll-up 4 title:** How do climate risks cascade across systems?

**Panel function:** Translate impact-chain analysis into a clear scientific visual: a climate hazard produces a direct impact, which can then cascade through connected systems. It also makes exposure, sensitivity and adaptive-capacity gaps visible. This is a teaching diagram, not a national impact assessment or forecast.

**Evidence boundary:**

- S04 — Climate Impact Chains Guidebook: impact-chain method and illustrative sequences.
- S02 — Thailand BTR/5NC adaptation chapter: Thailand’s cross-sector context.
- S03 — IPCC AR6 extract: cascades and compounding across systems and regions.

Roll-up 4 must closely match Roll-up 1’s visual authority and composition logic. It replaces Roll-up 1’s IPCC response-risk diagram with a reconstructed impact-chain diagram, while retaining the same diagram-led hierarchy.

**Style reference:** `img/Casual loop diagram CID WGII Chapter16.png` is a visual-language reference only. Borrow its generous white space, soft-grey driver bubbles, compact coloured category dots and uncluttered curved cascade arrows. Do not reproduce its labels, relationships or dense whole-system network.

**Supporting-illustration reference:** `../00_project/reference_images/Infographic_Climate-threat-to-the-Arctic-1024x725-1-1024x700.png` establishes the permitted scene language: low-detail flat editorial fragments, layered land/water/sky bands, sparse silhouettes and a limited palette. Translate this into Thai-relevant cues; do not reproduce the Arctic composition, polar subjects, copy, figures or claims.

## 2. Core style statement

Create a 60 × 160 cm vertical scientific-editorial roll-up centered on a large impact-chain diagram. It should feel like the next IPCC-style scientific panel after Roll-up 1, expressed with the supplied IPCC causal-loop reference’s minimal visual language: pale and quiet background, a small set of soft circular bubbles, compact coloured category dots and strong, uncluttered cascade arrows. Primary connectors are deliberately thicker than Roll-up 1’s annotation lines so the chain reads immediately at roll-up scale.

The roll-up should feel:

- scientific and evidence-led;
- contemporary and clear;
- institutional but not bureaucratic;
- readable from exhibition distance;
- diagram-led rather than illustration-led;
- visibly related to Roll-up 1.

Avoid:

- generic climate-campaign styling;
- a decorative network or spiderweb of arrows;
- rectangular process cards, filled colour blocks or a dashboard treatment inside the main diagram;
- detailed scenic illustration that competes with the bubbles, enters a bubble, or crosses a primary arrow;
- a dashboard of independent cards;
- a map, regional ranking or Thailand place labels;
- dramatic disaster scenes, photorealism, glossy 3D or generic sustainability graphics;
- a linear “cause = inevitable outcome” claim.

## 3. Narrative logic

**Primary message:** A climate hazard can trigger direct impacts that then disrupt connected systems, livelihoods and services.

**Reading sequence:**

1. Title question: how do climate risks cascade across systems?
2. Method strip: identify the hazard, trace direct and cascading impacts, map risk determinants, then identify the resulting risk and entry points.
3. Main impact-chain diagram: climate hazard → direct impact → cascading impact.
4. Three evidence-anchored pathways: urban infrastructure/livelihoods; agriculture/water/income; coast/ecosystems/tourism.
5. Determinant rail: exposure, sensitivity and adaptive-capacity gaps explain why a cascade travels further or lasts longer.
6. Footer takeaway: connected risks need connected responses.

The three pathways are selected teaching examples. They do not represent a complete Thailand impact assessment.

## 4. Composition system

### 4.1 Canvas and grid

- **Format:** 60 cm wide × 160 cm high vertical roll-up.
- **Safe margin:** minimum 4 cm from left/right edges; keep critical content clear of the top and bottom 8–10 cm.
- **Working zone:** approximately 8–152 cm high.
- **Grid:** use Roll-up 1’s 8-column grid, 6–7% outer margin, 2–3% horizontal gutter and 3–5% vertical section spacing.

### 4.2 Recommended vertical zones

### Zone A — Header / title

**Approximate height:** 12–16 cm.

- Roll-up marker `04`.
- Title: `How do climate risks cascade across systems?`
- Short subtitle: `A first climate impact can trigger disruption elsewhere.`

### Zone B — Impact-chain context strip

**Approximate height:** 18–22 cm.

- One compact, pale scientific strip that introduces the method before the examples.
- Use four short moves: `identify hazard` → `trace direct and cascading impacts` → `map exposure, sensitivity and capacity gaps` → `define risk and entry points`.
- Use three small circles and two short heavy arrows only for the first causal sequence; use concise text labels for the determinant and risk-analysis moves.
- State explicitly: an impact chain makes cause-and-effect relationships and vulnerability factors visible across connected systems.
- Treat it as Roll-up 1’s AR5→AR6 context strip equivalent: secondary context, not the main visual.

### Zone C — Main scientific anchor

**Approximate height:** 68–74 cm.

- A large reconstructed impact-chain diagram with three stacked pathways.
- Each pathway has three aligned circular bubbles: `Climate hazard` → `Direct impact` → `Cascading impact`.
- Use a shared pale-grey interaction field behind all three pathways to show that systems are connected.
- Preserve generous spacing between pathways; default to no cross-links. Add one subtle cross-link only if it clarifies a shared dependency without reducing the clarity of the three primary paths.
- Add one low-detail flat editorial scene cue per pathway, placed as a shallow edge fragment or quiet background band: rain/road/service for the urban lane; dry field/water/crop for the agriculture lane; wave/reef/coast/visitor-service for the coastal lane. Scene cues remain below the bubble-and-arrow hierarchy and leave all text zones open.
- Place three concise external mechanism annotations beside each lane: `exposure`, `sensitivity / capacity gap`, and `resulting risk`. These explain why the same hazard produces unequal consequences; they are analytical annotations, not extra causal nodes.

**Illustrative pathways:**

1. intense rain / river overflow → flooded roads and stressed services → disrupted movement, work and economic activity;
2. drought, heat and irregular rain → reduced water and disrupted production → pressure on farm income, supply and food security;
3. storms, coastal water and warmer sea → ecosystem and coastal-asset pressure → disrupted tourism operations and local income.

### Zone D — Translation callouts

**Approximate height:** integrated around Zone C.

Use one compact reading key outside the main diagram, plus the lane-specific external mechanism annotations:

- **Direct impact:** the immediate effect of a climate hazard on a system.
- **Cascading impact:** the next consequence triggered in a connected system.
- **Exposure:** people, assets and services in the path of disruption.
- **Sensitivity / capacity gap:** conditions that amplify disruption or delay recovery.
- **Resulting risk:** the consequence for services, livelihoods or wellbeing that the chain makes visible.

Use thin grey connectors and white circular endpoints. These annotations explain the mechanism; they do not add a fourth causal node.

### Zone E — Amplifiers and system bridge

**Approximate height:** 22–28 cm.

- Compact determinant rail: `What makes a cascade travel further?`
  - **Exposure:** people, assets and services lie in the path of disruption;
  - **Sensitivity:** systems rely on water, transport, energy or ecosystem services;
  - **Adaptive-capacity gap:** savings, information, public services, backup systems or livelihood alternatives are limited.
- Small linked-node bridge: `Connected risks need connected responses.`
- Do not introduce detailed adaptation actions; Roll-up 5 carries the action pathway.

### Zone F — Footer / sources

**Approximate height:** 8–10 cm.

- Source note: S04 impact-chain guidebook; Thailand BTR/5NC adaptation chapter; IPCC AR6 cascading-risk framing.
- `Illustrative impact chains — not a local forecast or risk ranking.`
- DCCE identity area, if used later.

## 5. Scientific diagram rules

- The main impact-chain diagram is the panel’s authority and must dominate the composition.
- Keep `climate hazard`, `direct impact`, and `cascading impact` visibly distinct; do not collapse them into a single generic arrow.
- Use three large circular bubbles per pathway, rather than rectangular cards. Each bubble carries a compact category label, a small coloured dot and one short plain-language statement.
- Use arrows only along the selected illustrative pathways. Their purpose is sequence, not certainty or universality.
- Main cascade arrows: black or near-black, 1.5–2.0 mm at final print size, with clean tapered arrowheads and a shallow, calm curve where needed. These arrows must be visually heavier than every annotation connector.
- Bubble outlines: 1.0–1.25 mm at final print size; fill white or very light grey with a restrained soft shadow. Use colour as a dot and label accent, not as a large filled node.
- Keep cross-links subtle, sparse and labelled only when necessary; avoid a dense network. Cross-links use pale grey, 0.75–1.0 mm, and must never compete with a primary cascade arrow.
- Do not add a fourth downstream step, a provincial marker, a numerical forecast or a national map.
- Keep all text outside dense diagram fields and add final text through controlled layout.

## 6. Illustration and icon style

Use a restrained flat editorial illustration language outside and at the edges of the scientific diagram, informed by the supplied Arctic reference. Within the scientific diagram, retain the supplied IPCC reference’s minimal bubble-and-arrow language:

- white callout nodes and thin grey connector rhythm;
- low-detail layered silhouette cues for rain, road, water, crop, coast, ecosystem, transport and services; keep them secondary to the bubbles;
- simplified landscape, waterline and infrastructure bands in pale cyan, grey and muted navy; use no more than two or three layers per scene fragment;
- low-opacity geometric texture only where it does not compete with the chain.

Do not use low-poly as the main scientific diagram, a globe composition, stock dummy content, polar subjects, decorative animals, or disaster-poster imagery. Do not use scene fragments to imply place-specific evidence or a complete causal pathway.

## 7. Colour system

Use Roll-up 1’s category logic closely:

| Concept | Working color | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Climate hazard | `#17375E` | left bubble, label chip and small hazard cue |
| Direct impact | `#00A6CF` | middle bubble and direct-impact label |
| Amplifier / limited capacity | `#F2A000` | lower amplifier rail and capacity callout only |
| Cascading impact / risk synthesis | `#8A1B61` | right bubble and system-consequence synthesis only |
| Institutional / response bridge | `#165A3C` | footer line and `connected responses` bridge only |
| Interaction field | `#E9E9E9` | shared background field behind the three chains |
| Canvas / diagram card | `#F1F2F3` / `#FFFFFF` | pale canvas and white diagram/callout fields |
| Connector / annotation | `#8A8A8A` | thin grey lines, secondary labels and source notes |

Use the category colours principally as one small dot beside each bubble label and a restrained text accent. Main cascade arrows remain near-black; light-grey lines are reserved for climate-driver context, callouts and optional cross-links.

Restrictions:

- Do not use magenta decoratively; reserve it for cascading impacts and risk synthesis.
- Do not use orange as generic warning; use it only for capacity/amplifier conditions.
- Do not use green inside the chains; reserve it for the final institutional/response bridge.
- Do not rely on colour alone; pair every category with a direct label and position.

## 8. Typography and content density

- Use the same modern scientific sans-serif hierarchy as Roll-up 1.
- Main title: readable from about 2 m; subtitle: one or two short lines.
- Bubble labels: direct and compact; pathway explanations: one short line per bubble.
- External mechanism annotations: one short sentence each, specific to the selected pathway.
- Source note in the footer only.
- Keep visible display copy around 250–330 words in the final language, excluding source microcopy. Preserve the full method strip and the pathway mechanisms before reducing type size; simplify supporting illustrations first.

## 9. Evidence and layout controls

- S04 is the primary source for impact-chain logic and illustrative sequences.
- S02 provides Thailand’s cross-sector context but does not turn the chains into local forecasts.
- S03 supports cascading and compounding across systems and regions.
- Mark the diagram `Illustrative impact chains` in controlled layout.
- Do not place Thailand names, place markers, casualty figures, loss estimates or rankings inside the three pathways.

## 10. Quality checklist

### Scientific structure

- [ ] The main impact-chain diagram is the dominant visual anchor.
- [ ] The method strip explains that the analysis includes hazard, impacts, exposure, sensitivity, adaptive capacity, resulting risk and entry points.
- [ ] Each pathway clearly separates climate hazard, direct impact and cascading impact.
- [ ] Each pathway names its exposure, sensitivity/capacity gap and resulting risk outside the three bubbles.
- [ ] The three primary paths use large circular bubbles and thick near-black cascade arrows.
- [ ] Cross-links are sparse and do not form a spiderweb.
- [ ] The panel does not imply that every hazard creates the same chain.
- [ ] The chains are visibly illustrative, not a local forecast.

### Composition and style

- [ ] The layout reads top-to-bottom through the same zone logic as Roll-up 1.
- [ ] Category colours and pale scientific fields visibly echo Roll-up 1.
- [ ] Callouts use thin grey connectors and white circular endpoints, visibly lighter than primary cascade arrows.
- [ ] Supporting flat editorial scene cues remain quiet, text-free and outside the bubbles and primary arrows.
- [ ] Footer contains source and attribution space.

### Accessibility

- [ ] Text contrast is sufficient and labels do not rely on colour alone.
- [ ] Lines and endpoints survive print at roll-up scale.
- [ ] The main diagram remains legible from 1–2.5 m.
