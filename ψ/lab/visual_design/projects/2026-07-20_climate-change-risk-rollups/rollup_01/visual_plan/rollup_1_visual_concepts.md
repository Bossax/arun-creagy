# Concept 1 — IPCC diagram as museum object

## Dominant visual metaphor

The IPCC AR6 response-risk diagram is treated as the main “scientific exhibit.” The surrounding layout acts like a guided museum label system that helps non-specialists read the figure.

## Composition

Top:
Large title and subtitle.

Upper-middle:
A compact strip derived from `SRL-image-0.png`, showing the progression:
AR5 risk graphic → AR6 response risk and complexity.

Center:
Large, clean insertion/adaptation of `SRL-image-1.png`, occupying roughly 45–50% of the roll-up height.

Around the central figure:
Four to five callouts connected to the diagram edges:

- hazard;
- exposure;
- vulnerability;
- response;
- central risk.

Bottom:
Short response-risk/maladaptation note and source footer.

## Reading direction

Top to bottom:
question → IPCC evolution → main AR6 diagram → translated callouts → takeaway.

## Visualized evidence

- E01 risk as potential adverse consequences.
- E02 hazard/exposure/vulnerability interaction.
- E06 response risk.
- E07 maladaptation.
- E08 dynamic risk.

## Information modules

1. Title/subtitle.
2. IPCC context strip.
3. Main IPCC AR6 diagram.
4. Plain-language callout ring.
5. Response-risk takeaway.
6. Source footer.

## Style treatment

- Pale grey canvas.
- White scientific diagram fields.
- IPCC category colors preserved.
- Thin grey connector lines and white circular endpoints.
- Minimal low-poly icons outside the diagram only.
- DCCE green as footer/series marker.

## Strengths

- Highest scientific fidelity.
- Directly satisfies the request to insert the IPCC diagrams.
- Makes the original science visually authoritative.
- Easy to trace source and adaptation.
- Good fit for a policy/workshop audience.

## Risks

- Could feel too much like a report figure if the surrounding callouts are too plain.
- The upper context strip may be small on a narrow roll-up.
- Requires careful scaling so IPCC diagram labels remain readable.

## Scientific cautions

- Do not crop away labels that make the IPCC diagram interpretable.
- Do not redraw the category positions incorrectly.
- Do not over-decorate inside the figure.

# Concept 2 — Risk system lens

## Dominant visual metaphor

The IPCC diagram is placed inside a large circular “lens” or magnifier, suggesting that Roll-up 1 gives audiences the lens for reading climate risk.

## Composition

Top:
Title and short statement.

Center:
Large circular frame containing the AR6 response-risk diagram from `SRL-image-1.png`.

Around the lens:
Low-poly climate icons and callouts point into the diagram:

- hazard;
- exposure;
- vulnerability;
- response.

Left or upper strip:
Small two-stage AR5 → AR6 context marker from `SRL-image-0.png`.

Bottom:
Dynamic-risk and response-risk takeaway.

## Reading direction

Center-out:
main IPCC risk lens → component callouts → response-risk implication.

## Visualized evidence

- E01 risk definition.
- E02 determinants.
- E03 climate impact-driver to hazard.
- E06 response risk.
- E08 dynamic risk.

## Information modules

1. Title.
2. Central lens with IPCC diagram.
3. Four concept callouts.
4. Mini AR5/AR6 evolution strip.
5. Bottom takeaway.

## Style treatment

- Stronger editorial presentation than Concept 1.
- Circular frame echoes the central risk node.
- Low-poly icon treatment is more visible.
- DCCE green appears as a small frame/rule.

## Strengths

- More visually engaging than a straight exhibit layout.
- Good metaphor: the framework is a lens.
- Can feel more like an infographic while preserving the IPCC diagram.

## Risks

- Circular frame may crop or distort the rectangular IPCC source figure.
- The “lens” metaphor may compete with the diagram’s own overlapping ellipse logic.
- Higher risk of looking decorative if the callouts become too prominent.

## Scientific cautions

- Do not crop the IPCC diagram so severely that relationships are lost.
- Do not imply the lens itself is part of the scientific framework.

# Concept 3 — Split translation: original figure + plain-language rebuild

## Dominant visual metaphor

The roll-up shows the original IPCC diagram on one side/section and a simplified plain-language translation beneath it.

## Composition

Top:
Title and subtitle.

Upper:
Original/adapted IPCC context from `SRL-image-0.png`.

Middle:
Original/adapted `SRL-image-1.png`.

Lower:
Simplified reconstructed version:
four colored components feeding into risk, with response-risk branch.

Footer:
Source note and caution that the simplified diagram is conceptual, not a fixed equation.

## Reading direction

Technical-to-plain:
IPCC diagram → simplified translation → takeaway.

## Visualized evidence

- E01 risk definition.
- E02 determinant interaction.
- E03 climate impact-driver to hazard.
- E06 response risk.
- E07 maladaptation.
- E09 cascading preview.

## Information modules

1. Title.
2. IPCC context strip.
3. Source diagram.
4. Simplified translation.
5. Takeaway/caution.

## Style treatment

- Uses IPCC colors in the simplified translation.
- More room for low-poly icons in the simplified lower section.
- Strong distinction between source figure and explanatory redesign.

## Strengths

- Very accessible because audiences see both original and translation.
- Gives control over final explanatory hierarchy.
- Useful if the original IPCC diagram is too dense.

## Risks

- Duplicates the same concept twice, which may waste limited roll-up space.
- The simplified diagram could be mistaken as replacing the IPCC figure.
- Higher risk of oversimplifying interaction into a linear formula.

## Scientific cautions

- Label the lower version as “plain-language guide” or “conceptual reading aid.”
- Avoid arrows implying fixed one-way causation.

# Concept recommendation

Recommended concept:
Concept 1 — IPCC diagram as museum object.

Reason:
It best satisfies the scientific and design constraints:

- keeps `SRL-image-1.png` dominant;
- uses `SRL-image-0.png` without overcrowding;
- preserves IPCC diagram authority;
- gives enough space for readable callouts;
- avoids overusing the low-poly stock style;
- fits a 60 × 160 cm roll-up better than a duplicated translation layout.

Concept 2 is a possible alternative if the user wants a more editorial/visual feel, but it carries more risk of interfering with the IPCC diagram structure.

Concept 3 should be avoided unless the original IPCC diagram proves unreadable at print scale.
