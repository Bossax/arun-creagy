# Generation Prompt — Roll-up 1

## Important scope note

Do not generate the final infographic text or redraw the IPCC diagrams inaccurately. The two IPCC reference diagrams should be inserted or carefully adapted in layout software. Use this prompt for generating only the surrounding visual system, background, callout styling, supporting icons, and composition prototype.

## Prompt

Create a vertical 60 × 160 cm scientific-editorial roll-up layout for Roll-up 1 of a climate change risk series.

Subject:
What is climate change risk?

Communication goal:
Explain that climate risk is more than hazard. In the IPCC AR6 framing, risk emerges from interactions among hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and response. Also show that key risks make the concept concrete and that maladaptation is a response that can create more risk.

Composition:
Use a clean top-to-bottom roll-up structure.

Top zone:
Leave clear space for the roll-up marker, title, and subtitle.

Upper context zone:
Reserve a wide rectangular area for a compact inserted/adapted IPCC AR5-to-AR6 risk framing strip.

Main middle zone:
Reserve the largest central area for the IPCC AR6 response-risk diagram. The diagram will show hazard, exposure, vulnerability, response, and central risk. Do not obscure this area. Keep it visually dominant.

Around the main diagram:
Create a system of thin grey connector lines, white circular endpoints, and small category-color chips for short explanatory callouts. The callouts should sit outside the main diagram area.

Lower zone:
Create a compact key-risk example module using five small geometric icon chips for:
coastal systems, ecosystems, infrastructure and services, health and cultural heritage, and food and water security.

Also create a prominent amber/orange maladaptation warning callout connected visually to the response area of the main diagram. It should signal that a response meant to reduce risk can create more risk if poorly designed.

Bottom zone:
Reserve a clean source/footer strip with restrained DCCE green framing.

Visual hierarchy:
1. Main IPCC AR6 response-risk diagram area.
2. Title/subtitle.
3. Maladaptation warning callout.
4. IPCC AR5-to-AR6 context strip.
5. Key-risk icon chips.
6. Cascading-risk bridge and source/footer.

Style:
Clean institutional climate-risk communication. Scientific editorial infographic. Pale grey background, white diagram surfaces, thin grey connectors, minimal shadows, geometric vector callouts, subtle low-poly supporting icons only outside the scientific diagrams.

Color:
Use IPCC-inspired category colors:
hazard navy, exposure cyan, vulnerability orange, risk magenta, response amber/brown. Use amber/orange for the maladaptation warning. Use restrained DCCE dark green only for footer/framing, not as a scientific category color.

Scientific constraints:
Preserve the IPCC category meanings. Do not imply risk is hazard alone. Do not imply all adaptation is maladaptation. Do not present key-risk examples as Thailand-specific. Do not introduce Thailand-specific hazard or vulnerability claims on this panel.

Text handling:
Do not render final text inside the generated artwork. Leave clear spaces for title, subtitle, callout labels, key-risk labels, maladaptation warning text, source notes, and DCCE identity to be added later in layout software.

Avoid:
Generic sustainability infographic style, decorative green campaign poster, glossy 3D globe, photorealistic disaster imagery, heavy black outlines, crowded dashboard, random climate icons, complex background illustration, and unsupported scientific claims.
