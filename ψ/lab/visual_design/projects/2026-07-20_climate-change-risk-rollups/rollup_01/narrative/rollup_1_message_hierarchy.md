# Level 1 — Main message

Climate risk is more than a climate hazard: in the IPCC AR6 framing, risk emerges from interactions among hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and response.

---

# Level 2 — Supporting messages

## 2.1 Risk means potential adverse consequences
Risk is about possible harm to human or ecological systems.

Evidence:
E01.

## 2.2 Hazard is only one determinant of risk
Climate impact-drivers become hazards when they can cause detrimental effects.

Evidence:
E02, E03.

## 2.3 Exposure defines what is in harm’s way
Exposure includes people, livelihoods, ecosystems, services, infrastructure, and assets.

Evidence:
E04.

## 2.4 Vulnerability shapes how severe harm may become
Vulnerability includes sensitivity and lack of capacity to cope and adapt.

Evidence:
E05.

## 2.5 Risk changes across space and time
Hazard, exposure, and vulnerability are dynamic and affected by climate, development, and decisions.

Evidence:
E08.

## 2.6 Response is part of the AR6 risk picture
Mitigation, adaptation, and sustainable development can lower risk, but failures, trade-offs, lock-in, and maladaptation can generate response risk.

Evidence:
E06, E07.

## 2.6a Maladaptation needs emphasis
Maladaptation is the clearest warning example of response risk: a response intended to reduce risk can instead increase, shift, or lock in future risk.

Evidence:
E07.

## 2.7 Risks can cascade
Climate-related events or impacts can trigger linked consequences across systems.

Evidence:
E09.

## 2.8 Key risks make climate risk concrete
Key risks are severe risks that merit societal attention. They can include risks to ecosystems, coastal socio-ecological systems, infrastructure and services, health and cultural heritage, and food and water security.

Evidence:
E10.
