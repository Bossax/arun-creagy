## Primary message
Climate risk is not a hazard alone. It is the potential for adverse consequences that emerges when climate hazards interact with exposure and vulnerability — and human responses can either reduce risk or create new risks.

## One-sentence takeaway
Climate risk is a dynamic system: hazards become risks when they meet exposed and vulnerable systems, and responses shape whether future risk falls or rises.

## Reading sequence

1. Open with the title question: what is climate change risk?
2. Show a compact IPCC context strip: AR5 risk framing → AR6 additions of response risk and complexity.
3. Move to the main AR6-style diagram: hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and response surrounding risk.
4. Use short callouts to translate each component into plain language.
5. Emphasize the central lesson: risk emerges from interaction, not from hazard alone.
6. Add a “key risks” module to show what climate risks look like concretely.
7. Add a prominent maladaptation callout: some responses meant to reduce risk can instead create more risk.
8. End with a small bridge: risk can cascade through connected systems, preparing the audience for later roll-ups.

## What the audience should remember

1. Hazard is only one part of risk.
2. Exposure and vulnerability determine who or what can be harmed.
3. Response is part of the AR6 risk picture.
4. Key risks are concrete severe risk domains, such as risks to ecosystems, infrastructure, health, food, and water.
5. Risk is dynamic, changing across space and time.
6. Responses matter because they can reduce risk or create new risk.
7. Maladaptation is the warning case: a response intended to help can increase or shift risk.

## What to avoid

- Do not show risk as only floods, heat, drought, or storms.
- Do not use a fixed mathematical equation as the main message.
- Do not imply all climate impact-drivers are automatically harmful.
- Do not imply adaptation always reduces risk.
- Do not include Thailand-specific evidence on Roll-up 1 unless the source scope changes.
- Do not introduce the full impact-chain method; keep cascading risk as a preview.
- Do not copy the low-poly stock image composition; use it only for icon/callout tone.
- Do not recolor the IPCC diagram categories in a way that changes meaning.
- Do not imply the key-risk examples are exhaustive or Thailand-specific.
