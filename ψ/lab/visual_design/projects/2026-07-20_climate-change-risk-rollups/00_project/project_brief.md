# Project Brief

## Working title
Overview of Climate Change Risk in Thailand

## Project type
Five connected 60 × 160 cm roll-up infographics.

## Core idea
Climate change risk is not a single hazard. It emerges from the interaction between changing climate hazards, exposed people/assets/ecosystems, vulnerability, and the quality of adaptation responses. For Thailand, this risk appears through connected pressures on water, agriculture, health, cities, coasts, ecosystems, tourism, and public infrastructure.

## Intended audience
Policy officers, DCCE/sectoral agency staff, local government officers, climate adaptation practitioners, and non-specialist technical stakeholders.

## Intended use
Exhibition / workshop roll-up display that introduces climate change risk as a coherent story before deeper technical discussion.

## Output format
- Physical roll-up banner
- Size: 60 cm wide × 160 cm high
- Orientation: vertical portrait
- Series length: 5 panels
- Intended viewing distance: approximately 1–2.5 m
- Text should be readable in a standing exhibition setting

## Storytelling objective
The five roll-ups should work as one left-to-right narrative sequence:

1. Define the climate risk logic.
2. Show how global climate change becomes Thailand-specific hazards.
3. Show where and who is exposed.
4. Show how risks cascade across sectors and places.
5. Show how risk-informed adaptation reduces risk and avoids maladaptation.

## Proposed five-roll-up sequence

### Roll-up 1 — What is climate change risk?
Primary message:
Climate risk results from the interaction of hazard, exposure, and vulnerability, while responses can reduce risk or create new risks.

Main visual:
Risk triangle / system diagram showing hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and response risk.

Role in series:
Sets the conceptual frame and defines the language used by all later panels.

### Roll-up 2 — What climate hazards are changing?
Primary message:
Warming changes climate impact-drivers such as heat, heavy rainfall, drought, sea level, and compound extremes.

Main visual:
Thailand connected to a global-to-regional climate system: warming atmosphere, monsoon variability, ENSO influence, sea-level rise, and extreme events. Below is CIDs that are driving the above impacts. Examples of climatic changes measured in Thailand

Role in series:
Moves from IPCC global science to the physical climate drivers relevant to Thailand.

### Roll-up 3 — Where does risk concentrate in Thailand?
Primary message:
Risk differs by geography because Thailand’s regions, livelihoods, ecosystems, infrastructure, and populations are exposed in different ways.

Main visual:
Thailand map with regional risk zones and sector icons: Northeast drought/agriculture, Central flood/agriculture/infrastructure, Bangkok heat/flood/urban exposure, South coast/erosion/tourism/ecosystems, protected areas/biodiversity.

Role in series:
Makes the risk spatial and concrete.

### Roll-up 4 — How do risks cascade across systems?
Primary message:
Climate impacts do not stay inside one sector. Hazards can trigger linked effects across water, food, health, ecosystems, cities, tourism, and public finance.

Main visual:
Network or flow diagram showing cascading pathways from hazards to sector impacts and social consequences. Use impact chain analysis as the core visual

Role in series:
Shows why climate risk assessment must be cross-sectoral rather than hazard-by-hazard only.

### Roll-up 5 — How can Thailand manage risk?
Primary message:
Risk-informed adaptation combines climate information, spatial risk assessment, sectoral planning, local implementation, monitoring, finance, and inclusive governance.

Main visual:
Adaptation cycle / implementation pathway from data → risk assessment → planning → action → monitoring → learning.

Role in series:
Ends with action, governance, and the practical function of CRDB/BTR/NAP systems.

## Required content
- IPCC AR6 risk framework: hazard, exposure, vulnerability, risk, response risk, maladaptation.
- Thailand national adaptation context from the BTR interim report.
- Climate risk examples from the TOR 5.5 article and infographic set.
- Thailand map or recognizable regional geography.
- Sector coverage aligned with Thailand adaptation priorities: water, agriculture, public health, natural resources, tourism, human settlements/infrastructure.
- Explicit distinction between hazard, exposure, vulnerability, impact, and response.
- Uncertainty or caution where evidence is scenario-based, regional, seasonal, or conditional.

## Constraints
- Do not generate final text inside AI-generated images.
- Add final titles, Thai/English labels, numbers, captions, and sources in a controlled layout tool.
- Avoid unsupported numerical claims.
- Preserve Thailand’s recognizable geographic form.
- Avoid showing climate impacts as uniform across all regions.
- Avoid implying one hazard has one simple direct consequence.
- Distinguish observed/current risk from projected/future risk.
- Mark uncertainty where projections, scenarios, or conditional impacts are involved.
- Keep all five roll-ups visually coherent as one series.
