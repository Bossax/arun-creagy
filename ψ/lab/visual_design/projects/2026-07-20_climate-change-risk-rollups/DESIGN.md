# 3. Design Principles

## Principle 1 — One message per panel
Each roll-up gets one core takeaway. Supporting modules must reinforce that takeaway rather than compete with it.

## Principle 2 — System before decoration
Use diagrams, maps, flows, and sector relationships as the visual structure. Decorative weather icons are secondary.

## Principle 3 — Thailand-specific but IPCC-grounded
Use IPCC concepts to frame risk, then localize the story through Thailand geography, sectors, institutions, and article examples.

## Principle 4 — Coherent series logic
Use repeated layout zones, consistent colors, repeated icon language, and panel numbering so the five banners function as one narrative sequence.

## Principle 5 — Evidence caution
Do not visualize a claim more strongly than the evidence supports. Regional, seasonal, scenario-based, and uncertain findings need visual caveats.

## Principle 6 — Text outside generated imagery
Final text, labels, numbers, and sources should be added in layout software, not generated inside image artwork.

# 4. Composition System

## 4.1 Dominant composition type

- [x] Flow from top to bottom
- [x] Map-centered
- [x] Modular grid
- [x] Sequential timeline
- [x] Other: five-panel story sequence

## 4.2 Main visual anchor

- Subject type: map, system diagram, or pathway diagram.
- Recommended size: 40–55% of each roll-up.
- Placement: upper-middle to center.
- Function: establishes the main conceptual or geographic structure.

## 4.3 Reading path

Series reading path:
Panel 1 concept → Panel 2 hazards → Panel 3 exposure/vulnerability geography → Panel 4 cascading impacts → Panel 5 adaptation response.

Within each roll-up:
Top title → main visual anchor → 2–4 supporting evidence modules → bottom takeaway/source strip.

## 4.4 Section structure

Recommended number of major sections per roll-up: 4

Typical sequence:

1. Panel number and headline.
2. Main visual anchor.
3. Supporting modules / examples.
4. Key takeaway and source note.

## 4.5 Grid

### Vertical
- Columns: 6
- Outer margin: 6–8%
- Gutter: 3–4%
- Section spacing: generous; avoid dense poster-wall effect.

### Roll-up physical planning
- Canvas: 60 cm × 160 cm.
- Safe content area: keep critical content at least 4 cm from edges.
- Top/bottom hardware caution: avoid critical content in top/bottom 8–10 cm.
- Main content zone: approximately 12–148 cm vertical.

## 4.6 Negative space

Preserve:
- top title zone;
- margins around Thailand map;
- clean footer/source strip;
- breathing room around the main diagram;
- quiet transition space between modules.

## 4.7 Balance

- Symmetry: mixed, with stable repeated top/footer structure.
- Weight distribution: strong central visual with lighter side modules.
- Preferred focal count: 1 primary + 2–4 secondary.

# 5. Shape Language

## 5.1 Dominant shapes

- Rounded rectangles for evidence modules.
- Curved arrows for climate pathways and cascades.
- Soft map silhouettes.
- Circular nodes for risk determinants and institutions.
- Ribbons or bands for flows across systems.
- Thin dividers for panel sequencing.

## 5.2 Edge quality

- Corner style: softly rounded.
- Outline style: thin or none.
- Outline color: slate / same-family dark neutral.

## 5.3 Containers

Preferred:
- map callouts;
- pathway bands;
- small sector modules;
- simple panels with clear labels.

Avoid:
- generic card overload;
- speech bubbles;
- decorative badges with no information function.

## 5.4 Connector language

- Arrow style: curved or segmented; avoid overly direct single-cause arrows.
- Line weight: medium for main pathways, thin for callouts.
- Endpoints: subtle arrowheads or nodes.
- Leader lines: short, direct, and visually quiet.

# 6. Illustration System

## 6.1 Illustration mode

- [x] Semi-flat vector
- [x] Editorial illustration
- [x] Geometric illustration
- [x] Technical diagram

## 6.2 Form treatment

- Level of simplification: medium.
- Technical accuracy: preserve meaningful geography and causal relationships.
- Perspective: mostly flat/front; limited layered depth.
- Shading: minimal, soft, only for hierarchy.
- Texture: none or very subtle grain.
- Highlight treatment: use accent color for risk or key transition points.

## 6.3 Object construction

Construct objects from large clear forms first, then add only details needed for recognition. Prefer meaningful diagrams over decorative environmental scenes.

## 6.4 Human figures

Use sparingly. If used, keep figures small, diverse, and diagrammatic. Avoid making people the main visual unless the panel is specifically about vulnerability.

## 6.5 Environmental elements

- Clouds: simple soft forms.
- Water: clean blue shapes with limited wave marks.
- Landforms: simplified silhouettes.
- Vegetation: minimal icons for ecosystems/agriculture.
- Weather effects: semantic and restrained; avoid disaster spectacle.
- Buildings/infrastructure: simplified urban blocks, roads, hospitals, water systems.

## 6.6 Icons

- Icon family: filled or duotone, rounded geometric.
- Stroke consistency: same stroke weight across all five panels.
- Detail level: low to medium.
- Background container: small circles or rounded squares only when needed for grouping.

## 6.7 Avoid

- clip-art appearance;
- photorealistic disaster imagery;
- heavy black outlines;
- random scientific symbols;
- over-dramatic flames/waves;
- cute character design;
- decorative globe clichés.

## 7.1 Palette strategy

- Dominant family: deep navy and slate.
- Secondary family: climate blues and teals.
- Accent family: warm amber/coral for risk and heat.
- Background family: soft off-white / pale blue.
- Neutral family: slate grays.

## 7.3 Color proportion

- Background: 50–60%
- Primary/slate neutrals: 15–20%
- Climate blues/teals: 15–20%
- Risk accents: 5–10%
- Other accents: under 5%

## 7.4 Semantic color mapping

| Meaning | Color |
|---|---|
| Positive / resilience | Sage Teal |
| Risk / stress | Coral Red |
| Water / climate | Sky Blue |
| Land / ecology | muted greens / teals |
| Future / scenario | Amber or dashed navy |
| Neutral context | Slate |

## 7.5 Gradient rules

- Allowed: limited.
- Direction: vertical or radial only when showing atmospheric/heat intensity.
- Maximum number of stops: 2–3.
- Intended function: depth, atmosphere, or risk intensity.

## 7.6 Color restrictions

- Do not use pure black.
- Avoid highly saturated neon greens or oranges.
- Use red/coral only for actual risk/stress signals.
- Do not rely on color alone; pair risk levels with labels, icons, or patterns.

# 8. Typography System

## 8.1 Typography character

Bold modern institutional display paired with highly readable sans-serif body text.

## 8.2 Type roles

| Role | Character | Weight | Case | Alignment |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Main title | authoritative, direct | bold | sentence case | left or centered |
| Subtitle | explanatory | regular/medium | sentence case | left |
| Section heading | compact | semibold | sentence case or short uppercase | left |
| Key number | strong | bold | numeric | centered/left |
| Body text | readable | regular | sentence case | left |
| Caption | restrained | regular/medium | sentence case | left |

## 8.3 Type scale

- Title: 1.00
- Subtitle: 0.45–0.55
- Section heading: 0.35–0.45
- Key number: 0.80–1.20
- Body: 0.25–0.32
- Caption/source: 0.16–0.22

## 8.4 Typography rules

- Maximum font families: 2.
- Recommended: Plus Jakarta Sans for headings, DM Sans or equivalent for body.
- Maximum line length: 6–9 words for display text, 12–16 words for body modules.
- Paragraph length: 1–2 short sentences per module.
- Line spacing: open; avoid compressed paragraphs.
- Uppercase: only for small labels/chips.
- Condensed type: avoid unless needed for narrow labels.

## 8.5 Text inside generated imagery

Default rule:
Generate illustration without final text. Add titles, numbers, labels, legends, source notes, and Thai text later in layout software.

Exceptions:
None at Stage 0.

# 9. Data Visualization Language

## 9.1 Preferred chart types

- risk framework triangle/system map;
- Thailand map with callouts;
- flow diagram;
- sector network;
- adaptation cycle;
- small multiples for hazard types;
- simple stacked or matrix diagrams.

## 9.2 Chart styling

- Axis style: minimal or omitted unless showing actual numeric data.
- Gridlines: subtle/selective.
- Labels: direct labels preferred over legends.
- Data markers: rounded dots, small icons, or clean bars.
- Background: plain or integrated with map/diagram.

## 9.3 Data integration

- [x] Mixed approach

Use charts only when evidence supports quantitative comparison. Otherwise use conceptual diagrams and sourced statements.

## 9.4 Quantitative integrity

- show units;
- identify time period;
- indicate rounded values;
- distinguish observed and projected data;
- avoid misleading area or volume;
- do not use 3D charts;
- preserve uncertainty where material.

# 10. Maps and Scientific Diagrams

## 10.1 Map style

- Geographic fidelity: simplified but recognizable.
- Coastline detail: medium for Thailand.
- Boundaries: regional/subnational boundaries only if needed.
- Relief: flat or lightly layered.
- Labels: direct or external callouts.

## 10.2 Diagram style

- Process arrows: curved, layered, non-overstated.
- Layer separation: use color bands or vertical zones.
- Cross-sections: allowed for climate drivers only if simple.
- Cause-and-effect representation: show multiple determinants, not single-cause chains.
- Uncertainty representation: dashed outlines, caution chips, or footnotes.

## 10.3 Scientific accuracy requirements

- Preserve hazard–exposure–vulnerability distinction.
- Do not show rainfall, heat, drought, or flood risk as uniform across Thailand.
- Maintain correct relative position of regions and coasts.
- Distinguish climate hazard from social vulnerability.
- Show responses as capable of reducing or increasing risk.
- Avoid implying adaptation automatically succeeds.

## 11.1 Depth model

- [x] Flat with overlap
- [x] Atmospheric depth

## 11.2 Shadow

- Type: soft/contact only.
- Opacity: low.
- Direction: consistent, subtle.
- Purpose: separation and hierarchy, not realism.

## 11.3 Texture

- Type: none or very subtle grain.
- Intensity: low.
- Applied to: background only, if used.

## 11.4 Special effects

- Bubbles: avoid unless representing water/sea and used minimally.
- Glow: avoid, except very soft highlight around key risk node.
- Motion lines: allowed only for wind/rain pathways.
- Transparency: allowed for overlays.
- Blur: avoid.

# 12. Content Density and Module Design

## 12.1 Density target

- Overall density: medium.
- Maximum major sections per panel: 4.
- Maximum key statistics per panel: 2–3.
- Maximum visible word count per panel: approximately 120–180 words.

## 12.2 Module anatomy

Each module may contain:

1. section heading;
2. one visual/icon;
3. one key finding;
4. one short explanation;
5. optional source marker.

## 12.3 Module shape

- open composition around main visual;
- bounded panel for definitions;
- map callouts for place-specific risks;
- integrated nodes for systems/cascade diagrams.

## 12.4 Module spacing

Modules must have enough breathing room to be read at exhibition distance. Avoid more than four callouts around one map.

# 13. Accessibility

- Minimum text contrast: dark navy/slate on white or pale background.
- Minimum final body-text size: large enough for 1–2.5 m viewing; validate in layout tool.
- Color-blind support: use icons, labels, patterns, and line styles in addition to color.
- Reading order: top-to-bottom, consistent panel numbering.
- Abbreviation treatment: spell out first use, especially IPCC, NAP, BTR, CRDB, DCCE.

# 20. Final Style Summary

## One-sentence description

A coherent five-panel scientific roll-up series using calm institutional colors, Thailand-centered maps, risk-system diagrams, and restrained sector icons to explain how climate hazards, exposure, vulnerability, and adaptation responses shape climate change risk.

## Essential features

1. One core message per roll-up.
2. Shared top title zone and bottom source/takeaway zone.
3. Consistent climate-risk color semantics.
4. Thailand-specific map or system anchor.
5. Evidence caution and no unsupported visual claims.

## Optional features

1. Subtle grain or atmospheric gradients.
2. Small sector icon set.
3. Panel number ribbon connecting the five banners.

## Prohibited features

1. Final text generated inside image artwork.
2. Distorted Thailand map.
3. Uniform national climate impacts.
4. Generic climate stock art.
5. Overly dramatic disaster spectacle.

## Recommended use cases

- Climate risk overview exhibition.
- DCCE stakeholder workshops.
- NAP/BTR/CRDB communication materials.
- Introductory policy-facing climate adaptation briefings.

## Poor-fit use cases

- Dense academic poster with full methods/results.
- Technical GIS risk map product.
- Social-media-first campaign graphic.
