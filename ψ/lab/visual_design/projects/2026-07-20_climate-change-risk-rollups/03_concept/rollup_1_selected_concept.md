# Selected Concept — Roll-up 1

## Selected concept

Concept 1 — IPCC diagram as museum object.

## Rationale

This concept gives the IPCC AR6 response-risk diagram clear visual authority while making it readable for a roll-up audience through surrounding explanation. It directly follows the user’s instruction to use the two IPCC diagrams and keeps the low-poly reference subordinate as graphic tone rather than scientific structure.

## Concept summary

The roll-up behaves like a guided exhibit panel:

1. The title asks the core question.
2. A compact IPCC context strip shows that risk framing evolved from AR5 to AR6.
3. The AR6 response-risk diagram is the main scientific anchor.
4. Plain-language callouts explain each component without changing the diagram.
5. A bottom takeaway emphasizes dynamic risk, response risk, and cascading risk.

## Core visual hierarchy

### Primary visual

Large adapted/inserted `SRL-image-1.png`.

Purpose:
Show hazard, exposure, vulnerability, response, and risk as interacting parts of the AR6 risk framework.

Scale:
Approximately 30–40% of usable roll-up height.

Placement:
Central vertical zone.

### Secondary visual

Compact adapted/inserted `SRL-image-0.png`.

Purpose:
Show the transition from AR5 risk framing to AR6 response risk and complexity.

Scale:
Approximately 15–18% of usable roll-up height.

Placement:
Upper context zone below title.

### Supporting visual language

Low-poly reference image used only for:

- callout endpoints;
- small supporting icons;
- faceted climate accents outside the IPCC diagrams;
- connector rhythm.

## Proposed reading path

1. `01` series marker.
2. Title: `What is climate change risk?`
3. Subtitle: `IPCC AR6 frames risk as an interaction among hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and response.`
4. Context strip: AR5 → AR6.
5. Main AR6 diagram.
6. Callouts around the diagram:
   - Risk: potential adverse consequences.
   - Hazard: climate conditions that can cause harm.
   - Exposure: what is in harm’s way.
   - Vulnerability: sensitivity and limited capacity.
   - Response: decisions can reduce or create risk.
7. Key risks module: examples of concrete severe risk domains.
8. Prominent maladaptation warning callout attached to the response area.
9. Bottom takeaway: `Risk is dynamic: it changes with climate, exposure, vulnerability, and the choices society makes.`
10. Source/footer.

## Locked decisions

- `SRL-image-1.png` is the dominant scientific anchor.
- `SRL-image-0.png` is secondary context only.
- S03 remains the only evidence source.
- No Thailand-specific evidence in Roll-up 1.
- Key-risk examples are illustrative IPCC domains, not a Thailand risk assessment.
- Maladaptation must be visibly emphasized as a response-risk warning.
- Do not use the low-poly stock image as composition.
- Do not recolor IPCC categories semantically.

## Open decisions for Stage 4

1. Whether `SRL-image-1.png` should be inserted as-is or redrawn/adapted for higher print clarity.
2. Whether `SRL-image-0.png` should show both panels `(a)` and `(b)` or be converted into a minimal two-step strip.
3. Final language: English only, Thai only, or bilingual.
4. Exact footer identity: DCCE logo placement and source attribution wording.

## Risks to test in Stage 4

- Main diagram may become too small if all callouts are added.
- Upper context strip may become unreadable if inserted as a full screenshot.
- Too many callouts could crowd the narrow vertical format.
- Low-poly icons could make the panel look like a stock infographic if overused.

## Stage 4 recommendation

Create a wireframe that first locks diagram scale and placement. Do not start with decoration. The main test should be whether `SRL-image-1.png` remains legible from exhibition distance while leaving enough room for concise explanatory callouts.
