# DESIGN-05.md — Roll-up 5 Design System

## 1. Purpose

This design system governs Roll-up 5, the closing panel of the five-panel climate-change-risk series.

**Roll-up 5 title:** How can Thailand manage climate risk?

**Panel function:** Turn the risk and cascade logic of Roll-ups 1–4 into a clear adaptation-management cycle: assess risk, plan, implement, then monitor/evaluate/learn. A feedback arc updates the next assessment and decision. The panel must show that good adaptation reduces exposure or vulnerability and must be checked for response risk.

**Evidence boundary:**

- S02 — Thailand BTR/5NC adaptation chapter: primary source for NAP process, institutions, sectors, implementation context, data systems and documented gaps.
- S03 — IPCC AR6 extract: response risk and maladaptation boundary.
- S04 — Climate Impact Chains Guidebook: bridge from identified risk to an intervention point.

**Content authority:** BTR2 is the source for all four cycle stages and their meanings: risk, vulnerability and impact assessment; formulation of policies, strategies and plans; implementation; and monitoring, evaluation and learning.

**Visual reference only:** `../00_project/reference_images/iterative_adaptation_cycle_unfccc.jpg` is a structural reference for a four-stage radial cycle: four stages around a clear central hub, large directional arcs and feedback returning to assessment. Borrow this geometry only. Do not reproduce its icons, gradient treatment, wording, colour sequence or content.

## 2. Core style statement

Create a 60 × 160 cm vertical scientific-editorial roll-up that concludes the series with a calm, practical management cycle. It must feel visually related to Roll-up 4: pale open canvas, precise circular nodes, thick near-black directional arrows, compact colour dots and generous white space. Replace the impact-chain lanes with a four-stage radial cycle, a central hub, a feedback arc and one prominent response-risk test.

Use low-detail flat editorial illustrations in the same manner as Roll-up 4, informed by `../00_project/reference_images/Infographic_Climate-threat-to-the-Arctic-1024x725-1-1024x700.png`: layered landscape/water/service fragments, sparse silhouettes, restricted palette and no embedded text. These illustrate system context; the cycle remains the dominant visual.

Translate that reference into a deliberately simplified graphic language: broad irregular colour fields, two or three flat layers, one recognisable silhouette at most in each scene, and occasional thin dotted callout connectors. No texture, hatching, realistic perspective, rendering, internal detail or decorative icon clusters. The illustration should be readable as a single quiet shape from distance, then reveal only one supporting cue on closer viewing.

The roll-up should feel:

- practical, evidence-led and institutionally credible;
- constructive without implying completion or certainty;
- legible from exhibition distance;
- diagram-led, with illustrations as quiet contextual support;
- visibly the final panel in the same series.

Avoid:

- a project catalogue, agency organogram or policy dashboard;
- generic green sustainability campaign styling;
- a linear chevron process that implies adaptation ends after implementation;
- a decorative loop with no decision questions or evidence conditions;
- a technology-as-solution poster, glossy 3D, photorealism or disaster imagery;
- dense text, a crowded network, a map or unverified performance statistics.

## 3. Narrative logic

**Primary message:** BTR2 shows that Thailand has core adaptation foundations in place. To tell whether any measure reduces risk in practice, test what it changes, who benefits, what trade-off it creates and what evidence proves the result.

**Reading sequence:**

1. Title question: how can Thailand manage climate risk?
2. One-sentence premise: adaptation manages risk through an iterative decision cycle.
3. Main cycle: assess risk, vulnerability and impact → formulate policies, strategies and plans → implement → monitor/evaluate/learn → feedback to the next assessment and decision.
4. Response-risk test: what changes → who benefits → what trade-off → what proof.
5. Feedback bridge: use results to update the next assessment and decision.
6. Footer: risk-informed adaptation remains a continuing process.

## 4. Composition system

### 4.1 Canvas and grid

- **Format:** 60 cm × 160 cm vertical roll-up.
- **Safe margin:** minimum 4 cm from left/right; keep critical content within 8–152 cm height.
- **Grid:** 8-column grid shared with Roll-ups 1 and 4; 6–7% outer margin, 2–3% horizontal gutter and 3–5% vertical section spacing.
- **Background:** pale grey `#F1F2F3`, with white or very-pale fields for the main scientific diagram.

### 4.2 Recommended vertical zones

### Zone A — Header / title

**Approximate height:** 12–16 cm.

- Roll-up marker `05`.
- Title: `How can Thailand manage climate risk?`
- Subtitle: `Risk-informed adaptation turns evidence into decisions, action and learning.`
- Keep the header quiet and free of scene illustration.

### Zone B — Adaptation premise

**Approximate height:** 14–18 cm.

- A compact method statement: adaptation is a managed cycle, not a one-time project.
- One small risk-to-decision strip: `risk information → decisions → action → learning`.
- Reserve a concise source/definition cue; do not build a second risk diagram.

### Zone C — Main scientific anchor: four-stage adaptation cycle

**Approximate height:** 62–70 cm.

- A large clockwise radial cycle occupying a white or very pale-grey field, structurally informed by the UNFCCC reference.
- Four equal soft-grey circular bubbles arranged around a quiet central hub, each with a small category dot, a short stage name and room for one decision question.
- Stages: `Assess risk, vulnerability and impact` → `Formulate policies, strategies and plans` → `Implement` → `Monitor, evaluate and learn`.
- A central hub states the cycle’s purpose in controlled layout: `Risk-informed adaptation cycle`.
- Primary arcs: near-black, 1.5–2.0 mm at final print size, with clear arrowheads. They show iteration, not guaranteed progress.
- Use one dark-green feedback arc from `Monitor, evaluate and learn` back to `Assess risk`; label this arc `Update the next decision` in controlled layout.
- Add a small low-detail flat editorial scene fragment at the outer edge of each stage: climate-data/map cue; local planning landscape/meeting cue; water/agriculture/coast/service cue; observation/feedback cue. Keep every fragment outside bubbles, arcs and text zones.

### Zone D — Response-risk gate: four decision tests

**Approximate height:** 22–26 cm.

- A prominent response-risk gate beneath the cycle, expressed through four questions: `What changes?` · `Who benefits?` · `What trade-off appears?` · `What proves the result?`
- Each question must make the decision test tangible: exposure, vulnerability or capacity at the risk point; people/systems/places actually exposed; risk transfer, unequal burden, lock-in or harm elsewhere; indicators and evidence beyond activities or expenditure.
- A compact BTR2 note explains why outcome proof matters: the M&E framework and database exist, while outcome indicators, avoided-loss evidence and policy-feedback links continue to be strengthened.
- Use six tiny neutral sector cues only as secondary context. They cannot replace the response-risk questions.

### Zone E — Learning returns to the next decision

**Approximate height:** 18–22 cm.

- A compact dark-green feedback bridge connected to the return arrow.
- Sentence: `Use outcome evidence to revise the next assessment, plan and implementation choice.`
- Do not turn this into a second checklist or imply every response is harmful.

### Zone F — Footer / series close

**Approximate height:** 8–10 cm.

- Closing line: `Risk-informed adaptation learns from outcomes and improves the next decision.`
- Source note: S02 Thailand BTR/5NC adaptation chapter; S03 IPCC AR6; S04 impact-chain guidebook.
- DCCE identity area, if supplied.

## 5. Diagram rules

- The four-stage cycle and its feedback arc are the panel’s authority and must dominate the composition.
- Each bubble contains a decision stage, not a generic action, policy slogan or agency name.
- The assessment stage must connect climate information, risk maps and local knowledge to the decision cycle.
- Use BTR2’s stage terms in controlled layout; shorten only the bubble heading if necessary, never the underlying meaning.
- The monitoring stage must ask whether risk fell and who benefited; do not reduce it to activity reporting.
- The feedback arc makes learning operational: it updates the next assessment and plan.
- The response-risk test must remain visibly connected to the cycle and be the largest secondary module; it cannot interrupt the primary reading order.
- Do not show a universal solution, project ranking, budget total, performance score or named local project.

## 6. Illustration style

Follow Roll-up 4’s low-detail flat editorial scene rules:

- layered ground, water, infrastructure and vegetation fragments in two or three flat planes;
- one sparse line/silhouette cue at most for gauge, map, water, crop, coast, health/service or observation; use a thin dotted connector only where it clarifies the relationship to a stage;
- pale cyan, grey, muted navy and limited green; no decorative coral-orange beyond the guardrail;
- each scene fragment stays below 10–15% of the visual weight of its associated cycle stage;
- no text, people in distress, stock-photo treatment, landmarks, polar subjects, texture, hatching, realistic perspective or place-specific claims.

## 7. Colour system

| Concept | Working colour | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Climate/risk information | `#17375E` | assessment bubble dot, data cues and source context |
| Planning / coordination | `#00A6CF` | planning bubble dot and collaboration cues |
| Implementation | `#165A3C` | implementation bubble dot and practical-delivery cue |
| Monitoring, evaluation and learning | `#8A1B61` | MEL bubble dot and evidence-feedback cue |
| Update / iterative decision | `#2E6F5E` | return arrow and final update bubble dot |
| Response-risk gate | `#F2A000` | four-question decision gate and only controlled warning use |
| Interaction field | `#E9E9E9` | shared cycle field |
| Canvas / callouts | `#F1F2F3` / `#FFFFFF` | background and white annotation surfaces |
| Connector / annotation | `#8A8A8A` | thin grey secondary lines and source notes |

Restrictions:

- Use green only for implementation and iterative updating; it must not turn the panel into generic sustainability branding.
- Reserve amber for response-risk checking, not generic warning or every adaptation stage.
- Pair every colour with a stage label and position; colour alone must not carry meaning.

## 8. Typography and content density

- Use the same modern scientific sans-serif hierarchy as Roll-ups 1 and 4.
- Main title: readable from approximately 2 m; subtitle: one or two short lines.
- Cycle bubbles: stage name plus one concise decision question; central hub: one compact cycle-purpose label.
- Response-risk test: four concise, decision-specific questions plus one BTR2 evidence note.
- Keep visible display copy around 300–380 words in the final language, excluding sources. Preserve the BTR2 diagnosis before simplifying scene fragments or secondary sector cues.
- Do not put final Thai copy inside AI-generated artwork; use controlled layout text.

## 9. Evidence and layout controls

- S02 is the primary source for the adaptation cycle, system conditions and Thailand context.
- S03 is the primary source for response risk and maladaptation boundaries.
- S04 only bridges risk analysis to intervention points.
- Use BTR2’s outcome-evidence detail as the rationale for the test, not as a national scorecard or a claim of failure.
- Do not claim that the Action Plan on Adaptation (2025–2030) is complete; S02 describes it as under development.
- Do not show an adaptation effectiveness claim without an evaluated source.

## 10. Quality checklist

### Scientific and policy structure

- [ ] The panel clearly presents adaptation as a four-stage iterative decision cycle with feedback to assessment.
- [ ] Each stage has a decision question, not merely an activity label.
- [ ] The assessment, planning, implementation and MEL stages reflect S02’s NAP process.
- [ ] The response-risk test makes S03’s trade-off and maladaptation boundary explicit.
- [ ] The BTR2 evidence note explains why activity reporting alone cannot demonstrate risk reduction.

### Composition and style

- [ ] The four-stage radial cycle and feedback arc dominate; the response-risk test is the largest secondary module.
- [ ] Primary arrows are thick and readable; annotation connectors remain thin grey.
- [ ] Low-detail flat illustrations follow Roll-up 4’s visual weight and stay outside bubbles and arrows.
- [ ] The palette remains restrained, institutional and series-consistent.
- [ ] Footer contains source and attribution space.

### Accessibility

- [ ] Labels do not rely on colour alone.
- [ ] Lines and nodes remain legible from 1–2.5 m.
- [ ] Thai controlled-layout copy can fit without shrinking the main cycle below exhibition reading scale.
