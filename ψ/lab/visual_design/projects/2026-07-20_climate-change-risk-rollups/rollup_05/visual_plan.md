# Roll-up 5 — Visual plan

## Selected concept — Four-stage iterative adaptation cycle

**Concept:** A large four-stage BTR2 adaptation cycle occupies the centre of the roll-up. It uses the same minimal scientific grammar as Roll-up 4—soft circular bubbles, compact category dots, thick near-black arrows, generous white space and low-detail flat editorial scene fragments. The UNFCCC reference supplies only the radial geometry: four stages around a quiet central hub with a visible feedback arc. A dark-green return arrow makes learning visibly update the next BTR2 risk assessment.

**Primary message:** BTR2 shows that Thailand has core adaptation-system foundations. A measure becomes risk-informed only when it can show what risk determinant it changes, who benefits, what trade-off it creates and what evidence proves the result.

**Why this concept:** It gives the series a practical close. Roll-ups 1–4 explain how risk is formed, changes, concentrates and cascades; Roll-up 5 shows the governance logic needed to manage that risk without reducing adaptation to one project or technology.

**Reading order:** title → BTR2 system context → four-stage cycle and feedback arc → expanded response-risk test → series-close footer.

## Composition blueprint

### Canvas and hierarchy

- **Format:** 60 × 160 cm vertical roll-up; working zone 8–152 cm high; minimum 4 cm side margins.
- **Grid:** Roll-up 1 and Roll-up 4’s 8-column grid, 6–7% outer margin, 2–3% horizontal gutter and 3–5% vertical section spacing.
- **Background:** pale climate grey `#F1F2F3`; white and very pale-grey fields create the reading surface.
- **Hierarchy:** the four-node radial cycle, central hub and feedback arc are the dominant visual. The expanded response-risk test is the largest secondary module and must read before supporting evidence notes.
- **Text:** all Thai titles, labels, source notes and evidence cues are controlled-layout elements, never generated inside artwork.

### Zone A — Header / title — 8–20 cm

- Series marker: `05`.
- Title space: `How can Thailand manage climate risk?`
- Subtitle space: `Risk-informed adaptation turns evidence into decisions, action and learning.`
- Optional thin dark-green rule beneath the header.
- Keep empty of illustrations, icons and process arrows.

### Zone B — Adaptation premise — 22–34 cm

- One compact scientific strip headed by a controlled-layout definition: adaptation is a managed cycle, not a one-time project.
- Left: small navy risk/vulnerability/impact-assessment bubble with a climate-data/grid cue.
- Centre: small soft-grey decision bubble.
- Right: small dark-green action/learning bubble.
- Connect the three with two short near-black arrows, then a small green feedback curve returning toward the first bubble.
- Reserve a second line: `Assess risk → plan → implement → learn → update the next assessment.`
- This strip introduces the logic; it must not duplicate the large four-stage cycle below.

### Zone C — Main adaptation cycle — 36–98 cm

- Place a large white or very pale-grey scientific field, approximately 44–48 cm wide.
- Use `../00_project/reference_images/iterative_adaptation_cycle_unfccc.jpg` as the radial geometry reference only: four stages around a central hub, with large directional arcs and feedback to assessment. BTR2 supplies every stage label and content. Do not reproduce the UNFCCC icons, gradients, wording or colour sequence.
- Build one clockwise radial loop from four equal soft-grey circular bubbles, approximately 9–10 cm diameter at final print size.
- Position the bubbles at the top (01), right (02), bottom (03) and left (04) of the central hub. Use the BTR2 stage wording in the table below as the sole source for controlled-layout labels.
- Put a quiet central hub between the four bubbles, with controlled-layout space for `Risk-informed adaptation cycle`.
- The exact geometry can be gently rounded, but each stage must remain visually distinct and the arrows must not cross.
- Each bubble has a category dot, a short stage label and a controlled-layout question. Keep the question to one concise line.

| Stage | Bubble label | Controlled-layout question | Evidence cue | Low-detail scene fragment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | `Assess risk, vulnerability and impact` | `Where are hazard, exposure and vulnerability concentrated?` | climate analysis, risk maps, projections and local knowledge | pale climate-data band, rain gauge or grid/map fragment |
| 02 | `Formulate policies, strategies and plans` | `Which option reduces risk here, for whom, and with whose responsibility?` | NAP priorities, sectoral/local planning and participation | simple contour/settlement band with small planning-table silhouette |
| 03 | `Implement` | `Can sectors and local actors deliver where risk occurs?` | six priority sectors and coordination across levels | quiet water/crop/service/coast fragments linked by one service line |
| 04 | `Monitor, evaluate and learn` | `Did risk fall, and who benefited?` | outcome tracking, monitoring and evaluation | observation post, simple measuring line and feedback ripple |

**Bubble and arrow treatment**

- Bubbles: white or `#F1F2F3` fill; neutral-grey 1.0–1.25 mm outline; very restrained soft shadow.
- Category dots: navy for assessment, cyan for planning, dark green for implementation, magenta for MEL and muted green for the update decision.
- Primary cycle arrows: near-black `#202020`, 1.5–2.0 mm at final print size, calm curves and crisp arrowheads.
- Feedback arc from `Monitor, evaluate and learn` to `Assess risk`: `#2E6F5E`, same thickness as primary arrows; label it `Update the next decision` in controlled layout. It is the only green connector in the central loop.
- No text is generated within bubbles. Keep each bubble’s interior clear for later Thai typography.

**Illustration boundary**

- One scene fragment per stage at most; use two or three flat layers and one recognisable silhouette only.
- Place fragments at the outer edge of the loop or in a shallow background band. They must not enter bubbles, cross arrows or look like a sixth process step.
- Use broad irregular colour fields, sparse silhouettes and occasional thin dotted connectors in the manner of the Arctic reference; exclude texture, hatching, realistic perspective and decorative icon clusters.
- Use system context, not named locations, agency branding, project claims or distressed people.

### Zone D — Response-risk gate: four decision tests — 101–132 cm

**D1. The response-risk gate**

- White or pale-grey module, headed: `A response is not risk reduction until it passes four tests.`
- Four equal question capsules, each with an amber dot and a short answer space:
  - `What changes?` — exposure, vulnerability or adaptive capacity at the identified risk point.
  - `Who benefits?` — people, systems and places actually exposed to the risk.
  - `What trade-off appears?` — risk transfer, unequal burdens, lock-in or harm to another system.
  - `What proves the result?` — indicators and evidence beyond activities completed or budgets spent.
- Add one compact BTR2 note below: M&E framework and database exist, but outcome indicators, avoided-loss evidence and policy-feedback links are still being strengthened.

**D2. Sector scope**

- Beneath or beside the four questions, use six tiny neutral line/silhouette cues: water, agriculture, tourism, health, natural resources, settlements/infrastructure.
- These are a scope reminder, not six independent adaptation cards.

### Zone E — Learning returns to the next decision — 135–143 cm

- Use a narrow dark-green bridge connected with one thin grey line to the feedback arc.
- Reserve one controlled-layout sentence: `Use the result to revise the next risk assessment, plan and implementation choice.`
- A small return-arrow/check cue may appear, but this is a feedback bridge, not a second diagnostic module.

### Zone F — Footer / series close — 146–152 cm

- Closing line space: `Risk-informed adaptation learns from outcomes and improves the next decision.`
- Source note space: `Sources: Thailand BTR/5NC adaptation chapter; IPCC AR6; Climate Impact Chains Guidebook.`
- DCCE identity area at right, if supplied.

## Evidence-to-visual controls

| Visual element                         | What it may communicate                                      | What it must not imply                                             |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Four-stage radial cycle + feedback arc | adaptation is iterative and managed through decisions        | all adaptation processes are identical or automatically successful |
| Assessment bubble                      | climate/risk information and local knowledge guide decisions | data alone solves risk or every place has equivalent information   |
| Planning and implementation bubbles    | sectoral/local coordination turns information into action    | one agency or technology can manage all risk                       |
| MEL bubble and green return arrow      | outcomes inform the next decision                            | all impacts or benefits are already measured                       |
| Four-question response-risk test       | whether a response changes risk, for whom, at what trade-off and with what proof | a national performance ranking or institutional failure scorecard |
| Sector cues                            | adaptation spans six NAP priority sectors                    | six separate project case studies or equal sector outcomes         |

## Print and accessibility rules

- The four cycle bubbles, central hub and their arrows must remain readable from 1–2.5 m; simplify the premise strip before reducing their scale.
- Pair colour with stage label, position and icon; no stage can be identified through colour alone.
- Primary arrows: 1.5–2.0 mm; bubble outlines: 1.0–1.25 mm; callout/annotation connectors: 0.75–1.0 mm grey.
- Keep the loop free of paragraph text. Place explanation and evidence cues in controlled surrounding positions.
- Keep supporting scenes low-detail and low-contrast; the cycle must read first.
- Aim for 300–380 visible final-language words excluding source microcopy. If constrained, simplify scene fragments and sector cues before cutting the response-risk test or its BTR2 evidence note.

## Stage 2 decision

Proceed with a **central four-stage BTR2 adaptation cycle, arranged in a radial geometry with feedback to assessment**, and a prominent four-question response-risk test. Stage 3 should create the text-free generation/layout handoff and authoritative Thai controlled-layout copy.
