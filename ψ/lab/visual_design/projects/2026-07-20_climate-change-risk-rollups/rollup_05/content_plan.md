# Roll-up 5 — Content plan

**Status:** Stage 1 — evidence and narrative plan complete

## Role in the series

Close the five-panel story by moving from Roll-up 4’s connected impacts to the public decisions that reduce risk. The panel explains adaptation as a continuing risk-management cycle, not as a list of projects or a one-time emergency response.

## Working title

How can Thailand manage climate risk?

## Primary message

BTR2 shows that Thailand has built core adaptation-system foundations—risk data, the NAP, cross-sector coordination and an M&E framework. The infographic’s key lesson is that a measure counts as risk-informed only when it can show what risk determinant it changes, who benefits, what trade-offs it creates and what evidence demonstrates the result.

## Audience takeaway

The key question is no longer whether an adaptation activity happened. It is whether Thailand can demonstrate that its information, planning, implementation and learning systems collectively reduced exposure or vulnerability for the people and systems at risk.

## Message hierarchy

1. **BTR2 defines a four-step iterative process:** risk, vulnerability and impact assessment → policies, strategies and plans → implementation → monitoring, evaluation and learning (MEL) → updated assessment and decisions.
2. **BTR2 records real foundations:** the Spatial Climate Risk Database and high-resolution projections are complete; the NAP provides direction; an integrated six-sector MoU, M&E framework and M&E database are in place.
3. **BTR2 makes outcome evidence decisive:** frameworks and databases exist, but avoided losses, vulnerability reduction, outcome indicators and policy feedback still require development before activity can be treated as demonstrated risk reduction.
4. **Every action needs a response-risk check:** test what determinant it reduces, who benefits, which trade-offs it creates, and what evidence proves the result.

## Core adaptation cycle

| Stage | Decision question | Thailand evidence boundary | What the visual may show |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 Assess risk, vulnerability and impact** | Which climate hazards, exposed systems and vulnerable groups need attention? | S02: DCCE climate analysis, risk maps, projections and sectoral/provincial assessment; S03: risk is shaped by hazard, exposure and vulnerability. | climate-data signal, map/grid, exposed system markers |
| **02 Formulate policies, strategies and plans** | Which option reduces risk in this context, for whom, and with what responsibility and resources? | S02: NAP sectoral priorities, national/local planning and stakeholder participation. | layered local landscape, planning table, pathway marker |
| **03 Implement** | Can sectors and local actors deliver the measure where risk occurs? | S02: implementation across water, agriculture, tourism, health, natural resources, and settlements/infrastructure; coordination across levels. | small water/agriculture/coast/service fragments, linked service lines |
| **04 Monitor, evaluate and learn** | Did exposure or vulnerability fall, who benefited, and what must change? | S02: NAP M&E framework, monitoring/reporting; gaps remain in outcome indicators and adaptation-effectiveness tracking. | observation marker, feedback arrow, evidence card |

**Feedback arc:** `Update the next assessment and decision with evidence from monitoring, evaluation and learning.` The response-risk gate sits on this return path; it asks what should be redesigned or stopped before risk is shifted, locked in or increased elsewhere.

## Response-risk test — the source-specific insight

Make this the panel’s largest secondary module beneath the cycle. It combines BTR2’s unresolved outcome-evidence issue with S03’s response-risk framing:

- **What changes?** Does the action reduce exposure, vulnerability or increase adaptive capacity at the identified risk point?
- **Who benefits?** Does it reach people, systems and places actually exposed to the risk?
- **What trade-off appears?** Does it transfer risk, create unequal burden, constrain future choices or harm another system?
- **What proves the result?** Which indicator and evidence show risk reduction beyond activities completed or budgets spent?

## Response-risk gate

Put one explicit guardrail on the feedback arc before it returns to risk assessment:

`Does this response reduce risk without transferring it, locking in future vulnerability, excluding affected groups, or creating new environmental and social costs?`

This reflects S03’s response-risk and maladaptation framing. It is a test, not a claim that every action causes harm.

## Evidence boundary

- **S02 — Thailand BTR/5NC adaptation chapter:** primary source for Thailand’s adaptation architecture, NAP process, priority sectors, climate data/risk information, implementation progress and documented system gaps.
- **S03 — IPCC AR6 extract:** primary source for the risk framework, response risk and maladaptation boundary.
- **S04 — Climate Impact Chains Guidebook:** supports the handoff from Roll-up 4: use a chain to locate the risk and potential intervention point; do not reuse its examples as adaptation performance evidence.

## Do not show

- a claim that Thailand has completed the full adaptation cycle or closed all gaps;
- a project catalogue, a list of agency logos, a detailed institutional organogram or a national scorecard;
- a single technology as a universal solution;
- unverified budget totals, beneficiaries, effectiveness figures or local project outcomes;
- adaptation as equivalent to mitigation, disaster response alone, or ecosystem restoration alone;
- a claim that all adaptation is maladaptation.
