## Visual-defensibility assessment

Strongly defensible:

- a risk triangle or interlocking system diagram;
- separate definitions of hazard, exposure, and vulnerability;
- a response-risk branch;
- a dynamic time/space cue;
- a small cascading-risk preview.

Use cautiously:

- CIDs as examples of hazards, because CIDs are value-neutral until they create detrimental effects for a system;
- adaptation, because responses can create risk when poorly designed;
- cascading impacts, because full impact-chain detail belongs to Roll-up 4.

Avoid:

- national risk statistics as the opener unless needed later for emphasis;
- detailed Thailand map overlays;
- showing climate risk as simply “floods/droughts/heat”;
- implying risk is static or purely physical;
- implying adaptation always reduces risk.

## Stage 1 conclusion for Roll-up 1

Roll-up 1 should be a conceptual gateway based only on S03. It should teach the audience how to read all later panels. The best visual structure is a dynamic risk-system diagram with four conceptual zones:

1. Climate impact-drivers / hazards.
2. Hazard–exposure–vulnerability interaction.
3. Response choices: risk reduction vs response risk/maladaptation.
4. Small cascading-risk preview.
