# Roll-up 01 — Content plan

## Title

From project idea to funding submission

## Reader outcome

The reader can identify what to prepare, how to screen funding options, which access route applies, and what happens after submission.

## Four-topic architecture

### 1. Prepare the project

Define the adaptation purpose, organize activities, and state the funding need.

### 2. Match the project to funding

Screen the five fields: target sector, eligible entity, supported activity, financial instrument, and co-financing option.

### 3. Choose the access route

Decide whether the project is submitted directly, through an Accredited/Implementing Entity, or by an Accredited Entity that brings in partners later.

### 4. Complete the funding lifecycle

Move from proposal development to review, approval, agreement, implementation, and monitoring.

## Insight Card

1. **Reader question:** What do I do first, and what happens next, if I want to submit an adaptation project for funding?
2. **Source-specific finding:** The guide organizes the journey into preparation, funding-source screening, and proposal-to-funding stages; screening uses a decision tree and a five-category funding matrix.
3. **Mechanism:** Project characteristics are progressively converted into a fund shortlist and then into an application route.
4. **Consequence:** A project team can identify its next preparation task before approaching a fund.
5. **Visual proof:** A left-to-right lifecycle with three phases and five screening fields embedded at the center.
6. **Evidence anchor:** Source 01, lines 170–183, especially lines 174–177 and 179–183.

## Action takeaway

Define the project → fill the five screening inputs → shortlist suitable funds.
