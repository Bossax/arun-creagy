CRI Workbook Pipeline Explainer

This page explains how the workbook-derived CRI datasets move from Bronze extraction to Silver normalization to the province-level notebook demo. It is designed to make the processing decisions visible: what each file contains, who owns the source data, what transformations were applied, what issues appeared, and why the current pipeline was built this way.

Scope of this explainer: only the workbook-derived streams and the notebook inputs. It does not attempt to document the full historical CRI repository.

2. Workbook streams and why they were processed differently

Heatwave

Owner: กองระบาดวิทยา กรมควบคุมโรค / Epidemiology Division, Department of Disease Control.
Bronze shape: province rows with wide metric/time columns.
Silver shape: one row per province + metric + time scope.
Why: the workbook already behaves like a compact province summary; reshaping was mostly about converting the wide layout into analytical rows.

GPP

Owner: NESDC / สศช.
Bronze shape: block-structured province sections with metric rows beneath them.
Silver shape: one row per area + metric + year.
Why: this workbook is not a clean flat table; it had to be unfolded vertically first, then melted across years.

Government Advance Payment

Owner: DDPM / ปภ.-style reserve / disaster spending source.
Bronze shape: three hazard-specific province tables with years in columns.
Silver shape: one annual long fact table plus one period-total table.
Why: it is naturally province-grained but hazard-split and year-wide, so the correct move is to melt hazards and years while preserving period totals separately.

Population

Owner: DOPA / กรมการปกครอง.
Bronze shape: one monthly table and one wide annual table, both mixing detail and aggregate rows.
Silver shape: monthly, annual, and period-total tables with geography classes separated.
Why: this source contains embedded aggregates, code formulas, and duplicate-grain collisions, so it needed the heaviest normalization logic.

4. Why the notebook processes data this way

The notebook is not using one single perfectly harmonized master dataset. It uses a set of sources that each play a different role: DDPM Gold facts for human impact, Population Silver for denominators, GPP Silver for economic denominator, Government Advance Payment Silver for economic impact, and Heatwave Silver as a companion metric.
  • Human impact is aggregated to province because the CRI demo is province-level.
  • Population is averaged across years to get a stable denominator for rates.
  • GPP is averaged across years and filtered to current-market-price GPP because the pilot logic compares monetary loss against current-price economic output.
  • Government Advance Payment is kept annual for scoring and period totals are kept separate so cumulative reporting does not pollute annual score logic.
  • Heat mortality is visualized because it matters analytically, but it is intentionally excluded from the CRI score because its time coverage and methodology role do not match the six pilot components.

7. Bottom-line interpretation

The pipeline was designed this way because the data are not born in one clean analytical model. Each stream had a different shape, owner, level of completeness, and semantic risk. The Bronze layer kept the source structure close to the original workbook. The Silver layer turned those structures into analysis-ready facts. The notebook then uses those Silver and Gold assets by role: human impact, denominator, government advance payment, economic denominator, and companion heat metrics.

That is why the notebook can already demonstrate a meaningful province-level CRI-style implementation, while still carrying cautions about missing-vs-zero ambiguity, proxy government advance payment logic, provisional GPP, and the non-equivalence of heat mortality to the six official CRI score components.
