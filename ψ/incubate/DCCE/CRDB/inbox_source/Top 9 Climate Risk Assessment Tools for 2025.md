## What is A Climate Risk Assessment?

A climate risk assessment helps companies understand the potential short and long-term climate risks that their business is exposed to and how those risks will impact their assets, revenue, and infrastructure.

A good climate risk assessment will give companies the likely cost of each climate risk they face under different warming scenarios so that they can determine their [return on investment](https://climate.ai/blog/introduction-to-climate-risk-assessment-for-investment-portfolios-a-qa-with-daniel-e-ingram/) for each adaptation measure.

Essentially, they are tools to manage the impacts and build corporate resilience around climate change.

### Why Are Climate Risk Assessments Important?

Climate change will be one of the biggest economic disruptors of the 21st century, and climate risk assessments are essential tools to help companies navigate this transition. They enable:

- **Identification of risk and its impact on the company:** Climate risk assessments provide companies with specific climate risks they face in the short and long term across their operational geographies and value chain.
- **Better decision-making:** This information can then be used to make better short-term resource and capital allocation and long-term strategic decisions.
- **Compliance:** Climate risk reporting is becoming an essential part of sustainability reporting regulations. Jurisdictions from the EU to California and Japan are asking companies that operate there to report on their climate risks, how risks affect their businesses’ financial performance, and how they plan to adapt to them.

## What to Look For in a Climate Risk Assessment Tool

Climate risks are complex. They require an accurate understanding of how upcoming weather events will unfold in the coming days, weeks, and months, and how trends will shape the climate over the longer year-to-year and decadal timelines.

They also need to understand your business. What assets do you have, and how will the climate impact them?

Considering these factors, this is what you should be looking for in a climate risk assessment tool:

- **A Strong Track History of Correct Predictions:** Climate risk assessment tools should have a [track record of getting predictions right](https://climate.ai/decision-accuracy/). Global weather systems and long-term climate forecasting are very complex undertakings. Tools have to be accurate and granular to ensure insights are actionable.
- **A Broad Coverage:** Some climate risk assessments focus on floods, wildfires, or another specific climate risk. Ideally, you want to look for a tool that considers all types of climate risks, both acute (floods, wildfires, hurricanes) and chronic (sea-level rises, precipitation patterns, changing seasons), across all warming scenarios (1.5°C – 8°C warming).
- **Sector Specificity:** While climate risks are similar for all companies, their interaction with each sector and geography varies significantly. Some broader tools may focus on cross-sectors but lack the depth required for that sector and its supply chains.
- **Usability, Actionability, and ROI:** Solutions should be intuitive enough to encourage adoption across teams while still offering robust insights. Consider the learning curve and how easily stakeholders, from procurement to executive leadership, can engage with the platform. Tools that deliver clear, actionable insights empower users to connect analysis directly to business decisions. This actionability is key: look for platforms that offer practical recommendations for mitigating risk and support both tactical and strategic planning. Ultimately, the ability to translate insights into measurable ROI is what separates a good tool from a great one.

### 2\. First Street:

First Street is a U.S.-based company that specializes in high-resolution, property-level climate risk modeling. Its flagship platform delivers detailed, building-specific assessments of how climate change—including flood, fire, and heat risks—will impact property value, income, and credit risk over the next 30 years.

Unlike broader tools, First Street uses physics-based deterministic models that incorporate the actual characteristics of each structure, enabling highly granular and accurate damage and downtime projections. The platform also considers surrounding infrastructure, demographic shifts, and macroeconomic factors, making it a trusted choice for banks, insurers, government agencies, and real estate platforms. Widely used for regulatory stress testing and policy development, First Street has become the gold standard for translating physical climate risk into financial exposure across the U.S. real estate landscape.

### 3\. EcoAct

EcoAct’s Climate Risk Tool (ECLR) is a flexible, site-specific platform that helps businesses assess exposure to a wide range of physical climate hazards—including floods, heatwaves, salinization, and ocean acidification—across multiple climate scenarios and timeframes.

Designed to align with frameworks like TCFD and the EU Taxonomy, ECLR enables companies to visualize site vulnerability, prioritize adaptation actions, and generate downloadable risk profiles. Making it a strong choice for multinational organizations looking to operationalize climate resilience.

### 4\. Meteomatics

Meteomatics is a hyper-local, 1-kilometer resolution weather model covering the entire contiguous U.S. and Gulf of Mexico. Designed to detect localized atmospheric phenomena such as isolated thunderstorms, sea breezes, and urban heat islands.

Its value for climate risk assessments lies in the ability to anticipate acute hazards (e.g., hurricanes, flooding, heatwaves) and translate them into actionable insights for industries like energy, agriculture, transportation, and emergency management. By combining high-frequency data assimilation with advanced downscaling to 90 meters, Meteomatics delivers industry-specific parameters such as soil conditions, solar output, and wind speeds at different heights.

## Climate Risk Assessment Tool FAQ

A climate risk assessment tool helps businesses evaluate how climate risks, such as floods, heatwaves, or sea level rise, could impact their assets, operations, and financial performance over time. These tools often use climate models, satellite data, and scenario analysis to deliver insights for strategic decision-making.

Climate change is already disrupting supply chains, infrastructure, and asset values. Companies need these tools to identify vulnerabilities, make informed capital allocation decisions, and comply with growing regulations such as the EU’s CSRD and California’s SB 261.

It depends on your industry, geographic footprint, and internal capabilities. Look for tools with broad climate risk coverage, sector-specific insights, scenario modeling, usability across teams, and proven ROI. Some platforms also specialize in compliance, asset-level modeling, or supply chain analysis.

Physical risks include extreme weather events and long-term climate shifts that directly affect infrastructure and operations. Transition risks refer to the business impacts of shifting toward a low-carbon economy, such as policy changes, carbon pricing, or reputational risk.
