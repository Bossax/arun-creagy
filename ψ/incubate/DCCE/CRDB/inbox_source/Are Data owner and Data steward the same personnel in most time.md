Short answer: usually not — in well‑governed organisations Data Owner and Data Steward are different people with distinct responsibilities; combining them happens in smaller teams or early-stage programmes but raises governance and capacity risks.

Why they’re normally separate

- Accountability vs. operational work: A Data Owner holds _accountability_ for a data domain (policy decisions, risk acceptance, access approvals); a Data Steward performs _operational_ stewardship (definitions, metadata, quality controls, incident triage). Mixing accountability and hands‑on tasks creates conflicts of interest and reduces checks and balances.
    
- Seniority and role focus differ: Data Owners are typically senior business managers or domain leads; Data Stewards are often subject‑matter experts or business analysts embedded in day‑to‑day data work.
    
- Continuity and workload: Ownership is tied to a position/role (so remains stable even if individuals change tasks), while stewardship is continuous, detailed work that needs dedicated time and tooling.
    

When organisations do combine them

- Small organisations or teams with limited headcount often assign both titles to one person for practicality.
    
- For low‑risk, low‑volume datasets the combined role can be acceptable if clearly documented and monitored.
    
- Early maturity stages of a data programme frequently use combined roles as a transitional arrangement.
    

Risks of combining Data Owner + Data Steward

- Reduced governance separation: policy makers approving their own operational work can obscure accountability.
    
- Overload and inconsistent quality: a single person may struggle to do strategic decisions and maintain daily quality/metadata.
    
- Slower scaling: combining roles often becomes a bottleneck as data volume, user base, or cross‑team sharing grows.
    

When combining is OK (controls to mitigate risk)

- Document the arrangement and criteria (which datasets can be combined).
    
- Put oversight in place: periodic review by a data governance committee, external audit of access and quality, and peer review of policy decisions.
    
- Timeboxing and training: ensure the combined person has protected time for stewardship tasks and receives governance training.
    
- Escalation path: clear escalation to a higher manager for policy/risk decisions the steward cannot objectively approve.
    

Practical recommendation (quick checklist)

- If organisation size ≥ medium and data is shared/sensitive → separate roles.
    
- If small or just starting → combined role only for non‑sensitive datasets, with governance review every quarter.
    
- Always record RACI for each dataset: who is Accountable (Owner), Responsible (Steward), Consulted, and Informed.
