The process to formulate and implement National Adaptation Plans (NAPs) is organized into five main modules, which are designed to be iterative and are often addressed in parallel rather than strictly sequentially. These modules integrate the adaptation cycle with the global goal on adaptation (GGA) framework:

- **Module A: Impact, vulnerability and risk assessment (IVRA):** As mentioned in our previous exchange, this module focuses on framing adaptation using the GGA thematic targets, developing medium- and long-term climate and socio-economic scenarios, documenting observed hazards, and conducting comprehensive assessments of climate exposure, vulnerability, and risk.
- **Module B: Plan development:** This module involves outlining the country's development vision in a changing climate, synthesizing risk assessment data, and identifying and ranking adaptation options to address key vulnerabilities. It also includes estimating adaptation costs, organizing priorities into actionable projects, and compiling the final NAP for submission to the UNFCCC.
- **Module C: Financing and implementation:** This stage translates the plan into action by determining financing needs, mapping priority projects to appropriate funding windows, developing a 5-year implementation programme, and executing the adaptation solutions.
- **Module D: Monitoring, evaluation and learning and reporting:** This module establishes systems for continuous data collection to track the progress and effectiveness of adaptation actions. It involves periodic evaluation, capturing lessons learned, and fulfilling national and international reporting requirements.
- **Module E: Cross-cutting (Building readiness and accessing funding):** This module supports all other steps by strengthening institutional arrangements and regulatory frameworks, building a robust data and knowledge base, and accessing capacity-building support and readiness funding (such as from the Green Climate Fund) to maintain the overall NAP process.
