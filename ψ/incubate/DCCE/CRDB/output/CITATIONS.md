# Citation Reference Guide: Exhibition Roll-Up Series

A clean reference guide of organizations, policy reports, and published literature directly cited in each roll-up banner across the exhibition series.

## Series 1: Overview of Climate Change Risk in Thailand (5 Roll-ups)

### Roll-up 1: What is Climate Change Risk?
- **IPCC (2022)** — *Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability* (Working Group II, Sixth Assessment Report). Cambridge University Press.  
  *Evidence: Core risk framework (Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability) and response risk / maladaptation.*
- **IPCC (2021)** — *Climate Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis* (Working Group I, Sixth Assessment Report). Cambridge University Press.  
  *Evidence: Definition and taxonomy of Climatic Impact-Drivers (CIDs).*

### Roll-up 2: What Climate Hazards are Changing?
- **DCCE & UNDP (2026)** — *Thailand’s Second Biennial Transparency Report (BTR2) and Fifth National Communication (5NC): Adaptation Chapter*. Department of Climate Change and Environment & UNDP Thailand.  
  *Evidence: 2024 annual mean temperature anomaly (+1.1°C record); CMIP6 precipitation intensity and dry-spell projections; eastern Gulf sea-surface temperature peak (~32.7°C, May 2024).*
- **IPCC (2021)** — *Climate Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis* (Working Group I). Cambridge University Press.  
  *Evidence: Conceptual framing of compound extreme events and interacting climate impact-drivers.*

### Roll-up 3: Where Does Risk Concentrate in Thailand?
- **DCCE & UNDP (2026)** — *Thailand’s BTR2/5NC Adaptation Chapter*. Department of Climate Change and Environment & UNDP Thailand.  
  *Evidence: Regional risk profiles — 2024 Northern slope saturation/landslides (Chiang Rai, Chiang Mai); Chao Phraya flood recurrence shifts (2040–2059); Bangkok low elevation (0.5–1.5 m MSL) and subsidence; 2024 Southern monsoon flash floods.*
- **Regional Climatology Research Group (2019)** — *Influence of El Niño Southern Oscillation on precipitation variability in Northeast Thailand*. *Theoretical and Applied Climatology*, 137, 2215–2230.  
  *Evidence: Northeast precipitation range across 27 monitoring stations (1,112.3 mm to 1,972.8 mm).*
- **Bangkok Climate-Health Working Group (2023)** — *Urban heat stress and human health in Bangkok, Thailand*. *International Journal of Biometeorology*, 67, 1891–1904.  
  *Evidence: 62.7% of surveyed Bangkok commuters reporting severe heat disruption during travel.*
- **Coastal Studies Unit (2022)** — *Coastal Erosion and Flood Coping Mechanisms in Southern Thailand*. *Ocean & Coastal Management*, 225, 106214.  
  *Evidence: Nakhon Si Thammarat mangrove shoreline change rates (−66 m/yr to +16.4 m/yr).*
- **Trisurat, Y., et al. (2018)** — *Vulnerability to climate change of species in protected areas in Thailand*. *Global Ecology and Conservation*, 16, e00487.  
  *Evidence: Projected increase in threatened species in Thai protected areas from 11% to 35%–54% by 2070.*
- **UNFCCC WIM ExCom (2021)** — *Slow Onset Events: Technical Paper on Sea Level Rise and Coastal Loss & Damage*. Warsaw International Mechanism for Loss and Damage, UNFCCC.  
  *Evidence: Biophysical limits of natural barriers (mangroves, coral reefs, dunes) under sea-level rise.*

### Roll-up 4: How Do Risks Cascade Across Systems?
- **DCCE & GIZ (2020)** — *A Guidebook for Climate Impact Chains: Visualizing and Analyzing Cause-and-Effect Relationships for Adaptation Planning in Thailand*. Department of Climate Change and Environment & GIZ.  
  *Evidence: Three-step impact chain methodology: Hazard → Direct Impact → Cascading Consequence intersected with Exposure, Sensitivity, and Adaptive Capacity.*
- **DCCE & UNDP (2026)** — *Thailand’s BTR2/5NC Adaptation Chapter*. Department of Climate Change and Environment & UNDP Thailand.  
  *Evidence: Agricultural vulnerability baseline (45.49% of poor population in farming households); marine cascade baseline (86.6% of coral reefs bleached nationwide in May 2024).*
- **IPCC (2022)** — *Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability* (Working Group II). Cambridge University Press.  
  *Evidence: Cross-sectoral and cross-regional compounding risk dynamics.*

### Roll-up 5: How Can Thailand Manage Risk?
- **DCCE & UNDP (2026)** — *Thailand’s BTR2/5NC Adaptation Chapter*. Department of Climate Change and Environment & UNDP Thailand.  
  *Evidence: Four-stage iterative NAP cycle; operational status of the Spatial Climate Risk Database (CRDB); 6-sector coordination MoU; national M&E database and indicator integration gaps.*
- **IPCC (2022)** — *Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability* (Chapter 17: Decision-Making Options for Managing Risk). Cambridge University Press.  
  *Evidence: Criteria for evaluating adaptation effectiveness, trade-offs, and avoiding maladaptation.*

## Series 2: Global Goal on Adaptation (GGA) & Belém Indicators (2 Roll-ups)

### Roll-up 1: Global Goal on Adaptation & Thematic Targets
- **UNFCCC (2023/2025)** — *UAE Framework for Global Climate Resilience (Decision 2/CMA.5) & Belém Adaptation Indicators (CMA.7)*.  
  *Evidence: 7 Thematic Targets under Paris Agreement Article 7.1: Water (9a), Food/Agri (9b), Health (9c), Ecosystems (9d), Infrastructure (9e), Poverty (9f), Cultural Heritage (9g).*
- **GGGI & DCCE (2026)** — *GGA Indicators Technical Brief: Aligning Thailand’s National Adaptation Plan (NAP)*. Global Green Growth Institute & DCCE.  
  *Evidence: Alignment of global targets with Thailand's 6 NAP priority sectors.*

### Roll-up 2: Dimensional Targets & National M&E Platform Alignment
- **DCCE & GGGI (2026)** — *National Adaptation Indicator Crosswalk (101 Belém Indicators)*. Department of Climate Change and Environment & GGGI.  
  *Evidence: Four Dimensional Targets: Assessment (10a), Planning (10b), Implementation (10c), MEL (10d).*
- **TDRI & Chulalongkorn University (2026)** — *Technical Review of National Adaptation M&E Database Architecture and Line Agency Climate Literacy*.  
  *Evidence: Integration analysis of TDRI bottom-up action planning and national database platforms.*

## Series 3: Climate Adaptation Finance Architecture (2 Roll-ups)

### Roll-up 1: Global Climate Finance Landscape & Access Instruments
- **DCCE, Creagy & UNEP (2026)** — *Guidance and Recommendations on Accessing Climate Adaptation Finance*. Department of Climate Change and Environment.  
  *Evidence: Multilateral climate funds architecture (GCF, Adaptation Fund, GEF) and financing instruments (grants, concessional loans, equity, guarantees).*
- **Green Climate Fund (2024)** — *GCF Strategic Plan and Access Modalities: Simplified Approval Process (SAP) & Project Preparation Facility (PPF)*. Incheon, Republic of Korea.  
  *Evidence: GCF investment criteria, readiness support, and NDA endorsement requirements.*

### Roll-up 2: National Access Routes & Project Screening Workflow
- **DCCE (2026)** — *National Climate Adaptation Finance Access Pathways and TAP-PF Operational Guidelines*. Department of Climate Change and Environment.  
  *Evidence: Three national access routes (Direct Access, Multilateral Accredited Entity, Bilateral/Donor); Thailand Adaptation Project Preparation Facility (TAP-PF); 5-step project screening workflow.*
- **Adaptation Fund Board (2023)** — *Operational Policies and Guidelines for Parties to Access Resources from the Adaptation Fund*.  
  *Evidence: Direct access accreditation standards and national endorsement protocols.*

## Series 4: Climate Data Downscaling & Projection Platform (2 Roll-ups)

### Roll-up 1: Dynamical vs. Statistical Downscaling Mechanisms
- **CORDEX-SEA & RU-CORE (2024)** — *Multi-Model Regional Dynamical Downscaling over Thailand using RegCM and WRF (3–25 km resolution)*. Ramkhamhaeng University Center of Regional Climate Change.  
  *Evidence: Regional atmospheric physics, convective schemes, and multi-model ensemble dynamics for Southeast Asia.*
- **IPCC (2021)** — *Climate Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis* (Chapter 10: Linking Global to Regional Climate Information). Cambridge University Press.  
  *Evidence: Scientific evaluation of Empirical Statistical Downscaling (ESD) versus Regional Climate Models (RCMs).*

### Roll-up 2: DCCE Thailand Climate Projection Platform
- **DCCE (2026)** — *Thailand Climate Data Visualization Portal & Downscaled Climate Database*. Department of Climate Change and Environment.  
  *Evidence: Platform architecture (RegCM, WRF-Chem, Statistical Downscaling, Gridded Datasets) and 5-step data request workflow for provincial adaptation planning.*

## Series 5: Nature-Based Solutions for Climate Resilience (1 Roll-up)

### Roll-up 1: Nature-based Solutions (NbS) & Ecosystem-based Adaptation
- **UNEP (2022)** — *Resolution 5/5: Nature-based Solutions for Supporting Sustainable Development*. United Nations Environment Assembly (UNEA-5.2), Nairobi.  
  *Evidence: Intergovernmental definition of NbS and multilateral environmental-social safeguards.*
- **IUCN (2020)** — *Global Standard for Nature-based Solutions: A user-friendly framework for verification, design and scaling*. Gland, Switzerland.  
  *Evidence: 8 Criteria and 28 Indicators covering biodiversity net gain, economic viability, and adaptive governance.*
- **IPCC (2022)** — *Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability* (Chapter 2: Terrestrial and Freshwater Ecosystems and their Services). Cambridge University Press.  
  *Evidence: Biophysical limits of Ecosystem-based Adaptation (EbA) and risks of maladaptive afforestation.*
- **OECD (2021)** — *Infrastructure for a Climate-Resilient Future: The Role of Nature-based Solutions*. OECD Publishing, Paris.  
  *Evidence: Implementation barriers in public procurement, grey path dependencies, and appraisal standards.*
