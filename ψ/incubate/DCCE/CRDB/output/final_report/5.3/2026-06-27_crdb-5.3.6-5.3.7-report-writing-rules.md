## Purpose

This file defines the active writing rules for the CRDB technical report of sections 5.3.6 / 5.3.7 repair pass as a standalone report-writing reference. It exists separately from the session plan so that the rules remain visible, binding, and easy to apply during drafting and revision.

## Audience and document boundary

1. This is an audience-facing report.
2. This is a technical report. shallow summary is unexceptable
3. Do not expose repo-internal document names, internal filenames, handoff artifacts, trace files, or internal workflow references in the prose.
4. Do not write as if the reader has seen the planning notes, edit notes, raw extracts, or internal architecture discussions.
5. If traceability is needed, preserve it in separate internal files, not in the report prose itself.

## Core voice rules

1. Use grounded Thai report prose.
2. Keep the tone formal, analytical, and readable.
3. Do not rely on abstract prestige words when a concrete institutional or technical term is available.
4. Do not assume tacit knowledge. Name the agency role, workflow, artifact, or consequence directly when the meaning would otherwise remain implicit.
5. Prefer operational wording over abstraction.

## Evidence-to-paragraph rule

Every substantive paragraph should do one clear job and maintain one evidence-impact chain:

1. state the concrete finding, condition, or institutional fact,
2. show the operational consequence or limitation,
3. explain why that consequence matters for disaster reporting, assessment, or design.

If a paragraph cannot identify a concrete task, artifact, workflow, field type, or decision consequence, revise it.

## Anti-abstraction rules

1. Avoid abstract phrases that do not point to a blocked task, missing artifact, operational limit, or design implication.
2. Avoid vague standalone words such as “system” unless the exact system is named.
3. Avoid inflated framing such as “most important,” “advanced,” “seamless,” or “perfect.”
4. Avoid filler transitions if the paragraph can begin directly with the fact.

## Audience-facing language rules

1. Explain institutional roles before discussing technical consequences.
2. Explain technical differences in plain report language before using specialist terms.
3. Introduce English terms in parentheses only where they improve precision.
4. Do not overload a paragraph with multiple frameworks unless the comparison is the job of that paragraph.

## Section-specific guidance for this report

### Section 1
- Focus on DDPM authority, mandate, command structure, and coordination role.
- Do not let Section 1 drift into detailed field lists or form architecture.

### Section 2
- Focus on operational reporting and assessment workflow.
- Detailed discussion of forms, fields, and early-stage data practice belongs here, not in Section 1.

### Sections 3–4
- Keep methodology comparison functional.
- Explain what each framework is designed to do, what it cannot do, and why that matters for Thailand’s reporting architecture.

### Sections 5–6
- Translate analytical findings into concrete data-architecture consequences.
- Name tables, layers, record types, or workflow stages only when they clarify the report for an external audience.

## Final drafting checks

Before accepting any revised section, confirm that:

1. the prose does not expose internal file names or internal process scaffolding,
2. each paragraph contains a concrete evidence-impact chain,
3. institutional and technical terms are explicit enough for an informed external reader,
4. no section repeats the job of the section before it.
