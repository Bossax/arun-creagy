# Item 1 — Business Objective / Platform Rationale

## 1. Why This Platform Exists

DCCE's own TORs describe **two nested problems**, at two different altitudes. They should not be collapsed into one "why" — CRDB and TOR70 are answering different questions.

**The deeper problem (CRDB-TOR §1): Thailand has no national climate-adaptation data governance capability.** Climate adaptation knowledge relevant to Thailand exists, but it is scattered across foreign and domestic sources (NAP Global Network, Santiago Network, IPCC, Germanwatch, DCCE's own past reports and policy documents) with no framework to synthesize or compare it systematically. DCCE explicitly names the consequence: data products don't get built to meet actual user needs, because no one has systematically inventoried what data exists, who needs it, or where the gaps are (CRDB-TOR §1, §2.2). This is a **capability gap**, not a communication gap — DCCE cannot yet answer "what data do we have, who owns it, and what's missing" for its own domain.

**The surface problem (TOR70-TOR §1): the public has no central, modern place to see any of it.** Once that underlying data and governance exist (or exist well enough), DCCE's second problem is that there is no unified "Data Hub" dashboard — no central, well-designed, easy-to-use platform that pulls together data from multiple sources and presents it in formats non-specialists can use (interactive visualization, infographics, storytelling-style articles). Academic reports and large studies currently just sit as documents; nobody translates them into something a policymaker or the public can act on quickly (TOR70-TOR §1, §5.3.10).

**Section 5 confirms this reading in both TORs, in ways §1 leaves implicit:**
- CRDB-TOR §5.2.1's foreign-framework study (Japan's A-PLAT, Germany's Klimadapt, the EU) is a **website/information-architecture benchmark**, not governance-framework design — its output is the groundwork for the Sitemap (Item 4), not Item 1. It should not be read as evidence of governance-capability sequencing.
- `(project fact, not TOR)`: the actual data-governance framework — who owns which data domain — was only just proposed, for the first time, at the **July 2, 2026 focus group meeting**, and it currently covers domain ownership specifically, not a fuller framework. This is live, current evidence that the capability gap named in CRDB-TOR §1 is real and only now starting to close — it should be cited as project history, not inferred from TOR sequencing.
- TOR70-TOR §5.5 (CMS with a draft → review → approve → publish workflow, explicit reviewer/approver roles) and §5.4.13–§5.4.14 (RBAC, audit trail) reveal DCCE anticipated a **trust problem**, not just a display problem: content published under DCCE's name has to be checked before it goes live, and every change has to be attributable to a specific person. That's a safeguard against a fear that isn't stated outright in §1 but is clearly designed against in §5.

## 2. Who It Serves

The two TORs name meaningfully different audiences. TOR text alone leaves this unresolved, but it is resolved by direct project guidance, cited below as a project fact rather than a TOR claim.

**CRDB-TOR §3 — broad, institutional:**
- *Primary:* officials in other agencies working on National Adaptation Plan sectors; officials involved in project screening and budget review; officials developing climate information services; DCCE's own staff; academics and climate-change experts.
- *Secondary:* civil society and private-sector organizations; international organizations; financial institutions, state enterprises, and private-sector actors needing climate data for risk management.

**TOR70-TOR §3 — narrow, public-facing:** DCCE staff and the general public. Full stop.

TOR70's own scope of work (§5.10 dissemination activities) names a broader participant list for its outreach events — government agencies, the 6 NAP sectors, private sector, academia, cooperation networks, and "interested general public" — closer to CRDB-TOR's list than TOR70 §3's two-line target-group statement suggests. On TOR text alone this reads as unresolved: under-specified §3, or a genuinely narrower system audience than its own outreach process.

`(project fact, not TOR — from a meeting with Director Toey)`: this is resolved in practice. Director Toey has stated the platform's users will **not** be the general public, at least in the first phase — the audience is CRDB-TOR §3's institutional/specialist list, not TOR70 §3's public-facing framing. This means content can stay data-heavy and domain-specific, using specialist language, rather than being simplified for lay readers from the start. TOR70 §5.10's broader public-facing outreach activity should be read as a later-phase or dissemination-event concern, not evidence that the *platform itself* targets the general public now.

`(secondary sources + this session's conversation — not TOR)`: within CRDB-TOR §3's institutional list, phase 1 has two primary personas — one external, one internal — plus a wider set of secondary personas who are served but not the primary design target.

**Sectoral and area-based policymakers/authorities (primary external persona).** Job: use the platform to inform planning at a high level — risk screening and prioritization of where climate risk in their sector/area warrants deeper, dedicated study — while the site's general climate-adaptation content builds the conceptual grounding (adaptation cycle, key concepts) needed to interpret and act on that data. This persona splits by sophistication, because current products only support a high-level overview, not granular per-sector/area analysis, and no user-feedback/engagement loop exists yet to mature them toward that:
- *Advanced users* (analysts/technical staff within these authorities) benefit from having necessary datasets pooled in one place for the first time, and can self-serve deeper analysis on that pooled data even though the platform doesn't yet tailor it to their specific sector or area.
- *Less-advanced users* (generalist policy staff, decision-makers) use the existing high-level tools (Spatial Risk Database, Hazard/Exposure Map, Climate Risk Index) plus the general info/knowledge pages to understand, learn, and get inspiration for policy frameworks — not to extract tailored, decision-ready answers.

**Explicit phase-1 limitation:** the platform cannot yet deliver tailored, granular, per-sector/area support — that requires product maturation via user feedback/engagement that hasn't happened yet. This is a phase-1 ceiling, not a permanent one.

**Without it today:** no pooled, authoritative dataset location at all; ad hoc, contractor-commissioned studies with no prioritization signal for where to focus; scattered and inconsistent sources; no shared conceptual vocabulary to translate climate data into planning decisions.

**DCCE staff/analysts (primary internal persona).** Job: operate and maintain the 5 core products and A-BTR without contractor dependency (already anchored via Section 2b's self-sufficiency success criterion). **Without it today:** contractor-dependent updates; manual, resource-intensive A-BTR compilation.

**Secondary personas, phase 1 (served, not the primary design target):** DCCE leadership (consumers of the executive-summary site section); academics and climate-change experts (users of the data catalog and existing analytical tools); budget and project-screening officials. All are in scope per CRDB-TOR §3, but phase-1 products and site structure are not designed around their specific job-to-be-done the way they are for the two primary personas above.

## 2a. Scope & Phasing

`(secondary sources + this session's conversation — not TOR)`: this section departs from the TOR-only grounding above by design, per Boss's direction to bring in CRDB's communication decks to answer the scope/phasing questions the TORs alone can't settle. Sources: the Strategic Alignment Deck (`00_Strategy_Reports/2026-06-11-Strategic-Alignment-Deck-Final.md`, accepted by Director Toey), the human-reviewed TOR70 briefing deck (`2026-05-18_TOR-Review/Slide-Deck-...md`), and the Director Engagement Slide Flow (`05_Data_Management_Framework/Governance_RACI/DCCE_Director_Engagement_Slide_Flow_v2.2.md`).

**One platform, delivered in phases — not two competing products.** DCCE's long-term vision is a single Information Service Platform (the Data Hub) hosting a catalog of **8 core data products/services** (Strategic Alignment Deck, Slide 16), spanning spatial risk analysis, financial/budget decision-support evidence, loss-and-damage assessment, infrastructure-engineering variables, multi-hazard early warning, adaptation policy M&E, and uncertainty-communication standards. The two-altitude problem framing in Section 1 (governance capability vs. public-dashboard) describes the same platform at two levels of abstraction, not two separate products.

**Phase-1 scope (this contract) has three parts:**
1. **5 core build products** — 3 existing DCCE analytical tools (Spatial Risk Database, Hazard/Exposure Map, Climate Risk Index) mapping to catalog item 2, plus 2 new products with validated use cases: disaster-loss-statistics analysis (mapping to catalog item 4), and **A-BTR** (see below).
2. **General climate-adaptation website/knowledge content** — the synthesized articles, infographics, and storytelling content both TORs require (TOR70-TOR §5.3.10–§5.3.11).
3. **The Data Hub itself**, as the foundational platform infrastructure that future products and services get built on.

**A-BTR is a distinct service category, not a gap-fill.** A-BTR (the Adaptation Chapter of Thailand's Biennial Transparency Report, a UNFCCC reporting obligation) does not belong to the 8-item external service catalog — it is an **internal, mandate-driven compliance service**. DCCE has a legal obligation to compile this report every two years (next submission: November 2026); the current process is manual, data-gathering-intensive, and covers all 4 stages of the adaptation cycle. Building A-BTR as a platform service is justified independently of the 8-catalog list: statutory mandate, high current resource cost, and strategic value (it forces DCCE to retain and structure quality datasets that other future services can reuse). The rationale doc should name this as a second scope category — **external service catalog vs. internal mandate-compliance service** — rather than force A-BTR into the catalog it doesn't belong to.

**Catalog item 3 (financial/budget decision-support) is deliberately out of phase-1 scope**, not an oversight: feasibility is currently low because no official methodology or dataset exists yet to build it on. This should be named explicitly in Item 8 (Recommendations) as a future item pending methodology development.

**Catalog items 5–8 are deferred to a future phase, with no trigger condition defined yet.** No source — TOR, deck, or this session's conversation — specifies what would signal "start phase 2." That absence is itself the answer: phase-2 timing depends on DCCE's own internal decision-making, and **CRDB's role is to propose trigger options** (contract/procurement timing, governance-framework maturity, demand signal from a specific division, budget cycle), not to decide or assume one on DCCE's behalf.

## 2b. Vision & Success Criteria

`(secondary sources + this session's conversation — not TOR)`: same departure as Section 2a, additionally grounded in the DCCE-internal FGD3 focus-group deck (`00_Strategy_Reports/Slide-deck-FGD3-2-July-2026.md`, room-accepted 2026-07-02 but not yet sealed as agency strategy), CRDB's own draft metadata plan (`archive/Data_Catalog_and_Metadata_Management_Plan.md`), and the DGA interview summary (`archive/Interview summary notes/Interview Summary - DGA.md`).

**Long-term platform vision:** DCCE's Information Service Platform — a governed, authoritative national source of climate-adaptation data and knowledge — delivering validated analytics and policy-ready evidence across the full 8-product catalog to DCCE leadership, policymakers, operational agencies, and eventually the financial sector and the public, in direct service of DCCE's own core mandate as the country's climate information and knowledge hub (FGD3 Slide 11: *"DCCE's core output is information and knowledge"*).

**Phase-1 / TOR70 implementation vision:** by the end of phase 1, DCCE operates a Data Hub organized around the five site sections DCCE itself proposed at FGD3 — executive summary (risk issues leadership must manage); a data center for policymakers/planners (topic/detail-level access for policy and strategic planning); adaptation-cycle-stage support data (operational units get what they need at whichever stage of strategy/project development they're in); a knowledge/tools/service hub (home for the 5 core products); and news/announcements/contact — plus the five core data products: the 3 existing analytical tools (Spatial Risk Database, Hazard/Exposure Map, Climate Risk Index), strengthened through a shared governed data layer rather than siloed computation, and 2 new mandate/use-case-driven products (A-BTR reporting, disaster-loss-statistics). Audience: DCCE staff and institutional/specialist agency users — not yet general public, not yet budget-authority decision-support.

**Long-term success outcomes** (FGD3 Slide 25, DCCE's own stated expected results, not CRDB's invention):
- Systematic, cross-domain knowledge development; fast search and synthesis of climate data
- Comprehensive M&E of adaptation policy, measures, and investment
- Deep, sector-specific adaptation data services for all stakeholder groups
- More granular risk/vulnerability data; resilient-measure and adaptation-pathway planning support

**Phase-1 success criteria** (checkable now, not aspirational):
1. **Governance-capability closed** — domain-ownership framework moves from FGD3 room-acceptance to formal ratification (a divisional data-governance committee established, per FGD3's own 6-month roadmap, Slide 23); baseline inventory meets a quality-defined bar (completeness/accuracy/timeliness, per CRDB's own draft Data Quality Score) — not just the raw ≥100-dataset count the TOR70 gap-analysis deck already flagged as an inadequate metric.
2. **Metadata coverage gap closed** — the Data Hub's metadata standard explicitly covers PDFs, web content, and infographics, which DCCE's own internal IT metadata project (FGD3 Slide 12) does not.
3. **Compliance** — public data disclosure aligns with DGA's 2020 gazetted Open Data Standard.
4. **Data consistency** — the same indicator/figure shown across the 3 enhanced existing tools never disagrees, because it is computed once in the shared data layer (closes the "Frankenstein Dashboard" failure mode documented in the TOR70 gap-analysis deck).
5. **Operational self-sufficiency** — DCCE staff independently update phase-1 products' content/data via the CMS, no contractor involvement required.
6. **A-BTR delivers on its mandate use case** — DCCE's compliance team compiles the Adaptation Chapter directly from the Data Hub's structured, retained data, with measurably less manual effort than the current process, ahead of the biennial deadline.
7. **Disaster-loss-statistics on demand** — standardized loss/damage statistics generated per event/sector without ad hoc manual compilation.

`(open — deliberately deferred)`: an **adoption/usage metric** (e.g., number of agencies actively pulling from a product, request frequency) is intentionally left undecided. No source names one, and CRDB should not invent a number — this is DCCE's call, alongside the phase-2 trigger (Section 2a). It's noted here as a known open item, not silently dropped.

## 3. What's Lost Without It

Read as evidence rather than aspiration, DCCE's TORs show what they were afraid would happen, by what they explicitly built safeguards against:

- **Without the governance framework (CRDB-TOR §5.2–§5.3):** data stays fragmented and un-synthesized; agencies keep collecting and holding data independently with no shared inventory, so effort is duplicated and gaps go unnoticed. CRDB-TOR §2.2 names this outright as the reason a Gap Analysis and an Information Product & Baseline Data Inventory are required at all.
- **Without defined data ownership and an approval gate (TOR70-TOR §5.4.13–§5.4.14, §5.5.8–§5.5.9):** anyone could edit or publish under DCCE's name, with no accountable owner and no audit trail — a direct governance risk given DCCE is a government agency publishing to the public. The TOR's insistence on RBAC, an explicit draft→review→approve→publish state machine, and a full audit log is DCCE writing a defense against exactly that failure mode into the contract itself, not an incidental technical requirement.
- **Without mandatory training and handover (TOR70-TOR §5.9):** DCCE explicitly required on-the-job training for system administrators, content/data staff, and end users, plus delivered manuals — meaning DCCE anticipated the risk of receiving a system it doesn't know how to run once the consultant's contract ends, and built the requirement to prevent that into the TOR rather than leaving it to chance.
- **Without translation of academic/technical material into accessible formats (both TORs — CRDB-TOR §5.5, TOR70-TOR §5.3.10–§5.3.11):** the underlying research and data would continue to exist but stay unusable by the people who most need it — policymakers, other agencies, and the public — because it never gets converted out of report form.
- **Without in-house content and digital-asset management capability (TOR70-TOR §5.5):** DCCE staff would remain dependent on the contractor for every content or data update. The CMS gives staff **content and digital-asset management** capability directly, plus **no-code manual data ingestion and data-product adjustment** — staff can update a data product without engineering support. `(project fact, not TOR — from TOR70-development-of-climate-adaptation-databse-comments.md)`: the TOR never names a discrete "database administrator" role; it's inferred by combining §5.4.14's RBAC/permission-matrix requirement (which enumerates administrator, data-entry officer, reviewer, designer, executive, general public) with §5.5's requirement that "the administrator and related officers" manage data, datasets, content, and spatial data through the CMS. Functionally this is a **CMS/content-administration role** (datasets, metadata relationships, approval workflow, spatial records, dashboard config) — not literal database-infrastructure administration. Still open: which specific DCCE role/person holds it in practice is an org-assignment question for DCCE, not a TOR-text question.

## 2c. Constraints & Dependencies

`(secondary sources + this session's conversation — not TOR)`: beyond the audience, scope, vision, and persona decisions above, the rationale's remaining dependency is governance maturity — and CRDB already has a concrete answer on record, not a placeholder. CRDB's own draft roadmap (`05_Data_Management_Framework/Governance_RACI/Proposed-governance-plan-to-DCCE.md`), the same one referenced at FGD3 Slide 23, proposes governance as an **incremental rollout, not a one-time installation**:

- **Phase 1 (0–6 months) — foundation, within the division:** set data standards (metadata standard, security/classification levels, data models, glossary); assign named Data Owners (directors) who designate staff as Data Stewards; stand up a Data Governance Committee (the Owners, collectively); build the digital-asset inventory (Owners/Stewards survey what their teams hold). These four items are the minimum bar for Section 2b's success criterion #1 (governance-capability closed) to be checkable at all — without a ratified committee and a real inventory, that criterion has nothing to verify against.
- **Phase 2 (1 year+) — apply and expand, outward-facing:** launch the internal Data Catalog into daily use; embed governance requirements directly into future TORs, so external consultants are contractually bound to the standard; formalize data-sharing agreements with external agencies; expand technical data-management policy across the full climate-domain lifecycle. This phase is not a phase-1-platform dependency — it can mature in parallel with or after the platform ships.

CRDB's role stays consistent with Section 2a's phase-2 framing: **propose this roadmap, don't set DCCE's pace through it.** How fast DCCE moves from Phase 1 to Phase 2 is an internal decision this rationale doc cannot and should not predict.

## 5. Diagnosis: Current State of the Business Objective / Platform Rationale

Reading the two TORs together, and now cross-checked against live project facts, the rationale for this platform is **real and load-bearing, but only partially settled** — it is closer to a well-evidenced problem space than a decided product brief.

**What's solid:** the two-altitude structure holds up under scrutiny — a national data-governance capability gap (CRDB-TOR) sitting underneath a public-communication product gap (TOR70-TOR) — and it is not an artifact of over-reading the TOR text. If anything, correcting the §5.2.1 misattribution sharpens it: the foreign-framework study was always a Sitemap input, not a governance-framework design step, which means the governance framework has no TOR-mandated design process at all — it is being built ad hoc, and the July 2, 2026 focus group is the first real evidence it is happening. The capability gap named in CRDB-TOR §1 is therefore not hypothetical; it is actively, currently open.

**What just got resolved:** three of the four blocking gaps the PM-lens review flagged.
- **Audience/MVP** — Director Toey's guidance (general public excluded from phase 1; audience is the institutional/specialist list) means the platform does not need to solve for lay-public accessibility yet.
- **Scope & phasing** (Section 2a) — resolved via secondary sources (Strategic Alignment Deck, TOR70 briefing deck, Director Engagement Slide Flow) plus this session's conversation: one platform delivered in phases, phase-1 scope is the 5 core products + website content + Data Hub foundation, A-BTR is justified as a separate mandate-compliance service category, catalog item 3 is a deliberate feasibility-driven exclusion, and the phase-2 trigger is explicitly undefined — CRDB's job is to propose options, not decide one.
- **Vision & success criteria** (Section 2b) — resolved via the FGD3 focus-group deck (room-accepted, not yet sealed), CRDB's own draft metadata plan, and the DGA interview: a two-level vision (long-term catalog vs. phase-1 concrete site structure + products) and 7 checkable phase-1 success criteria, with the adoption/usage metric explicitly and deliberately left open rather than invented.
- **Personas** (Section 2) — audience moved from a flat role-title list to real personas with jobs and "without it" baselines: sectoral/area-based policymakers-authorities as the primary external persona (split by sophistication — advanced users self-serve pooled data, less-advanced users use existing high-level tools and info content), DCCE staff/analysts as the primary internal persona, and DCCE leadership/academics/budget officials named explicitly as served-but-secondary. Includes an explicit, stated phase-1 ceiling: no tailored per-sector/area support yet, pending a user-feedback loop that hasn't happened.
- **Constraints & dependencies** (Section 2c) — the database-administrator ambiguity is resolved to a functional definition (CMS/content-administration, not infrastructure DBA), and the open governance dependency now has a named, concrete answer: CRDB's own already-drafted 2-phase roadmap (Proposed-governance-plan-to-DCCE.md), not a vague "it depends" — with the pace of Phase 1 → Phase 2 explicitly left as DCCE's internal call, consistent with the phase-2-trigger pattern from Section 2a.
