# CRDB Business Service Example Plan

## Objective
Create one concrete, business-style example that shows how DCCE can act as a credible climate-data service provider, not just a governance writer.

## Chosen wedge
**Loss and Damage Economic Impact Briefing Service**

This is the strongest first example because the project evidence already shows repeated demand for:
- distinguishing relief payments from true economic loss,
- converting physical damage into economic categories,
- supporting budget, recovery, and macroeconomic decisions with defensible evidence.

## Why this wedge is defensible
- The service intelligence report defines **Service 4** as historical Loss and Damage assessment and states that compensation spending is not enough to understand true economic loss.
- The same report defines **Service 3** as financial and budget decision support through avoided-loss and cost-benefit logic.
- The MVD / Loss & Damage operational plan already exists and defines the implementation track, required outputs, and validation checkpoints.
- The deliverable registry points to canonical assets for this track, so the example can reuse hard evidence instead of inventing a new concept.

## Artifact to draft
Create a one-page example note with four blocks:

1. **Urgent use case**
   - A named customer problem.
   - Example: NESDC and DDPM need a way to translate disaster records into an evidence-ready economic loss briefing, because relief payments do not equal true loss.

2. **One measurable outcome**
   - Keep it to one outcome only.
   - Example: for one pilot event or one pilot geography, produce a briefing package that clearly separates relief payments, direct damage, indirect economic loss, and uncertainty notes.
   - Acceptance criteria:
     - names the data sources and owners,
     - separates at least three loss categories,
     - states what cannot yet be estimated and why.

3. **One service package**
   - Event context brief.
   - Loss-category mapping table.
   - Proxy-value calculation template.
   - Uncertainty / limitation note.
   - Stewardship and metadata sheet.

4. **Willingness-to-adopt test**
   - NESDC: would this help macroeconomic reporting or budget justification?
   - DDPM: would this reduce ambiguity in converting damage records into economic categories?
   - DCCE division leadership: would they assign an owner or steward for the service lifecycle?

## Plan of work
1. Write the use-case paragraph in plain business language.
2. Define the measurable outcome as a decision result, not a platform metric.
3. Specify the minimum service package as a repeatable briefing bundle.
4. Add a short adoption rubric that records whether stakeholders:
   - name a focal unit,
   - agree to review a pilot package,
   - supply one dataset or one assumption,
   - request the package for a real reporting event.
5. Add a short business critique explaining why this is a good market-entry wedge and what failure modes remain.

## Why this is the right first example
- It is narrow enough to be credible.
- It is useful enough to matter to real decision-makers.
- It reuses already sealed CRDB assets.
- It demonstrates DCCE as a service provider with a clear customer problem, a measurable result, and an adoption test.

## Success condition
The example succeeds if a director can answer all three questions in under five minutes:
1. who needs this first,
2. what decision it improves,
3. what DCCE must own or steward to make it credible.
