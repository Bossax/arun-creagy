## 📖 Executive Summary & Scientific Narrative

This study presents a national-level **Climate Change Risk Assessment (CCRA)** for Thailand's tourism sector, aligned with the **IPCC AR5 framework** (Risk = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability). It evaluates how past climate variability and future projections threaten key "Values at Risk" (economic revenue, visitor safety, comfort, asset protection, and natural resources). 

Tourism is a macro-economic driver for Thailand, representing over **15% of national GDP**. However, because **88% of arrivals are leisure holidaymakers** (who are highly flexible and sensitive to conditions), the country's economic dependence on tourism represents a significant climate exposure. The study combines global index ratings (where Thailand sits in the highest risk quintile) with localized stakeholder workshops and a detailed survey of **193 accommodation businesses** on Koh Tao and Koh Phi Phi to map climate impacts from the national scale down to specific island destinations.

## 🔬 Key Scientific Insights (How Thailand's Tourism is Impacted)

### 1. Dual Island Vulnerability Profiles (Koh Phi Phi vs. Koh Tao)
*   **The Science**: Climate exposure is heavily shaped by local topography and resource availability. 
*   **Koh Phi Phi (Physical Exposure)**: A 12.25 km² island where nearly all resort infrastructure is concentrated on a low, narrow sandy isthmus. This was demonstrated during the 2004 Tsunami when 6-meter waves destroyed 70% of guest rooms. Despite this risk, infrastructure has doubled to support up to 10,000 visitors daily, vastly increasing physical exposure to storm surges and sea-level rise.
*   **Koh Tao (Resource & Economic Exposure)**: A 21 km² granitic island rising to 379 meters. While its elevated topography shields hotels from direct coastal flooding, its economy is intensely vulnerable due to a near-total dependence on marine dive tourism (highly exposed to coral bleaching and ocean acidification) and chronic freshwater shortages.

### 2. Disappearing Beaches & Accelerated Sea Level Rise
*   **The Science**: Sea level rise (SLR) in the Gulf of Thailand was recorded at **3 to 5 mm per year** (1993–2008), which is 3 to 5 times the global average rate.
*   **The Impact**: By 2100, Thailand's sandy coastlines are projected to lose between **45.8% (low-emission, RCP2.6)** and **71.8% (high-emission, RCP8.5)** of their beach areas. This threat implies that between **8 and 23 of the country's 51 sandy beach zones could disappear completely**, eroding the primary asset of Thailand's coastal tourism economy.

### 3. Immediate Stresses on Island Accommodations (The 193-Hotel Survey)
*   **The Science**: The survey of 193 accommodation suppliers across Koh Tao and Koh Phi Phi shows that climate change is already a current business constraint, not a distant threat.
*   **Key Business Impacts**:
    *   **81.3%** report negative impacts from a perceived reduction of natural beauty.
    *   **78.7%** report impacts from changing weather seasons.
    *   **75.6%** have suffered storm or wind damage to their premises.
    *   **69.9%** experience chronic freshwater shortages.
    *   **60.6%** suffer from floods/extreme rain events.
    *   **58.6%** report operational/demand impacts from coral bleaching.

### 4. Northern/Central Mainland Extremes: Access and Activity Blockages
*   **Extreme Heat (>42°C)**: Historical destinations in Sukhothai, Tak, and Phitsanulok face temperatures exceeding 42°C, making daytime outdoor activities uncomfortable for visitors and degrading destination competitiveness.
*   **Decline in Cold Period**: Northern provinces face a projected decline in the "cold period" (days <16°C) from 2–2.5 months to just 1–2.5 months by mid-century, directly impacting winter peak tourism in areas like Chiang Rai.
*   **Access Blockages (Landslides)**: Intense rainfall is triggering landslides that block roads in Mae Hong Son, Uttaradit, and Phetchabun, completely cutting off tourist access. A landslide at Phu Chi Fa (Chiang Rai) damaged resort infrastructure and decreased visitor confidence.
