# Verified Facts: ART08_Tourism_Risk_GIZ

## 1. Direct Physical Threats to Tourist Safety and Demand
- **Direct Hazards:** Tropical storms (e.g., Pabuk in 2019), heatwaves, flooding, landslides, forest fires, and poisonous jellyfish outbreaks (e.g., Portuguese Man-of-War) pose direct physical threats to tourist safety and comfort.
- **Impact on Demand:** The report explicitly states that reputational damage from extreme weather events leads to "reduced demand."
- **Example:** During Tropical Storm Pabuk, 30,000 to 50,000 tourists evacuated island resorts, and subsequent visitors "avoided travelling to the affected areas."

## 2. Natural Capital Degradation and the Eco-Economic Cascade
- **Coral Bleaching Projections:** Thailand will face "annual severe bleaching" by 2046 under a high-emission scenario. Past severe events include the Andaman Sea (2016) and Gulf of Thailand (2018).
- **Coastal Erosion:** 830 km of coastline is currently eroding. Between 8 and 23 out of 51 sandy beach zones are projected to completely disappear by 2100.
- **Inland Droughts:** Natural attractions like the Phra Sadet and Than Thip waterfalls dried up completely during the 2016 drought.
- **Eco-Economic Cascade Example:** The 2016 Andaman Sea bleaching event led to the closure of 10 beaches, causing a direct "income loss for tourism supply chain in the areas."

## 3. Vulnerability and Maladaptation
- **Resource Constraints:** Koh Tao and Koh Phi Phi are highly dependent on limited freshwater reserves, making them extremely vulnerable to climate-induced droughts.
- **Maladaptive Practices:** Coping with hotter temperatures by increasing air conditioning use leads to higher business costs and creates a "maladaptive effect in terms of increased carbon emissions." Note: The report's survey of 193 providers did not detail specific adaptation measures like insurance or private water treatment, focusing instead on observed impacts.

## 4. Urgent Structural Recommendations
- **Systemic Reform:** The sector must "rapidly improve the sustainability of tourism generally to reduce pressure on social and environmental systems."
- **Addressing Root Causes:** The report stresses that climate hazards exacerbate risks driven by human action and "uncontrolled tourism growth."
- **No-Regret Measures:** Addressing non-climate risks (e.g., limiting deforestation to reduce landslides, minimizing water pollution to aid coral recovery) is a critical "no-regret measure."
- **Reframing Adaptation:** Adaptation must be framed as "good practice, long-term planning and business competitiveness," not an additional environmental task.
