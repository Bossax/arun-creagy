# Phase 2: Topic Selection & Decision Log - ART10_WIM_ExCom_Sea_Level_Rise

## 1. Executive Summary
This document is a technical policy guide prepared by the Warsaw International Mechanism for Loss and Damage (WIM ExCom). It focuses on assessing risks and implementing approaches—categorized into Protect, Accommodate, and Retreat—to address loss and damage associated with the cascading impacts of sea level rise in vulnerable developing countries. The guide highlights critical thresholds, projected sea level changes under different emission scenarios, and offers a comprehensive toolkit for managing retreat and securing adaptation finance.

## 2. Key Scientific Insights & Local Relevance
- **Accelerating Hazards:** Global mean sea level rise is accelerating (from 2.13 mm/year to 4.77 mm/year), with high-emission scenarios projecting up to a meter of rise by 2100. This poses existential threats to low-elevation coastal zones (LECZs) where 900 million people currently live.
- **Cascading Impacts:** Sea level rise acts as a systemic risk multiplier. Initial hazards like flooding and saltwater intrusion cascade into supply chain breakdowns, public health crises, and severe non-economic losses (e.g., displacement, loss of cultural heritage, and exacerbated gender vulnerabilities).
- **Adaptation & Tipping Points:** While Nature-based Solutions (NbS) offer cost-effective accommodation, policymakers must identify "tipping points" where these approaches fail. When protection is no longer viable, Managed Retreat becomes necessary—a highly complex and expensive intervention (USD 10k–100k per person) requiring robust legal tools and international finance (e.g., GCF, Loss & Damage Fund).
- **Relevance to Thailand:** Although the report focuses on global archetypes (urban atolls, Arctic, large deltas), its frameworks are highly applicable to Thailand's coastal risks (e.g., the Chao Phraya delta, Gulf of Thailand erosion). The emphasis on integrating social protection, MEL (Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning), and NbS into National Adaptation Plans provides a strong policy blueprint for the country.

## 4. Candidate Narrative Directions (AI Suggestions)
*Review these 3 distinct storytelling angles to help frame the article. You can select one, combine them, or create your own below.*

**Option A: The Tipping Point & The Inevitability of Retreat**
- **Focus Issues:** E01, E02, E04, E09
- **Hook:** Sea level rise is accelerating (4.77 mm/year), threatening the 900 million people living in low-elevation coastal zones.
- **Body:** Emphasize that while hard structures like seawalls buy us time, they have physical limits against extreme 2100 scenarios. When protection fails, cascading impacts hit supply chains and livelihoods.
- **Conclusion:** Introduce "Managed Retreat" as a costly (USD 10k–100k per person) but increasingly necessary reality that requires proactive, equitable policy planning today.

**Option B: The Invisible Crisis: Social Fabric & Non-Economic Loss**
- **Focus Issues:** E04, E05, E06, E08
- **Hook:** Sea level rise isn't just about submerged land; it's a systemic risk multiplier that destroys human dignity and social cohesion.
- **Body:** Focus on the human cost: forced displacement, the loss of cultural heritage, and severe mental health trauma (PTSD). Highlight how this disproportionately burdens marginalized groups and exacerbates gender inequalities.
- **Conclusion:** Advocate for the urgent need to integrate social and financial protection mechanisms (cash transfers, microinsurance) into national adaptation plans to shield the most vulnerable.

**Option C: Working with Water: The Power of Nature-Based Solutions**
- **Focus Issues:** E01, E03, E07, E08
- **Hook:** As the ocean rises, our most cost-effective and resilient defense may be the natural ecosystems we have neglected.
- **Body:** Contrast the accelerating risks against the power of Ecosystem-based Adaptation (NbS). Detail how restoring mangroves, salt marshes, and reefs (Accommodate) buffers storm surges while providing co-benefits like carbon sequestration and fisheries support.
- **Conclusion:** Push for scaling up adaptation finance to fund local ecosystem restoration and integrating "blue infrastructure" into Thailand's climate strategy.
