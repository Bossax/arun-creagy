# Verified Facts: ART10_WIM_ExCom_Sea_Level_Rise

## 1. Report Background (WIM ExCom)
- **Origin & Purpose:** Prepared by the Expert Group on Slow Onset Events of the Executive Committee of the Warsaw International Mechanism for Loss and Damage (WIM ExCom).
- **Goal:** To establish a common understanding for managing the systemic impacts of slow onset events and to assist developing countries in integrating sea-level-rise-related loss and damage responses into national planning.

## 2. Nature-Based Solutions (NbS) Tipping Points
- **Physical Limits:** NbS like mangroves, coral reefs, and sand dunes are bound by physical constraints (habitat width, water depth). If sea levels rise too high, these natural barriers lose their protective capabilities.
- **Environmental Failures:** Saltwater intrusion in equatorial regions threatens mangrove survival. Ocean acidification and higher salinity lead to habitat loss and erosion of coral reefs. 
- **Tipping Points:** Monitoring systems must actively identify tipping points where NbS become ineffective due to biophysical limits being breached.

## 3. Concrete Non-Economic Loss and Damage (NELD)
- **Cultural Loss (Forced Migration):** In Small Island Developing States (SIDS), coastal inundation submerges traditional burial sites, altering practices and diminishing cultural identity. Indigenous communities relocated from sacred grounds face irreversible loss of traditional ecological knowledge and spiritual well-being.
- **Gender Inequality:** In Bangladesh, women face heightened mortality due to inability to swim (constrained by traditional clothing) and refusal to use safety shelters (fear of sexual assault). In Fiji, women faced heightened exposure to domestic abuse and human trafficking post-disaster.
- **Mental Trauma:** Women suffer disproportionately higher rates of PTSD and anxiety following coastal disasters, compounded by their caregiving roles. Forced relocation triggers severe psychosocial anxiety and diminished perceived safety.

## 4. Policy Evolution for Managed Retreat
- **Policy Shift:** Retreat must evolve from a "last resort" to a well-governed, inclusive strategy prioritizing human rights. Relocation requires formal social protection mechanisms (social transfers, housing support, livelihood diversification).
- **Funding & Costs:** Relocation costs range from USD 10,000 per person (Fiji) to USD 100,000 per person (US). Funding requires leveraging Multilateral Climate Funds (e.g., GCF, Fund for responding to Loss and Damage) and domestic taxation.
- **The Fiji Model:** Fiji serves as the global pioneer with a comprehensive package: 2018 Planned Relocation Guidelines, 2021 Climate Change Act, and the world's first Climate Relocation of Communities Trust Fund, sustainably funded by taking 3% from the national Environment and Climate Adaptation Levy.
