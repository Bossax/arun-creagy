# Verified Facts: ART07_Urban_Heat_Stress_Bangkok

## 1. Survey Percentages on Heat Interference
- **Daily travel:** 27.9% reported Moderate interference, 37.2% Quite a lot, and 25.5% Almost every day. (A combined 62.7% face severe daily travel interference).
- **Work:** 31.5% Moderate, 36.2% Quite a lot, 22.4% Almost every day.
- **Exercise:** 35.2% Moderate, 34.1% Quite a lot, 14.1% Almost every day.

## 2. Health and Well-being Impacts
- **Physical health (energy level issues):** 39.0% reported Moderate problems, 16.4% Quite a lot.
- **Mental health (emotional problems):** 58.2% reported Slight problems, 31.1% Moderate.
- **Life satisfaction:** 49.5% reported Good, 31.1% Fair, 4.8% Poor, 1.6% Very poor.

## 3. The Paradox of Air-Conditioning (AC)
- **High Prevalence:** 91.2% of respondents have AC at home, and 74.6% at work.
- **Limited Benefit:** AC at home significantly helps only with sleeping, but fails to reduce heat stress during daily travel or exercise.
- **Negative Externality:** Relying on individual AC is "counter-productive" because the increased energy consumption "will contribute further to increase UHI [Urban Heat Island] intensity."
- **Disproportionate Burden:** Low-income populations bear the brunt of ambient heat and externalized UHI effects, as they spend more time outdoors in traffic and rely on non-air-conditioned public buses, which remain their most affordable transit option.

## 4. Systemic and Policy Interventions
- **Urban Planning:** Adaptation must shift to the city level. Integrated strategies are needed over individual AC reliance.
- **Green Spaces:** Creation and protection of green open spaces directly lower UHI intensity and heat stress.
- **Green Building Codes:** Implementing zoning policies that require rooftop gardens, offer floor area bonuses to developers for indoor green open spaces, and promote specialized building materials that reduce city temperatures.
- **Public Health:** Improved heat early warning systems and comprehensive emergency response planning.
- **Governance:** Effective management requires systematic collaboration at both the city and national levels.
