## 📖 Executive Summary & Scientific Narrative

This study analyzes the 30-year historical relationship (1993–2022) between the **El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO)** sea surface temperature anomalies (SSTA) across four Pacific regions and **monthly precipitation anomalies (PPTA)** in **Northeast Thailand (Isan/Korat Plateau)**. 

The core takeaway is that ENSO is a primary driver of interannual rainfall variability in Northeast Thailand, but the relationship is **highly asymmetrical** (La Niña dominates) and operates with a **clear, predictable time lag**.

---

## 🔬 Key Scientific Insights (How Northeast Thailand is Impacted)

### 1. The 4-to-5 Month Predictability Window (Time Lags)
*   **The Science**: The atmospheric and oceanic changes in the equatorial Pacific Ocean do not affect Thailand's weather immediately. The negative correlation between Niño indices and rainfall peaks at a **4 to 5-month delay**.
*   **Specifics**: Peak delay is **5 months** for Niño 1+2 and Niño 3, and **4 months** for Niño 3.4 and Niño 4.
*   **Hazards Implication**: This lag provides a crucial **predictability window**. Agricultural and water resource managers have a 4-to-5 month lead time to adjust irrigation plans and reservoir levels once a strong El Niño or La Niña signature is detected in the Pacific.

### 2. The Asymmetrical Dominance of Strong La Niña
*   **The Science**: La Niña has a far more pronounced and statistically significant impact on precipitation patterns in Northeast Thailand than El Niño. While El Niño correlates with moderate dry spells, a **strong La Niña phase drives massive surges in rainfall** (highly correlated, e.g., $r = 0.76$ for Niño 1+2, and up to $-0.65$ for the other regions).
*   **Hazards Implication**: The region is highly vulnerable to extreme wet hazards during strong La Niña phases, whereas weaker La Niña phases or El Niño phases have a more moderate and scattered influence.

### 3. Historical Extremes & Rainfall Swing
*   **The Baseline**: The region's mean annual rainfall is **1,470.8 mm**, but it swings dramatically depending on ENSO.
*   **The Deluge (+34.13%)**: During the strong La Niña year of **2017**, rainfall peaked at **1,972.8 mm** (an extra 502 mm of water), triggering widespread flooding and agricultural damage.
*   **The Drought (-24.38%)**: During the low-rainfall El Niño year of **1993**, rainfall dropped to **1,112.3 mm**, causing severe water scarcity, crop failures, and increased forest fire risks.

### 4. Wavelet Periodicity (2-to-7 Year Cycles)
*   **The Science**: Wavelet coherence shows that ENSO's influence on local rainfall is strongest in the **2-to-7 year band** (matching natural ENSO cycles) but also strongly modulates annual and semi-annual seasonal cycles (0.5 to 1-year band).
*   **Hazards Implication**: Planning cannot rely on "average" annual rainfall; water management must adapt to a fluctuating 2-to-7 year wet-and-dry cycle.
