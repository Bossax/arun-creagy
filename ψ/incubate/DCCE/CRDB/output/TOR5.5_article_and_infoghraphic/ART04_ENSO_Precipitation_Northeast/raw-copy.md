# Step 2A
**1. Relationship between Niño regions and precipitation anomalies (PPTA)** In general, there is a **negative correlation** between Sea Surface Temperature Anomalies (SSTA) in the Niño regions and precipitation anomalies in Northeast Thailand; when the Niño indices are high (indicating El Niño), precipitation tends to decrease, and when they are low (indicating La Niña), precipitation tends to increase. However, this relationship varies spatially during extreme events: during a Strong La Niña, the **Niño 1+2 region exhibits a strong positive relationship** (precipitation increases as the index rises), whereas the **Niño 3, 3.4, and 4 regions show a moderate to strong negative relationship**.

**2. Effect of time lag (0 to 12 months) on the relationships** The influence of ENSO on precipitation is delayed due to the propagation of oceanic and atmospheric waves. The negative correlation between the indices and PPTA **gets stronger as the time lag lengthens, peaking at around a 4 to 5-month delay**. Specifically, the peak negative correlation occurs at a **5-month lag for the Niño 1+2 and Niño 3 regions**, and at a **4-month lag for the Niño 3.4 and Niño 4 regions**.

**3. Influence of different strengths of El Niño and La Niña** **La Niña events have a much more pronounced effect** on precipitation patterns in Northeast Thailand compared to El Niño events. A **strong La Niña phase significantly increases precipitation**, demonstrating strong correlation coefficients across all Niño regions (0.76 for Niño 1+2; and -0.64, -0.50, and -0.65 for Niño 3, 3.4, and 4, respectively). In contrast, all severities of El Niño (weak, moderate, strong) and the weaker phases of La Niña (weak, moderate) only display a **low-to-moderate correlation** with precipitation anomalies.

**4. Key findings regarding periodicity and cycles (Wavelet Coherence)** The Wavelet Transform Coherence (WTC) analysis demonstrates that the relationship between ENSO signals and PPTA is highly dependent on specific times and frequencies. The most significant coherence between the two variables is found at **periodicities of 2 to 7 years**, which perfectly mirrors the natural 2 to 7-year cycle of ENSO events and their subsequent shifts in atmospheric and monsoon circulation. Additionally, the analysis detected periods of strong coherence at shorter **0.5 to 1-year intervals**, indicating that ENSO also heavily modulates semi-annual and annual seasonal precipitation cycles.

# Step 3A
**1. Limitations and Data Gaps**

- **Data Coverage and Quality**: The study relies on available meteorological data from 27 stations, which inherently presents **limitations in coverage, accuracy, and consistency** throughout the 30-year study period.
- **Time Period Constraints**: The authors note that an extended research duration would have provided a deeper understanding of the ENSO-climate relationship, but they were restricted to the 1993–2022 timeframe due to **limited data on climate variables**.
- **Geographic Focus**: The findings are explicitly noted to be **specific only to the Northeast region of Thailand**, meaning they may not represent the climate dynamics of the entire country.
- **Dataset Preprocessing**: The raw collected precipitation (PPT) data exhibited **missing values, errors, and inconsistencies**. To resolve this, the authors used a Multi Linear Regression (MLR) technique to estimate and impute the missing data using information from nearby stations.

**2. Statistical Parameters of the Methodology**

- **Pearson Correlation Rules**: The correlation coefficient ranges from -1 to 1; values approaching 1 indicate a direct linear relationship, values approaching -1 indicate an opposite (negative) relationship, and values near 0 indicate no relationship. The study utilizes statistical significance thresholds of **5% (denoted by *)** and **1% (denoted by **)**.
- **Wavelet Transform Parameters**: The study uses the **Morlet wavelet** as the mother reference wavelet because it balances temporal and frequency localization. It applies a central frequency of **$\omega_0 = 6$ radians per unit time** to satisfy the required admissibility condition.
- **Wavelet Coherence Significance**: To establish statistical significance in the wavelet coherence spectrum, the authors used **Monte Carlo simulations**. The plotted Cone of Influence (COI) represents a **95% significance level** against background noise. Additionally, only strong coherence vectors where **$R^2_n > 0.7$** are explicitly marked with arrows on the spectrum.

**3. Author Warnings, Recommendations, and Future Research Needs**

- **Warnings**: The authors warn that El Niño phases severely reduce rainfall, which **exacerbates drought conditions, reduces water availability, and increases the risk of forest fires**. Conversely, strong La Niña phases cause significant increases in precipitation that, while beneficial for crops, pose **high risks of flooding and related hazards**.
- **Recommendations for Planning**: The authors recommend using ENSO forecasts to predict agricultural outputs so that Thai farmers and planners can **develop better tactics to deal with extreme weather**. Understanding ENSO variability is crucial for mitigating adverse impacts and **enhancing the resilience of local communities and economies**.
- **Future Research Needs**: Future studies should seek to expand on these findings by **incorporating more extensive datasets and utilizing advanced analytical methods**. This will help refine climate predictions and better inform policy decisions necessary for sustainable development in Northeast Thailand and similar regions globally.
