
### Slide 1: The Starting Line — Why Are We Talking About "Governance"?

- **The Message**: Next year, the DCCE is launching a major national climate platform (NCAIF). But a platform is just empty code without human rules to run it. Today, we are focusing strictly on our institutional roles and mandates, not technical IT coding.
    
- **Background Build-up**: Establishes a clear timeline (platform next year) and removes immediate anxiety about technical data engineering.
    
- **Transition Logic**: _“Before we look at the platform itself, let’s take a step back and look at what our department actually does every single day.”_
    

### Slide 2: Our True Business — We Work in the "Knowledge Sector"

- **The Message**: Unlike other ministries, the DCCE doesn't build physical roads or infrastructure. Our main outputs are knowledge, data, and environmental reports. Everything we create is a digital asset.
    
- **Background Build-up**: Connects their day-to-day work (environmental science and public information) to the core concept of "data assets".
    
- **Transition Logic**: _“Because our primary value is knowledge, our biggest challenge isn't producing it—it's knowing what we already have.”_
    

### Slide 3: The Current Pain Point — Hidden Data & Dead Ends

- **The Message**: Right now, we lack a shared central inventory (Data Catalog) of our products. Our knowledge sits in isolated silos. We constantly have to call large meetings just to ask, _"Who holds this Excel sheet?"_ This leaves our directors and the public blind to the true capacity and value of our department.
    
- **Background Build-up**: Validates their real-world experience (e.g., why FGD 1 had to happen just to find out who owned what data).
    
- **Transition Logic**: _“To fix this, we need a simple way to separate the technology from the actual content. Let’s look at it like a local marketplace.”_
    

### Slide 4: The Market Analogy — Separating the Stall from the Goods

- **The Message**: Think of the new NCAIF platform as a central marketplace. The Central Database Group acts as the market owner—they build the stalls, turn on the lights, and maintain the infrastructure. But each individual division is the merchant. You own the products (the risk data, profiles, and maps) on your shelves.
    
- **Background Build-up**: Demystifies "platform architecture" by replacing it with a familiar commercial structure. It reassures staff that IT won't steal or alter their specialized environmental work.
    
- **Transition Logic**: _“Now, how do we organize this market so users can actually find what they need? We draw a map.”_
    

### Slide 5: The Conceptual Data Model — Zoning the Forest

- **The Message**: We use a "Conceptual Data Model" (CDM) as our master map. Before you build a town, you map the rivers and forests first. The CDM just draws the big boundaries—identifying where our "bamboo forests" or "wetlands" of data sit. We are establishing broad climate domains; we are not counting every individual tree or trail yet.
    
- **Background Build-up**: Leverages their environmental science background to explain an abstract data mapping concept.
    
- **Transition Logic**: _“Once the map is drawn and the zones are clear, we need to assign park rangers to look after each zone.”_
    

### Slide 6: The 3 Human Roles — Who Guards the Ecosystem?

- **The Message**: To keep our data alive and accurate, we divide responsibilities into three simple human roles:
    
    - **Data Owner (The Director)**: Holds the official mandate, sets broad policy, and makes high-level decisions.
        
    - **Data Steward (The Staff/Ranger)**: The operational staff who understands the data's true context, how it was collected, and why it's useful.
        
    - **Technical Custodian (IT Support)**: Handles the backend storage and technical upkeep.
        
- **Background Build-up**: Breaks down data governance into clear organizational hierarchy. It explicitly shows that a Data Steward’s job relies on environmental context (e.g., knowing if population data came from a survey or a civil registry), not coding skills.
    
- **Transition Logic**: _“By stepping into these roles, we change how we deal with outside contractors entirely.”_
    

### Slide 7: Our Next Chapter — Becoming 100% "Product Owners"

- **The Message**: Moving into the 2027 implementation phase, the DCCE must act as the absolute Product Owner. We define the real-world policy demands and user requirements. The external contractors simply build the technical systems to match our instructions.
    
- **The New Habit**: To make this sustainable, the days of sharing standalone, un-indexed PDFs or mystery Excel files are over. Every piece of knowledge we produce must be organized, shareable, and clearly documented.
    
- **Background Build-up**: Concludes with a strong, empowering call to action that outlines their long-term professional value.
