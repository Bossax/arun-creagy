## Direct answers

### BRD-Q1 — Standard pre-design phases and their artifacts
Standard practice (per the sources used) sequences five phases before physical system design: (1) **Data Strategy & Strategic Alignment** → roadmap/modernization plan, AI & analytics strategy; (2) **Data Governance & Policy Definition** → governance framework, ownership/stewardship model, data policies + business glossary; (3) **Requirements Discovery & Specification** → BRD, PRD, System Requirements Document; (4) **End-User Validation & Visual Prototyping** → prototypes/diagrams, validated sample data, formal sign-off; (5) **Target Data Architecture** → architecture blueprint, technology stack definition [Q1, business-requirement-sw-dev]. The cited discipline table explicitly separates "data governance" deliverables (governance framework, ownership model, policies, **business glossary**) from "data architecture" deliverables (blueprint, tech stack) — i.e., glossary is classified with governance, not with the conceptual/data model [Q1, business-requirement-sw-dev].

### BRD-Q2 — Minimum content of a use case & demand analysis; validated vs. descriptive
Minimum content: problem statement + measurable business impact, actor/persona profiles + access patterns, as-is vs. proposed process flow + semantic data understanding, prioritized functional requirements (Critical/High/Medium/Low/Future), NFRs/constraints, and explicit testable acceptance criteria [Q2, business-requirement-sw-dev]. The validated/descriptive distinction is evidenced, not merely asserted: a **descriptive** use case rests on unverified stakeholder wishes and untestable phrasing ("should be easy to use"); a **validated** use case requires end-users to review representative sample data in the expected output format, observed real access patterns, and a recorded formal sign-off — and transformation logic must not start before that sign-off [Q2, business-requirement-sw-dev].

### BRD-Q3 — Structure, evidence base, format of gap analysis & recommendations
Structure: executive summary → current-state ("as-is") profile → target-state ("to-be") design → dimensional gap analysis → strategic recommendations → implementation roadmap with cost/benefit [Q3, business-requirement-sw-dev]. Evidence base must combine quantitative operational metrics (data quality audits, latency, cost), stakeholder/end-user interview evidence, systematic conformance/requirement matrices, and explicit business-impact/risk framing [Q3, business-requirement-sw-dev]. Format: dimension-scored matrices (1–5 scale comparing as-is vs. to-be per dimension), visual data-flow/architecture diagrams, and prioritized backlogs using the same Critical/High/Medium/Low/Future rating scale as requirements [Q3, business-requirement-sw-dev].

### BRD-Q4 — Is "data product inventory" distinct from raw "data inventory"?
Yes — explicitly and consistently. A raw data inventory tracks **data assets** (raw, catalogued, technical-metadata-only, centrally/IT-owned, passively discoverable). A data product inventory tracks **data products** (curated, packaged with business+technical metadata, usage policies, SLAs; domain-owned; actively consumable via defined interfaces) [Q4, business-requirement-sw-dev]. This is corroborated independently by the EDA notebook (see EDA-Q3 below) — a genuine cross-source convergence, not a single-source claim.

### BRD-Q5 — Minimum viable, non-production deliverables for an analysis-only scope
For each of the five phase-categories in BRD-Q1, the source gives an explicit "MV artifact" vs. "omitted" split: e.g., governance phase → MV = logical governance framework, ownership model, core business glossary; omits physical AD/LDAP configs, live RBAC code, automated lineage crawlers. Requirements phase → MV = problem statement, personas, prioritized functional/non-functional requirements, testable success metrics; omits physical schemas/API endpoints. Use-case validation → MV = visual prototypes + sample data + recorded sign-off; omits live ETL/UI code. Gap-analysis/architecture phase → MV = logical architecture blueprint + as-is/to-be dimensional matrix; omits hardware/network/VM-level design [Q5, business-requirement-sw-dev]. The universal boundary rule across all categories: *do not start backend/transformation/physical build until requirements are validated and signed off* [Q5, business-requirement-sw-dev].

### EDA-Q1 — Standard components of a "Data Management Framework"; integrated or independent?
Per DAMA-DMBOK, data management comprises **11 disciplines**, of which the framework maps to: Business Glossary (metadata management discipline), Conceptual & Logical Data Models (data architecture discipline), Governance & Stewardship Policies (governance discipline, RACI-based operating model), and Master/Reference Data Management (its own discipline) [Q1, enterprise-data-architecture]. Critically, the source's own explicit recommendation is **NOT** to deliver these as one integrated framework at once: "boiling-the-ocean syndrome" (scoping all domains/components simultaneously) is named as a recognized *failure mode*, and best practice is phased, prioritized, independent delivery — start with the disciplines most urgent to the business, sequence governance before architecture pattern, and scope to the top-10 critical assets rather than the whole estate [Q1, enterprise-data-architecture].

### EDA-Q2 — CDM vs. domain-specific data model (e.g., LDM) relationship
**Gap, confirmed.** The source explicitly states it does not contain information on Loss & Damage data models or on whether a domain model is a specialization within a CDM or a separate artifact type [Q2, enterprise-data-architecture]. Adjacent-but-non-answering material surfaced: Zachman Framework's "Conceptual" tier as one of six classification perspectives (a taxonomy, not a modeling relationship) [Q2, enterprise-data-architecture]; traditional data-warehouse dimensional/star-schema modeling with upfront schema enforcement [Q2, enterprise-data-architecture]; and Data Mesh's domain-oriented model, where domain owners design their own data-product schemas under federated governance rather than a single centrally-owned schema [Q2, enterprise-data-architecture]. None of this establishes a CDM→domain-model specialization relationship — the specific question posed in the query pack remains unanswered by these sources.

### EDA-Q3 — Enterprise data product inventory: structure and difference from raw inventory
A data product inventory structurally captures, per entry: federated domain ownership/stewardship, the underlying data+code+infrastructure, standardized consumption interfaces (APIs, views, sharing options), business metadata/KPI definitions, SLAs/SLOs, multi-dimensional automated quality scores (metadata quality, data quality, availability), and usage/behavioral analytics [Q3, enterprise-data-architecture]. Contrast table vs. raw/source inventory: raw inventory = physical schemas/connections, centralized/reactive IT ownership, unstructured/undocumented, no consumption contract; data product inventory = curated/consumption-oriented, domain-driven ownership, human-readable business glossary mapping, contractual SLAs with automated enforcement ("circuit breakers") [Q3, enterprise-data-architecture]. This is the same distinction as BRD-Q4, independently derived — strong convergent evidence.

### EDA-Q4 — Governance mechanism to keep glossary consistent with an evolving CDM/LDM
The mechanism described is: a centralized metadata platform/catalog acting as single source of truth, linking business metadata (glossary, KPI logic) to technical metadata (schemas, constraints) [Q4, enterprise-data-architecture]; automated metadata harvesting / active metadata (AI-inferred terms, continuous scanning) instead of manual sync [Q4, enterprise-data-architecture]; automated column-level lineage and "blast radius" impact analysis when the model changes [Q4, enterprise-data-architecture]; and human-in-the-loop governance workflows where data stewards must approve AI-suggested glossary/model changes before they take effect [Q4, enterprise-data-architecture]. **Caveat**: every one of the 15 citations in this answer traces to a single source document (`dfd61006-6db9-4046-a052-44523bf8cace`) — this is a single-source answer, not a triangulated one, and should be treated with correspondingly lower confidence.

### EDA-Q5 — What can be deferred vs. must exist at draft stage for a pre-system-design blueprint
Must exist at draft stage: an ~80%-coverage current-state inventory of systems/owners/consumers; identification of the top-10 critical data assets (not the full estate); 6–10 high-level architecture principles (not a full design); regulatory/compliance-to-asset mapping; a governance RACI + stewardship-role assignment; and an initial business glossary with KPI definitions [Q5, enterprise-data-architecture]. Legitimately deferred: specific technology/vendor/tool selection, production ingestion pipelines and storage builds, full Data Mesh/domain-ownership transitions (requires high organizational maturity — premature attempts create "decentralized chaos"), real-time streaming architectures, AI/ML infrastructure, and automated quality monitoring extended across all (non-priority) domains [Q5, enterprise-data-architecture]. Notably, the source used to support "AI/ML infra can be deferred" is itself an **NFR-definition guide** cited only in passing [Q5, enterprise-data-architecture] — consistent with, and indirectly reinforcing, CRDB's decision to treat NFR/RTM as out of scope (TOR70 territory), since even the EDA notebook treats NFR-level concerns as a later-phase artifact.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **Both notebooks independently converge on "phase, don't bundle."** BRD-Q5's phase-by-phase MV/omit split and EDA-Q1/EDA-Q5's explicit "boiling-the-ocean" anti-pattern and phased roadmap say the same thing from two different practice areas: a credible draft-stage deliverable set is built incrementally and prioritized (top-10 assets, 6–10 principles), not delivered as one maximal document. This directly validates CRDB's "coherent-draft, not production-ready, 2-week" strategy as methodologically sound — but it argues *against* compressing CDM+Glossary+Governance+RefData into one single deliverable object (see Hypothesis Check).

2. **The BRD-side discipline table and the EDA-side DAMA-DMBOK disciplines classify components differently, and neither co-locates all four hypothesis items.** BRD-Q1's table puts business glossary under *governance* and CDM under *architecture* as separate disciplines with separate deliverables [Q1, business-requirement-sw-dev]. EDA-Q1 maps glossary to *metadata management*, CDM to *data architecture*, governance/stewardship to *data governance*, and RefData/MDM to its *own* discipline [Q1, enterprise-data-architecture]. Across both sources, these are consistently treated as **related but distinct disciplines**, not sub-parts of a single artifact.

3. **Data product inventory vs. raw inventory is the most solidly triangulated finding of the round** — independently and consistently derived in BRD-Q4 and EDA-Q3 from different source sets, with matching dimensions (refinement level, metadata depth, ownership model, consumption contract). This is a structural gap in the source base, not an answerable-but-missed question — iteration 2 cannot resolve it by re-querying these same notebooks differently; it needs a different (domain-specific) source.

5. **"Sitemap" as a CRDB deliverable was never addressed by either notebook.** No query or answer in either notebook maps to a "sitemap" artifact type. This is a silent gap: DCCE's ask includes sitemap, but neither practice-area source base surfaced any standard equivalent (information architecture practice was not in scope of either notebook).

6. **RefData/Master Data Management is acknowledged as a real, distinct discipline but was never queried on its own structural shape.** EDA-Q1 names MDM as one of the framework's four component disciplines [Q1, enterprise-data-architecture], but no query in this round asked what a "RefData Matrix" deliverable should structurally contain — it was assumed into the bundle rather than independently verified like glossary/CDM/inventory were.

7. **Single-source concentration risk in EDA-Q4.** Unlike every other answer in this round (which drew on 4–9 distinct sources), EDA-Q4's entire answer rests on one document. Given that this question underpins how CRDB would justify glossary-CDM consistency mechanics, it needs deeper triangulation before being treated as settled practice.

## Hypothesis check

**Working hypothesis:** CDM, Glossary, Governance, and RefData Matrix are legitimately sub-parts of ONE "Data Management Framework" deliverable rather than four independent pillars.

**Verdict: Partially corrected, not confirmed as originally framed.**

- **What holds up:** All four items are recognized, related disciplines that belong to the same overall program/framework umbrella (DAMA-DMBOK's 11 disciplines, of which these four are named) [Q1, enterprise-data-architecture]. It is legitimate and even expected to *narrate* them under one "Data Management Framework" story for a dissemination event — this part of the hypothesis is supported.
- **What is corrected:** Standard practice explicitly does **not** recommend producing them as one single, monolithic, simultaneously-built deliverable. Two independent sources (BRD-Q1's discipline table and EDA-Q1's DAMA discipline mapping) classify glossary, CDM, governance, and MDM/RefData as **four separate disciplines with four separate typical deliverables**, and EDA-Q1/Q5 explicitly warn that trying to deliver all framework components at once ("boiling-the-ocean") is a named failure mode that causes programs to "deliver nothing in the first 12 months" [Q1, enterprise-data-architecture]. The credible framing for CRDB's dissemination event is therefore: **one narrative wrapper ("Data Management Framework") containing four clearly-labeled, individually-scoped sub-deliverables** (glossary, CDM, governance/stewardship model, RefData/MDM matrix) — not four pillars merged into an undifferentiated single document. This is a nuance correction to the working hypothesis, not a refutation of its core intuition, but it matters for how the redirection plan should be structured: keep the four as visibly distinct artifacts under one framework heading, each independently coherent-draft-quality, rather than flattening them into one combined document.

**LDM / domain-model question:** **Unresolved — a genuine, confirmed gap**, not a finding either way. Neither the spot-checked EDA-Q2 nor any other file in this round provides evidence on whether an LDM is a specialization within the CDM or a separate artifact type. Treat this as open pending iteration 2, not as "leaning toward X."

**2. Industry Standards Sanity Check (DAMA-DMBOK2, TOGAF, BABOK)**
- **Alignment**: Claude's synthesis aligns perfectly with DAMA-DMBOK2's 11 knowledge areas (with Data Governance at the center, surrounded by Architecture, Modeling, Metadata, etc.). 
- **The "Phase, Don't Bundle" Approach**: This is highly consistent with enterprise architecture frameworks like TOGAF, which emphasizes iterative architecture development (Phase C: Data Architecture) rather than big-bang monolithic deliverables.

**3. Missing Nuances & Practical Recommendations for the 2-Week Scope**
- **Data Product Inventory Feasibility**: While the distinction between Data Inventory and Data Product Inventory is theoretically sound, a true Data Product Inventory (with SLAs, automated quality scores, and consumption contracts) is extremely heavy. For a 2-week "coherent draft" deliverable, it is highly recommended to scope this down to an *MVP Data Inventory* (focusing on identifying top-10 critical data assets and their technical metadata/ownership) rather than attempting a full Data Product Inventory. 
- **The LDM/CDM Gap**: The lack of clarity on the LDM vs. CDM relationship is a known industry pain point. In practice, Domain Models (LDMs) often serve as bounded contexts (borrowing from Domain-Driven Design) that map back to a central, high-level Enterprise Conceptual Model. For the CRDB 2-week scope, do not get bogged down in formalizing this hierarchy. Instead, focus on a high-level conceptual model of the core entities.
- **Sitemap**: As noted, a "sitemap" is an artifact of Information Architecture (UX/UI), not Data Architecture. If DCCE expects a sitemap, it should be treated as a separate UX/Portal deliverable, distinct from the Data Management Framework.
