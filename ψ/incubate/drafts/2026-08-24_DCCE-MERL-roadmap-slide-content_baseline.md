# Drafting baseline: DCCE MERL roadmap strategic review

## Audience and purpose

- Audience: Boss, as author of the original 2026–2030 roadmap.
- Purpose: retain the roadmap's system ambition while adjusting sequence, dependencies, and the role of the fixed 2026 MERL project.
- Decision sought: endorse moderate roadmap modifications and the components to be blended into the current project.

## Required content

1. Assumptions underlying the analysis: fixed TOR, DCCE institutional position, Thai line-agency coordination model, roadmap ambition, risk posture, and analytical limitations.
2. High-risk components in the original plan.
3. Strategic dependencies and activities required to establish them.
4. Proposed additions and modifications to the original roadmap.
5. Focus, components, and key deliverables of the next fixed MERL project.

## Technical structures that must remain visible

- Six components: governance; results framework; data handling; quality review; evaluation and research; learning and use.
- DCCE position: institutional stage two, moving weakly into stage three; monitoring is ahead of evaluation and learning.
- Critical sequence: authority and ownership; access to existing information; repeatable collection; internal quality review; evaluation; learning; management consideration; change; next monitoring round.
- Roadmap expansion controlled by readiness rather than calendar targets alone.
- Three-layer project design: six-sector TOR coverage; one-sector deep operational test with 10–15 existing indicators; system-learning record for the next cycle.
- Fixed project parameters: 240 days, 3 million baht, three delivery stages at days 60, 150, and 240.
- Third-party verification is excluded from this project.
- Data improvements use plain operational language and follow DCCE's pace of adoption.

## NCAIF style controls

- Evidence-first Thai institutional prose.
- Name the responsible actor and product.
- Use direct Thai verbs and restrained claims.
- Avoid dramatic metaphors, promotional language, and unnecessary English terminology.
- Avoid presenting system-design terminology as a new obligation for DCCE or line agencies.
- Distinguish observed condition, analytical inference, recommendation, and deferred ambition.
