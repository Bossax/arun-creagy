# Adaptation M&E standards in Thailand are fragmented

To be super honest, the tasks of developing national M&E frameworks have been poorly executed. The tasks are assigned to different consultants; TDRI (who authored NAP action plan) and Chulalongkorn's department of water resource engineering research group (who authored [`Adaptation M&E framework`](obsidian://open?vault=Arun_Creagy&file=%CF%88%2Fincubate%2FUNDP%2FBTR%2Finbox%2Factive%2F%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B9%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A1%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%A5%E0%B8%B0%E0%B8%9B%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B0%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%9C%E0%B8%A5%20Climate%20Change%20Adaptation) and the data architecture design of DCCE's M&E platform).

However, these two development streams, although claimed to be based on National Adaptation Plan, come from different ground and use different approaches

# TDRI's NAP Action Plan
The goal of NAP action plan is to break down sectoral strategies (แนวทาง) and measures (มาตรการ) that are set by NAP to 'activity'. The project discussed with key line agencies to search for matching 'activity/project' of a line agency. There fore, the 'activities' in the NAP action plan is the actual funded projects of the line agencies who agree that their projects align with NAP's strategy and measure.

NAP also sets measure-level indicators. However, these indicators are broad and not well-defined. This project also refines the broad indicators and point to where exactly variables for indicator calculation can come from. This results in some indicators getting redefined but keeping the same overall target.

**The variable mapping is**
- Strategy:Measrue  is 1:1
- Measure:Activity is 1:1 :LiArrowBigRight: The activities are 'matached' with line agency's projects
- KPI:Measure is many:many which has changed from NAP where KPI:Measure is 1:1. This is many:many. This is because some measure-level indicators are broken down, refined into smaller but practical indicators. By doing so, it also changes the 1:1 relationship between measure and indicator to many:many relationship.
- Even there is a change in how the sectoral goals are measured and attributed (activities and measures), the structure remains unchanged

**Criticism**
- Since NAP's indicators are not well-designed. There is a mix of output (process-based) and outcome level indicators which out a clear hierarchical structure, NAP action plan's refined indicators inherit this structural flaw. 
- The NAP's measure-activity matching is not science-based. The NAP measures are not devised based on comprehensive literature review and adaptation scenario planning either. Hence, NAP action plan cannot go beyond business-as-usual activities executed by line agencies. The whole task can be viewed as 'filling the box' job.
- However, it preserves consistency between National level plan (still level 3 plan in national planning hierarchy, which is not so power full)

# DCCE M&E Platform
This work stream starts with the development of [[ψ/incubate/UNDP/BTR/inbox/active/คู่มือการติดตามและประเมินผล Climate Change Adaptation|คู่มือการติดตามและประเมินผล Climate Change Adaptation]]. This document sets out the framework for adaptation M&E. However, it is deeply flawed (per my comments in the file). It does not make a clear distinction what M&E actually means. The M&E here does not tie to the one of the 4 steps of [[ψ/incubate/UNDP/BTR/inbox/writing_notes/Hypothesis_Adaptation Cycle|Hypothesis_Adaptation Cycle]]. It conflates M&E to "reporting"; that is why it covers physical climate situations which is needed in A-BTR reporting. A-BTR covers 4 phases of the adaptation cycle plus other data fields not specific to climate change adaptation domains. 

In the next project, the [[ψ/incubate/DCCE/CRDB/inbox_source/DCCE-MandE-platform-2025|DCCE-MandE-platform-2025]] is developed by Ditto (software engineer) and the Chulalongkorn researchers. The approach is totally different from NAP action plan. The hierarchical structure of the plan is measure(แนวทาง) and activity(แผนงาน). It does not preserve the 'strategy' and 'measure' of NAP. Instead, it is rooted in sectoral master plans or sectoral adaptation plans devised by the focal points. Relationships of measure:activity is 1: many and activity:indicator is 1:many. This approach can be viewed as even more business-as-usual because the only way that these activities are considered adaptation activities is that they must be developed from the planning process that has incorporated climate risk and impacts. It depends on climate-literacy maturity levels of the line agencies who own the plans. As of July 2026, only 2 sectors that have climate-focused plans; Public Health and Agriculture. The water security sector adopts 'national water management strategic plan', whose process is unknown whether it is grounded in climate change adaptation.
