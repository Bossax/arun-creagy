## Agent assessments

### Claude — Section 1.2

- **Strengths:** comprehensive structure; all required structures and the relative-vs-absolute risk safeguard were present.
- **Failure patterns:** unsupported causal bridge; overclaiming from a predecessor platform; incomplete system/stage framing.
- **Assessment:** the largest substantive rewrite. The receipt passed form and payload shape without validating the truth of the causal mechanism.

### Antigravity — Section 1.3

- **Strengths:** concise participation-to-change narrative; low source-character replacement despite three changed body paragraphs.
- **Failure patterns:** repeated document introduction naming; generic rather than named governance artifact; insufficient decision-use completion.
- **Assessment:** its clean receipt is not comparable to the others: there is no evidence sidecar, the contract is thin, and an Antigravity reviewer reviewed an Antigravity draft.

### Codex — Section 1.4

- **Strengths:** the committed draft clearly separated a design blueprint from an operating platform; its structural content was strongest when read against the actual plan.
- **Failure patterns:** contract calibration accepted a detailed readiness metric that the chapter plan excludes; explanatory order was revised toward data foundation before web presentation.
- **Assessment:** do not score the 16/26/33 deletion against Codex. The contract author and harness calibration failed to resolve a plan conflict before drafting.

## What the harness could not see

1. It accepted a formally shaped evidence-to-action chain without proving its causal mechanism.
2. It did not test the draft against the project stage and the executive-summary plan boundary.
3. It treated clean review outcomes as comparable despite very different contract thickness, sidecar availability, and reviewer independence.
4. It did not require document-level continuity checks for terminology already introduced in an adjacent section.

## Recommended roles and collaboration model

These assignments apply to this CRDB executive-summary workflow, not as a universal ranking of the agents.

| Agent | Main role | Secondary role if another agent is usage-capped |
|---|---|---|
| Codex | Plan and contract architect: map the approved plan to section jobs, evidence boundaries, required structures, and cross-section consistency. | Draft architecture-heavy sections; review plan-boundary compliance. |
| Claude | Evidence-rich drafter: build comprehensive, traceable sections from complex sources while preserving required details. | Build evidence sidecars; verify contract coverage. |
| Antigravity | Compression and narrative editor: turn validated material into concise executive-summary prose, especially process-to-decision storytelling. | Draft shorter participation or transition sections. |

### Recommended handoff

1. Codex establishes the section contract from the approved plan and flags exclusions before drafting.
2. Claude builds the evidence-grounded source draft and traceability sidecar.
3. Antigravity compresses the validated material for executive readers without changing claims.
4. A different agent reviews causal logic and plan compliance; the original drafting agent and a same-family reviewer must not certify that review.
5. Boss resolves substantive comments before the formal editorial gate is rerun.

### Constraints revealed by this review

- Antigravity should not be sole evidence authority or self-reviewer where the contract is thin or a sidecar is absent.
- Claude should receive an independent causal-significance review before a draft is accepted.
- Codex must resolve plan-versus-contract conflicts before drafting begins.
