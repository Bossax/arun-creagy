## 1) Reality check: what the current guide misses

Current guide is strong at:
- making people react to the sitemap with low friction.

Current guide under-captures (does not force explicit answers):
- the key messaging decision already flagged in the sitemap: **Risk determinants vs Impacts/L&D** as the primary framing for provincial profiles
- what users actually need to do on the website (tasks) vs what they only want to read
- what “trust” requires on the page (metadata, last-updated, limitations, contact point)
- what “too technical” looks like (terms needing plain-language + examples)

## 2) Expectation set to capture (workshop outputs)

These are the minimum expectation prompts implied by the sitemap v3, expressed explicitly as **website and content expectations** (what pages exist, what content appears on them, and what makes the pages usable to real users).

### E1 — Provincial profile primary message
Choose one primary headline framing for province profiles (what users want to see first):
- Option A: Risk determinants (Exposure/Vulnerability/Adaptive Capacity)
- Option B: Impacts and Loss & Damage (observed costs + impacts)
- Option C: Both, but with a strict rule for ordering and disclaimers

### E2 — What NCAIF should feel like
For each top-level NCAIF section (website IA node), capture user expectation of the page type:
- “Read a short explainer and reuse it in reports”
- “See a map/indicator and export a figure”
- “Find the official dataset and how to use it”
- “Find who to contact / how to request access”

### E3 — Trust signals required on key pages
For high-stakes pages (province profile, national situation, dataset pages), what trust blocks are required?
- last updated / version
- limitations / safe-use language
- source agency
- contact point

### E4 — “Official baseline” expectation
When users ask “which dataset should I use?”, what is the minimum the website must provide?
- a recommended baseline list
- a comparison view
- a plain-language rule-of-thumb
- a disclaimer only (no recommendation)

### E5 — Minimum information required before a dataset/product is shown
From the user perspective, what must be present on-page before they trust/reuse it?
- cadence
- limitations
- download/access link
- license/classification note (high-level)

### E6 — Uncertainty and methods: how much is enough?
What level of explanation is needed on the website?
- Level 0: one-paragraph plain-language “How to read this”
- Level 1: short methods + limitations
- Level 2: full technical appendix

### E7 — Language clarity and glossary needs
Which terms/labels in the sitemap are unclear, and what is the plain-language replacement?
