## 6.1 Contextual Foundation: Bridging Gap Analysis to Draft Recommendations

### Narrative Flow:
The chapter opens by establishing a direct analytical link between the empirical findings of the gap analysis conducted under Section 5.3.8 and the draft policy and technical recommendations required by TOR Scope Item 5.3.9. The text explains that the national cataloging exercise identified 260 datasets across government agencies, but revealed that the primary constraint facing climate change adaptation in Thailand is not an absolute shortage of scientific observations. Instead, the core impediment lies in how data is curated, verified, and delivered to decision-makers. Over 51 percent of cataloged datasets remain subject to restricted access, and more than 80 percent of inter-agency data requests currently rely on manual, ad-hoc administrative correspondence. These procedural barriers prevent planners from accessing timely and reliable data during local development planning and budgetary review cycles.

The narrative then outlines the five recurring user demands documented during provincial and sectoral consultations: (1) access to officially certified baseline reference datasets, (2) downscaled spatial projections at sub-provincial and sub-district scales, (3) clear translation tools that convert physical climate variables into engineering design criteria and economic valuation metrics, (4) predictable and automated data access protocols, and (5) standardized guidelines for communicating model projection uncertainties. 

To address these five systemic barriers, the narrative establishes the foundational stance of the draft recommendations: the Department of Climate Change and Environment (DCCE) cannot function merely as a passive digital storage repository for external climate files. Instead, DCCE must position itself as the national coordinator and certification authority for climate adaptation information services. The text concludes this introductory section by outlining the structural organization of the draft recommendations, which are structured across two operational axes (Policy and Technical) and mapped directly to the three target components specified in the TOR: Baseline Data Inventory, Information Product Inventory, and Long-Term Data Management.

## 6.2 Policy Recommendations (ข้อเสนอแนะเชิงนโยบาย)

### 6.2.1 Policy for Baseline Data Inventory Development (ด้านการพัฒนารายการชุดข้อมูลพื้นฐาน)

#### Narrative Flow:
This subsection addresses the policy mechanisms required to transition Thailand’s baseline adaptation data from fragmented institutional silos into an accessible national inventory. The narrative argues that past efforts failed because departments relied on informal, personal connections or one-off administrative requests to obtain data. To establish a dependable data supply, DCCE must institute formal technical partnerships with primary data-producing agencies—specifically the Thai Meteorological Department (TMD), the Geo-Informatics and Space Technology Development Agency (GISTDA), the Department of Water Resources (DWR), the Royal Irrigation Department (RID), and the National Statistical Office (NSO). 

The narrative specifies that these partnerships must operate across three structured modalities:
1. *Data Exchange Agreements*: Formalizing bilateral protocols that define data formats, delivery channels, and recurring update schedules.
2. *Methodology Co-Creation*: Collaborating with source agencies to develop shared downscaling, bias-correction, and interpolation standards so that baseline records align with international scientific benchmarks.
3. *Service Co-Design*: Working alongside sector agencies to design user interfaces and data delivery tools that meet the actual operating needs of frontline planners.

Next, the text addresses data accessibility. To resolve the restriction that currently locks 51 percent of national datasets behind administrative gates, DCCE must formulate an institutional data access and classification policy. This policy defines three operational tiers: (1) *Open Public Access* for non-sensitive spatial baselines, historical weather observations, and national risk indexes; (2) *Restricted Government and Academic Access* for detailed cadastral, infrastructure, and demographic records that require user verification and research agreements; and (3) *Confidential Security and Commercial Access* governed by statutory privacy and critical infrastructure protections. By publishing clear data licenses and access terms, DCCE eliminates administrative ambiguity and creates legal certainty for data sharing under the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) and national open data mandates.

### 6.2.2 Policy for Information Product Inventory Development (ด้านการพัฒนารายการผลิตภัณฑ์ข้อมูลและสารสนเทศ)

#### Narrative Flow:
The narrative in this subsection redirects DCCE’s procurement and system development strategy from acquiring software platforms toward managing an ongoing portfolio of information services. The text explains that conventional government IT projects frequently deliver technically functional databases that fall into disuse because they treat the software installation as the final product. To deliver sustained public value, DCCE must manage climate risk systems as an evolving service portfolio organized around specific decision-making workflows.

The narrative introduces four initial core service clusters that DCCE must establish:
1. *Reference Datasets and Data Certification Services*: Publishing authoritative historical climate baselines, official future climate projections, and sector-specific vulnerability indicators that serve as the mandatory reference point for national planning.
2. *Spatial Climate Risk Analysis Services*: Delivering interactive GIS map layers, hazard exposure overlays, and risk index calculations that allow municipal and provincial officers to visualize local climate threats.
3. *Evidence Support Services for Budgeting and Finance*: Providing analytical tools and summary profiles that state agencies and local administrative organizations can cite to justify climate adaptation budget requests before the Bureau of the Budget and international climate funds.
4. *Uncertainty Management and Interpretation Standards*: Providing executive summaries, scenario explanation guides, and confidence-level ratings that help non-specialist administrators interpret multi-model climate projections without misjudging statistical risks.

The narrative concludes by articulating DCCE’s institutional role. DCCE should not duplicate the primary observation networks of specialized line agencies, such as TMD’s weather stations or RID’s hydrological gauges. Instead, DCCE focuses its institutional mandate on establishing data standards, certifying authoritative reference layers, and translating complex climate physics into actionable decision parameters for policy makers, urban planners, and financial evaluators.

### 6.2.3 Policy for Long-Term Data Management (ด้านการบริหารจัดการข้อมูลในระยะยาว)

#### Narrative Flow:
This subsection outlines the organizational and governance policies required to maintain the integrity of adaptation data over multi-year horizons. The narrative explains that software platforms deteriorate rapidly if clear operational ownership and maintenance routines are not institutionalized within the responsible agency. To secure operational continuity, DCCE must establish an internal data governance structure centered on six core operational elements:
1. *Data Owner*: Designating senior division directors with formal authority over dataset release, classification, and access approvals.
2. *Data Steward*: Assigning operational staff responsible for day-to-day data quality monitoring, catalog updates, and inter-agency coordination.
3. *Technical Custodian*: Assigning IT personnel to oversee database architecture, server security, storage backups, and system uptime.
4. *Minimum Metadata Standard*: Enforcing compulsory documentation rules across all cataloged holdings.
5. *Unified Data Dictionary*: Maintaining a standardized vocabulary of climate and adaptation terminology.
6. *Certification Workflow*: Operating an internal review pipeline that validates datasets before they receive official publication status.

The narrative emphasizes that DCCE must formalize this structure by issuing a departmental order establishing a dedicated Data Governance Working Committee within the Climate Change Adaptation Division.

The text then addresses institutional protection for administrative decision-making. In public administration, civil servants are frequently hesitant to base infrastructure plans, zoning ordinances, or budget allocations on probabilistic climate models due to audit concerns and potential legal liability. To overcome this systemic risk aversion, DCCE must institute an official review and certification mechanism. When local officers utilize DCCE-certified climate reference projections and approved assessment methodologies, the administrative validity of their planning documents is institutionalized, protecting officers against claims of arbitrary decision-making.

Finally, the narrative details policies for resource allocation, technical infrastructure, and staff development. The text notes that maintaining analytical systems requires continuous investment in cloud infrastructure capable of handling high-volume spatial queries and automated API traffic. Furthermore, DCCE must move beyond general administrative staffing by developing multi-disciplinary professional networks that connect climate data scientists with sector engineers, environmental economists, and hydrological modelers. To support these operational requirements over the long term, DCCE must actively pursue technical assistance and grant co-financing from international mechanisms, such as the Green Climate Fund (GCF) and the Global Environment Facility (GEF).

## 6.3 Technical Recommendations (ข้อเสนอแนะเชิงเทคนิค)

### 6.3.1 Technical Specifications for Baseline Data Inventory Development (ด้านการพัฒนารายการชุดข้อมูลพื้นฐาน)

#### Narrative Flow:
The technical recommendations establish concrete specifications across two foundational data layers: the Metadata Standard and the Conceptual Data Model (CDM). 

The narrative details the metadata specification, which establishes 12 mandatory attributes that every incoming dataset must possess prior to catalog entry. These attributes comply with international geographic standards (ISO 19115) and national guidelines issued by the Digital Government Development Agency (DGA):
1. *Dataset Unique Identifier* (machine-readable system code)
2. *Official Dataset Name* (standardized Thai and English nomenclature)
3. *Source Agency and Organizational Unit*
4. *Contact Information of Dataset Custodian*
5. *Geographic Bounding Box and Spatial Coverage*
6. *Spatial Resolution* (grid cell size or administrative unit boundary)
7. *Temporal Range* (baseline historical period and projection horizons)
8. *Update Frequency* (real-time, monthly, quarterly, annual, decadal)
9. *Access and Licensing Restrictions* (open, restricted, confidential)
10. *Technical File Format and Encoding Standard* (GeoJSON, NetCDF, GeoTIFF, CSV)
11. *Quality Assurance and Certification Status*
12. *Associated Conceptual Data Model Domain*

The narrative then details the Conceptual Data Model (CDM), which serves as the structural framework linking disparate datasets into a unified analytical graph. The CDM organizes all adaptation data into eight interrelated domains: (1) *Climate Hazard*, (2) *Physical and Social Exposure*, (3) *Vulnerability and Sensitivity*, (4) *Observed and Projected Impact*, (5) *Adaptation Measures and Projects*, (6) *Finance and Resource Allocation*, (7) *Greenhouse Gas and Climate Science Reference*, and (8) *Spatial and Administrative Context*. Accompanying the CDM is a Unified Data Dictionary that standardizes cross-cutting variables (e.g., standard definitions for drought intensity indices, flood return periods, and population vulnerability classes), eliminating semantic conflicts between datasets from different ministries.

### 6.3.2 Technical Specifications for Information Product Inventory Development (ด้านการพัฒนารายการผลิตภัณฑ์ข้อมูลและสารสนเทศ)

#### Narrative Flow:
This subsection outlines the technical architecture needed to transform raw cataloged datasets into decision-support products. The narrative specifies three mandatory technical layers:

First, DCCE must establish a centralized *National Risk Methodology Catalog (ระบบสารบัญระเบียบวิธีวิเคราะห์ความเสี่ยง)*. The text notes that risk assessments in Thailand currently suffer from severe methodological divergence: one agency uses qualitative indicator matrices while another applies complex probabilistic hydrodynamic simulations, leading to conflicting risk priorities for the same geographic area. The technical catalog documents approved assessment algorithms, categorizes their theoretical foundations (indicator-based versus process-based probabilistic models), and provides clear applicability criteria indicating which method is valid for specific planning contexts (e.g., district-level master plans versus major engineering infrastructure designs).

Second, the text defines *Science-to-Decision Translation Standards*. The narrative explains that municipal engineers and finance officers cannot directly interpret NetCDF files containing global climate model projections of sea surface temperatures or atmospheric circulation patterns. DCCE must develop standardized mathematical transfer functions, proxy metrics, and calculation manuals that convert physical climate outputs (such as millimeter changes in 24-hour rainfall intensity) into concrete engineering design criteria (e.g., revised stormwater drainage return-period curves) and economic asset damage estimates.

Third, the narrative specifies *Uncertainty Communication Standards*. Because climate projections inevitably contain epistemic and model uncertainty across multiple greenhouse gas emission pathways (SSPs), technical products must avoid presenting single deterministic numbers that mislead decision-makers. The technical protocol requires that all analytical visualizations and summary reports explicitly distinguish between scenario choices and probabilistic forecast ranges, present numerical confidence intervals (e.g., 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles), and clearly disclose underlying model assumptions and spatial resolution limitations.

### 6.3.3 Technical Specifications for Long-Term Data Management (ด้านการบริหารจัดการข้อมูลในระยะยาว)

#### Narrative Flow:
This subsection details the automated data pipelines, validation rules, and progressive implementation horizons that ensure the long-term technical sustainability of the adaptation platform. 

The narrative specifies the technical requirements for *Interoperability and Automated Data Services*. DCCE must build RESTful Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) and standard Web Feature Services (OGC WFS/WMS) to automate data exchange between DCCE and external partner agencies. The technical design mandates strict API version control, OAuth 2.0 authentication protocols, machine-readable data payloads, and comprehensive technical documentation to enable automated ingestion without requiring manual file uploads by human operators.

Next, the text details the implementation of *Automated CMS Quality Validation Rules*. Rather than relying solely on manual proofreading, DCCE’s Content Management System (CMS) must embed technical validation scripts directly into its data entry and ingestion pipelines. When a data steward or external agency submits a dataset, the automated validation rules immediately verify compliance with the 12 mandatory metadata fields, validate spatial boundary coordinates, verify file encoding integrity, and check parameter nomenclature against the Unified Data Dictionary. Any submission failing these automated checks is rejected with an error log, preventing incomplete or corrupted records from entering the national catalog.

The chapter concludes with an analytical *Three-Horizon Developmental Roadmap* that structures implementation into clear, manageable phases:
- **Horizon 1: Foundational Credibility (Short-Term, Year 1)**: Focuses on internal governance setup, issuing the 12-attribute metadata standard, publishing the Unified Data Dictionary, and completing the registration and quality certification of DCCE’s internal digital assets.
- **Horizon 2: Service Enablement and Connectivity (Medium-Term, Years 2–3)**: Deploys automated API ingestion pipelines, establishes bilateral data exchanges with the first five core source agencies, launches pilot spatial risk and budget screening services, and issues user manuals for provincial planners.
- **Horizon 3: Systemic Institutional Integration (Long-Term, Years 4–5)**: Scales automated data pipelines across all relevant line agencies, operationalizes multi-model ensemble analysis, integrates climate risk certification directly into the national e-Budgeting system, and supports international reporting obligations under the UNFCCC.
